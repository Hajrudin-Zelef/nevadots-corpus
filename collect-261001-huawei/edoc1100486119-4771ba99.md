                    Foreword
    ⚫     As a pioneering innovation in WLAN converged sensing and communication, channel state information
          (CSI) sensing has emerged against the backdrop of the evolution of wireless communication
          technologies and the diversification of application scenarios. With the rapid development of WLAN
          technologies, especially the popularization and performance improvement of Wi-Fi 7, people's
          requirements for wireless networks are no longer limited to improved data transmission rates. Also,
          environment sensing capabilities are required. It is in this context that CSI sensing technology emerges.

    ⚫     The core of CSI sensing lies in utilizing the propagation characteristics of wireless signals to obtain
          information, such as object movement and location changes in the environment, by analyzing CSI.



         For more information about CSI sensing technologies, see CSI Sensing Technology White Paper.

                       Key General Constraints on CSI Sensing
  Key general constraints on CSI sensing:
  1. The CSI sensing function can operate only on radios with Wi-Fi services enabled. When an AP is configured to enter the power-saving mode or its radios are disabled, the CSI
     sensing function of the AP becomes unavailable. CSI sensing requires that the channel utilization of Wi-Fi services be less than 50%, and the function increases the channel
     utilization by less than 3%. Otherwise, detection results may be inaccurate.
  2. If there are continuously moving objects or living beings within the sensing area, such as fans, robotic vacuum cleaners, curtains with metallic fittings (high reflection coefficient),
     mechanically vibrating equipment, trees outside the window, or pets, the system may misinterpret these as human motions, micro-motions, or breathing. Therefore, ensure that
     the environment is free of such interference sources.
  3. At least 40 MHz channel width must be configured for AP channels used for CSI sensing. (In hotel scenarios, at least 80 MHz channel width is required.) Currently, 20 MHz
     channel width is not supported.
  4. If obstacles that cause penetration loss greater than 10 dB in the 5 GHz frequency band (such as concrete walls, solid wood doors, and solid wood tables) completely block
     signals, the CSI sensing function will be unavailable.
  5. For obstacles with a 5 GHz penetration attenuation of less than 5 dB, such as glass, thin wood panels, or gypsum boards, the CSI sensing of APs is subject to the following
     restrictions:
  • If the door is located in a corner, you need to walk one or two steps into the room after opening the door to trigger the light to switch on. To prevent lights from being turned
     on by mistake when people walk outside the walls, it is recommended that the light-on distance be set to the radius of the inscribed circle.
  • If a person walks close to the glass outside the room, there is an extremely low probability that the lights are turned on by mistake (less than 0.1% for a single time, with a 1-
     meter error that cannot be completely eliminated).
  • If people keep walking in the corridor outside the room or in the adjacent room, the lights may not be turned off in a timely manner or may not be turned off at all. The lights can
     be turned off normally when no one is present at night.
           • Light-on distance: Set it to the radius of the inscribed circle in the room (to prevent false triggering).
           • Light-off distance: Set it to the radius of the external tangent circle (to avoid turning off the lights by mistake). If there is strong external interference, set it to the radius of
              the internal tangent circle (but there may be small-scale missing detection in the corners of the room).
  6. If existing AP locations are not adjusted for CSI network planning, or if the room layout is irregular or deployment is non-standard, CSI sensing may result in over-coverage or
      blind spots, and the CSI sensing effect cannot be guaranteed. It is recommended that only the "light-off" function be enabled and the detection distance be set to cover the
      entire room. False detection outside the room is allowed. Even if interference occurs, the light can still be turned off at night when no one is in the room.
  7. For X776 models with 4T4R radios, CSI sensing is not supported on 2T2R radios. Ensure that the 4T4R radios are enabled. (Note: If a model supports 4T4R only on 6 GHz radios,
      CSI sensing is available only in countries or regions where 6 GHz is supported). For models with only 2T2R radios, CSI sensing is supported only on radio 2 (third radio); therefore,
      ensure that radio 2 is enabled. For other capabilities, refer to the specifications.
  8. For XX73 series, CSI sensing is supported only on 5 GHz radios. In scenarios such as hotels that depend on breathing detection, the radio bandwidth must be set to at least 80
      MHz. In scenarios such as conference rooms that do not depend on breathing detection, the radio bandwidth must be set to at least 40 MHz.

                                                                   Deployment suggestions and constraints
  •   Use APs that support CSI sensing (4 or more spatial streams). The inter-site distance between APs must be less than 12 m to ensure complete coverage. If the distance is greater than 12
      m, detection holes will occur.
  •   For office buildings with atriums, to reduce cross-floor interference, the distance between APs and glass curtains on the atrium side must be greater than 3 m, as shown in the preceding
      figure.
  •   If an AP is close to a wall made of glass, thin wood boards, or gypsum boards, signals will extend into the conference room, causing false detection. It is recommended that the AP be
      placed at least 3 m away from such walls and the sensing distance be adjusted to reduce false detection.
  •   CSI sensing is affected by the Wi-Fi signal strength. Different room structures and wall materials affect the identification effect. Therefore, you need to configure a proper sensing distance
      based on the site requirements.
  •   In indoor open energy-saving scenarios, the application system must consider the detection results of multiple APs in the area and make an energy-saving judgment. CSI sensing is not
      applicable to precise light control.

                                                              Deployment suggestions and constraints

   • APs supporting CSI sensing (4 or more spatial streams) are recommended for coverage. 2T2R APs are recommended for small conference rooms when additional APs are
     required. Wall penetration coverage is not recommended.
   • Configure the intrusion detection function on iMaster NCE-CampusInsight. (This function is also applicable to conference rooms.)
   • When an AP is deployed in a conference room with partition walls made of glass, thin wood boards, or gypsum boards, Wi-Fi signals can easily penetrate the walls. In this
     case, signals are extended to the outside of the conference room and are easily affected by passers-by, causing misjudgments. Therefore, the AP must be deployed at least
     3 m away from glass walls, so as to control the sensing range within the room. The sensing distance sensitivity level can be adjusted to adjust the sensing distance and
     reduce misjudgments. For details, see the network planning guide.
   • In a conference room, there may be breath-like interference such as wind-blown curtains and disturbances outside glass walls. Therefore, you are advised to disable breath
     detection to prevent continuous misjudgments as human breaths.
   • In energy-saving scenarios such as conference rooms, the human absence-to-presence sensing delay is 3s (device: 2.3s, including 0.2s as the reserved network transmission
     delay, and 0.5s for other upper-layer applications, ensuring that the total delay meets the requirements of network transmission or application processing).
   • In conference room scenarios, motion and micro-motion detection is supported, allowing recognition of small actions such as raising the head, lifting a hand, or shifting
     the body. However, detection is not supported when the human body remains completely still or when only hand movements occur. When a person is stationary or
     moving, it may be incorrectly detected as unattended. Therefore, it is recommended that the application system should determine no person in a conference room when
     there is no person for more than 10 minutes, and then perform energy-saving processing such as turning off lights. Alternatively, set the presence aging time for APs to
     more than 10 minutes.
   • If the distance sensing sensitivity level needs to be adjusted due to partition walls (such as glass partitions), the sensing radio channel must be fixed and the AP installation
     direction must remain unchanged before the sensitivity adjustment. If the channel or direction is changed, the sensitivity needs to be re-adjusted. Note: In R025C00 and
     later versions, this limitation is eliminated because it is recommended that the sending distance range be configured.

 1. Suites or large resort hotel rooms cannot be fully covered by a single AP. In this case, multiple APs are required for a room.
 2. The AP cannot be installed behind a TV, behind metal decorative panels, or inside thick wooden cabinets. Thin wooden panels or gypsum board can be used for decorative concealment.
 3. Ensure that no air-conditioning outlet is located within 1 meter around the AP to prevent false detections caused by airflow.
 4. When the AP is wall-mounted in a concealed manner, the area directly adjacent to the wall on the mounting side lies in the antenna's side-lobe null zone, where micro-motion detection may be ineffective.
 5. A centrally deployed AP cannot cover the doorway area, which may affect light-switch automation. As such, an additional independent sensor needs to be installed at the doorway for coordinated detection.
 6. Solid walls or partitions of bathrooms cause significant signal penetration loss. Additional independent sensors are required to cover these areas for coordinated detection.
 7. In hotel scenarios, the system supports motion and micro-motion detection, capable of recognizing small actions such as head raising, hand waving, or body swaying. Static (respiratory) detection is only supported
 directly in front of the AP (bed area). To avoid missed detections when transitioning from the human presence to absence state, it is recommended that the energy-saving application system require continuous detection
 of human absence for more than 10 minutes before triggering actions such as turning off lights, or configure the human absence aging time on the AP to at least 10 minutes.
 8. To prevent false detections outside the room, you need to configure the detection distance from an AP to the door.
