## Which general assistant should you pick?

Three general assistants dominate student use, and universities have started buying them. In the HEPI 2026 survey, 38% of students said their institution provides an AI tool, up from 9% in 2024, most often ChatGPT, then Microsoft Copilot. The differences below are about caps, context and study modes, not raw intelligence.

Check the campus licence first. A seat that is already paid for changes every other decision.

### ChatGPT: Best for everyday questions and drafting

ChatGPT is the tool most students already have. In the Digital Education Council's 2024 Global AI Student Survey of 3,839 students in 16 countries, 66% used it, more than double the next tool, and OpenAI's own student page pitches it as study partner, career coach and daily assistant.

The Free plan now allows unlimited text chats, subject to abuse guardrails, with limited uploads, limited deep research and roughly 12 pages of input text per prompt, according to the ChatGPT pricing page (checked Sep 2026). Study mode, which works through a problem step by step instead of handing over the answer, is included on every plan, Free included.

Go costs $8/month and may show ads. Plus, at $20/month, adds Projects, scheduled tasks, custom GPTs, larger uploads and the advanced reasoning models. OpenAI lists no student discount for Plus; ChatGPT Edu exists, but the university buys it, not the student.

What it does not do is read a course. Files are uploaded per chat or Project, and there is no LMS sync.

### Claude: Best for long readings and writing feedback

Claude's context window runs up to 1 million tokens depending on the model, which suits a semester of readings or a 60-page thesis draft pasted in one go.

The claude.com pricing page (checked Sep 2026) lists a Free plan with web search, file creation, memory and up to 5 Projects; Pro at $20/month, or $17/month billed annually, with unlimited Projects, Research and more models; and Max from $100/month.

The feature students ask about is Learning mode. Anthropic introduced it with Claude for Education in April 2025: instead of answering, Claude asks how the student would approach the problem and what evidence supports the conclusion. The mode opened to all users as a style option in August 2025.

There is no student discount. Claude for Education is an institutional plan, so access at Northeastern, LSE or Champlain comes through the university's licence, not a .edu checkbox.

For essays, the practical use is feedback on an argument's structure. With Learning mode on, that is also the default behaviour.

### Google Gemini: Best for the free student AI Pro year

The offer decides this one. On 19 August 2026, Google gave eligible US college students 12 months of Google AI Pro free, a plan that otherwise costs $19.99/month, with students in 140+ other markets getting a year of Google AI Plus instead. Redemption runs until 31 December 2026, requires SheerID verification and a payment method, and the plan auto-renews at $19.99/month after the trial.

The bundle matters as much as the model: higher Gemini usage limits, Gemini inside Gmail and Docs, 5 TB of Drive storage, and NotebookLM at Pro limits.

Two study features sit on the free tier. Guided Learning, launched August 2025, breaks problems into steps and quizzes instead of answering outright. The Student Hub at gemini.google.com/students, launched with the 2026 offer, builds Study Notebooks from an uploaded syllabus, runs a diagnostic quiz to find gaps, and can push exam dates into Google Calendar.

Set a renewal reminder on day one. A free year claimed in September starts billing in September 2027.

## Which tools handle readings and research?

The reading pile is where general chatbots run out of context and where two specialists earn a place. One works only from what is uploaded. The other works only from the live web.

### NotebookLM: Best for turning readings into overviews

NotebookLM answers only from the sources inside a notebook, which is the point. Google's help center (checked Sep 2026) sets the free Standard plan at 100 notebooks, 50 sources per notebook, 50 chats a day, 3 Audio Overviews and 3 Video Overviews a day, 10 flashcard or quiz generations a day, and 10 Deep Research runs a month. Google's education documentation puts each source at up to 500,000 words.

Google AI Pro, free for a year under the student offer above, raises that to 500 notebooks, 300 sources per notebook, 500 chats and 20 Audio Overviews a day.

The workflow for a reading-heavy course: drop the week's PDFs and lecture slides into one notebook, generate a mind map, then an Audio Overview for the commute. Answers carry citations back to the passage, and Google states uploads are not used to train the model unless feedback is submitted.

NotebookLM has no LMS connection. Each file is still uploaded by hand.

### Perplexity: Best for cited web answers

Perplexity's answer engine returns numbered citations on every response and searches the live web, which makes it the fact-checking layer of a student stack rather than the drafting layer. Its help center (checked Sep 2026) lists Pro at $20/month or $200/year, with unlimited Pro searches, file uploads and a choice of underlying models.

Students get the same plan for half the price. Education Pro is $10/month after SheerID verification of university enrolment, and adds Study Mode with flashcards and quizzes plus extended access to Perplexity Academic, a mode that restricts sources to scholarly papers. The Free plan keeps the citations but limits Pro searches to a small daily allowance.

Comet, Perplexity's browser, is free and pairs with the Pro plan. For coursework, the practical use is verifying a claim before it enters an essay, then following the citation to the primary source.

Perplexity does not read an LMS, a lecture recording or a scanned textbook chapter.

## Which tools capture lectures and organize notes?

Lecture capture and note organization are separate jobs with separate leaders. One records the room. The other files the semester.

### Otter.ai: Best for standalone lecture and meeting transcripts

Otter's free Basic plan gives 300 transcription minutes a month, a 30-minute cap per recording and 3 file imports for the life of the account, according to the Otter pricing page (checked Sep 2026). A 50-minute lecture therefore has to be split into two recordings on Basic, which is why students who rely on it move to Pro.

Pro costs $16.99/month, or $8.49/month billed annually, and lifts the limits to 1,200 minutes a month, 90 minutes per recording and 10 imports a month. Students and teachers with a .edu address get 20% off: $13.59/month, or $79.99 a year.

The strengths are speaker identification, live transcription inside Zoom, Teams and Meet, and an AI chat that answers across past recordings, which makes Otter strong for group projects and seminar discussions.

It records a session. It does not connect that session to the readings or the exam.

### Notion: Best for organizing a semester

Notion's student offer is the cleanest on this list: individual students and educators at accredited universities get the Plus plan free by signing up with a school email, per Notion's education help page. That lifts the Free plan's 5 MB upload cap, extends page history to 30 days and allows 100 guests, in a single-member workspace.

The catch is the AI. Since Notion retired its AI add-on in 2025, full Notion AI comes only with the Business plan, $20 per member per month billed annually or $24 monthly, while Free and Plus include a limited trial of AI responses. The free student plan is a planner, database and notes tool first; the AI writing and Q&A layer is a paid upgrade.

For a semester system, that is still a lot: an assignment database with due dates, one page per course, and thousands of templates shared by student communities.

The AskSia roundup of note-taking apps for students compares Notion with Obsidian, GoodNotes and Apple Notes on the note-capture job specifically.

## Which tools polish writing and drill recall?

The last two jobs happen at the end of the week: cleaning up a draft and memorizing what the draft was about.

### Grammarly: Best for polishing essays

Grammarly's parent company renamed itself Superhuman in October 2025; the product kept its name and its job. The Free plan checks grammar, spelling and tone inside Google Docs, Word, Gmail and most browsers, with 100 AI prompts a month. Pro costs $12/month billed annually ($144 a year) or $30 month to month, per the plans page checked Sep 2026, and raises the allowance to 2,000 prompts.

The Pro features that matter for graded work: plagiarism detection against web pages and academic databases, an AI-text detector, full-sentence rewrites, and citation checks that flag formatting errors in APA, MLA and Chicago. Grammarly's own student-features page frames the citation tool around points lost to formatting.

Two limits apply. The checker is built for English, and the plans page lists no student discount.

Some universities licence it campus-wide: HEPI's 2026 survey lists Grammarly among the tools institutions provide.

### Quizlet: Best for shared flashcard sets

Quizlet's advantage is the library: hundreds of millions of student-made sets, and a reasonable chance that a classmate's deck for the same course already exists. The iOS app holds a 4.8-star rating across more than 1 million App Store ratings (checked Sep 2026).

The free plan has narrowed. Quizlet's help center confirms Learn mode is a Plus feature, with free users limited to a set number of rounds per set, and Test mode allows one practice test per set, with ads. Plus costs $7.99/month or $35.99 a year and adds Magic Notes, which turns pasted notes into study sets, the Q-Chat tutor, offline access and custom images.

Even Plus keeps monthly caps on Learn rounds and practice tests (20 rounds and 3 tests a month, per third-party captures of the upgrade page in July and August 2026). Plus Unlimited, $44.99 a year, removes them.

For decks built from scratch, the free spaced-repetition options in the Quizlet alternatives roundup cover the same job at $0. Quizlet earns its place when the set already exists.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there any AI better than ChatGPT?

For specific jobs, yes; as a general assistant, the gap is small. ChatGPT remains the most used tool, cited by 66% of the 3,839 students in the Digital Education Council's 2024 survey, but the same survey found students average 2.1 AI tools each, which is the real answer: they add a second tool for a job ChatGPT does less well. Perplexity attaches numbered citations to every answer, so it wins for claims that must be verified. NotebookLM answers only from uploaded sources, 50 per notebook on its free plan, so it wins for a reading pack. Claude's context window reaches 1 million tokens on some models, which suits long drafts. AskSia answers from a synced course and cites the page, slide or timestamp. The test is simple: run the same question through two tools with your own lecture deck attached and check which answer points back to the slide. AskSia's Multi-source Q&A is built for exactly that check.

### Which AI do students use the most?

ChatGPT, by a wide margin, on every published survey. The Digital Education Council's 2024 Global AI Student Survey (3,839 students, 16 countries) put ChatGPT at 66%, with Grammarly and Microsoft Copilot next at 25% each. The HEPI/Kortext 2026 survey of 1,054 UK undergraduates reported which tools universities provide: where an institution offers one, now the case for 38% of students, up from 9% in 2024, it is most often ChatGPT, followed by Microsoft Copilot, with Gemini, Perplexity and Grammarly also named. Usage itself is near universal: 95% use AI in some way and 94% use it for assessed work. What the numbers do not show is a single winner by task, which is why the profiles above are sorted by job. Before paying for anything, check the university's IT or library portal for a campus licence; a Copilot or ChatGPT Edu seat that is already paid for changes the maths on every other subscription.

More of them than the price lists suggest, once student offers are counted. Free tiers with no verification: ChatGPT Free (unlimited text chats, Study mode included), Claude Free (5 Projects, web search), NotebookLM Standard (100 notebooks, 50 sources each), Grammarly Free (100 AI prompts a month), Otter Basic (300 minutes a month), Quizlet's free plan and AskSia's Free plan, none of which require a card. Verification-based offers as of September 2026: Google AI Pro free for 12 months for eligible US college students, with AI Plus for students in 140+ other markets, redeemable until 31 December 2026; Notion Plus free with a school email; Perplexity Education Pro at $10/month; Otter Pro at 20% off; AskSia at 15% off with a .edu address. Deadlines and eligibility change, so confirm on the vendor's own page before relying on any of them. For a task-by-task list of no-cost options, see the AskSia guide to free AI homework helpers.

### Are AI tools allowed in college?

Usually yes, within limits set per course rather than per campus, and the limits are moving. In the HEPI/Kortext 2026 survey, 65% of students said assessment had changed significantly in response to AI, some reporting harder exams and others AI built into assignments, while 37% felt their institution encouraged AI use and 36% did not. The share pasting AI-generated text directly into assessed work rose to 12%, from 8% in 2025 and 3% in 2024, which is the behaviour most integrity policies target. Tools that explain, summarise or quiz are generally permitted; submitting generated text as original work generally is not. Read the unit outline for the specific permission level and keep drafts and prompts as evidence of process. The AskSia explainer on where the AI line actually sits covers the common frameworks, and AskSia's AI detector flags AI-written sentences in your own drafts before submission.

### What is the best AI tool for engineering students?

A pairing, because engineering coursework needs two things a chatbot alone does poorly: symbolic accuracy and sources. For derivations and worked steps, ChatGPT's Study mode and Claude's Learning mode both guide through a problem rather than dumping a result; ChatGPT's reasoning models sit on Plus at $20/month, while Claude Free handles most homework-length problems. For datasheets, standards and papers, Perplexity Education Pro at $10/month returns numbered citations and an Academic mode. Photo-based solvers such as Photomath and Wolfram Alpha are compared in the AskSia guide to the best AI for math. Inside AskSia, the AI Solver takes a photo of a problem, the AI tutor explains the same problem three different ways, and the Concept Map lays out a course from foundations to synthesis, which matters in prerequisite-heavy programs such as control systems or signals. Whichever pairing you choose, verify every numeric result against a worked example from the course, since all of these tools can produce plausible wrong answers.
