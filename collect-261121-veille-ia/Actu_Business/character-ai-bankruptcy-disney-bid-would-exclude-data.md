Character.AI filed a voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy on October 12, 2026 to sell the company under court supervision, and says the platform will be wound down if no bid lets it keep operating. Disney, the opening bidder, has proposed a $20 million licence that, according to the Bankruptcy Observer, is “not for the company or its app” and leaves out user data, so on the proposed terms the bid does not include your chats. What happens to chats under any other buyer is the open question: the New York attorney general’s October 14 release warns that the data “could be transferred to a new owner through bankruptcy”, and bids are due November 24, 2026.

## Filed October 12: a court-supervised sale, or a wind-down if no one bids

Character.AI’s own post says the Chapter 11 process exists “to facilitate a court-supervised sale of the company”. The company hopes the sale yields a proposal that allows continued operations; if no such bid is available, the platform will be wound down. The company says its privacy and security practices are unchanged and that your information stays under its Privacy Policy. On October 15, 2026 it filed the Notice of Commencement of the case, which includes the meeting of creditors under section 341 of the Bankruptcy Code.

In its October 14 release, the New York attorney general puts the platform at more than 20 million monthly users, more than two million of them accounts registered to users self-declared as under 18. The release says Character.AI holds private chats and voice calls, mental health disclosures, ages and identifying device data, and that the company earns revenue from that information and uses it to train its AI. What the filing means for Character.AI’s finances and its competitors is set out in a separate report on the Chapter 11 fallout for AI rivals.

## Disney’s $20 million buys a licence, and the licence leaves your chats out

The court record confirms who the bidder is. Character.AI’s transaction notice, filed in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware under case No. 26-11603 (BLS), says the court approved a Stalking Horse Transaction Agreement dated October 13, 2026 with Disney Worldwide Services, Inc. for the sale or licence of assets the notice calls the “Disney Target Assets”, subject to higher or better offers under the bidding procedures (motion at Docket No. 15, order of November 4 at Docket No. 106; the notice says copies are available through the company’s claims agent, Stretto, Inc.). The price and the exclusion list come from the Bankruptcy Observer, which reports that Disney is offering $20 million for a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive licence to some of Character.AI’s technology, and that the licence excludes, among other things, the consumer app, user data, personal information, user inputs and outputs, training data and finished models. We have that exclusion list only from the Observer, not from the agreement’s own schedules. It is a proposal, not an approved sale. The table sets the categories the attorney general says Character.AI holds against those exclusions.

| Data or asset | Where it is described | In Disney’s proposed licence? | 
|---|---|---|
| Private chats and voice calls | Collected, per the New York attorney general | Excluded (user data, inputs and outputs) | 
| Mental health disclosures, ages, device data | Collected, per the New York attorney general | Excluded (user data and personal information) | 
| Training data | Exclusion list, per the Bankruptcy Observer | Excluded | 
| Finished models | Exclusion list, per the Bankruptcy Observer | Excluded | 
| Specified Character.AI technology and IP | The “Disney Target Assets” in the October 13, 2026 agreement, per the transaction notice; licence terms per the Bankruptcy Observer | Included: perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive | 
| The company and its consumer app | Exclusion list, per the Bankruptcy Observer | Excluded; any bid for them is due November 24 | 

As we read the two sources, the exclusions are terms of Disney’s licence, which covers some technology rather than the company or its app. They do not bind a different bidder for the company and app, the part Disney is not buying, and that is the case the attorney general’s transfer warning describes. Training is the harder question: the licence excludes training data and finished models, while the attorney general says Character.AI uses private conversations to train its AI, and the states have asked the court for limits on training use without valid consent.

## Bids due November 24, auction December 1, sale hearing December 3 and 4

Character.AI’s transaction notice sets the schedule in the table below.

| Date | Event | 
|---|---|
| October 12 | Character.AI announces the voluntary Chapter 11 process | 
| October 14 | New York attorney general and 39 others file in the bankruptcy | 
| October 15 | Notice of Commencement filed, with the section 341 creditors’ meeting | 
| November 4 | Bidding procedures order entered | 
| November 24, 12:00 p.m. ET | Bid deadline | 
| December 1, 10:00 a.m. ET | Auction | 
| December 3 and 4 | Sale hearing | 

In our assessment, the sale hearing is the date to measure user decisions against. It is the last step on the published calendar, and the attorneys general have said they may object to sale procedures or a bankruptcy plan.

## What 40 attorneys general asked the court to require before any sale

New York Attorney General Letitia James and 39 other attorneys general, a bipartisan group, filed in the bankruptcy on October 14, 2026, according to the New York release, warning that Character.AI should not be sold without strong protections for the people whose private conversations it holds. Their requests are a comprehensive privacy program; plain disclosure of how personal data is used; working opt-outs from targeted advertising; limits on using private chats to train AI or for other commercial purposes unless users validly consent; and a chance for consumers to see and delete their information before any sale. They also kept open the option of asking for an independent consumer privacy ombudsman and of objecting to sale procedures or a plan. Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Campbell’s office titled its October 14, 2026 release “AG Campbell Seeks Consumer Protections In Character.AI Bankruptcy”, and it says she joined the coalition that filed the notice that day.

For parents, the Washington attorney general’s release says Character.AI has recently restricted minors’ access to certain chatbot features on its own initiative, but the states regard those limits as optional, partial, and something a later buyer could work around or undo. The New York release cites independent testing in the filing that logged 669 harmful interactions in 50 hours on accounts registered to children aged 12 to 15, roughly one every four and a half minutes by our arithmetic. The California attorney general says several states, California among them, began investigating after a 2024 lawsuit claiming that the company’s chatbots contributed to a child’s death by suicide. Wisconsin’s filing and the wider 40-state challenge get their own report.

## On the company’s estimate, a data export requested now does not arrive before the sale hearing opens

Character.AI’s help page on downloading your data says the export is requested through profile settings, may take 3-4 weeks, and arrives as an email with a download link to the address on your account. The link is valid for 7 days from the time the email is sent. Adding 21 to 28 days to a request made on November 12 gives December 3 to December 10, so the earliest arrival is the opening day of the sale hearing, and the link then lasts until between December 10 and December 17. A request on November 19 arrives December 10 to December 17, and one on November 26 arrives December 17 to December 24, after the hearing. These dates are our calculation from the company’s estimate, worked out on November 12, 2026, not measured delivery times; each later day of requesting pushes the whole window back a day.

Set against the schedule, Character.AI’s stated expectation, in its October 12 post, of finishing the sale by the end of November or early December comes before, or around the same time as, the earliest an export requested on November 12 could land. In our reading, the export is a way to keep a copy, not a way to decide anything before the sale. The states want consumers to be able to access and delete their information before any sale, and on the company’s own 3-4 week estimate the export tool does not fit inside that timetable.

## Delete, export or both: the rights you can invoke

The company’s Privacy Policy says that, depending on your region, you may have rights to access the data it holds about you, request corrections or deletions, object to or restrict processing, and port your data to another provider. The same page says the company may share limited data with service providers who help run the service, and states: “We do not sell your personal information.” That line describes the company’s own practice; the attorney general’s warning concerns what could happen to the data through the bankruptcy. The company’s October 12 post adds that you can delete your account or export your data through the app or the website. California residents have a statutory route as well: according to the California attorney general, state law lets them ask AI chatbot companies to erase their personal information and close their open accounts.

Subscriptions stay active and billing is unchanged for now, per the same post, which adds that if the service changes in a way that affects paid features, the company intends to notify subscribers in advance. Paying or cancelling is therefore a separate decision from the data one.

If you want a copy of your chats, request the export from profile settings as soon as you can, because on the company’s estimate it will not arrive before the hearing opens. If you want your data removed, delete the account in the app or on the website, or, in California, send the deletion and account-closure request that state law provides. If you want both, request the export first and delete only after you have downloaded it, which keeps the account and its chats in place through the December 3 and 4 hearing. The states’ concern that minor protections could be reversed by a buyer is one reason a family may not want to leave that decision until after the sale, and it has to be weighed against the export wait.
