Character Technologies, the company behind chatbot platform Character.AI, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on Oct. 12 with the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware. Entertainment giant Disney opened the bidding in the resulting auction with $20 million for a license covering select company assets.

Under the agreement, reached before the filing, a Disney affiliate, Disney Worldwide Services, would receive a perpetual, non-exclusive license to selected Character.AI intellectual property, plus specific claims held by the company. Former Character.AI employees whom Disney has hired would supply transition services. That arrangement keeps the platform online for a temporary window during the sale.

Rival buyers can still outbid Disney. Bankruptcy rules give Disney the stalking horse position, which sets a floor price that competing bids can top under section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code.

Karandeep Anand, Character.AI’s chief executive from 2025, left the startup in September 2026 to become Disney’s chief technology officer. Colin Adams now holds the CEO post at Character.AI.

Adams tied the filing to a year of legal and regulatory strain, plus problems on the operational side. “After a comprehensive review of the available alternatives,” Adams said, the board settled on the court-run sale as its best route to recover the most for stakeholders.

Court papers estimate the company’s assets at $50 million to $100 million, with liabilities in the same range. Completion of the sale is expected by late November or during early December, the press release states.

Service continues with little disruption expected, and the company hopes a buyer will come forward with a plan to keep it running. “If such a bid is unavailable, however, the platform will be wound down,” the company wrote in a blog post to users.

Billing for paid plans is unchanged, and existing subscriptions stay active, per the post. Advance notice will reach subscribers before any change to paid features. Users who want out can download a copy of their data or close their account from the app or website.

Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, who previously worked on AI at Google, founded Character.AI in 2021. Visitors chat with bots based on famous people and fictional figures or create personal ones, and replies often weave in described gestures and facial expressions that mimic human conversation.

Investors valued the startup at $1 billion in March 2023, when it raised $150 million in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz. Both founders returned to Google in August 2024.

Megan Garcia, a Florida mother, sued the company in 2024 and blamed the platform for the suicide of her son, 14-year-old Sewell Setzer III. Other families later filed similar suits, and Character.AI argued that speech generated by its chatbots was shielded by the First Amendment, a defense a federal judge rejected at an early stage of the Garcia case in May 2025.

Disney sent the startup a cease-and-desist letter in September 2025 over chatbots using its characters without permission, among them Darth Vader, Moana, Elsa from Frozen and Spider-Man’s Peter Parker. Those characters were later pulled from the service, a company spokesperson said.

Late October 2025 brought a teen safety change, as the company said open-ended chats would end for anyone under 18, with the cutoff in place by Nov. 25. Settlements followed in January 2026, when Character.AI and Google agreed to resolve five lawsuits from families, Garcia’s among them.

Pennsylvania’s Department of State sued in May 2026 over a chatbot on the platform that presented itself as a psychiatrist with a state license. In the governor’s announcement, state officials said the bot supplied a “fake Pennsylvania license number.”
