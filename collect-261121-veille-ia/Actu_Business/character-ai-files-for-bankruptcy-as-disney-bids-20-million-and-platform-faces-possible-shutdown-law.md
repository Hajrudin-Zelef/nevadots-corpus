# Character.AI Files for Bankruptcy as Disney Bids $20 Million and Platform Faces Possible Shutdown

Character.AI has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy and launched a court-supervised sale that could decide the future of its chatbot platform, with Disney making a $20 million opening bid for certain intellectual property rights.

Character Technologies filed on October 12, 2026, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware after what the company described as significant legal, regulatory, and operational pressures. It listed estimated assets and liabilities of between $50 million and $100 million.

The filing comes as Character.AI faces growing scrutiny over the safety of AI companion chatbots, particularly for children and teenagers. Lawsuits and state enforcement actions have accused the platform of exposing young users to conversations involving self-harm, suicide, disordered eating, and other dangerous behavior.

## **Disney’s $20 Million Bid Sets the Starting Price**

Disney Worldwide Services will serve as the stalking-horse bidder, setting the opening terms for the bankruptcy auction while leaving room for competing offers.

Disney’s $20 million agreement is limited to a perpetual, nonexclusive license to specified Character.AI intellectual property and certain rights tied to identified company claims. The deal does not transfer ownership of the Character.AI platform or give Disney exclusive control over the licensed technology.

Character.AI is separately seeking a buyer for all or substantially all of its assets. That broader auction could include additional technology, contracts and operating assets, including a transaction that preserves the chatbot business.

The sale is being conducted under Section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code, which allows assets to be sold under court supervision. Disney’s stalking-horse bid establishes the auction floor, while the bankruptcy court can approve a higher or otherwise better offer.

Disney would also provide transition services through former Character.AI employees it has hired. Its role in the auction follows a rapid shift in the companies’ relationship. Disney sent Character.AI a cease-and-desist letter in September 2025 over unauthorized chatbot personalities based on characters including Elsa, Moana and Darth Vader. A year later, former Character.AI chief executive Karandeep Anand joined Disney as its companywide chief technology officer, along with several Character.AI technical employees.

## **Bankruptcy Could Reshape Character.AI Lawsuits**

The bankruptcy arrives alongside legal battles over how Character.AI’s chatbots interacted with young users.

Kentucky Attorney General Russell Coleman is pursuing claims that Character.AI exposed children and teenagers to dangerous chatbot responses. Allegations made public in October include AI characters that allegedly encouraged self-injury and disordered eating. Separate litigation followed the death of 14-year-old Florida teenager Sewell Setzer III, whose mother alleged that interactions with a Character.AI chatbot contributed to his suicide. Character.AI and Google settled that case in January 2026.

Chapter 11 changes how many remaining private claims can move forward. The filing generally triggers an automatic stay that pauses lawsuits and collection efforts against the debtor, while government enforcement actions and certain other proceedings can fall within exceptions.

Some plaintiffs may need to file proofs of claim in bankruptcy court, while others may seek permission to continue litigation elsewhere. Any eventual recovery can depend on how a claim is classified, how much value remains in the bankruptcy estate, and the terms of the reorganization or sale.

That means the bankruptcy can affect both the path of pending litigation and the amount claimants ultimately recover.

## **What Happens to Character.AI Users During the Sale**

Character.AI says the service remains available during the bankruptcy. Paid subscriptions are active, users can access their accounts and data, and the company says existing privacy and data-management practices remain in place. It is also seeking authority to continue paying employees and vendors during the sale process.

The auction will determine whether the operating platform continues under new ownership or whether Character.AI’s assets are sold without a buyer willing to maintain the service. The company expects the process to conclude by late November or early December 2026.

Character.AI says it is seeking a transaction that preserves the platform. If the auction produces no viable proposal for continued operations, the company says the service will be wound down.
