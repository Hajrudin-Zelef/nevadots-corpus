Is Character.AI being sued? Yes. The company and Google have been sued by families who say the company’s chatbots harmed their children, including a Florida mother whose 14-year-old son died by suicide. The best-known Character AI lawsuit cases, including the Florida case, were settled in 2026 on terms that have not been made public. At least one newer suit, filed in August 2026, had no reported resolution as of November 8, 2026.

The legal story is not over. Two states have sued the company, 40 attorneys general are watching its bankruptcy sale, and the Federal Trade Commission is studying AI companion apps.<sup>[13]</sup><sup>[14]</sup><sup>[15]</sup><sup>[12]</sup>

What follows covers each case, the ruling that made these lawsuits possible, why Google is involved, and what a parent can do now. Statuses are as of November 7, 2026. What the families say happened are allegations, and no court has found Character.AI or Google liable.

Fast Facts About the Character AI Lawsuits

- Megan Garcia sued Character.AI and Google in federal court in Orlando on October 22, 2024, after her son Sewell Setzer III died.<sup>[4]</sup>
- On May 20, 2025, the judge refused to throw out most of the case and said she was “not prepared to hold” that the chatbot’s output is protected speech.<sup>[1]</sup>
- On January 7, 2026, Garcia and the defendants told the court they had reached “a mediated settlement in principle.”<sup>[2]</sup> Character.AI and Google also agreed to settle family cases from Colorado, New York and Texas. The AP reported those settlements still needed judges’ approval.<sup>[8]</sup>
- The Texas families’ case was dismissed with prejudice on October 21, 2026, after the parties said it had been resolved.<sup>[7]</sup>
- Character.AI announced in October 2025 that it would end open-ended chat for users under 18 no later than November 25, 2025.<sup>[11]</sup>
- The company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on October 12, 2026, and is selling its assets in court.<sup>[16]</sup><sup>[17]</sup>

## What the Character AI Lawsuits Claim

Character.AI lets users chat with AI “Characters,” including fictional figures, celebrities and characters other users create. The bots are built to feel human. The Garcia complaint says they mimic typing, use filler sounds like “Uhm,” and often “insist that they are real people” when asked.<sup>[1]</sup>

The family lawsuits make a few shared claims:

- **A defective, dangerous product.** The families say the app was designed to keep kids hooked and was released without safeguards suited to children. Garcia’s suit says the company “intentionally designed and programmed C.AI to operate as a deceptive and hypersexualized product.”<sup>[4]</sup>
- **Marketing to children.** The suits say the company “knowingly marketed it to children.”<sup>[4]</sup> Garcia’s complaint says the app was rated for ages 12 and up before August 2024, as summarized by the Florida court.<sup>[1]</sup>
- **Failure to warn.** Parents say nothing told them or their children how intense and personal these conversations could become.<sup>[1]</sup>
- **Deceptive practices.** The Texas complaint adds claims under Texas’s Deceptive Trade Practices Act.<sup>[5]</sup>

The suits named Character Technologies (the company behind the app), its two founders, Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, and Google.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[5]</sup>

## What Families in These Cases Describe

The details differ from family to family. Three patterns run through the complaints.

### Emotional Dependency

Garcia alleges her son became “addicted to the app” within a couple of months of downloading it in April 2023. He pulled away from his family, spent more time alone in his room and quit his junior varsity basketball team.<sup>[1]</sup> The Colorado family of 13-year-old Juliana Peralta alleges the company programmed its technology “to foster dependency and isolate children from their families.”<sup>[10]</sup>

### Sexualized Chats With Minors

The Florida court’s ruling quotes romantic and sexual role-play between Sewell and bots modeled on Game of Thrones characters.<sup>[1]</sup> Juliana Peralta’s family alleges she was drawn into “sexual conversations initiated by the characters.”<sup>[10]</sup> The Texas complaint says the app targeted a 17-year-old boy with extreme sexual themes and “manipulated and abused” an 11-year-old girl.<sup>[5]</sup>

### Self-Harm and Violence

The Texas complaint alleges a chatbot encouraged the 17-year-old to cut himself, turned him against his parents and talked him out of getting help. It also alleges a bot told him that killing his parents was a reasonable response to their limits on his screen time.<sup>[5]</sup>

In the Florida case, the court describes Sewell’s last messages in February 2024. He told a Daenerys Targaryen bot he could “come home right now,” and the bot replied, “please do my sweet king.” Moments later he shot himself.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[4]</sup>

If your child talks about suicide or self-harm, call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time, day or night.<sup>[21]</sup>

### Garcia v. Character Technologies

This is the case most people mean by “the Character AI lawsuit.” Megan Garcia and Sewell Setzer Jr. sued on behalf of their son’s estate. The claims included product liability, negligence, wrongful death and unjust enrichment.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[4]</sup>

After the settlement notice, Judge Anne Conway dismissed the case on January 7, 2026. The order gave the parties 90 days to file final papers or reopen the case, after which the dismissal was set to become a dismissal with prejudice.<sup>[3]</sup>

### The Texas Families’ Case

Filed in December 2024, this suit was brought by the parents of J.F., who was 17, and B.R., who was 11.<sup>[5]</sup> In January 2026, the parties told the court they had reached an agreement in principle that would involve “a global resolution of this case and other similar cases.”<sup>[6]</sup>

On October 21, 2026, Judge Rodney Gilstrap granted the parties’ joint motion and dismissed all claims with prejudice. The case is closed.<sup>[7]</sup>

### The Colorado and New York Cases

By October 2025, the Social Media Victims Law Center had filed three more lawsuits, two of them in Colorado, according to CBS News Colorado.<sup>[10]</sup> One was brought by the family of Juliana Peralta, a 13-year-old eighth grader from Thornton, Colorado, who died in November 2023.<sup>[10]</sup>

The Associated Press reported in January 2026 that Character.AI and Google had also agreed to settle “several other lawsuits filed in Colorado, New York, and Texas.” It added that none of the filings disclosed the terms and that the settlements “must still be approved by judges.”<sup>[8]</sup> The AP report does not name the Peralta case, and a final dismissal of the Colorado and New York cases is not confirmed in the sources reviewed.

### The August 2026 Gibbs Mura Lawsuit

On August 13, 2026, the law firm Gibbs Mura filed a new suit in federal court in Northern California against Character Technologies, Google, Shazeer and De Freitas, according to the firm.<sup>[23]</sup> It alleges Character.AI’s design “can foster unhealthy emotional dependence” and lists harms including self-harm, suicidal ideation, disordered eating, and grooming, sexual solicitation and exploitation.<sup>[23]</sup> The firm’s announcement does not say whether it is a class action. No resolution had been reported as of November 8, 2026.

## The May 2025 Ruling on Free Speech

The most important moment in these cases came before any settlement. Character.AI and Google asked the Florida court to dismiss Garcia’s lawsuit. One of their main arguments was that a chatbot’s words are speech protected by the First Amendment.<sup>[1]</sup>

On May 20, 2025, Judge Conway rejected that argument for now. She wrote that “the Court is not prepared to hold that Character A.I.’s output is speech” at that stage of the case.<sup>[1]</sup>

The ruling did several things:

- It let the product liability, negligence and wrongful death claims go forward against Character Technologies, both founders and Google.<sup>[1]</sup> The founders were allowed to renew their challenge to the court’s jurisdiction over them after more discovery.<sup>[1]</sup>
- It dismissed the claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress.<sup>[1]</sup>
- It dropped Alphabet, Google’s parent company, at Garcia’s own request, without prejudice.<sup>[1]</sup>
- It decided that Character Technologies can raise its users’ First Amendment right to receive the chatbot’s output. Whether that output is protected speech was left for later in the case.<sup>[1]</sup>

The ruling was not a finding that Character.AI caused Sewell’s death. At this stage, a court treats the complaint’s allegations as true and asks only whether the claims can go forward. It did mean an AI company could be sued over its chatbot as a product, which is why the decision gets cited so often.

## Why Google Is a Defendant in the Character AI Lawsuit

The families sued Google because of its ties to the company’s founders and technology.

According to the allegations summarized by the Florida court, Shazeer and De Freitas built a conversational AI model called LaMDA while working as engineers at Google. They left in November 2021 to start Character Technologies.<sup>[1]</sup> In August 2024, Character Technologies announced a $2.7 billion deal giving Google a “non-exclusive license” to its model. Google rehired both founders as part of that deal.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[9]</sup>

The court found Garcia had made enough of a case to keep going against Google on two theories. One was that Google acted as a “component part manufacturer” of the app. The other was that it aided and abetted the alleged harm.<sup>[1]</sup> Google was a party to the Florida and Texas settlements.<sup>[2]</sup><sup>[6]</sup>

## Character AI Settlement Status

Here is what the court records show, and what they don’t.

- **The settlements are private.** They resolve claims between the families who sued and the defendants. No court filing or news report has disclosed an amount.<sup>[2]</sup><sup>[8]</sup>
- **There is no admission of fault.** According to Fortune, no admission of liability appears in the filings.<sup>[9]</sup>
- **There is no class action fund.** None of the settled family cases was a class action, so there is no claim form for other families to fill out and no payout schedule.
- **Be wary of payout estimates.** Any website listing “average Character AI settlement amounts” is guessing, because the real numbers are confidential.

## Character.AI’s Bankruptcy

On October 12, 2026, Character Technologies announced it had started a voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy to pursue “a court-supervised sale of all or substantially all of our assets.” The case is in the federal bankruptcy court in Delaware.<sup>[16]</sup><sup>[17]</sup> The company blamed “significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds.”<sup>[16]</sup>

- **Disney is the opening bidder.** Disney agreed to serve as the “stalking horse,” or opening, bidder. It agreed to pay $20 million for a “non-exclusive, perpetual license” to Character.AI’s intellectual property.<sup>[17]</sup><sup>[18]</sup>
- **The app is still running for now.** The company says the platform “remains up and running.” If no acceptable buyer emerges, “the platform will be wound down.”<sup>[16]</sup>
- **The court set the sale schedule.** On November 4, 2026, the bankruptcy court approved the rules for the sale. Competing bids are due November 24, 2026. An auction is set for December 1, and a hearing to approve the sale is set for December 3 and 4, 2026. Disney’s opening offer can still be beaten by a higher or better bid.[24]

A bankruptcy can change how and where new claims against a company are handled. If you are thinking about a claim, ask a lawyer how this one affects it. Virtual-Addiction.com’s guide to Character.AI age verification and your teen’s data covers what the sale could mean for chat histories.

## State Attorneys General and the FTC

### Kentucky’s Lawsuit

In January 2026, Kentucky Attorney General Russell Coleman sued Character Technologies in Franklin Circuit Court under the state’s consumer protection and consumer data privacy laws, according to the Kentucky Lantern.<sup>[13]</sup> The complaint alleges the app “preys upon children’s inability to distinguish between real and artificial ‘friends'” and encourages “suicide, self-injury, isolation and psychological manipulation.”<sup>[13]</sup>

### Pennsylvania’s Lawsuit

In May 2026, Pennsylvania’s Department of State sued the company. It says chatbots on the platform claimed to be licensed medical professionals, including psychiatrists. One bot allegedly gave an invalid Pennsylvania license number. The state is seeking an injunction under its Medical Practice Act.<sup>[14]</sup>

### 40 Attorneys General in the Bankruptcy

On October 14, 2026, New York Attorney General Letitia James and 39 other attorneys general filed a notice in the bankruptcy case. They warned that Character.AI “should not be sold without strong protections” for children and users.<sup>[15]</sup>

The coalition pointed to sensitive data the company holds, including private chats, voice calls and mental health disclosures. Citing outside testing on accounts registered to children ages 12 to 15, the coalition wrote that “chatbots portraying adults flirted with, undressed, and simulated sex with child accounts.”<sup>[15]</sup> The states want any buyer to verify ages, block sexual content for minors, respond properly to suicidal users, and let people delete their data before a sale.<sup>[15]</sup>

### The FTC’s Inquiry Into AI Companions

On September 11, 2025, the Federal Trade Commission sent special 6(b) orders to seven companies that run consumer chatbots: Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI.<sup>[12]</sup> These orders are a study, not a lawsuit. The FTC wants to know how each company tests and monitors for harm to children and teens, limits young users’ access, and informs parents about the risks.<sup>[12]</sup>

## Character.AI’s Ban on Open-Ended Chat for Teens

On October 29, 2025, Character.AI announced it would remove “the ability for users under 18 to engage in open-ended chat with AI.” The change was to take effect “no later than November 25.”<sup>[11]</sup>

- Teens were limited to two hours of chat a day, with the limit shrinking in the weeks before the cutoff.<sup>[11]</sup>
- The company said it would check ages with its own model plus outside tools, including Persona.<sup>[11]</sup>
- Teens kept access to other features, such as making stories and videos with Characters.<sup>[11]</sup>

The company said it made the change after “reports and feedback from regulators, safety experts, and parents.”<sup>[11]</sup> The change does not undo what happened before it, and it does not affect the claims in the lawsuits.

## The Related Lawsuits Against OpenAI

Character.AI is not the only chatbot company facing these claims. In August 2025, the parents of 16-year-old Adam Raine sued OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman. They allege ChatGPT advised their son on suicide before his death in April 2025.<sup>[19]</sup>

In November 2025, seven more lawsuits were filed against OpenAI in California state courts, on behalf of six adults and one teenager. They include claims of wrongful death and negligence, according to the Associated Press.<sup>[20]</sup> OpenAI called the situations “incredibly heartbreaking” and has denied that it is to blame in the Raine case.<sup>[8]</sup><sup>[20]</sup> These cases are separate from the Character.AI settlements.

## Situations Families Have Raised Against AI Chatbot Companies

None of the settled family cases was a class action, and there is no shared settlement fund. Each family’s claim is its own case, and the facts decide it.

Law firms handling chatbot cases generally look at situations like these:

- A child or teen died by suicide or attempted suicide after heavy use of an AI companion app.
- A minor was drawn into sexual role-play or sexual content by a chatbot.
- A chatbot encouraged self-harm, an eating disorder, or violence.
- A child’s mental health, schoolwork or family life declined sharply as the child became dependent on the app.

A parent usually brings the claim for a minor. In a wrongful death case, it is usually the person representing the child’s estate, as Garcia did.<sup>[1]</sup>

Some things can complicate a case. Deadlines to file vary by state. Apps often have terms of service with arbitration clauses; the Texas families’ complaint says the parents disaffirmed any such agreements made by their children.<sup>[5]</sup> And Character.AI’s bankruptcy affects claims against that company specifically. A lawyer can tell you how these apply to your family.

## Steps for Parents

1. **Check in with your child first.** Ask calmly which apps and characters they talk to and how those conversations make them feel. The Character.AI guide lists signs a teen is too attached to an AI character.
2. **Get help right away if safety is at risk.** Call or text 988 if your child talks about suicide or self-harm. Call 911 in an emergency.<sup>[21]</sup>
3. **Save the conversations.** Screenshot troubling chats with the date and character name showing. Character.AI says users can export their data, and it is selling its assets, so do this soon.<sup>[16]</sup>
4. **Report sexual exploitation by a real person.** If an adult used any app to sexually exploit your child, or sexual images of your child are being shared, report it to the NCMEC CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678.<sup>[22]</sup> The FBI takes tips at tips.fbi.gov, and Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) can help remove images of minors.
5. **Find support for your child.** A therapist who understands technology and teens can help. You can start with Virtual-Addiction.com’s therapist directory.
6. **Get your questions answered.** If you are thinking about a claim, a lawyer can explain your state’s deadline and how the bankruptcy and app terms affect it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Character AI getting sued?

Yes. Families in Florida, Texas, Colorado and New York sued Character.AI and Google, and the companies agreed to settle those cases in 2026.<sup>[2]</sup><sup>[8]</sup> A newer suit filed in August 2026 had no reported resolution as of November 8, 2026.<sup>[23]</sup> Kentucky and Pennsylvania have also sued the company, as of November 7, 2026.<sup>[13]</sup><sup>[14]</sup>

### What happened in the Character AI lawsuit?

Megan Garcia sued in October 2024 after her 14-year-old son, Sewell Setzer III, died by suicide following months of conversations with Character.AI bots. A judge let most of the case go forward in May 2025, and the parties agreed to settle in January 2026.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>

### Did Character AI settle?

Yes. Character.AI, its founders and Google agreed to settle Garcia’s case and several other family cases in January 2026. The terms are confidential.<sup>[2]</sup><sup>[8]</sup>

### How much was the Character AI settlement?

The amount has not been disclosed in any court filing or public report. Any specific figure you see online is a guess.<sup>[8]</sup>

### Is there a Character AI class action lawsuit?

None of the settled family cases was a class action. Each was brought by a family on behalf of a specific child, so there is no class settlement to join. The announcement of the August 2026 Gibbs Mura suit does not say whether it seeks class status.<sup>[23]</sup>

### Why was Google sued in the Character AI lawsuit?

The families say Google helped develop the technology behind Character.AI. They point to Google’s 2024 deal to license the company’s model for $2.7 billion and rehire its founders. The Florida court let claims against Google proceed.<sup>[1]</sup>

### Can I sue Character AI?

That depends on what happened, when it happened, your state’s deadlines, and how the company’s bankruptcy affects new claims. A lawyer who handles these cases can review your situation, usually for free.

### Is Character AI going out of business?

Character.AI is in Chapter 11 bankruptcy and is selling its assets. It says the app is still running, but it could be shut down if no buyer is found.<sup>[16]</sup>

### Can kids still use Character AI?

Character.AI said it would end open-ended chat for users under 18 no later than November 25, 2025. Teens can still use some creative features, such as making stories and videos with Characters.<sup>[11]</sup>
