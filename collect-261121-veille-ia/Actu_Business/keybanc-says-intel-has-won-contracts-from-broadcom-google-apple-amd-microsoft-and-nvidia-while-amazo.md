KeyBanc analysts have come away from their recent trip to Asia with an overwhelmingly positive view of Intel's foundry-related momentum, echoing the rosy outlook espoused by Piper Sandler back in September.

As per the emerging picture, Intel Foundry is on track to win sizable orders from the who's who of the AI/hyperscaler world for its 18A-P and 14A processes, with the bulk of the orders skewed towards the latter, however.

## KeyBanc echoes Piper Sandler's bullish prognosis by declaring that Intel has secured orders from Broadcom, Google, Apple, AMD, Microsoft, And NVIDIA

KeyBanc has just declared that Intel has now secured the contracts to manufacture networking switches for Broadcom and server CPUs for Google. It will also manufacture server CPUs for Microsoft and gaming GPUs for NVIDIA. Moreover, a number of potential customers, including Anthropic, Amazon, and SpaceX/Tesla Terafab, continue to evaluate Intel's 18A-P and 14A processes, though most are likely to favor the latter.

KeyBanc also believes that Intel has roped in Apple for a multi-gen and multi-series contract related to its M-series and A-series chips, and that too on its 14A process instead of the 18A-P process that has been bandied about frequently in the rumor mill, winning a rare rebuke from a seasoned semiconductor expert.

Do note that Intel's 14A is a 1.4nm-class chip fabrication process that features:

1. A backside power delivery system called PowerDirect, which moves electrical wiring entirely to the back of the silicon wafer, freeing up the front-side routing for data signals, thereby driving down electrical resistance and boosting density.
2. The RibbonFET 2 transistors and the second-gen Gate-All-Around (GAA) architecture, which allows for tighter electrostatic control over increasingly miniature transistors, resulting in a better drive current and minimum power leakage.

KeyBanc believes Apple's orders alone could represent a volume of 600,000 wafers on Intel's 14A process, corresponding to a revenue opportunity of between $15 billion and $20 billion. Meanwhile, Anthropic's orders for a bespoke ASIC could command a volume of between 5 million and 10 million units, which equates to a revenue opportunity of between $40 billion and $80 billion after factoring in advanced packaging.

Speaking of advanced packaging, Intel's EMIB-T stands to earn between $12 billion and $15 billion on 5 million to 6 million units of Google's Humufish TPUv9 and similar units of its Icefish TPUv10, according to KeyBanc's estimates. It also stands to earn between $2 billion and $3 billion by packaging around 1 million units of Qualcomm's AI300 ASIC. Intel's EMIB-M is also expected to gain traction via Amazon's Trainium 3 and Microsoft's Cobalt 400 orders.

For the benefit of those who might not be aware, Intel's EMIB connects multiple silicon chips (chiplets) inside a single processor package using tiny silicon "bridges" embedded directly inside the organic substrate, with high-density micro-bumps placed only at the edges where the chiplets meet the bridge.

The EMIB-T takes this approach a step further by sporting Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs) drilled directly through the embedded silicon bridges. These vertical routing channels allow power and high-speed signals to flow straight up from the bottom of the chip package, through the bridge, and directly into the processors or memory sitting on top, enabling 3D stacking.

As a refresher, the substrate used within Intel's EMIB-T consists of:

1. The base organic panel that features precision-drilled cavities where the silicon bridges are placed, and manufactured by Ibiden (primary anchor), Shinko, Unimicron, and AT&S.
2. Dielectric Build-up Layer, which are insulation layers laminated directly over the embedded bridges, and utilize industry-standard Ajinomoto Build-up Film (ABF).
3. The silicon bridges themselves that contain Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs) to route power vertically.

Coming back, the KeyBanc analysts note, however, that Intel's EMIB-T substrate yields are currently averaging 50 percent to 60 percent at Ibiden and just 20 percent to 30 percent at UMTC, which could constrain Humufish ramp up to just around 5 million - 6 million units.

Even so, UBS believes that Intel's EMIB-T can support over 2 million substrates in 2028 to satisfy MediaTek's demands. Do note that MediaTek is Google's primary strategic design and development partner for the next-generation TPU v9 AI chip series, including the 2nm "Humufish" and its upgraded variant, "Triggerfish."

Elsewhere, we reported recently that NVIDIA might adopt Intel's Foveros packaging technology in 2028, which should act as a significant tailwind for the chipmaker. For the benefit of those who might not be aware, Foveros brings true 3D active-die stacking - face-to-face or face-to-back stacking directly over a base die/interposer - to the proverbial table, replete with very high interconnect density and extremely low signal latency due to short vertical paths offered by copper-to-copper hybrid bonding.
