The **November 2026 Pixel Drop** started rolling out on November 10, a month earlier than the usual schedule, and it ships alongside Android 17 QPR2. The headline additions are Gemini Intelligence with the Rambler dictation tool, an AI-built home screen widget feature called Create My Widget, and Google Messages support for the HiLight LED on the Pixel 11 Pro. The catch: not every feature reaches every phone, so this guide sorts them by model.

## What the November 2026 Pixel Drop includes

Google says the rollout continues over several weeks, so your phone may not show the update on day one. Even if you do not see new features right away, the update also carries the November security patch, which matters for every supported Pixel.

The drop is large for Gemini and other AI tools, and it leans heavily on the Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 families. Older phones still get a handful of useful extras, covered below.

Google also announced the **Pixel Tag**, a tracker that goes on sale November 11 for $29.99, or $99.99 for a four-pack. Some outlets round the single price to $29, so confirm the final number on the Google Store before you buy.

You can read Google’s own rundown in the official November 2026 Pixel Drop post.

## Which Pixel phones get Gemini Intelligence and Rambler?

**Gemini Intelligence** is coming to the Pixel 10, Pixel 10 Pro, Pixel 10 Pro XL and Pixel 10 Pro Fold. The Pixel 10a is left out, which will frustrate buyers of the cheaper model.

Gemini Intelligence bundles three things: personalized suggestions, multi-step task handling, and Rambler.

### What Rambler does

**Rambler** is a dictation feature for Gboard. You speak your thoughts, and it removes filler words such as “um” and formats what is left into clear text. You then review the result and send it.

It suits long, rambling voice notes you want to turn into a tidy message. Availability varies by language and market, so check that your language is supported before you hunt for the option.

### Multi-step tasks

Gemini can now handle multi-step jobs, such as placing a coffee order. Google is rolling these out by region, and some reports do not list the US for multi-step tasks. If you do not see them, that is likely a regional limit rather than a fault on your phone.

Reports say Pixel Watch support for some of these features is coming soon, but treat that as a timeline Google has not locked down.

## Create My Widget: how to build an AI home screen widget

**Create My Widget** lets you describe a widget in plain language, or pick from suggestions Google offers. A monthly spending tracker is one example of what you might ask for.

It is available on the Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 series, foldables included, in select regions and languages. Reports conflict on older models such as the Pixel 9, so rely on Google’s list of Pixel 10 and 11 for now.

### What data can a widget use?

Widgets are limited to information available through Google search, such as stock prices, sports scores and weather. They can also draw on selected Google account data, such as Gmail and Calendar. Do not expect a widget to pull from any random third-party app.

### How to add one

Menu wording can shift between builds, so treat these steps as a guide:

1. Press and hold an empty area of your home screen.
2. Tap the option to add widgets.
3. Look for Create My Widget in the widget list.
4. Type what you want, or choose one of Google’s suggestions.
5. Review the preview, then place the widget on your home screen.

If you do not see the option, your phone may not have received the update yet, or your region and language may not be supported. Create My Widget is not yet on Pixel Watch, though Google says it is coming soon.

## HiLight now works with Google Messages on Pixel 11 Pro

**HiLight** is the LED in the camera module that is unique to the Pixel 11 Pro models. At launch, it only responded to incoming calls.

Now it also supports Google Messages. When a favorite contact texts you, HiLight lights up in colors so you can spot who is messaging without picking up the phone.

There are limits. Third-party messaging apps such as WhatsApp and Telegram still do not work with HiLight. And because this is a hardware feature, Pixel 10 owners do not get it, whatever software they run.

## Pixel Search, custom icons and other updates for older Pixels

This is where owners of older phones find something to enjoy.

- **Pixel Search:** Expands to the Pixel 6a and newer. It can find apps, settings, messages, photos, emails and calendar events from one search.
- **Custom icon styles:** Rebuilt on a newer Nano Banana model, with 10 new home screen icon styles. They are available back to the Pixel 6a.
- **Notification summaries:** Expand to the Pixel 9 and newer. A-series models such as the Pixel 9a and Pixel 10a are excluded.
- **Extreme Charging Mode:** Now on the Pixel 10 series, except the Pixel 10a.
- **Pixel VIP and Family Hub:** The drop adds a Pixel VIP location sharing option and a Family Hub section in settings.

Note that the Pixel 10a misses out on several of these, including Extreme Charging and notification summaries.

## Android 17 QPR2: what changes in system settings

Android 17 QPR2 rolls out to Pixel phones from the Pixel 6a upward. The Pixel 6 and Pixel 6 Pro are not on the build list.

The most noticeable change is organizational. Battery settings are now consolidated in one place, under **Settings > Battery & charging**. You will also see:

- New Quick Settings tiles for audio streams and voice broadcast.
- A battery icon outline that appears when you charge or use Battery Saver.
- A wallpaper color slider, plus Neutral, Soft, Bright and Bold theme styles.
- Window handles on foldables for split screen.
- Auracast voice broadcasting.
- More than 30 bug fixes.

None of this is flashy, but the battery menu cleanup and the theme controls make daily use a little smoother.

## Should you install it, and what if your phone is not listed?

For most people, yes. The November security patch alone is a good reason, and it reaches phones that miss the new features.

### How to check for the update

1. Open **Settings** .
2. Go to **System** , then**System update** .
3. Tap **Check for update** and follow the prompts.

If nothing shows up, wait. Google staggers the rollout over several weeks, and your phone may simply be later in the queue. Keep your phone on Wi-Fi and plugged in when the download appears.

### If you are in the Android 17 QPR2 Beta

Beta users must opt out of the beta program to receive the stable release. Do that first, then check for the update. Read the on-screen warnings before you confirm, since moving between beta and stable builds can sometimes involve a data wipe.

### If your phone is not on the list

- **Pixel 6 and 6 Pro owners:** You are not on the Android 17 QPR2 build list, so you miss both the new settings and the Pixel Drop extras. Check that you have installed the latest update available for your model.
- **Pixel 10a owners:** You get Pixel Search and the custom icons, but not Gemini Intelligence, Rambler, notification summaries or Extreme Charging.
- **Pixel 9 and older owners:** Expect fewer AI features. Pixel Search and icon styles are the main wins, depending on your model.

If AI tools matter to you, a Pixel 10 or 11 is where Google is putting its effort. Otherwise, the update still brings security and a cleaner settings menu to many older phones.
