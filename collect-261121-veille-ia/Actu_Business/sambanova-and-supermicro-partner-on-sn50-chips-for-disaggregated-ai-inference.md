**SambaNova and Supermicro Partner on SN50 Chips for Disaggregated AI Inference**

SambaNova and Supermicro announced a partnership around the **SN50 RDU**, and the commitment is concrete: Supermicro will initially purchase **10,000 SN50 RDUs** for disaggregated inference systems that combine **NVIDIA GPUs with SambaNova RDUs**. The SN50 was introduced in February 2026. According to **company claims**, the chip runs **5 times faster** than competing chips and delivers **3 times lower TCO**, vendor figures rather than independently measured results.

The architecture explains why two chip types share a rack. SN50 uses **three tier memory** holding models up to **10 trillion parameters**, and up to **256 accelerators** link through an interconnect with bandwidth in the **tens of terabits per second**. Disaggregated inference splits serving stages across suited hardware, and agentic workloads reward exactly that: long tool calling chains stress prefill compute and memory capacity differently from single prompt chat.

Delivery starts with **neocloud** and **sovereign cloud** providers, where inference demand is loudest. These buyers rent compute by the token and optimize throughput per dollar rather than benchmark records, so pairing RDU efficiency with GPU compatibility attacks the procurement decision directly. Supermicro's integration role matters as much as the chip, since rack level integration is where deployment friction lives. In practice, integration quality decides results as much as chip speed.

**Impact.** Inference hardware is specializing beyond GPUs, and hybrid racks are becoming a shipped configuration rather than an experiment. Treat vendor claims as claims until measured on your workloads, and watch neocloud rental pricing as the fastest public signal of real cost per dollar. If the numbers hold, the cost per token of agentic applications falls for every team paying for inference.
