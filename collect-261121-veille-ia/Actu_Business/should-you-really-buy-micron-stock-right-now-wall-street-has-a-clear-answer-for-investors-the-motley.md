## Key Points

- Micron has a median target price of $1,575 per share among 55 Wall Street analysts; that implies 107% upside from its current share price of $760.
- Bulls argue that AI is a durable source of demand for memory chips and long-term supply deals should minimize the impact of future drawdowns.
- Bears argue that memory chip manufacturers will eventually oversupply the market in an effort to maximize profits, driving prices and earnings lower.

**Micron Technology** (MU -13.89%) has taken investors for a wild ride in 2026. Shares have added 175% this year, yet they have also fallen 35% from their record high in June. Investors are weighing extraordinary demand for memory chips against skepticism about how much longer the boom can last.

Wall Street generally sees a buying opportunity. Among 55 analysts, Micron has a median target price of $1,575 per share, implying 107% upside from the current share price of $760. But the bullish consensus deserves scrutiny because the seemingly inexpensive valuation comes with an important caveat.

Here are the important details.

## The bull case: AI demand and a seemingly cheap valuation

Micron is a semiconductor company that manufactures DRAM memory and NAND flash storage. DRAM holds information processors need to access quickly, while NAND retains data over long periods. Both technologies are essential to artificial intelligence (AI), which positions Micron to benefit as technology companies expand their data centers.

Indeed, demand for AI infrastructure has led to an unprecedented supply shortage, driving memory prices higher across the board. DRAM and NAND contract prices are on pace to quadruple this year. And that trend has naturally translated into strong financial results for Micron. Revenue soared 379% to $54.2 billion in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, marking the fifth straight acceleration in growth.

Bulls believe those favorable conditions will persist. Following supply chain checks in Asia, KeyBank analyst John Vinh wrote, "Supply chain commentary continues to point to a tight memory environment through 2027." Continued shortages would support pricing and give manufacturers more time to capitalize on exceptional demand.

Micron currently trades at 10 times adjusted earnings, essentially its lowest valuation since 2023. That multiple looks especially cheap because Wall Street expects adjusted earnings to grow at 65% annually through fiscal 2028. But the price-to-earnings ratio has been falling because investors are worried about the durability of Micron's earnings growth.

## The bear case: Strong demand does not eliminate cyclicality

Memory chips are more commoditized than logic chips like CPUs and GPUs. While product performance still matters, pricing is primarily driven by supply and demand. And periods of strong profits encourage manufacturers to expand output capacity, which eventually leads to oversupply, falling prices, and shrinking margins.

The last industry downturn occurred during the pandemic. Initially, intense demand for consumer electronics and cloud infrastructure fueled tremendous growth, but as demand normalized and inventories accumulated, DRAM and NAND prices dropped about 70%. In turn, Micron reported declining revenue and negative earnings in five straight quarters.

**Morningstar** analyst William Kerwin expects the current cycle to peak in early 2028. He calls the current situation "a tremendous cycle, but a cycle nonetheless," anticipating that additional supply will ultimately pressure prices and profits. His commentary illustrates an important distinction between durable demand for AI infrastructure and elevated profits for memory chipmakers.

That distinction matters when evaluating Micron and other semiconductor stocks involved in memory chip production. Cyclical stocks often look cheapest near a cyclical peak, but Micron may not be as cheap as it appears. For instance, the stock currently trades at 4.4 times forecasted adjusted earnings in fiscal 2028. That is only a slight discount to its low of about 5.2 times adjusted earnings near the peak of the last cycle in mid-2022.

So, should investors really buy Micron stock right now? My answer is yes. Investors can buy a small position today, provided they know the risks and are comfortable with volatility. The stock could move substantially higher if the memory chip supply shortage lasts longer than expected, but the stock may be overvalued today if the cycle peaks in early 2028.
