📅 November 6, 2026 • Tom's Hardware

**Tesla unveils SI accelerator roadmap - SI6 targets 80x the compute of AI4**

Per Tom's Hardware, Tesla has disclosed a revamped accelerator roadmap and renamed its chip line under a super intelligence branding push. The AI4, AI5 and AI6 generations become **SI4, SI5 and SI6**. The headline number points forward - per Tesla, **SI6**, the sixth-generation inference chip, is claimed to deliver **80x** more compute performance than the fourth-generation AI4. Claims of that scale set expectations for the whole program, and from today they carry the new naming in their name.

The renaming is a signal in itself. Moving from AI to SI reframes the silicon around a super intelligence narrative rather than driver assistance, and it shows how Tesla wants its roadmap read - as compute infrastructure for training-scale ambitions, not only for the car in the driveway. Inference chips are where products actually meet users, so a named generation ladder with an **80x** claim at the top gives everyone tracking the program concrete milestones to watch even where the silicon itself sits years out. Branding and silicon rarely move this explicitly in sync - when they do, the naming becomes part of the roadmap.

The roadmap is not only about inference. Per Tom's Hardware, the **D3** (Dojo 3) training accelerator drops the SRAM-centric architecture of the earlier design in favor of a multi-chiplet design with stacked HBM. That is a notable architectural pivot. Chiplets plus high-bandwidth memory is the direction the rest of the accelerator industry has converged on, so moving Dojo onto that pattern reads as aligning Tesla training hardware with the mainstream stack rather than fighting it - a pragmatic turn for a program that started as a bespoke bet. Around training compute, memory bandwidth next to the math is often the real constraint, which is why the HBM detail matters as much as the chiplet count. For a training program consistency matters too - predictable parts and interfaces beat exotic custom designs once the fleet scales.

**IMPACT:** For engineers watching the autonomy and robotics stack, the rebrand makes the roadmap legible - SI4, SI5 and SI6 mark one lineage where, per Tesla, the **80x** compute claim lands at SI6, and D3 signals chiplets with stacked HBM for training. Teams planning around Tesla hardware now have named milestones to track across both inference and training, and the burden of proof now sits with the silicon that ships next. The mapping to watch is how SI generations land in shipped vehicles and robots, because that is where the claims get tested.
