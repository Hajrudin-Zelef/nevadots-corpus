## Transitioning From Automotive FSD to Dedicated Super Intelligence Branding

In an extensive disclosure of its semiconductor and compute roadmap, Tesla has unveiled a sweeping rebranding and architectural redesign of its proprietary silicon portfolio. According to technical presentations reviewed by AI News Today, the company has formally retired the Full Self-Driving (FSD) and Hardware (HW) chip monikers in favor of "Super Intelligence" (SI) branding, aligning its silicon naming conventions across automotive, humanoid robotics, and datacenter supercomputing pillars.

Under the revised taxonomy, the deployed Hardware 4 vehicle computer is retroactively designated SI4, while the upcoming 5th-generation silicon enters production as SI5. Most notably, Tesla projected that its future 6th-generation inference accelerator, SI6, will deliver an astounding 80-fold increase in raw compute density over the current SI4 baseline. This exponential leap is designed to power real-time multimodal perception for the Optimus humanoid robot and autonomous transport fleets without relying on cloud offloading.

## Multi-Chiplet Architecture and Stacked High-Bandwidth Memory in Dojo 3

On the training and datacenter front, Tesla detailed significant microarchitectural pivots for its proprietary Dojo supercomputer program. The upcoming D3 (Dojo 3) processor will abandon the bespoke, SRAM-heavy system-on-wafer approach that characterized early D1 tiles. In its place, Tesla is adopting an advanced multi-chiplet packaging topology paired with multi-layer stacked High Bandwidth Memory (HBM).

Industry analysts at AI News Today technology coverage note that while ultra-low-latency SRAM offered massive theoretical throughput for convolutional vision networks, modern frontier transformer models require vast parameter capacity and memory bandwidth that only high-density HBM stacks can sustain economically. By embracing 2.5D/3D chiplet interconnects, Dojo 3 seeks to directly challenge hyperscale accelerators from Nvidia and custom TPU designs.

## Preparing Compute Fabrics for Zero-Gravity Orbital Deployments

Perhaps the most audacious aspect of the disclosure is the planned integration between Tesla's compute silicon and SpaceX infrastructure, now formalized under the SpaceXSI banner. The roadmap outlines plans to evaluate hardened Dojo 3 compute clusters for deployment in low-Earth orbit, aiming to harness continuous solar irradiance and deep-space radiative cooling to power orbital datacenter nodes.

While space-based datacenters face formidable challenges regarding radiation shielding, cosmic ray single-event upsets, and high-bandwidth downlink attenuation, Tesla's unified silicon strategy indicates an aggressive long-term convergence between robotics, high-performance silicon, and aerospace engineering.
