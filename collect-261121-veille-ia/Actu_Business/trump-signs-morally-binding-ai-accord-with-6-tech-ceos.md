# Trump Signs 'Morally Binding' AI Accord With 6 Tech CEOs

Trump and six AI CEOs signed the White House Accord on Superintelligence on Sept 29, committing to voluntary self-regulation. Trump also renamed AI to 'superintelligence.'

President Trump signed a voluntary self-regulation agreement with six technology executives at the White House on September 29, calling it "morally binding" and comparing it to a constitution.

The document, titled "The White House Accord on Superintelligence: A Joint Commitment on Frontier SI Responsibilities," was also signed by Google's Sundar Pichai, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, OpenAI's Greg Brockman and Elon Musk.

 

On the same day, Trump signed an executive order renaming artificial intelligence as "superintelligence" across executive branch communications.

## **Six CEOs Signed Alongside Trump, Committing to Audits and Board Review**

The signing followed a lunch at the White House attended by approximately 20 tech executives. Trump was seated next to Musk and Huang, with Zuckerberg and Pichai nearby. The full attendee list included Jeff Bezos, Satya Nadella, Lisa Su, Alex Karp, Hock Tan and Chamath Palihapitiya, among others.

Trump described the document after the meeting:

*"It's almost like a constitution, in a way. And the biggest people in the world signed that, and I signed it as president."*


When asked whether the accord was legally binding, Trump said: "I think it's morally binding."

Zuckerberg described the substance on social media:

*"We believe superintelligence will create significant new opportunities for all people and businesses."*


He elaborated that the accord covers "*a set of principles and commitments around building robust internal controls and detecting if there are any issues with the technology, coupled with multiple layers of auditing and controls, starting with internal risk review, external auditors and evaluators.*"

He added that each signatory company's board of directors would independently review the auditors' reports.

House Speaker Mike Johnson, who attended the meeting, described the agreement as voluntary on the industry's part. He also said "*we don't need a moratorium*" on AI development and warned that excessive regulation would mean "*we're going to lose this race to China.*"

Trump said there is "*a belief that tremendous self-regulation is needed*" and noted that the Department of Justice and FBI already perform oversight functions. He indicated he is considering a committee of approximately 10 people to oversee AI activity, without specifying who would be on it or whether its decisions would be binding.

## **"Superintelligence" Replaces "AI" in Government Language**

Trump signed an executive order on September 29, later referred to as the "America.gov" order, directing the executive branch to use "superintelligence" in place of "artificial intelligence" in official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents and other non-statutory materials.

The order uses "SI" as the abbreviation going forward. Huang said after the meeting that data centers should be called "superintelligence factories." Experts cited by media said the term "superintelligence" is typically used in the industry to refer to __AI systems more advanced than those currently available__, making the renaming a departure from conventional technical usage.

Trump also unveiled America.gov, an AI-powered federal government website built by AirBnB co-founder Joe Gebbia that consolidates information from approximately 29,000 federal government sites.

Trump separately said __companies building AI data centers__ should support surrounding communities, with examples such as funding local teachers. He added that companies that do not contribute may "be forced to go elsewhere or to different locations," a statement directed at data center siting disputes that have become a political issue in several states.

## **Context: Sam Altman Absent, GPT-6.1 Sol Launches Same Day**

Sam Altman did not attend the White House meeting. He remained at OpenAI's DevDay event, where the company announced GPT-6.1 Sol, a lower-cost model, on the same day.

The release came one day after OpenAI pulled GPT-6.1 Astra from its planned launch schedule, citing safety concerns. OpenAI President Greg Brockman represented the company at the White House and signed the accord in Altman's place.

Both Amodei and Altman had previously called on the government to increase AI oversight before the meeting. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 73% of Americans believe AI companies have not done enough to prevent serious harm to society. Trump has separately posted on Truth Social that concerns about AI destroying humanity are a "hoax."
