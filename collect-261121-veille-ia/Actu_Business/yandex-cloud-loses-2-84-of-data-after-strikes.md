## Yandex Cloud Data Loss

Yandex Cloud customers who used the platform’s backup tools lost nothing when drones hit the company’s data centers in October. Customers without backups were not so lucky. Russian tech group Yandex said on Tuesday that it had restored 97.16% of customer data in Yandex Cloud, while the remaining 2.84% is gone for good.

### **Key Takeaways**

- Yandex restored 97.16% of Yandex Cloud customer data after drone strikes damaged three of its data centers in early October.
- The remaining 2.84% is permanently lost because equipment was critically damaged and the affected customers had no backups on the platform.
- Yandex will enable backups by default and is spreading computing resources across new locations in several Russian regions.

## What Could Not Be Saved

The company explained the loss in a short statement. “The remaining 2.84% cannot be recovered due to critical damage to equipment and the absence of backups on the platform by customers,” Yandex said.

Until now, Yandex Cloud customers had to switch on backups themselves. Many did not. Yandex said it will enable backup by default in the future to save as much data as possible in any later disruption. Backups are now on by default for new virtual machines.

## Three Sites Hit in October

Ukrainian drone strikes damaged three Yandex data centers in early October. The attacks disrupted infrastructure and services, mainly Yandex Cloud. More than 80 services in Russia were affected over several days.

One strike hit Sasovo in the Ryazan region, where a fire broke out at the site housing a Yandex data center. That facility suspended operations completely. Yandex reported another attack, on a data center in Kaluga, on 9 October.

Supercomputers located in two of the damaged sites survived. Yandex said earlier that it had moved them to other locations.

## Spreading Out the Risk

Yandex is now changing how it places its hardware. “Engineers continue to distribute Yandex Cloud computing resources across new independent locations in different regions of the Russian Federation. The number of locations will continue to increase,” the company said.

The Kremlin is tightening its grip on the same infrastructure. President Vladimir Putin recently ordered Russian data centers to reserve 10% of their capacity for the state.

## When the Cloud Becomes a Target

The attacks show that data centers now count as strategic targets in the war. Physical damage hits harder than most cyberattacks, because no patch can rebuild a burned server hall. Russian companies have started to respond. Some are reserving data center capacity abroad, particularly in Kazakhstan, to protect their data from further strikes.

The episode also carries a lesson for cloud users everywhere. Providers often protect their own systems, but many customers assume that data stored in the cloud is automatically safe. Reports noted that customers who used Yandex’s backup tools lost no data at all. Cloud incidents of other kinds keep surfacing too, such as the second breach disclosed by Fireworks AI, which reached customer datasets through a cloud provider.
