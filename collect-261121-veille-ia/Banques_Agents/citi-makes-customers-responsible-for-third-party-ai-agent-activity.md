- Citi treats third-party AI agent access as customer-authorized even when credentials later leak to others.
- Customers must monitor agent activity for accuracy and compliance while Citi can cut off access without notice.
- Granting an agent account access also authorizes Citi to disclose sensitive customer data outside its privacy notice.

Citi’s November 2026 update to its Citi Online User Agreement makes customers responsible for actions by third-party AI agents they allow into their accounts, including activity using credentials later shared or leaked. TechCrunch reported the revised terms on November 18, 2026.

The new Section H applies when third-party AI agents or agentic AI access accounts or digital services through Citi Online or Citi Mobile. Citi defines an AI agent broadly as software or other technology that takes autonomous, semi-autonomous, or non-autonomous action for a customer or someone acting on the customer’s behalf. Agents, tools, functions, and features provided by Citi itself through those channels are excluded.

## Agent Activity Becomes Customer-Authorized

Citi cautions customers against using third-party agents to access accounts, provide information or instructions, or conduct transactions. A customer who nevertheless supplies online credentials or otherwise permits access accepts that Citi will treat the agent’s access and actions as authorized.

That treatment extends beyond the original agent. If credentials provided to an agent are subsequently shared, leaked, or otherwise distributed to other people or AI agents, access and actions using those credentials are also treated as customer-authorized. Citi has no responsibility or obligation to investigate the authority or validity of those actions, even when they conflict with the customer’s prompts or other instructions.

The customer is responsible for actions or errors resulting from an agent’s incorrect interpretation or execution of instructions, unintended actions, and actions exceeding the authority granted, except as otherwise permitted under applicable law. The agreement also makes monitoring the agent’s activities for accuracy, completeness, and compliance with the granted authority the customer’s responsibility rather than Citi’s.

## Access Controls and Customer Data

Citi reserves the right to limit, prevent, suspend, or terminate access by any third-party AI agent at any time without notice. The bank can restrict an agent’s route into its digital services, while the contractual responsibility for activity already permitted remains with the customer.

Granting an agent account access also authorizes Citi to disclose customer information, including sensitive personal information, to that agent. The Citi Privacy Notice does not apply to the third-party agent, and Citi does not control how it collects, uses, stores, protects, or shares customer data. The provisions make data exposure part of the same access decision: the agent may receive sensitive information, but its data practices sit outside Citi’s stated control and privacy notice.

## Funds Transfers Follow the Same Approach

Citibank’s separate Agreement for Online Funds Transfers carries its own November 2026 update notice addressing third-party AI agents. Its Section 4 makes customers solely responsible for instructions supplied to Citi by an agent acting on their behalf.

Agent-initiated transfers are deemed initiated and directly authorized by the customer without further investigation by Citi. That applies even when the instructions exceed or conflict with prompts or other actions the customer took with the agent.

Together, the agreements distinguish a customer’s prompt from the authority created by granting account access. For personal-finance AI providers, the result is a demanding accountability model: Citi can cut off agent access without notice, while customers retain responsibility for monitoring agent conduct and for transfers the bank treats as directly authorized.
