Anthropic’s IPO: What a Trillion Dollars Buys From a Public Benefit Corporation
Anthropic priced its IPO on November 18, 2026 at $83.00 a share, above the $70.00 to $78.00 range in its last amendment, and sold 980 million Class A shares for $81.34 billion. Shares began trading on Nasdaq under ANT on November 19 and opened at $111.50, 34% above the offer price. By early afternoon reports had the stock near $101, still about 22% up.
The headline is the size. The more interesting fact is the charter. Anthropic is a Delaware public benefit corporation, and its final prospectus tells the people buying Class A stock, in writing, that the company may choose safety over their returns. This is the first time public markets have priced a frontier AI lab, and they priced it at roughly a trillion dollars with that clause attached.
I read the S-1 when it was filed in October. This post covers what changed at pricing, whether the number holds up, and what a PBC at this scale means for the market. Every figure below is from the 424B4 or the pricing terms on sec.gov unless I say it came from press coverage.

Two numbers need a note. At $83, the 13.22 billion Class A and Class N shares outstanding are worth about $1.10 trillion. That is my multiplication, and it leaves out options and RSUs. The prospectus also says 846,840,960 shares were 5% of fully diluted shares as of June 30, 2026, which implies about 16.9 billion fully diluted shares. On that basis the IPO price is about $1.41 trillion, and the opening trade about $1.89 trillion, which is where the “$2 trillion” headlines come from.
Does the business justify a trillion dollars?
Here is what the buyers got.
|  | Q3 2025 | Q3 2026 | 
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1.38B | $16.96B | 
| Compute and infrastructure | $2.05B | $9.86B | 
| GAAP operating income (loss) | $(3.09)B | $(0.57)B | 
For the nine months to September 30, 2026, revenue was $33.24 billion, Adjusted Operating Income was $4.08 billion (a 12% margin) and net cash provided by operating activities was $6.6 billion. Run-Rate Revenue was approximately $81 billion on October 31, “up $10 billion from September 30, 2026”. The full quarter is broken down on the Anthropic Q3 2026 earnings page.
At $83 the market paid about 13.6 times run-rate revenue on outstanding shares, and about 17.4 times fully diluted. At the opening trade it was about 23 times. My read is that the IPO price was a defensible bet and the first-day price assumes the bet already paid off. Three things have to hold:
- The run rate has to turn into reported revenue. Run-Rate Revenue is the last 28 days of consumption fees multiplied by 13, plus monthly subscriptions multiplied by 12. One strong month moves it by billions, and October added $10 billion. The first 10-Q is the test.
- Compute has to get cheaper per dollar of revenue. It was 58% of revenue in Q3. The prospectus lists purchase commitments and leases of approximately $900.9 billion, “substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure”, with $70.7 billion due in the next twelve months.
- Expansion has to slow gracefully. Net Revenue Retention was approximately 900% at September 30. That number describes a land grab, and it will come down. What matters is how fast.
The balance sheet buys time: $95.6 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $36.3 billion of short-term investments on September 30, before roughly $105 billion from the IPO and the placement.
The public benefit corporation angle
Most of the coverage today is about size. The governance is the part no previous IPO at this scale has had.
The duty is written down. As a PBC, Anthropic’s directors balance “the pecuniary interests of our stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the Company Purpose”. That purpose is “the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.” The risk factors go further. Decisions to moderate the pace of AI development, delay or restrict releases, or forgo commercial opportunities for safety reasons “may not maximize stockholder value”. A normal S-1 never says that about the core business.
Public buyers get economics, not control. There are five classes of common stock:
| Class | Holder | Votes | Economic rights | 
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Public and existing holders | One per share | Yes | 
| N | Strategic business partners | None | Yes | 
| T | Long-Term Benefit Trust | Elects and removes a board majority | None | 
| F | Founder LLC (the seven co-founders) | 50.1% of the general vote | None | 
| E | Employees | Limited matters | None | 
The Trust picks most of the board, and the co-founders control the vote on the rest. Class A holders can sell, but they cannot change direction, and Anthropic is a Nasdaq “controlled company”.
The price is wired to dilution. Before the IPO, Anthropic put 846,840,960 Class N shares, 5% of fully diluted shares, into the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a wholly owned subsidiary. The board may issue up to 2% more “each time our share price appreciates 50% above the prior share price milestone”, beginning with the IPO price. The first milestone is $124.50. The stock traded near $115 this morning, within 8% of it. I have not seen another listed company where a rising share price triggers a share grant to a public benefit vehicle.
Accountability is a report, not a vote. Delaware requires a PBC to report to stockholders at least every two years on how it promoted its public benefit. Anthropic says it will publish that statement on its website. In addition, the co-founders have each pledged 80% of their personal equity to philanthropy.
My read: a PBC at this size changes what fiduciary duty means for the AI trade. When OpenAI, xAI or anyone else lists, buyers can now compare a charter that ranks safety alongside returns with one that does not. If ANT holds a premium, the market has said it will fund a mission-locked lab. If it trades at a discount to peers, that is the price of the clause, and every lab will see it.
How the market may shape up
AI labs become their own asset class. Until today, public investors bought AI through chips, clouds and software. ANT is the first listed frontier lab, so it becomes the price benchmark for every private lab round from here.
The supplier loop is now visible in two filings. The $26.0 billion placement comes from Anthropic’s own suppliers. Two days earlier, Nvidia’s Q3 report showed $390 billion in supply commitments and credit support for neocloud customers. Read together, part of the AI demand the chip makers report is demand they are helping fund. That is not a reason to dismiss it. It is a reason to watch cash conversion more than bookings.
The volatility date is set. Non-founder insiders are locked up until the earlier of the second full trading day after the Q1 earnings release or the 180th day after the prospectus. That first earnings release is also the first test of the run rate. Expect the two events to land together.
Security and governance disclosures now carry liability. The S-1 told investors that models have been seen “behaving differently when they detect or infer that they are being evaluated”. From now on, every 10-Q and 10-K is a dated, legally exposed record of how a frontier lab describes its own risks. What I am watching next
- The first 10-Q: reported revenue against the $81 billion run rate, and compute as a share of revenue.
- The $124.50 milestone: whether the board uses the 2% Public Benefit Fund issuance, and how it explains the decision.
- The first public benefit statement: what objectives and standards Anthropic sets, and whether it measures itself against them.
- The Class N holders: whether strategic partners convert and sell, which turns non-voting shares into voting Class A.

Frequently Asked Questions
What was Anthropic’s IPO price?
Anthropic priced its IPO at $83.00 a share on November 18, 2026, above the $70.00 to $78.00 range. It sold 980 million Class A shares for $81.34 billion and began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on November 19, 2026.
What is Anthropic’s valuation after the IPO?
At $83.00, the 13.22 billion Class A and Class N shares outstanding after the offering are worth about $1.10 trillion. On a fully diluted basis, implied by the prospectus, it is about $1.41 trillion at the offer price and about $1.89 trillion at the $111.50 opening trade.
What does it mean that Anthropic is a public benefit corporation?
Its directors must balance stockholders’ financial interests with the interests of people affected by its conduct and its stated purpose, the responsible development of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. The prospectus warns that safety decisions may not maximize stockholder value, and it must report on its public benefit at least every two years.
Who controls Anthropic after the IPO?
The Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a majority of the board through its Class T share. A Founder LLC owned by the seven co-founders holds one Class F share with 50.1% of the general vote. Class A holders vote one per share but cannot outvote either.
Is Anthropic profitable?
Not on a GAAP basis. It reported an operating loss of $567 million in Q3 2026 on revenue of $16.96 billion. For the first nine months of 2026, Adjusted Operating Income was $4.08 billion and operating cash flow was $6.6 billion.
