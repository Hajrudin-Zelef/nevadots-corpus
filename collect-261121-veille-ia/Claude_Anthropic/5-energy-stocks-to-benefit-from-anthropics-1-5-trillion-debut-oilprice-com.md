Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday night, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed, and raised $81.3 billion, CNBC reported. Shares in the Claude maker (Nasdaq: ANT) were due to start trading Thursday, and no first trade had printed when this was written.

Anyone hoping that cash lands in power stocks should look at the calendar first. The prospectus puts Anthropic's computing and infrastructure commitments at about $895 billion, but Inc. counted only about $70 billion of that due before September 2027. Most of what Anthropic needs from the energy industry was ordered, leased or reserved before the book closed, so the useful question is who already holds a signed contract.

Inc. reported that Fluidstack, a New York company that won't own any chips, is building and running custom data centers for it in Texas and New York under a $50 billion program announced last November, with Google backstopping the debt on several sites. One of them, a Nexus Data Centers campus in Hubbard, Texas, sits beside a gas pipeline and will run on its own turbines. Anthropic rents more than it builds, which is why landlords and equipment makers are where this money lands.

Caterpillar, Riot Platforms and Hut 8 have documented ties, though Caterpillar's rests on one newspaper report and Riot's tenant is named by Inc., not by Riot. Entergy supplies the power for Hut 8's Louisiana site. GE Vernova has no disclosed Anthropic contract at all. It's here because Anthropic's prospectus, as Fortune reported, warns that the company may pay above-market prices for equipment with long lead times, and GE Vernova's order book shows what a long lead time looks like.

**Caterpillar Got a Richer Buyer for Engines It Had Already Sold**

Caterpillar (NYSE: CAT) had an awkward September, if the Financial Times is right. A San Antonio equipment supplier called OnePWR Solutions had ordered heavy-duty gas generators from Caterpillar about two years earlier and needed financing to complete the purchase. Anthropic stepped in, took over the order and reportedly paid $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts, along with OnePWR's engineering and construction capabilities, according to Techzine's summary of the FT report. The source is unnamed, and none of the coverage I found carries a confirmation from Caterpillar or Anthropic.

Caterpillar and OnePWR were already close, with a collaboration alongside Vero3 announced in March on a 500 MW gas-fired site with carbon capture and data centers named as a target customer. Divide the FT's numbers and the order works out to roughly $2,000 per kilowatt, near the $2,157 that BloombergNEF put on a whole combined-cycle plant last year, as Oilprice.com reported. The FT figure includes installation, so the comparison is loose.

Power generation sales at Caterpillar rose 30% on data center demand in the third quarter, Utility Dive reported, and CEO Joe Creed called it the "highest sales quarter ever." The order backlog hit $78 billion, nearly double a year earlier, and the company expects moderate sales growth and higher margins in the fourth quarter.

Tariffs are the part that doesn't fit the story, at about $600 million in the quarter and roughly $2.2 billion forecast for the year, along with a Resource Industries miss on mining shipment delays. Caterpillar is expanding output of large engines and turbines, and Creed said the company is managing the operational strain of that growth. CFO Kyle Epley said end markets remain healthy.

**Riot Platforms Gave Anthropic a Veto Over Power Cuts**

Riot Platforms (Nasdaq: RIOT) leased its entire Corsicana, Texas campus to "a leading Frontier AI Lab" for 15 years, in a deal it values at about $28 billion, according to the company's third-quarter release. The release never says Anthropic. Inc. does, and it adds that Anthropic holds a veto over voluntary power cuts.

The lease covers 910 MW and costs about $5.5 billion to build. Riot funds roughly $2 million a megawatt of site and electrical work and owes the tenant the rest in phases, once each phase is delivered and paying. Rent starts within 60 days of powered pads being handed over.

Rent doesn't begin until the first 400 MW is delivered in September 2027, and the last 310 MW isn't due until July 2028. Riot reported $4.0 million of net income last quarter against $104.5 million a year earlier. The average rent of $1.8 billion a year is a long way off. Riot has been paying for the wait with bitcoin, selling 20,264 coins in the first nine months for about $1.5 billion, and CEO Jason Les said the company doesn't expect to issue equity for its announced projects.

Wall Street split almost at once. Jefferies upgraded Riot to Buy on Nov. 10 and called the deal "a reset for the market." Keefe, Bruyette & Woods went the other way, cutting it to Market Perform on heavy reliance on a single tenant and no investment-grade credit support behind the lease, Stocktwits reported. KBW still raised its price target to $23 from $20 on the value of the lease. Corsicana is 910 of the 1,151 MW Riot has contracted, or about 79%, with the rest split between an AMD contract and an earlier Rockdale lease with the same lab.

Anthropic's IPO helps with the credit worry, because $81 billion of fresh capital makes the tenant look sturdier than it did when the lease was signed. That's my read, not an analyst's. What the IPO can't fix is Texas. James Mercer of Metro Colo Advisory told Inc. that IPO cash "can't buy a place in line for the grid." The governors-elect of Texas, Ohio and Georgia have all called for data center construction moratoriums, Inc. reported, and ERCOT is due to report on Dec. 10 on a connection queue more than five times the state's all-time peak demand.

**Hut 8 Made $189.5 Million on $71.1 Million of Revenue**

Hut 8 (Nasdaq: HUT) reported third-quarter revenue of $71.1 million. Net income was $189.5 million, because of $413.8 million in mostly unrealized gains on digital assets, according to the earnings release. Adjusted EBITDA before those marks was $33.0 million.

KBW, the same firm that downgraded Riot, trimmed its Hut 8 price target to $81 from $92 on uncertainty about a Texas groundwater permit for the second phase of its Beacon Point campus, which it gave a 75% chance of approval. It kept an Outperform rating. Beacon Point is a separate campus from River Bend, the Anthropic project.

Hut 8's December 2025 announcement laid it out: Fluidstack operates the clusters, Anthropic is the customer, and James Bradbury, Anthropic's head of compute, said capacity should come online in early 2027. The latest results target initial data hall delivery in the second quarter of 2027, with steel and foundations finished across all four halls. Hut 8's latest release doesn't name the tenant at all.

CEO Asher Genoot said when the deal was announced that scaling frontier AI infrastructure is "at its core, a power challenge," which is true and also why the next number matters. The firm commitment is 245 MW. The 2,295 MW ceiling includes a right of first offer on 1,000 MW that depends on adding power to the site, plus 1,050 MW of optional capacity elsewhere. Genoot also said Hut 8 expects the projects to be funded entirely at the project level, while the company reported about $6.6 billion in cash, restricted cash and short-term investments.

**Entergy Sells the Power Behind Hut 8's River Bend Site**

Entergy (NYSE: ETR) has no contract with Anthropic. It has one with Hut 8, which is building River Bend in West Feliciana Parish, Louisiana, for Fluidstack with a Google financial backstop on the lease, Entergy said. Entergy Louisiana supplies an initial 330 MW for 245 MW of computing load, with potential for up to 1,000 MW later. Phillip May, who runs Entergy Louisiana, called energy one of the state's greatest competitive advantages.

A first phase of up to $10 billion brings about 1,000 construction workers at peak and at least 75 permanent direct jobs once it's running.

Third-quarter earnings came in at $1.98 a share against a consensus of $1.71, and retail sales rose 7.4%, Zacks reported. Industrial demand was driven by data centers and primary metals, but residential sales grew faster, up 12.6% against 4.9% for industrial, a reminder that weather moves these numbers too. Weather-adjusted retail growth was 4.5%. The cost is a $5.2 billion equity financing plan alongside $55 billion of capital spending planned for 2027 through 2030. Entergy's outlook for 2030 is $7.05 to $7.35 a share, against $4.35 to $4.45 this year.

**GE Vernova Is Already Selling 2032 Delivery Slots**

GE Vernova (NYSE: GEV) is the odd one out, since nothing I found ties it to Anthropic. CEO Scott Strazik told analysts the company "sold multiple slots for 2032 delivery," Industrial Info reported. Backlog and slot reservations for gas equipment grew to 122 GW from 116 GW in the quarter, and GE Vernova expects at least 130 GW under contract by the end of the year.

Data centers make up about a fifth of its roughly 100 customers in 25 countries, Strazik said, so an Anthropic order would join a crowded line. Industrial Info described gas turbine orders as strong but slower than in recent years, though the company's third-quarter release put total orders at $29 billion, up 96% organically. The board also doubled its buyback authorization to $20 billion, which is what a company does when it isn't short of customers. The wider market is tight too: PJM's latest capacity auction cleared at its price cap and ended up 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement.

Our August look at the turbine shortage put GE Vernova's output at roughly 20 GW a year, with a goal of 24 GW by 2028, against what Wood Mackenzie estimates is about 110 GW of orders across the industry for 60 to 70 GW of annual capacity. At GE Vernova's rate, 122 GW is about six years of production.
