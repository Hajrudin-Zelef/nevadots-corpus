Google said on 29 October that “Jules will wind down on December 31, 2026”, according to a notice in the Jules changelog. Users have until 11 November to finish sessions, after which Google says it will turn Jules “to read-only until the end of the year”.
By our arithmetic, that leaves 13 days between the notice and read-only mode, and 591 days between Jules’ launch on 19 May 2025 and its shutdown. Google’s own instructions for exporting Jules data tell users to open “a CLI coding agent, such as Claude Code”, which is Anthropic’s tool.

What Google Announced
The notice says the shutdown is “part of our work to unify our agentic coding efforts”. It says what users built with Jules “taught us where agentic development is headed” and that those learnings are shaping products across Google, “including our agent-first platform, Google Antigravity”.
The Jules account on X put the plan this way: “We’re working on bringing our learnings from Jules into @antigravity so you get the best of both worlds: autonomous remote background task execution and an agent-first development environment”. The post adds: “As we focus on this unified experience, Jules will be winding down”.
The Jules post, above, had about 118,000 views when we read it on 30 October.
Google’s Export Guide Names Claude Code
Google says users “can now export everything Jules knows about your work into a local folder”, including account settings, environment variables, scheduled tasks, connected repositories, memories, setup scripts and every session. Code, branches and pull requests “already live in GitHub, so they stay where they are”, the notice says.
To run the export, the notice tells users to install Jules Tools, sign in, and “Then open a CLI coding agent, such as Claude Code, from your home directory and paste the export prompt below”. The agent then “checks your setup, runs the export, and tells you what it saved”. Claude Code is the only agent the notice names as an example.
What Jules Was
Google launched Jules on 19 May 2025 as “a new AI coding agent” that works “autonomously on tasks in your GitHub repo”, according to the changelog. When it left beta on 6 August 2025, Google wrote that “Since launch just two months ago, Jules has passed over 140k public commits”, and it introduced higher task limits “through the Google AI Pro and Ultra plans”.
Where Google’s Coding Agents Now Sit
Google has said a team used Gemini 4 Argon agents in Teamwork, the multi-agent framework inside Antigravity, to compress an estimated one-year roadmap into a 5-hour sprint, as we reported on 27 October. In September we reported that Google opened Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5 to its engineers inside its agentic development environment.
Microsoft said on 28 October that developers can build agents on its Copilot harness through Microsoft Foundry, free until 1 December. Google Cloud launched Agent Compute Engine for running AI agents on 22 October.
TestingCatalog, which tracks product changes, summarised the shutdown on X, above, writing that Jules’ “planned functionalities are about to arrive to Antigravity instead”.
