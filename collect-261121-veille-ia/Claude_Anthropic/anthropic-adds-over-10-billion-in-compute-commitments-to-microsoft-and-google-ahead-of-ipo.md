A revised S-1 filing shows the Claude developer has locked in roughly $895.4 billion in total compute and infrastructure obligations

Anthropic has added more than $10 billion in new minimum compute commitments to Microsoft and Google, according to a revised S-1 filing tied to its planned initial public offering.

The updated disclosure raises the Claude developer’s minimum obligation to Microsoft to at least $44.9 billion. Its commitment to Google now sits at approximately $121.3 billion.

## What the filing shows

The amended S-1 reflects the added commitments as of early November 2026. It follows Anthropic’s initial S-1, which was dated October 26, 2026.

The Microsoft agreement runs through mid-2033. The Google deal extends through July 2033.

Both are largely non-cancelable, with the main exception being a material breach.

They also require payment whether or not Anthropic actually uses the computing capacity it has reserved. The agreements include provisions for true-ups, which are periodic reconciliations that settle the gap between what was committed and what was consumed.

Industry shorthand for this arrangement is **take-or-pay**. The buyer either takes the capacity or pays for it anyway. For Anthropic, the effect is to convert what would otherwise be variable computing costs into fixed obligations.

## The bigger infrastructure picture

Anthropic’s total compute and infrastructure commitments stood at around $895.4 billion as of late September 2026.

Amazon accounts for a minimum commitment of approximately $110 billion. Broadcom is tied to equipment leases estimated at about $161.2 billion.

Google, at approximately $121.3 billion, ranks as the largest single cloud commitment named in the disclosure. Microsoft, at least $44.9 billion, is smaller but still sizable.

Anthropic previously announced a commitment of roughly $30 billion to Azure, Microsoft’s cloud platform, in November 2025.

Anthropic is preparing to list on the Nasdaq under the proposed ticker ANT.
