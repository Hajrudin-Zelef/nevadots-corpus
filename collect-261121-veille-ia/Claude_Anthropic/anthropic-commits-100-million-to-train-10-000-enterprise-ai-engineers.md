The new Claude Frontier Academy borrows from medical residencies to close the gap between AI models and the people who deploy them

Anthropic is putting $100 million behind a plan to train 10,000 engineers who can actually get AI working inside large companies.

The program is called the Claude Frontier Academy, and it was announced on October 2, 2026.

## A residency, but for software

The Academy’s core offering is the Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency. As the name suggests, it is modeled on the way doctors are trained.

The program has three stages. Participants start with multi-day, in-person training. They then move through simulated enterprise deployments, practice runs designed to mirror the mess of a real corporate rollout.

The final stage is a 12-week residency. During that stretch, engineers lead actual Claude projects inside their own organizations.

Graduates will earn credentials. The first two badges, Claude Resident Engineer and Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer, are expected to arrive in early 2027.

The overall goal is to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers, or FDEs, by the end of 2027.

## Who is in the first cohorts

The opening cohorts are already running in San Francisco, New York, and London.

The roster of companies nominating engineers reads like a who’s who of global consulting and finance. Participants come from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley, and Novo Nordisk.

Accenture is planning to go considerably further. The firm intends to use the Academy’s resources to train approximately 30,000 professionals on Claude.

That figure alone is triple the Academy’s own 10,000-engineer target.

Steve Corfield, Anthropic’s Global Head of Business Development, framed the effort around parity. The idea, as he described it, is to train outside engineers the way Anthropic trains its own, and in doing so set a new standard for how businesses integrate AI.

## Not the first $100 million

This is the second nine-figure training commitment from Anthropic this year.

In March 2026, the company launched the Claude Partner Network, backed by its own $100 million investment. That initiative focused on training partners to implement Claude across their operations.

The Academy expands on that earlier effort. Where the Partner Network was about bringing firms into the fold, the Academy zeroes in on individual engineers and a structured path to certification.

Two $100 million checks in roughly seven months points to a consistent read inside the company. The bottleneck for enterprise AI, in Anthropic’s view, is increasingly human rather than technical.

## What this means

The milestones to watch are straightforward. The first badges are expected in early 2027, which will show whether the pipeline is moving on schedule. Accenture’s plan to train approximately 30,000 professionals will indicate how far the curriculum can travel beyond Anthropic’s own classrooms.

And the end-of-2027 target of 10,000 engineers will be the clearest scorecard of all.
