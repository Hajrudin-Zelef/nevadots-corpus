Anthropic, the developer behind the Claude chatbot, revealed contracts and leases totalling $895.4 billion, almost entirely dedicated to data centres and chips. The company disclosed the commitments on November 2 in an amended filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) ahead of its planned initial public offering.

The total commitment is nearly seven times the $132.0 billion in cash and short-term investments Anthropic held at the end of September, according to Yahoo Finance analysis. About $69.8 billion comes due within the 12 months following September 30, and approximately $447.5 billion is scheduled before December 31, 2030, with the remainder extending from 2031 to 2045. Data Center Dynamics reported that the company's previous computing commitments stood at $417.0 billion.

The filing outlines minimum commitments across multiple vendors: at least $121.3 billion to Google between April 2026 and July 2033, $110.0 billion to Amazon through April 2036, and at least $44.9 billion to Microsoft starting November 2026. The contracts with Google, Amazon, and Microsoft require payment regardless of capacity usage and allow termination only upon material vendor default, according to Data Center Dynamics. Additional commitments include around $161.0 billion for equipment leases from Broadcom and up to $120.5 billion to SpaceX's AI unit, which can be terminated with 90 days of notice starting in March. Infrastructure firms Nscale and Lambda account for $44.6 billion through 2033 and $41.3 billion through 2034, respectively.

About $213.8 billion of the total is subject to termination rights or preconditions such as financing, site delivery, or regulatory approvals. An additional $125.0 billion to $150.0 billion for Google chips expected between 2028 and 2031 remains excluded from the headline figure. Mark Mahaney, an analyst at Evercore ISI, noted after the initial prospectus that Anthropic acts simultaneously as a customer, competitor, distribution channel, and investment for cloud providers.

In the third quarter, Anthropic generated $17.0 billion in revenue, up from $11.6 billion in the second quarter, representing 47% sequential growth compared to 144% in the prior period, CNBC reported. The company moved from an operating profit of $974 million to an operating loss of $567 million after buying $5.0 billion in short-term compute when demand exceeded contracted capacity. Jay Goldberg, an analyst at Seaport Research Partners, told Yahoo Finance that while the business may turn a profit for a quarter or two, losses are likely to resume. A portion of the shares will be available to retail investors through Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, and Morgan Stanley's E*Trade.
