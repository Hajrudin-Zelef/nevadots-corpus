Anthropic is a San Francisco AI-safety and research company, founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers led by siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei, that develops the Claude family of large language models.

As of October 19, 2026 its widely released lineup spans Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Haiku 5.5 (generally available October 7), Claude Sonnet 5.5 (September 28), Claude Opus 5.5 (September 22), and Claude Fable 5.1, plus invitation-only Claude Mythos 5.1. Claude reaches customers through the claude.ai apps, the Claude API, enterprise and government plans, and Claude Code - its agentic coding tool, which runs at a $2.5B+ run-rate with 2M+ weekly active users - alongside the Claude Cowork 'AI coworker' and the June 2026 Claude Science research workbench, built on the ~$400M acqui-hire of ex-Genentech drug-discovery startup Coefficient Bio, that positions Anthropic in AI-driven pharma R&D. Anthropic differentiates on safety and interpretability research and runs one of the industry's largest multi-vendor compute footprints spanning Amazon (Project Rainier; Amazon's committed capital reached $33B in April 2026), Google (TPUs, up to $40B plus a 3.5 GW Broadcom-built expansion), AMD, CoreWeave, and newer data-center leases with TeraWulf, Riot Platforms, Volta Infra, Nscale and Lambda, plus a first Australian inference lease signed September 16, 2026. Annualized run-rate revenue topped $65B as of late July 2026 (up from ~$47B in May and ~$9B at the end of 2025), more than 1,000 customers now spend over $1M/year, and Q2 2026 revenue exceeded $11.5B with its first profitable quarter since founding. The Financial Times reported September 13, 2026 that Anthropic has told shareholders Q3 adjusted operating income will be positive for a second straight quarter, with gross margins above 80% before partner revenue-sharing and training costs. In May 2026 it raised a $65B Series H at a $965B valuation and confidentially filed for an IPO in June 2026; CNBC confirmed September 14 that Anthropic has picked Nasdaq as the exchange. The Class A offering closed on Nov. 20, 2026 at $83.00, including the full underwriters option. The Nov. 20 announcement confirms 1,127,000,000 Class A shares and 313,253,007 Class N shares and states gross proceeds only. Unaudited Q3 revenue in the Nov. 2 Amendment No. 1 remains $16.956 billion. IFR, via a person familiar and reported by Newsquawk on October 15, said a debut in mid-November; that is not a company date. Reuters and CNBC reviewed a prospectus describing a Broadcom facility of up to $42 billion against a $125.2 billion five-year TPU lease, with notes not expected to be sold before the IPO, and Anthropic declined to comment. On August 27, 2026 a San Francisco federal judge (Rita Lin) ruled the Pentagon's supply-chain-risk designation of Anthropic unlawful; the D.C. Circuit later upheld the designation. The same day Anthropic opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a model-agnostic specification started with HHMI Janelia that lets AI agents operate lab and manufacturing hardware, with a plan to open-source the standard after the preview. On September 16 it said it will open a Singapore office in October, its fifth Asia-Pacific site. Reuters reported September 18, 2026 that Anthropic has set up a Bay Area wet lab for physical biology experiments, confirmed by life-sciences head Eric Kauderer-Abrams; a spokesperson later said the facility is not specifically for drug discovery, and Kauderer-Abrams said Anthropic is not running clinical trials for now. On October 2 it launched Claude Frontier Academy with a $100 million commitment to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. Dario Amodei attended the September 29 White House AI lunch.

Expand the Model Hardware Standard beyond its research preview

Target2026 and beyond

Grow the August 27, 2026 research preview of the Model Hardware Standard, a model-agnostic specification letting AI agents safely discover, operate and troubleshoot physical lab and manufacturing equipment, from an initial small group of science, robotics and manufacturing partners toward a planned open-source release. Anthropic's newsroom still listed the August 27 preview as the latest MHS update as of September 17, 2026, with no expansion beyond the research-preview waitlist and no open-source date. Early pilots have already shown concrete results, including a faster drug-discovery assay at Genentech, a weeks-to-a-day imaging speedup at Janelia, and a laser-recovery process at QuEra improving from 58 to 99.3 percent success. Reuters reported September 18 that Anthropic has now confirmed an in-house Bay Area wet lab, which Kauderer-Abrams described as still in the very early innings of using AI to automate lab execution, giving MHS an internal proving ground alongside partner sites. A spokesperson said human oversight remains essential.

Defend against the Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell copyright suit

TargetOngoing

Respond to a lawsuit filed by Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music naming Anthropic, CEO Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann individually, alleging Claude was trained on tens of thousands of the publishers' copyrighted compositions obtained via piracy, including BitTorrent downloads from Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror, with statutory damages sought up to $150,000 per infringed composition. An Anthropic spokesperson told Variety the case is recycling allegations from cases already before the courts, that training generative AI models is transformative fair use as the court held in Bartz, and that the company will defend itself robustly. No later court date has been disclosed as of September 17, 2026. The suit follows the September 2025 $1.5 billion settlement of a similar piracy-based claim brought by authors, in which a court found that training on copyrighted works was not itself unlawful but that obtaining them through piracy was.
