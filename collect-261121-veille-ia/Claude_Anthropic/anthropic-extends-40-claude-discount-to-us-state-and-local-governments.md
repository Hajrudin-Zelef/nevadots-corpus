Anthropic expanded its agreement with the US General Services Administration on 27 October 2026, offering a 40% discount on Claude products to state, local, tribal and territorial governments for the first time, according to the company’s announcement. The arrangement extends OneGov, GSA’s programme for government technology purchases, beyond the federal agencies covered by the earlier offer. Agencies will pay for the Claude usage they consume, without a minimum spending commitment, Anthropic said.

## Key points

- The 40% discount covers Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the Claude API, Anthropic said.
- Anthropic estimates the discounts will save the government $1 billion, with agencies paying only for usage consumed.
- The company says approximately 500 US government organisations use Claude, while weekly Claude for Government active users have risen about eightfold since February.

## GSA moves Claude offer to usage pricing

The new terms apply to Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the Claude API, Anthropic said. Agencies can obtain the discount when buying directly from the company. Anthropic expects purchases through cloud providers to become eligible in the coming months. Government buyers can place orders through GSA’s Multiple Award Schedule, its route for purchasing from approved suppliers, the company said.

GSA described the arrangement as a 40% reduction in token-based charges across the Claude models available to government buyers, Unite.AI reported. Under the agreement, each agency bears the cost of its own consumption, while Anthropic offers the lower price without a guaranteed minimum purchase. Anthropic’s $1 billion savings estimate depends on government use of the discounted products.

GSA’s original Anthropic OneGov announcement, issued on 12 August 2025, offered Claude for Enterprise and Claude for Government to the three branches of the federal government for $1 per agency for up to a year, Unite.AI reported. The publication also reported that GSA’s OneGov listing described the earlier offer as $1 per user and set its expiry at 31 October 2026. The expanded agreement opens the discount to additional levels of government as that earlier offer approaches its end.

GSA said its OneGov strategy had generated more than $1.8 billion in savings since it began, including approximately $1.6 billion through its super intelligence agreements, according to Unite.AI. Those figures concern the wider programme. Anthropic’s estimate concerns discounts under its expanded agreement.

## Anthropic puts government Claude users at 500 organisations

Approximately 500 federal, state and local government organisations use Claude, Anthropic said. The company reported that weekly active users of Claude for Government had grown about eightfold since February and that usage across federal civilian agencies had increased about 20-fold in a year. Both growth measures describe use of the product, rather than spending under the new pricing agreement.

Anthropic cited a four-week Utah pilot as an example of work inside state government. Utah gave Claude Code to 34 developers across 10 state agencies, the company said. Of those developers, 80% had a working environment within 30 minutes, and 71% reported saving 20 or more hours by the pilot’s end. The results concern that group of developers, rather than a wider agency deployment.

The company also said a state IT worker transferred close to 900 API functions to a new software structure in two days, against a month originally allotted for the project. Separately, AI Affairs reported a planned sole-source Claude contract at the US Department of Health and Human Services, which was preparing a wider AI competition.

## Claude for Government reaches FedRAMP High

Claude for Government became generally available on 30 September at FedRAMP High, a federal authorisation level for sensitive unclassified work, Anthropic said. The company describes the product as offering coding and agent-based capabilities with features close to those in its commercial services. That authorisation gives eligible agencies a route to use Claude for work subject to those federal requirements.

Agencies developing their own tools can also access Claude through Amazon Bedrock or Google Cloud’s Vertex AI, Anthropic said. The company says Claude on both platforms is authorised for FedRAMP High. It is developing a separate authorisation path for Claude Enterprise: Anthropic said that product is undergoing FedRAMP Moderate authorisation through the FedRAMP 20x programme.
