Key Takeaways
- Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above its $70-to-$78 range, for a valuation of about $1.5 trillion.
- The price landed in a rough year for new listings, as 2026’s 10 biggest U.S. IPOs are down about 20% on average from their offer prices.
Anthropic is a five-year-old company that loses money and is central to an industry facing a broad public backlash. Wall Street is now set to make it among the most valuable companies on Earth.
Anthropic priced its initial public offering (IPO) at $83 a share Wednesday, above the $70-to-$78 range it pitched to investors, valuing the company behind Claude at about $1.5 trillion.1 Trading is set to start Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker “ANT.”
The AI company and its selling stockholders are offering 980 million shares, up from the 825 million first planned, Anthropic said.2 The deal raises about $81 billion, the most of any IPO, ahead of SpaceX (SPCX), which raised $75 billion in June.
Seven tech companies, including Nvidia (NVDA), Alphabet’s (GOOGL) Google, and Microsoft (MSFT), agreed to buy another $26 billion of stock at the same price.3
Investors have put in orders for far more stock than Anthropic was set to sell. Institutions placed about $400 billion in orders and individual investors close to $100 billion, though none were binding, CNBC reported Wednesday, citing people familiar with the deal.4
IPO research firm Renaissance Capital, in a note published last week, called the deal “the first significant test of investor appetite for a frontier AI story,” and the company’s changing valuations this year reflect a heady one for AI.5
Anthropic’s valuation has swung significantly with the AI trade this year. Private investors valued Anthropic at $965 billion in May, and analysts put its value at about $2 trillion in September.67 That was before the market cooled for AI stocks this fall, though the set price is above the $70-to-$78 range Anthropic published on Nov. 10.8
IPOs haven’t fared well this year, either. Shares of the 10 biggest U.S. IPOs in 2026 have dropped about 20% on average from their offer prices, according to a Financial Times analysis.9
In Anthropic, buyers get a fast-growing business that lost $104 billion in the first nine months of this year, much of that related to a non-cash accounting charge tied to its financing. Revenue reached $33.2 billion in the same period, up about 1,200% from a year earlier, and annualized revenue was about $81 billion as of the end of October.10
Related Education
Morningstar senior equity analyst Malik Ahmed Khan wrote last week that the share price range was “a reflection of the current market sentiment,” not the business itself. He said the fair value was $92 a share.11
Anthropic has set aside up to 20% of the offering for individual investors, who could request shares through Charles Schwab, E*TRADE, Fidelity, Robinhood, or SoFi.3
Everyone else can buy in at the market price once trading opens.
