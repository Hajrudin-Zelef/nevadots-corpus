The AI trade’s biggest test yet is over, and results were mixed.
Anthropic, the AI lab behind the Claude chatbot, made its stock market debut Thursday just before noon after carrying out the biggest IPO on record. The stock is now trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker “ANT.”
Once trading began at $111.50 per share, Anthropic joined the ranks of the world’s most valuable companies, with a market cap of about $2 trillion at the open. Shares climbed to about $115 in the minutes after the stock opened but closed at $97.17, 17% above the $83 IPO price.
More than 813 million Anthropic shares changed hands, beating SpaceX’s opening-day volume of 522 million shares in June.
The stock market has been rattled lately by growing concerns about the sustainability of the AI-fueled gains that have underpinned the market’s record performance in recent years. Anthropic’s IPO was a high-stakes test of how much investors will pay for a piece of the AI boom.
Anthropic Closes Its First Day at $97.17, Up 17% From IPO Price
Anthropic finished up its first day of trading at $97.17 a share, 17% above its $83 IPO price.
The stock opened at $111.50 and climbed to about $115 within minutes before dropping below $100 for much of the day. ANT ended the day 13% below its first trade.
At the closing price, the maker of Claude is worth about $1.8 trillion, counting stock options and unvested stock awards, putting it among the top-10 most valuable public companies worldwide.
While IPO investors ended the day ahead, the rest of the market didn’t: The Dow fell about 1,100 points, and the S&P 500 was down about 2%.
Anthropic Investors Are Buying More Than a Chatbot
Anthropic is in the tokens business.
Nearly four-fifths of Anthropic’s $33 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026 came from customers paying per token, a measure of how much data the Claude model reads and writes.
The subscriptions Claude is best known for among the public make up the rest. The company’s filings describe them as “top-of-funnel” for generating demand for its lucrative enterprise business.
Anthropic’s biggest customers are responsible for a good chunk of the company’s revenues. Nearly 12,000 of them, each spending $100,000 or more a year, produced about three-quarters of the revenue in the previous 12 months through September.
The money coming in is going out the door fast. The $81 billion Anthropic raised in its IPO is about 9% of the $901 billion in minimum payments Anthropic has committed to for chips and data centers.

Amodei Used His Nasdaq Speech To Lean Into Anthropic’s Public Mission as Protesters Blocked Nasdaq’s Entrance
Buying Anthropic stock gets you a piece of a company that’s legally built to weigh more than your returns, CEO Dario Amodei reminded investors in a speech at the Nasdaq today before the opening bell. The company’s IPO filings asked investors not to treat its stock as “purely transactional.”
Delaware law classifies Anthropic as a public benefit corporation. That status requires the board to weigh shareholders’ financial interests alongside the stakeholders affected by the company’s actions and its stated purpose: “the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.”
Shareholders don’t pick most of the board, either. Most seats go to directors chosen by a five-member trust without a financial stake in the company. Members include former Federal Reserve Chair Ben Bernanke, who was seen at the Nasdaq Thursday. The seven co-founders hold 50.1% of the vote, controlling the remaining seats and other shareholder matters.
Before ringing the Nasdaq’s opening bell, Amodei emphasized the company’s broader mission, pledging that the company “will not bend” to business or political pressure. The IPO’s proceeds, he said, leave Anthropic better able to walk the line between profits and AI safety.
Some investors don’t think shareholders will be on the losing side of any trade-offs. Peter Singlehurst, head of private companies at Baillie Gifford, told Bloomberg Thursday that ensuring AI safety is “completely compatible with maximizing long-term shareholder value.”
Protesters outside the Nasdaq weren’t impressed by Amodei’s talk of the public benefits of Anthropic’s mission, demanding a freeze on AI development. Banners urged passersby to “stop tech oligarchs,” and police arrested demonstrators who blocked the Nasdaq’s doors.
Claude Maker Debuts as the World’s Sixth Most Valuable Public Company
Anthropic’s first trades on the Nasdaq today make it one of the most valuable public companies on Earth. Its $111.50 opening price makes the company behind Claude worth about $2 trillion, putting it at sixth place among public companies worldwide.
That figure counts stock options and unvested stock awards. Counting only shares already issued, Anthropic’s market value would be closer to $1.5 trillion, about the same as Meta Platforms (META).
Shares Open Far Above Damodaran’s $77 Estimate, Below Wall Street’s Targets
One of the best-known experts on public company valuations said this morning the stock is worth just under $77 a share, about 30% under the opening share price.
Aswath Damodaran, the New York University finance professor, put Anthropic’s value at $76.98 a share in a valuation he updated before trading began at $111.50 Thursday. That values the company’s equity at about $1.35 trillion, about a third below the market value indicated by its opening share price. Damodaran’s analysis assumes data centers will become much more costly to build and Democratic victories in the midterm elections will lead to tighter regulations on AI.
Research firms have been far more bullish. Four started coverage Thursday with buy-equivalent ratings. Price targets include $125 at Citizens, $135 at Wolfe Research, $150 at Needham, and $155 at Melius Research, all above the opening trade. Morningstar’s fair value estimate is $92.
These camps are measuring different things. A price target is a guess at where a stock will trade in a year, while Damodaran’s estimate gives what he thinks the business is worth today, after accounting for the value of 1.77 billion employee stock options.
How shares get counted, Damodaran wrote, is “the Wild West in investing,” making it even trickier for valuation experts to get a handle on a stock’s true value.

When Will Anthropic Shares Be Trading?
Dario Amodei, co-founder and CEO, and his sister, Daniela Amodei, co-founder and president, may have rung the Nasdaq’s opening bell this morning, but that doesn’t mean Anthropic’s stock started trading just yet.
Newly listed stocks open only after the exchange and banks underwriting the deal match buy and sell orders in an opening auction. For an IPO this size, that can take a while. The first trade is expected in the late morning or early afternoon.
Shares of SpaceX (SPCX), the last mammoth IPO, first traded at 11:46 a.m. ET in June.
Anthropic and SpaceX Account for Two-Thirds of the Money Raised in 2026 IPOs
Anthropic’s IPO is the biggest in a mixed year for new listings.
Fewer companies are going public, but those that do are raising tons more money.
So far this year, 124 IPOs have launched in the U.S., down more than a third from this time a year ago, according to IPO research firm Renaissance Capital. The money raised has been stratospheric, though: $230.3 billion, more than six times the amount at this point in 2025.
Two IPOs make up two-thirds of the year’s total: Anthropic’s $81.3 billion and SpaceX’s $75 billion.
Bigger hasn’t meant better for buyers. The 10 largest U.S. IPOs of 2026 have fallen about 20% on average from their offer prices, according to a recent Financial Times analysis.

Shares Priced Higher Than Expected Amid Booming Demand
The company’s IPO price of $83 a share is well above the $70 to $78 range it had pitched to investors.
Anthropic also sold more stock than initially planned, about 980 million shares, up from 825 million. The sale raised $81.3 billion, the most of any IPO on record, topping the $75 billion SpaceX (SPCX) raised in June. The share price values Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion.
Anthropic’s valuation has swung significantly with the AI trade this year. Private investors valued Anthropic at $965 billion in May, and analysts put its value at about $2 trillion in September. That was before the market cooled for AI stocks this fall, though Anthropic’s IPO share price suggests significant market demand for a pure play in the AI space.
