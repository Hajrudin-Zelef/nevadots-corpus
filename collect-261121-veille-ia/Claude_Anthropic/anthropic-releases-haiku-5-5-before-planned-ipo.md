Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7, the third model in its Claude 5.5 family in the past month, as the company expands its AI lineup ahead of a planned public listing (Reuters, 2026).

Haiku 5.5 is built for high-volume, cost-sensitive tasks such as classification, summarization, and extraction, including live support, voice agents, and in-app assistants. It costs 75 percent less than its predecessor, Haiku 4.5, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts under 100,000 tokens. For longer prompts, the price rises to $0.50 and $2.50 respectively (Reuters, 2026).

The model is Anthropic's first Haiku with built-in safeguards for a narrow set of high-risk cybersecurity requests. The company said most everyday tasks would not be affected (Reuters, 2026). The release follows Opus 5.5 on September 22 and Sonnet 5.5 on September 28, completing a rapid rollout of the 5.5 generation.

Anthropic confidentially filed draft IPO paperwork with the SEC on June 1, targeting a Nasdaq debut in late 2026 at a valuation that investors expect could reach $2 trillion or more. The company has pushed its listing window toward November, according to reports. The steady stream of model releases in the weeks before a listing serves both a technical and a commercial purpose, giving enterprise customers a clear upgrade path while showing investors a product pipeline that spans high-end reasoning and low-cost, high-volume workloads.

The Haiku 5.5 pricing puts it in direct competition with low-cost offerings from Chinese firms such as DeepSeek, which has built its strategy around undercutting Western models on price. By offering a capable small model at a fraction of previous costs, Anthropic is positioning itself across more of the market, from advanced research to routine automation.
