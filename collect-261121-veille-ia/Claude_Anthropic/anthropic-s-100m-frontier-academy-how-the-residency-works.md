On October 2, 2026, Anthropic launched Claude Frontier Academy, a training programme backed by a $100 million commitment. The target is 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027: engineers who can take a Claude project from an idea to a production system inside their own company. The first programme is a residency, and the only way in is to be nominated by your employer.

Written October 3, 2026 and dated October 2, 2026 from Anthropic’s announcement and the Frontier Academy programme page, both read on October 3. Anthropic has not published a fee, a curriculum outline or the assessment criteria.

1. 01Nomination onlyEmployers nominate engineers. Each nominee arrives with a named Claude project to lead back home.
2. 02Two badges, two testsA graded practical after the in-person days earns Claude Resident Engineer. A final practical after 12 weeks earns Frontier Deployed Engineer.
3. 03Consultancies dominateFive of the eight named first-cohort organisations are consulting firms, so many graduates will reach businesses as outside advisers.
4. 04First graduates in 2027Anthropic expects the first Frontier Deployed Engineer badges in early 2027 and says more programmes will follow that year.

## 01 — The releaseWhat Anthropic *announced*

Anthropic’s announcement frames the Academy as an answer to a talent problem. Companies want AI inside their operations, and the people who can make it work there are scarce. Anthropic says a small group of skilled people drives a large share of what AI delivers in the companies it works with, and that the Academy is built from its own deployments inside large enterprises and the consultancies that serve them.

The four headline figures come from the announcement and the programme page. The page describes the $100 million as an initial investment that starts with the residency.

##### Initial commitment

Anthropic’s stated investment, starting with the FDE Residency.

##### Engineers by end of 2027

The number of Frontier Deployed Engineers the programme aims to train.

##### On a real project

Leading a Claude deployment at the engineer’s own organisation.

##### First cohort locations

San Francisco, New York and London, running now.

## 02 — The mechanismHow the residency *runs*

Anthropic compares the Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency to how doctors train: learn from practitioners, work on realistic cases, and pass an assessment before working alone. In practice that means four stages and two badges. The programme page gives the timings that the announcement leaves out.

| Source: Anthropic announcement and Claude Frontier Academy programme page, October 2, 2026, read October 3. |  |  | 
|---|---|---|
| Stage | Length | What happens | 
|---|---|---|
| In-person build | 3 days | With Anthropic engineers and licensed instructors, residents build a Claude system for a made-up company, starting at the first customer request and ending with security sign-off and handover. | 
| Graded practical | Day 4 | A new scenario, graded. A pass earns the Claude Resident Engineer badge and entry to the residency. | 
| Residency | 12 weeks | The engineer leads a real Claude deployment at their own organisation, with cohort mentoring and support from Anthropic engineers. | 
| Final practical | End of residency | A pass earns the Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge. Anthropic expects to award the first in early 2027. | 

The design choice that matters is the second half. A certification ends with an exam. Here the exam is the entry ticket, and the credential depends on shipping something real inside the engineer’s own company. One of the partner quotes in the announcement puts the point well.

What I’m hearing from clients isn’t about certification counts or training volume; it’s about knowing firsthand how to solve real-world problems that create value to our clients.Geoffroy Pajot, Capgemini, quoted in Anthropic’s announcement, October 2, 2026

## 03 — EligibilityWho can get a *place*

The announcement lists four traits: hands-on software engineers with strong fundamentals, a record of building with large language models, experience helping other people adopt AI, and a habit of working on their business’s most important problems. Prior experience building AI agents is not required.

There is no open application. Organisations nominate engineers, and each nominee arrives with a named Claude project they will lead when they return. Anthropic tells organisations to ask their Anthropic account team or Partner Account Manager whether they are eligible. That wording points to companies that already work with Anthropic, either as a customer with an account team or as a partner firm. Anthropic has not said whether anyone else can be nominated.

Neither page states a price, whether the $100 million covers residents’ costs, how many places each organisation gets, or what the graded practicals measure. Anthropic also says further Academy programmes will be announced in 2027 without naming them. Treat any figure beyond the four above as unconfirmed.

## 04 — ContextWho is in the *first* cohorts

Anthropic names eight organisations in the first cohorts: Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk. Five are consulting firms. Three are enterprises that run their own engineering teams: a bank, an investment bank and a drug maker.

That mix says who the programme is for in its first year. The consultancies will carry residency-trained engineers into client work, which extends Anthropic’s reach well beyond its own staff. The enterprises use it to build internal capacity. Commonwealth Bank of Australia’s chief technology officer is quoted saying its teams produced up to three times more code changes in the past year; that is the bank’s own figure, given in Anthropic’s announcement, with no method attached.

The pattern follows the large consulting commitments we covered earlier this year, including PwC’s plan to train 30,000 professionals on Claude and Cognizant’s 30,000 trained staff. The Academy is a deeper tier for a smaller number of people.

## 05 — ComparisonWhere it sits *beside* certifications

Anthropic now runs three levels of Claude training. The announcement says professionals across 46,000 firms have earned more than 175,000 Claude certifications through the Claude Partner Network, and that nearly 4,000 people have completed Basecamp, which teaches the curriculum Anthropic’s own Applied AI staff receive at onboarding. The residency sits above both.

## 06 — Practical implicationsWhat a business should *do*

The right response depends on whether you can get a place at all. The routes below follow from the eligibility rules, not from any figure Anthropic has published about demand.

The project matters more than the person. A nominee must bring a named Claude project, and the final badge depends on it shipping. A company that picks a vague or low-stakes project wastes its place. A good candidate is one process with a clear owner, data the engineer can reach, and a security review the company already knows how to run.

Most businesses will not get a place, and they do not need one to act. The role itself, which we explain in our guide to the forward deployed engineer, can be hired or grown. For teams that want that capability alongside their own staff, our AI transformation work covers use-case selection, build and handover on the same path the residency teaches.

## 07 — The failuresFour ways to *misread* the Academy

A launch with a large number attached gets read for more than it says. The illustrative misreadings below each take a figure or a phrase from Anthropic’s two pages and extend it past what they state. They are patterns of interpretation, not things any particular reader has done.

The open application: an engineer prepares to apply. There is no application; organisations nominate, each nominee brings a named Claude project, and Anthropic points organisations to their account team or Partner Account Manager, which is why the router’s third row sends a company with no commercial relationship to build the skills internally. The 10,000 as graduates: a procurement note describes a pool of ten thousand certified engineers. The figure is a target for the end of 2027; the first cohorts are running now and Anthropic expects the first Frontier Deployed Engineer badges in early 2027. The $100 million as a bursary: a budget assumes residents’ costs are covered. Neither page states a price, whether the commitment covers residents’ costs or how many places each organisation gets, as the callout above lists, so the line stays unconfirmed. The three times as a measurement: the Commonwealth Bank figure of up to three times more code changes is quoted as a result of the programme. It is the bank’s own figure, given in Anthropic’s announcement with no method attached, and it describes the past year rather than the residency, which had not produced a graduate when the figure was given.

The four headline numbers in the capsule grid are the programme figures the pages support. Any other programme figure is either on the not-published list or an inference, and a plan built on an inference should say so.

That discipline matters most for the router’s first row. A company with an account team that asks now will get answers to the unpublished questions directly, and those answers, not this article’s reading of the two pages, are what a nomination should be planned around.

A $100 million initial commitment, a target of 10,000 engineers by the end of 2027, a 12-week residency after three in-person days and a graded practical, and first cohorts in three cities. Treat any other programme figure, including costs, places and pass rates, as not yet published.

### Choose the project first, then the engineer

If you can nominate, write down the one process a Claude system should change and who owns it before you pick a name. If you cannot, run that same project internally. The badge is a signal; the deployment is the asset.
