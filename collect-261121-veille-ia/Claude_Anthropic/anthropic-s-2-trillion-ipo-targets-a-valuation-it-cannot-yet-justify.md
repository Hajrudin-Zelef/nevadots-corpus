*A five-year-old company with no profits and $30 billion in compute obligations wants to go public at a price that requires it to out-earn Nvidia, twice over.*

**Key takeaways**

- Anthropic filed its public Form S-1 with the SEC on October 26, 2026, targeting a Nasdaq IPO under ticker ANT at a reported valuation of $1.5 to $2 trillion, which would make it one of the largest IPOs in history.
- Independent research firm New Constructs values Anthropic at $150 billion and says reaching $2 trillion would require annual profits roughly double Nvidia's trailing total, from a company that has never posted a profit.
- The IPO crystallizes the broader AI capex dynamic: enormous capital concentrating into a handful of firms with deep government ties, absorbing energy and compute that competes directly with Bitcoin mining infrastructure.

Anthropic filed its public Form S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission on October 26, 2026, setting up a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT with a reported target valuation of $1.5 to $2 trillion. The company was founded on January 26, 2021. It has never turned a profit.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering, per the S-1 filing on SEC EDGAR. The earliest possible first trading day, per standard SEC timing rules, is November 16, 2026.

## The Numbers Behind the Ask

Anthropic's last private valuation was $965 billion, set by a $65 billion Series H closed May 28, 2026, led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia Capital. That round disclosed an annualized revenue run rate of approximately $47 billion. The company has raised more than $120 billion in total across all funding rounds.

The proposed IPO range would add another 50 to 100 percent on top of that $965 billion private mark. Nvidia, the company Anthropic depends on for the chips that run its models, was founded in 1993. It took 31 years to reach its current valuation above $5 trillion. Anthropic wants $2 trillion at age five.

New Constructs, an independent research firm, published a report calling the deal "the most ridiculous IPO of 2026" and pegging Anthropic's fair value at $150 billion. The firm stated that justifying a $2 trillion valuation would require Anthropic to generate profits roughly double Nvidia's trailing four-quarter total, from a company currently sitting at zero net income. That is a 13x gap between where independent analysis puts the company and where the IPO is targeting.

The S-1 also discloses a commitment to purchase $30 billion of computing capacity from Microsoft Azure, which itself runs on Nvidia hardware. Anthropic contributed shares equal to 5% of fully diluted equity to a new public benefit fund, per its S-1 filed October 26, 2026. Nvidia is reported to be in talks as a potential anchor investor, with its participation reportedly valued at up to $10 billion, per Reuters. That would make Anthropic's primary chip supplier also one of its largest public shareholders at listing.

## What the Valuation Gap Actually Reflects

The 13x spread between New Constructs' $150 billion fair value and the $2 trillion IPO target is the gap between what a profitable business is worth and what a capital-absorption story trades at in a fiat-money system still flushing excess liquidity through the highest-conviction narrative available.

The Anthropic IPO is the clearest single expression of that dynamic right now. A five-year-old firm with no profits, $30 billion in committed compute spend, and a valuation that implies a profitability level no AI company has approached is being handed a public listing because the alternative, leaving capital sitting in low-yield instruments, is worse for the funds writing the checks.

That incentive structure rewards whichever firm absorbs the most capital fastest, not the one with the most durable product or the best unit economics. The write-down risk embedded in this kind of capex cycle is real, and Anthropic's S-1 is the IPO manifestation of that same bet.

For Bitcoin miners and the broader energy stack, this matters directly. Every megawatt of power and every rack of Nvidia hardware absorbed into the Anthropic and hyperscaler buildout tightens the same grid Bitcoin mining competes on. AI compute demand is now the dominant marginal driver of U.S. power markets.

The concentration risk runs deeper than energy: a handful of firms, all with government and defense entanglements, are building the dominant cognitive infrastructure of the next decade. The Anthropic IPO is a bid to lock that position in at public-market scale.

## What to Watch

The S-1's specific loss figures for H1 2026 and the exact gross margin trajectory will be the numbers that determine whether New Constructs' $150 billion ceiling or the Street's $2 trillion floor has more support. If the filing shows expanding gross margins trending toward 60% and a credible path to covering compute costs through organic revenue growth, the valuation gap narrows. If it shows losses widening alongside revenue growth, the New Constructs thesis holds. The roadshow and final pricing, expected before November 16, will be the first real test of whether public markets apply the discipline private rounds did not.

## Update, November 2, 2026

Anthropic filed an amended S-1 today that materially changes the financial picture. Q3 revenue came in at $16.96 billion, up 47% sequentially, but the company swung back to a $567 million operating loss after posting $974 million in operating profit in Q2. That Q2 profit was the only quarter of operating income in the entire filing's quarterly table, and it lasted exactly one period. The amendment also quantifies price cuts at about $600 million of forgone revenue.

The amendment put a hard number on Anthropic's total compute obligations for the first time. The filing discloses approximately $895.4 billion of purchase commitments and lease agreements, with about $69.8 billion due in the 12 months after September 30, and outside the main contractual-obligations table sits approximately $451.2 billion of estimated undiscounted obligations under executed leases not yet commenced, plus $100.2 billion of commitments entered after September 30.

By one measure, those unstarted leases alone stand at roughly $1,018 per $1 of Q3 capex.

The unstarted lease figure grew from $50.2 billion at year-end 2025 to $451.2 billion at September 30, 2026, with obligations generally set to commence between 2026 and 2028 on terms ranging from less than one year to 20 years. The full bill is almost seven times the roughly $132 billion of cash and short-term investments Anthropic held at the end of September.

Anthropic says $5.0 billion of Q3 compute came from short-term commitments that the filing says currently costs significantly more than its multi-year capacity , and that expense drove the operating swing. The filing describes up to $120.5 billion of Nvidia-based compute from SpaceX.AI, about $3.1 billion a month once fully delivered, which either side can cancel on 90 days' notice after non-cancelable periods that end in January and March 2027.

Anthropic warned that revenue is primarily consumption-based and much is not under long-term contract, while compute obligations extend for years and can include minimum-purchase and take-or-pay terms.

The governance structure disclosed in the original S-1 compounds the financial risk. The co-founders have each pledged to donate 80% of their personal Anthropic equity to charity -- pledges the S-1 describes as voluntary, revocable, and not legally binding -- and as founders give shares away, their economic stake shrinks while their voting control through the Founder LLC remains intact. Anthropic is a public benefit corporation whose directors are required to balance shareholders' financial interests against broader stakeholder interests, a mandate the S-1 plainly states may not maximize stockholder value. About $214 billion of those commitments are subject to termination rights or conditions that must be met before they take effect , but buyers at a $1.5 to $2 trillion valuation are being asked to fund a compute dependency empire with no durable operating profit, no guaranteed voting influence, and a governance structure that explicitly deprioritizes their returns.

## Update, November 3, 2026

The $895.4 billion committed figure from the amended S-1 now has a harder all-in number attached to it. The contractual-obligations table excludes deals that do not specify a fixed dollar commitment, for which Anthropic estimates payments of approximately $150 billion to $175 billion; add those to the $895.4 billion and the total comes to roughly $1.05 to $1.07 trillion. That is the number the company itself cannot put in a single line because the aggregate total field in the filing is left blank.

The supplier concentration inside that trillion-dollar pile is worth tracking. Lease commitments tied to Broadcom alone amount to about $161.2 billion.

Deals with Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Broadcom combined total $437 billion and are non-cancellable, with some requiring payment whether or not Anthropic uses the capacity.

Some agreements also include annual price increases and fuel costs passed through without a cap, leaving the company exposed to rising energy prices with no contractual ceiling. That structure puts energy cost risk directly inside the balance sheet, not outside it.

On the equity side, institutional investors are already repricing the deal. The Wall Street Journal reported that institutional investors want a valuation of $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion, below the roughly $2 trillion discussed earlier. The Bitcoin-mining infrastructure overlap is also sharpening. Nearly half of the contracted computing capacity of TeraWulf, a publicly traded Bitcoin miner expanding into data centers, is leased to Anthropic, while about 25% of data center operator Hut 8's AI leases are with Anthropic. Miners pivoting to AI leases to chase Anthropic revenue is the same grid-capacity competition described in the original article, now with named counterparties and dollar-weighted exposure on the table.

## Update, November 4, 2026

The amended S-1's $23.29 billion Q3 net loss now has a cleaner decomposition worth flagging. The $23.29 billion figure was actually down from $66.31 billion in Q2, and $21.94 billion of it, 94.2% of the total, came from fair-value changes on convertible notes and related instruments.

The filing links those charges directly to Anthropic's rising valuation this year. In other words, the company's own ballooning private-market price is mechanically generating the headline loss number. The more the market bids Anthropic up, the worse its reported net income looks.

The operating picture is a separate and more concrete problem. Compute absorbed 58 cents of every revenue dollar in Q3, up from 48 cents in Q2.

Q3 revenue came in at roughly $17 billion, up about 1,127% from a year earlier, but sequential growth slowed to 47% from 144% the prior quarter. A company priced for hypergrowth is showing deceleration precisely as its infrastructure costs accelerate in the other direction.

For the first nine months of 2026, Anthropic generated $33 billion in revenue and reported a $104 billion net loss, most of which is the same convertible-note accounting effect compounding across quarters. Investors pricing the IPO at $1.5 to $2 trillion need to decide how much weight to put on a loss figure that is 94% non-cash accounting noise versus the operating loss that is very real, and very tied to a compute cost structure the company itself says runs significantly more expensive when sourced short-term.

## Update, November 11, 2026

The IPO now has a price. Anthropic's amended S-1 filed with the SEC on November 10 sets a range of $70 to $78 per share for 825 million Class A shares, implying a fully diluted valuation of roughly $1.3 to $1.4 trillion, well below the $2 trillion figure that opened this story. Sources told CNBC that pricing is expected November 18 with trading on Nasdaq under ANT beginning November 19.

The same filing formalizes the supplier-as-shareholder structure. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC have agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price in a concurrent private placement, with Google and Nvidia each putting in $10 billion. Pair that $26 billion against the aggregate $900.9 billion in compute purchase commitments and leases disclosed in the filing, and the equity raise covers roughly 2.9 cents on every dollar of committed infrastructure spend. The companies handing Anthropic capital are the same ones collecting on the other end of that $900.9 billion tab.

The valuation compression from $2 trillion to $1.3 to $1.4 trillion did not change the structural picture. The compute obligation grew from the ~$895.4 billion figure in the November 2 amended filing to $900.9 billion in the latest version. The debt load went up as the equity price came down.

## Update, November 14, 2026

The amended S-1's language flagging natural gas as an energy risk has since been confirmed by a concrete transaction. The Financial Times reported on November 8 that Anthropic agreed in late September to pay about $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas-fired generators from OnePWR Solutions, a San Antonio equipment supplier.

OnePWR had ordered the generators from Caterpillar two years ago at pre-surge prices but needed financing to complete the transaction; instead of becoming a OnePWR customer, Anthropic took over the Caterpillar equipment orders outright.

The Financial Times characterized the deal as the biggest single purchase of power-generation equipment known to have been made by an AI lab.

The amended S-1 explains why Anthropic moved this aggressively. The filing states that "certain of our planned data center facilities rely on dedicated on-site natural gas-fired generation, which exposes us to fuel price volatility and supply disruptions that are outside of our control," and separately lists turbines and generators among equipment facing extended delivery timelines, price increases, and allocation constraints, noting some infrastructure must be ordered years in advance with large pre-delivery payments.

Waiting two to three years to connect a new data center to regional electrical grids is forcing developers to install their own natural gas turbines on site. Melius Research managing director James West put it plainly to clients: "We know power is a constraint to AI and Anthropic's S-1 filing did nothing to assuage that view. AI compute is a natural gas demand story."

The implications for Bitcoin miners are direct. AI labs securing natural gas supply and pipeline capacity ahead of regional grid interconnection queues are competing for the same fuel and generation infrastructure that underpins behind-the-meter mining operations. The filing itself warns that "as data center operators increasingly rely on natural gas-fired generation... we expect increased competition among users for the same natural gas supply and pipeline capacity in those areas."

The filing positions power supply as one of the hardest limits on how fast Anthropic can grow, and uncapped energy costs with no contractual ceiling could produce unpleasant surprises for profitability. The bottleneck is no longer chips alone; it is fuel, pipelines, and turbines, and Anthropic just placed the largest known single order to corner as much of that stack as it can before IPO day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## What does Anthropic's S-1 say about its path to profitability?

The S-1 filed October 26, 2026 confirms Anthropic has never been profitable. The filing's risk factors and financial statements will detail the H1 2026 net loss and gross margin trajectory. Those figures, available directly on SEC EDGAR, are the key inputs for any independent profitability analysis. New Constructs' base case of $150 billion assumes current loss levels do not support a materially higher valuation without a step-change in margins.

## How does Anthropic's compute buildout affect Bitcoin mining energy costs?

Anthropic has committed to $30 billion in Microsoft Azure compute capacity, and its broader AI buildout is part of an industrywide surge in data center power demand that is tightening U.S. grid capacity. Bitcoin miners compete for power on the same grid. As AI data center demand grows, marginal electricity costs rise and available capacity shrinks, which compresses mining margins directly. The AI capex cycle going cash-flow negative in 2026 does not slow that power demand; the capital has already been committed.

## What does Nvidia's potential anchor investment mean for the IPO structure?

Nvidia is both Anthropic's primary chip supplier and, per Reuters, a prospective anchor investor at up to $10 billion. That arrangement means Anthropic's compute vendor has a direct financial interest in the success of the company paying it for hardware. Public market investors will need to weigh that alignment carefully when evaluating whether Anthropic's $30 billion Azure commitment (which runs on Nvidia systems) reflects arm's-length commercial terms or a circular capital arrangement.
