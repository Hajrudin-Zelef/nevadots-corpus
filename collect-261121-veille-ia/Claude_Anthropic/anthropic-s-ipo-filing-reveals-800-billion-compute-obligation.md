Anthropic's S-1 prospectus shows first-half 2026 revenues outpacing compute costs nearly two-to-one, but the company has locked itself into contracts totaling up to $807 billion through 2036, with most non-cancellable whether Anthropic uses the capacity or not.

Anthropic filed its IPO prospectus Monday. The S-1 reads like a warning label wrapped in growth projections. The company earned revenues in the first half of 2026 that exceeded compute and infrastructure costs by nearly two to one. Anthropic reported operating losses of $693 million, down from $2.14 billion in the first half of 2025. Those figures suggest improvement. They also hide a spending curve that climbs faster than revenue.

Anthropic has signed compute contracts that could total between $782 billion and $807 billion. The filing lists obligations by provider:

| Provider | Minimum Commitment | Timeline | Exit Terms | 
|---|---|---|---|
| Microsoft | At least $44.9B | 2026-2033 | Non-cancellable; pay whether used or not | 
|  | At least $114.5B | 2026-2033 | Non-cancellable | 
| Google TPUs / other | $125B to $150B | 2028-2031 | Non-cancellable | 
| Amazon | At least $110B | 2026-2036 | Non-cancellable; pay whether used or not | 
| Broadcom | Approximately $161.2B | Starting 2026 | Non-cancellable | 
| SpaceXAI | Up to $120.5B | 2026-2029 | 90-day cancellation from early 2027 | 
| AMD | More than $20B | Initial tranche 2027 | Can satisfy via Microsoft leases | 
| Nscale | Up to $44.6B | Through 2033 | Exit only on delivery failure or breach | 
| Lambda | Approximately $41.3B | Through 2034 | Exit only on delivery failure or breach | 

Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Broadcom contracts bind Anthropic whether it uses the capacity or not. Nscale and Lambda extend through 2033 and 2034; Anthropic can leave only if those providers miss deadlines or breach terms. The SpaceXAI lease allows cancellation with 90 days' notice starting in early 2027. AMD's initial tranche exceeds $20 billion, though Anthropic can cover part of that obligation by leasing capacity through Microsoft.

If Anthropic's revenue growth stalls, the company risks default. The filing names the risk plainly: Anthropic's ability to meet these obligations depends on generating significant revenue growth. A revenue shortfall could push Anthropic into default, which the filing warns could trigger a cascade. Anthropic holds one backstop: up to $42 billion in convertible promissory notes to Broadcom. The company could also issue equity or debt. Both options dilute shareholders or load the balance sheet with expensive obligations.

Anthropic's largest compute suppliers also compete with it. Google, Amazon, and Microsoft run their own frontier models. They sell Anthropic capacity today and could restrict access tomorrow. The filing acknowledges this tension. Anthropic may need to finance compute upfront through capital leases, prepayments, or debt-financed equipment purchases. The company already leases capacity through Broadcom. Meta has pursued similar arrangements with Google TPUs, so Anthropic's path isn't unique.

Geopolitics adds another variable. Conflict in the South China Sea could sever supply chains for AI accelerators. Anthropic depends on those chips.

Anthropic justifies the gamble with a sweeping claim. The company believes AI abundance will transform every economic sector "in ways greater than the impact of industrialization, electricity, semiconductors, and the internet." Anthropic frames the $800 billion not as a cost, but as the entry fee to the largest economic shift in modern history.

Watch revenue growth against compute commitments. The first half of 2026 showed a 2:1 revenue-to-cost ratio, but obligations scale faster. Monitor whether Anthropic can maintain that ratio as contracts with Google, Amazon, and Microsoft come due. Track cancellation rights: the SpaceX deal alone offers a 90-day exit starting in 2027. Everything else binds Anthropic whether market conditions favor it or not. If you build an analysis around this filing, measure the gap between contracted spend and reported revenue every quarter. That gap determines whether Anthropic builds a frontier model company or collapses under its own infrastructure.
