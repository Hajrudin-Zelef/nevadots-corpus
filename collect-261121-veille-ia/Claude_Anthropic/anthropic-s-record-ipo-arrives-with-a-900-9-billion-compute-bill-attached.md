The Claude maker raised more than $107 billion, but its disclosed obligations to Google, Amazon, and Broadcom stretch all the way to 2045

Anthropic just pulled off the largest IPO by proceeds to date. It also disclosed future commitments of more than **$900.9 billion**, mostly tied to the computing power that keeps its Claude models running.

## The biggest IPO ever, by the numbers

Anthropic priced its offering on November 19, 2026. It sold 980 million shares at $83 apiece, raising around $81.3 billion.

The company also lined up a concurrent $26 billion private placement from strategic partners, a group that includes Google and NVIDIA.

Put together, gross proceeds from the IPO and the placement topped $107 billion.

Anthropic reported $17 billion in revenue for the third quarter of 2026. As of late October 2026, its annualized revenue run-rate stood at $81 billion.

## Where the $900.9 billion goes

The disclosure covers aggregate purchase commitments and lease agreements. The bulk of it relates to computing infrastructure, and the obligations run out to 2045.

The counterparties read like a who’s who of big tech:

- **Google:** more than $121 billion in commitments
- **Amazon:** $110 billion
- **Broadcom:** $161 billion in equipment leases

Approximately $70.7 billion of those commitments comes due in the 12 months following September 30, 2026. Per the research findings, the full scale of the commitments far surpasses the company’s post-IPO liquidity resources.

## The cost of running Claude

Data-center expenses ate up 58% of revenue in the third quarter of 2026.

## Background: the compute arms race

Google is both a major investor in the private placement and one of Anthropic’s largest infrastructure vendors. NVIDIA, whose chips power much of the AI boom, also showed up as a strategic backer.

## What this means for investors and the AI sector

Anthropic has an $81 billion run-rate and more than $107 billion in fresh gross proceeds. But obligations stretching to 2045 mean the company has committed to spending heavily for a very long time.

The commitments represent enormous future revenue for Google, Amazon, and Broadcom. Approximately $70.7 billion in near-term obligations comes due within the first year following September 30, 2026.
