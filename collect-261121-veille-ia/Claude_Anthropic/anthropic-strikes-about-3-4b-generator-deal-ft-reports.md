Anthropic has struck a deal worth about $3.4 billion to secure gas-fired power generators for data centers, the Financial Times reported on Sunday. The agreement transfers commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty Caterpillar generators from OnePWR Solutions, an equipment supplier based in San Antonio, Texas. Anthropic signed the contract in late September, according to the report.

The Financial Times described the agreement as the largest known single purchase of power-generation equipment by an AI lab. Rather than acquiring OnePWR itself, Anthropic assumed the equipment orders alongside the engineering and construction capabilities required to install the units. None of OnePWR's employees transferred to the AI startup, and the eventual deployment locations for the generators remain undisclosed.

OnePWR had ordered the generators from Caterpillar about two years ago but needed money to complete the purchase, the FT reported. Anthropic initially planned to buy electricity directly from OnePWR before opting to take over the equipment orders.

A separate transaction disclosure by investment bank Piper Sandler recorded a $3.4 billion sale of OnePWR's power-generation platform to an unnamed AI lab in September. Piper Sandler, which served as OnePWR's exclusive financial adviser, did not identify Anthropic as the buyer.

## Infrastructure commitments

Strong demand for Anthropic's Claude family of models has prompted the company to secure additional computing and power capacity. The transaction aligns with massive financial plans detailed ahead of Anthropic's planned stock market listing. An amended prospectus filed on Nov. 2 showed Anthropic intends to spend more than $895 billion in coming years, mostly on computing capacity and infrastructure.

The long-term outlay extends well into the next decade. Reuters reported in September that Anthropic expects to spend more than $500 billion over the next 10 years building AI infrastructure.

Anthropic's broader capital preparations have drawn investor scrutiny. Silicon Report reported on Nov. 7 that three major investors planning to buy into Anthropic's initial public offering told The Wall Street Journal that the company's expected $1.5 trillion valuation will likely need to come down.

Silicon Report also reported on Oct. 28 that Amazon held convertible notes with $6.2 billion in principal outstanding as of June 30, which accounted for Anthropic's entire debt load according to the artificial intelligence company's Form S-1 registration statement.

Power procurement also carries self-imposed costs beyond generator hardware. In a policy statement issued in February, Anthropic promised to fund every grid improvement required for its data center connections and to add equivalent new energy supply. The company likewise promised to shoulder any hikes in consumer power rates resulting from facilities developed with partners.

Gas-fired generation has provided quick power for other computing developers, though it has sparked legal challenges. Elon Musk's xAI faces a lawsuit over gas turbines at its Memphis data center site. Neither Anthropic nor OnePWR responded to requests for comment from Reuters and Bloomberg, and Reuters could not immediately verify the report.
