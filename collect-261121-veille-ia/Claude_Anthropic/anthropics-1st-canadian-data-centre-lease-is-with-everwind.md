EverWind, a Canadian clean fuel and wind energy company, will build a 420MW data centre to support AI and digital services, an investment Trent Vichie, Founder and CEO of EverWind, calls a new chapter

EverWind is investing more than CAD10bn (US$7.1bn) in a new data centre campus and renewable energy portfolio in Point Tupper, Nova Scotia.

The campus makes up more than CAD8bn (US$5.68bn) of the CAD10bn total, while CAD2bn (US$1.42bn) will be spent on expanding EverWind’s Nova Scotia onshore wind portfolio.

Anthropic has signed a long-term lease agreement for the first 95MW phase of the data centre side of the project.

“Anthropic has signed a lease with EverWind for data centre capacity at Point Tupper, Nova Scotia,” says Jacob Glick, Anthropic’s Head of Public Policy, Canada in a LinkedIn post.

“It's our first data centre lease in Canada.

“Canadians use Claude at some of the highest rates in the world, and this brings our infrastructure closer to the people who rely on it.

The first phase is set for completion in late 2027 and will be followed by a further 325MW to be operational in 2028. The timeline for both phases is subject to commercial, technical and regulatory conditions.

## Managing electricity and grid demand

To ensure the development does not increase electricity rates for existing customers, the campus integrates several hundred MWh of battery storage.

This storage capacity helps manage demand on the provincial electricity grid while supporting the facility.

The data centre will be designed to reduce or halt its electricity draw from the grid during peak times to protect overall reliability.

EverWind will fully fund all power network upgrades and connection costs attributable to the campus.

“Point Tupper has supported industry in the Strait Region for more than 50 years,” says Trent Vichie, Founder and CEO of EverWind.

“Today’s announcement represents major new investment in Nova Scotia’s digital and energy infrastructure and highlights the role Nova Scotians can play in driving Canada’s renewed economic ambitions, marking a new chapter for the site and creating opportunities for rural Nova Scotia.

“For decades, Nova Scotians have helped build major projects across Canada, often leaving home to find well-paying work.

“With this investment, we’re creating more opportunities for people to find jobs and build their careers at home in rural Nova Scotia.”

The company plans to use this combined wind investment and battery storage to support the broader integration of renewable energy.

These energy resources will also complement the developer’s existing plans to produce green hydrogen and ammonia at the site.

## Environmental and water protection

The Point Tupper facility will use a closed-loop cooling system that circulates a water-glycol mixture.

Heat extraction will primarily rely on air cooling, using water assistance only during the highest temperature periods. The data centre’s average water use will be around 100,000 litres per day for the first phase, rising to approximately 500,000 once the full 420MW build-out is complete.

Nova Scotia’s cool climate naturally reduces the cooling-water requirements for the digital infrastructure throughout much of the year.

“Responsible development means protecting ratepayers, safeguarding local water resources and delivering lasting benefits for communities,” says Trent.

“We applaud the federal and provincial governments for setting high standards for responsible development, and we will continue to work with communities to ensure that our work meets their expectations.

“Nova Scotians should expect us to demonstrate that we can protect ratepayers, safeguard local resources and deliver lasting community benefits, and we intend to be accountable for those commitments.”

Anthropic’s Jacob expressed similar sentiments.

“Canada and Nova Scotia have both set a high bar for how data centres get built, he adds

“We've signed the federal Responsible Data Centre Development Principles, and we're committed to meeting Premier Houston's conditions: protecting ratepayers, safeguarding water and making sure Nova Scotians benefit.

“That includes committing tens of millions of dollars to community priorities, which local leaders and residents will help decide.”

## Economic sovereignty and local investment

The project represents the largest privately funded investment in Nova Scotia’s history and will provide a boost to employment.

“The investment includes a new data centre campus in Point Tupper and in wind energy projects across Nova Scotia,” says Tim Houston, Premier of Nova Scotia in a LinkedIn post.

“Together, these projects are expected to support approximately 2,000 construction jobs and 215 permanent jobs.

“That's thousands of opportunities for Nova Scotians to work on major projects right here at home.

“Of course, projects of this size must be developed responsibly. EverWind has committed to meeting the expectations of both the federal and provincial governments to protect electricity ratepayers, safeguard local water resources and ensure communities share in the benefits. We will hold them to that.

“Nova Scotia has the people, the resources, and the potential to compete for major investments like this.”
