Anthropic said Wednesday it is selling shares in its initial public offering at an even higher price than previously marketed, setting it up for the largest Wall Street debut of all time.

Anthropic confirmed the IPO includes a sale of 980 million shares at $83 a share, raising $81.3 billion — surpassing SpaceX’s $75 billion IPO as the biggest on record.

The IPO is a major event for Wall Street and a pivotal moment for the artificial intelligence boom that has become a driving force behind the stock market.

The final pricing comes after Anthropic spent the past few days pitching its public offering to different investors. Anthropic initially set a target range for its shares at $70 to $78 a share, but the new pricing above the original range signals strong investor demand. Anthropic also raised the total number of shares being sold, up from 825 million.

Anthropic is selling roughly 956 million shares, while the company’s seven co-founders are selling roughly 24 million shares total, bringing the total number of shares to 980 million.

Anthropic is set to start trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker “ANT.” At $83 a share, the company’s valuation could be around $1.5 trillion — an enormous figure for a company founded barely six years ago, reflecting the excitement around AI.

The strong IPO pricing comes despite recent market jitters. The tech-focused Nasdaq Composite has dropped more than 10% since its record high; and Nvidia, which has been a major component of the AI trade, is down 30% in the past month. Investors have been looking for signs of strength especially since the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who left after admitting to an extramarital affair.

The IPO price is set by the company and the bankers underwriting the offering after gauging investors’ interest, and it’s what investors participating in the public offering will pay. But it’s not necessarily the price people will be paying when the stock hits the market.

The opening price of the stock is determined based on the fundamentals of supply and demand. For example, SpaceX set its IPO price at $135 a share, and opened on the stock market at $150 a share. Once trading begins, the price will fluctuate.

Investors at major institutions and financial firms often participate in IPOs, and they get first dibs on the shares. That’s not to say that retail investors who don’t work buying stocks for big companies will be left out in the cold. Companies can set aside shares for retail investors — or everyday people who trade or buy stocks in their own brokerage accounts — in IPOs. Anthropic is no different, and it’s set to put aside 20% of its offering for retail investors.
