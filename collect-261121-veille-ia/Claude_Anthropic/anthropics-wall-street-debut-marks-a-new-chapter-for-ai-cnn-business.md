Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI chatbot, is set to begin trading Thursday in the largest stock market debut of all time.

The AI developer sold 980 million shares at $83 a share in its initial public offering, raising $81.3 billion — a figure that surpasses SpaceX’s record-setting $75 billion debut earlier this year.

The blockbuster IPO underscores the enormous amount of money being invested in AI, and the growing influence that the technology has on everything from the stock market to the broader US economy.

The Anthropic offering comes alongside a separate $26 billion investment from major tech companies like Nvidia, bringing the total amount raised to $107.3 billion. That’s before an additional option for banks to sell more shares, which could lift Anthropic’s total haul even more — further outpacing SpaceX’s total $86 billion figure, accounting for its own option for banks to sell more shares.

“Both of them are much bigger than any previous IPO,” said Jay Ritter, professor emeritus and director of the IPO Initiative at the University of Florida. “What’s also remarkable is that Anthropic is less than six years old.”

The third- and fourth-largest IPOs of all time are Saudi Aramco at $29.4 billion in 2019 and SK Hynix at $26.5 billion this year.

Anthropic’s debut could vault it into the ranks of the world’s largest publicly traded companies. At $83 a share, the company’s valuation could be roughly $1.5 trillion. It will trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker “ANT.”

## From startup to trillion-dollar company

Anthropic was founded in 2021 when a group of employees at OpenAI left to start a new company with a focus on AI safety. That startup released its first version of Claude to the public in March 2023, a few months after OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public in November 2022.

When Anthropic released Claude Code in early 2025, it quickly gained traction with software developers. Revenue continued to soar after the launch of Claude Cowork in early 2026 and as Anthropic established itself as a core provider of agentic AI tools.

But, as a private company, there was less visibility into Anthropic’s finances. Going public means the company will now be required to disclose more information about its revenue, spending and losses every quarter, offering a pulse check on the health of the AI boom.

The path to its public debut has not been without controversy. The Trump administration at one point barred Anthropic from releasing its advanced models to the public. Meanwhile, there are growing concerns about the potential for AI agents to go rogue and for the increasingly powerful technology to be used in harmful ways.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei spearheaded calls to “pace the frontier” of AI in September after former Anthropic employees sounded the alarm over concerns about safety at the AI lab.

## Anthropic as an AI bellwether

Stock market strategists point to mid-October 2022 as the start of the AI bull market, which took off after ChatGPT was released on November 30, 2022. The Nasdaq Composite has gained roughly 135% since the start of the bull market, adding $26 trillion in market value. Now, one of the core AI companies is set to join the public markets.

By going public, Anthropic is in part carrying the weight of the AI boom on its shoulders. Investors are keen to see how the frontier labs can turn their models into steady profit streams, justify enormous spending on the data center buildout and deliver returns.

Anthropic earns revenue through companies and people using its Claude products. Anthropic’s revenue has experienced dramatic growth, helping the company fetch its more than $1 trillion-expected valuation and putting it at the forefront of how AI companies are trying to become profitable businesses.

Anthropic is not profitable yet because it is still investing in its AI tools. Anthropic has spending commitments of roughly $900 billion in the coming years as it spends money to get compute power to train its cutting-edge AI models and support people using them. The company needs to lock in extraordinary revenue growth to justify its business model and valuation.

Optimistic investors believe that demand for AI tools will continue to surge as more companies adopt the technology. Analysts expect Anthropic to pursue other methods for revenue growth, including the development of specialized models that can be used in industries such as health and science.

But skeptics question whether the company can maintain its growth and revenue as competition intensifies. A bearish case for Anthropic is that AI tools are going to become more like a commodity, where customers can pick and choose between different providers with ease, making it difficult to retain strong pricing power and eating into revenue.

The IPO also comes as enthusiasm for AI stocks is cooling: The tech-focused Nasdaq Composite is down more than 10% from its record high six weeks ago.

Anthropic’s IPO could help restore confidence in the AI trade, which has been shaken in recent weeks by concerns about a slowdown to the data center buildout. But if it falls short of expectations during trading, that could add to investors’ doubts.

“Investor sentiment toward AI has been extremely volatile, and while a successful IPO will likely turn that sentiment positive in the near term, the long-term trajectory of Anthropic’s business and performance is what could really instill greater confidence in the AI trade,” said Avery Marquez, director of investment strategies at Renaissance Capital, a provider of pre-IPO research and IPO-focused ETFs.
