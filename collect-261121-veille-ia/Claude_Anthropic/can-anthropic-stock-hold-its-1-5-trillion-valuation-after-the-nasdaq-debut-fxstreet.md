Anthropic begins trading on Nasdaq on November 19 after pricing its initial public offering at $83 per share, above the previous $70 to $78 range. The company sold 980 million Class A shares and raised approximately $81.3 billion, giving Anthropic a fully diluted valuation of about $1.5 trillion.

The final pricing confirms that institutional demand for the offering was strong enough for Anthropic and its underwriters to increase both the size of the deal and the price. The focus now shifts from the IPO bookbuilding process to the public market, where investors will determine whether the valuation can be sustained once the shares begin trading freely.

## Anthropic priced above its original IPO range

Anthropic initially marketed 825 million shares at between $70 and $78. Strong demand allowed the company to increase the offering to 980 million shares and set the final price at $83. The larger deal raised approximately $81.3 billion and made the offering the largest IPO on record.

The higher price is important because it changes the starting point for public investors. At $83, Anthropic already enters the market with a valuation of about $1.5 trillion. Any significant first-day gain would push that valuation even higher, while a move back toward the IPO price would indicate that the institutional bookbuilding process had already absorbed much of the available demand.

## Revenue growth supports the valuation, but expectations are already high

Anthropic has one of the fastest growth profiles in the AI industry. Its annualized revenue run rate reached approximately $81 billion by October, while the number of customers spending at least $100,000 per year increased to around 11,900.

Those figures explain why investors were willing to accept a valuation normally associated with the world's largest technology companies. Claude has expanded rapidly across enterprise AI, software development and coding, giving Anthropic a clear commercial position alongside OpenAI and Google.

The valuation, however, already assumes that this growth will continue at a very high rate. At $1.5 trillion, investors are not only paying for current revenue. They are also paying for the expectation that Anthropic will continue expanding its customer base, increase enterprise spending and maintain a competitive position in frontier AI.

## Computing costs remain the main financial constraint

Anthropic's growth requires substantial spending on computing infrastructure. The company spent nearly $9 billion on infrastructure during the first half of 2026 and has committed close to $900 billion to future computing capacity. It also reported a third-quarter operating loss of $567 million as computing costs increased.

This cost structure is central to the investment case. Anthropic needs large amounts of GPUs, data center capacity, networking equipment and power to train and operate increasingly advanced AI models. Those investments support revenue growth, but they also place pressure on margins and cash flow.

For public investors, the relevant issue is therefore not whether Anthropic can continue growing revenue. The key financial question is whether revenue growth can eventually outpace the cost of maintaining its computing infrastructure. That relationship will determine whether the current valuation can be supported over time.

## Strategic investors strengthen Anthropic's capital base

The public offering is accompanied by a separate private placement worth approximately $26 billion. Participants include AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, with the shares purchased at the IPO price.

This gives Anthropic access to additional capital while also deepening its relationships with major companies across the AI infrastructure supply chain. Many of these investors are either suppliers, cloud partners or strategic participants in the broader AI ecosystem.

The structure also shows how closely connected the current AI investment cycle has become. Semiconductor companies, cloud providers and AI developers increasingly depend on one another for capital, infrastructure and customer demand. Anthropic's ability to convert that ecosystem into sustainable revenue will be more important than the size of the financing itself.

## Retail demand could increase first-day volatility

Anthropic's first trading session is also expected to attract significant retail participation. Underwriters have reserved up to 20% of the offering for retail investors, while market estimates suggest first-day retail net buying could reach around $250 million.

The company is also entering the market alongside a growing range of leveraged products linked to ANT. Several issuers have prepared single-stock leveraged ETFs around Anthropic's listing, increasing the number of ways traders can take directional exposure after the stock begins trading.

These products are likely to increase short-term trading activity, but they do not change Anthropic's fundamental valuation. The more important signal will be whether the stock can maintain its market value after the initial allocation process, retail demand and listing-day speculation begin to normalize.

## The public market will provide a clearer valuation signal

The $83 IPO price reflects the outcome of the institutional bookbuilding process. Public market trading will provide a broader test because buyers and sellers will be able to reassess Anthropic's valuation continuously.

If ANT trades consistently above $83 after the opening session, the market will be assigning Anthropic a valuation above the $1.5 trillion level implied by the IPO. If the stock returns toward the offer price, it will indicate that the IPO valuation already captured most of the available demand.

This distinction matters because strong IPO demand and sustainable public-market valuation are not the same thing. Oversubscription shows that investors wanted access to the offering. Continued trading above the IPO price would show that investors are also willing to maintain that valuation after the allocation process ends.

## Anthropic is becoming an important test for AI valuations

Anthropic is entering the public market while investors are becoming more selective toward AI companies. The sector continues to benefit from strong demand, but concerns around infrastructure spending, financing requirements and long-term profitability have increased.

Anthropic sits directly at the center of that debate. Its revenue growth is strong, its strategic position is clear and investor demand for the IPO has been substantial. At the same time, the company requires very large amounts of capital to maintain its competitive position.

The first trading sessions will therefore provide a useful indication of how public investors currently value frontier AI companies. The key issue is straightforward: whether Anthropic's expected future growth is sufficient to support a valuation of approximately $1.5 trillion once the stock begins trading in the open market.
