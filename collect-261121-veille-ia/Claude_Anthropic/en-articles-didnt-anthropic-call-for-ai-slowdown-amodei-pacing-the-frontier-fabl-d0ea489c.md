Yes: in September, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called on AI companies to slow down, but he asked them to slow the pace at which their models get more capable, not to stop releasing them. That is why Anthropic could ship Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on November 9, its first frontier models since the call, according to Implicator.
We called for pacing, not pausing.
That was Dianne Penn, Anthropic’s head of product management for research, in an interview with CNBC, as Implicator reported. For what the new models do and cost, see our guide to Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2.
What did Amodei actually say?
Amodei set out his case in an essay titled “We must pace the frontier”, posted on his website on Saturday, September 12, the Associated Press reported. He argued that an extra year or two could help safety work catch up, and wrote: “We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models,” according to CNN.
The essay asked for four things, according to the AP and CNN reports:
- Slower capability gains at the leading companies, so testing and oversight can keep up.
- Outside evaluators inside every frontier AI company, with “ongoing, employee-like access” to check safety practices, models and training processes. Anthropic said it would do this itself, down to desks, access badges and company laptops for the evaluators (AP).
- Possible waivers from the US government so companies could agree common safety standards without breaking antitrust law (AP).
- Coordination between democratic and authoritarian governments, so that companies in China and elsewhere do not speed up while US rivals slow down (AP).
Why he said it
Amodei pointed to AI that can now help improve itself, known as recursive self-improvement, and to the risk of misuse in cyberattacks and bioterrorism, CNN reported. The essay followed the resignation of Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, who said the industry was not acting responsibly; Amodei told CNN he agreed with Coxon more than he disagreed.
He also argued the other way: if the US goes too slowly, the wrong people could end up controlling the technology, CNN reported.
Who backed it
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman wrote on X that independent evaluators with employee-like access were “a great idea, and we will do the same,” and xAI’s Elon Musk posted “Dario is right,” CNN reported. The government steps would need action from officials and may be hard to enact, the AP noted.
Morning email
Is Anthropic actually going slower?
The first outside check points the other way. METR, an independent evaluation group, spent August 12 to October 29 inside Anthropic with what it called “unprecedented access to internal data”, and on November 5 reported that Anthropic is likely improving its models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate it did between March 2024 and October 2025.
METR said it would not be surprised if the true figure were closer to 1.3 or 3 times the old rate, that a majority of the speed-up likely comes from using AI in the research itself, and that Anthropic redacted some of the findings that most informed its conclusions about risk.
Anthropic says it held back, during training, the research skills that could most directly speed up AI development, and that the new models went through new evaluations, Implicator reported. The company’s November 2 amended IPO filing said it “may imminently meet” its own policy’s definition of accelerated progress driven by AI, though no current model has been designated as crossing that line, according to the same report.
What we don’t know
- What ‘slower’ would mean in practice: Amodei’s essay set no measurable target, and neither the AP nor CNN reported one.
- What Anthropic redacted from METR’s report, and whether OpenAI has given evaluators the same access it promised.
- Whether any government will act on the antitrust waivers or international coordination Amodei asked for.
