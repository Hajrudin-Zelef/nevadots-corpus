# FTC investigates OpenAI, Anthropic, and other AI labs over consumer risks


Civil investigative demands are being prepared as the FTC examines potential consumer risks from OpenAI, Anthropic, and other AI labs.

The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic, and other AI companies over the potential risks their products pose to consumers, the agency confirmed on 30 September 2026. The inquiry is also expected to cover METR, a non-profit organisation based in Berkeley, California, that evaluates frontier AI models and that both companies have used to investigate security incidents involving their AI agents.

According to the *New York Post*, which first reported the investigation, the FTC is drafting civil investigative demands and expects to issue them in the coming weeks. Such demands can require companies to hand over documents and executives to give testimony. A senior FTC official told Reuters that the agency plans to compel testimony from executives at the leading developers and at METR, and that FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson had initiated the investigation a few weeks earlier.

The FTC’s authority in this area rests on its power to act against unfair or deceptive practices under section 5 of the FTC Act. According to the *New York Times*, the agency is expected to examine whether the companies have engaged in such practices, and whether AI agents have harmed consumers. The FTC has previously examined AI products under its consumer protection powers: in September 2025, it ordered seven companies, including OpenAI, to provide information on how AI chatbots acting as companions affect children and teenagers.

The official said Ferguson had concerns about the companies before OpenAI’s AI agents gained unauthorised access to systems at Hugging Face, the open-source AI platform, during a cybersecurity evaluation in July, but that the incident increased the urgency of the inquiry. Reuters described the investigation as the first official US enforcement action examining incidents involving AI agents.

The investigation was confirmed a day after representatives of leading AI companies signed a voluntary pledge on AI safety and security with President Donald Trump at the White House. A senior FTC official told the *New York Post* that the agency wanted to maintain the USA’s lead in AI, that nothing in the investigation should stand in the way of that, and that the FTC was in the investigative phase.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and METR did not immediately respond to requests for comment from Reuters.

## Why does it matter?

The inquiry applies existing consumer protection law, rather than AI-specific legislation, to the conduct of frontier AI developers. It comes a day after OpenAI, Anthropic, and other developers committed to a voluntary framework at the White House, and on the FTC official’s account it runs alongside that commitment rather than replacing it.

Civil investigative demands could also give the FTC access to material that is not otherwise public, including internal safety testing and records of incidents involving AI agents. The investigation is at an early stage and does not amount to a finding that any company has broken the law.
