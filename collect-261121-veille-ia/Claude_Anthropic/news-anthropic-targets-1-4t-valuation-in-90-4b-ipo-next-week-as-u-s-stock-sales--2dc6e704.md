Anthropic is targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion as it aims to raise $90.4 billion in the largest initial public offering in history next week on November 18, according to Bloomberg. This total beats the $86.2 billion raised in Elon Musk’s SpaceX SPCX -7.69% ▼ listing in June.
The Claude AI maker is offering 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, plus $26 billion in private stock to backers including Nvidia NVDA -14.01% ▼, Microsoft MSFT -2.77% ▼, and Alphabet’s Google GOOGL -2.70% ▼. Trading on the Nasdaq is expected to start November 19 under the ticker ANT. Using extra share options after trading begins would push the deal past $100 billion, topping Meta Platforms’ META -5.93% ▼ roughly $98 billion equity plan announced last month.
AI Listings Have Pushed U.S. Share Sales Past 2021 High
U.S.-listed companies have raised over $425 billion in 2026 through IPOs and direct share sales, Bloomberg data shows. That figure also includes completed open-market stock programs. This beats the prior record of about $408 billion set in 2021.
Counting Anthropic, America’s three biggest IPOs ever have landed within months of one another to fund large AI costs. Even after inflation, 2026’s top listings dwarf past record-holders Saudi Aramco and Visa V -0.09% ▼.
South Korean chipmaker SK Hynix SKHY -13.11% ▼ raised $26.5 billion in July, marking the largest U.S. debut by a foreign firm. Adjusted for inflation, however, that listing still trails past debuts from Alibaba BABA -2.68% ▼ in 2014 and SoftBank SFTBY -17.35% ▼ in 2018.
Tech Giants Are Selling Shares to Pay for AI Growth
Rising AI spending bills have also pushed tech giants to sell stock for the first time in decades. Mark Zuckerberg‘s Meta leads corporate fundraising with its $98 billion package. Most of that total comes from a planned $60 billion at-the-market program, which lets companies sell shares gradually in the open market.
Bitcoin treasury firm Strategy MSTR -9.67% ▼ used open-market programs to raise $21 billion in 2024 and another $21 billion in 2025.
In 2026, Alphabet’s roughly $85 billion funding plan includes a $40 billion open-market stock program. Oracle ORCL -8.48% ▼ also sold about $20 billion in shares through its own program alongside tens of billions of dollars in extra stock and debt sales.
Together, these deals show that even cash-rich tech giants are turning to the stock market to help pay for costly AI data centers and chips.
