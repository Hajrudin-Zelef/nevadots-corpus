AI frontier labs finally have a price tag.
Anthropic priced its highly anticipated IPO at $83 a share, above its initial range of $70 to $78, evidence of strong demand. The IPO raised $81.3 billion for Anthropic and its cofounders, valuing the Claude maker at around $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis.
The debut will be the biggest test yet of whether public-market investors will underwrite AI’s huge valuations and costly infrastructure. Shares are set to start trading on Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. Anthropic sold 956.4 million Class A shares.
It’s the largest tech IPO by capital raised, besting SpaceX, which raised $75 billion in June, according to PitchBook data. Adding a $26 billion private placement with its compute suppliers, including Nvidia, Google, and Microsoft, would bring Anthropic’s total to $107.3 billion.
The raise vaults Anthropic ahead of its chief rival OpenAI in total capital raised. Counting the IPO and private placement, Anthropic has raised $219 billion, compared to OpenAI’s $186.4 billion, according to PitchBook data. OpenAI has ruled out going public in 2026, and CFO Sarah Friar has pointed to 2027.
Less than two months ago, Anthropic was eyeing a roughly $2 trillion valuation, Bloomberg reported. But a public market selloff of AI stocks pushed the company to set a lower range.
In the end, demand was high enough that Anthropic could price above its range and upsize its offering from 825 million shares. The offering marks a roughly 1.5x step-up from Anthropic’s last private valuation of $965 billion in May, according to PitchBook data.
Anthropic hopes its fast growth can offset the steep costs of training and developing AI models. For the first three quarters of 2026, the company reported $33.2 billion in revenue, up a staggering 1,206% year over year, according to SEC filings.
But the costs of training AI are just as steep. Anthropic has disclosed over $900 billion in purchase commitments and leases, all for compute and infrastructure access.
