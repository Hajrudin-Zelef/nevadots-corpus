## TL;DR

- Samsung SDS is in early-stage talks to sign Anthropic and OpenAI as tenants for Korean AI data centres, the Financial Times reports.
- Samsung SDS broke ground on an AI-only data centre in Gumi on 2 November with 60MW initial capacity, expandable to 120MW.
- Analysts cited by the FT expect Samsung SDS's data centre revenue to reach Won6.6tn ($4.9bn) by 2031, roughly six times current levels.

Samsung SDS is in early-stage talks with Anthropic and OpenAI to sign them as tenants for its Korean AI data centres, the Financial Times reported on 15 November. The talks are preliminary; no terms or capacity have been disclosed, and none of the three companies have confirmed them publicly.

The pitch, per the FT, is that Samsung aims to be a "one-stop shop that builds, runs, and powers data centers". Samsung C&T, the construction arm, puts up the buildings and power plants; Samsung SDS designs and operates them. Samsung SDS broke ground on an AI-only site in Gumi on 2 November with "60 megawatts of initial capacity, expandable to 120 megawatts", scheduled to begin operations in March 2029.

Analysts cited by the FT expect Samsung SDS's data centre revenue to reach Won6.6tn ($4.9bn) by 2031, roughly six times current levels. Seoul Economic Daily breaks the figure down as 1.6 trillion won of existing revenue, 2 trillion from Gumi, 2 trillion from the National AI Computing Center, and 1 trillion from design-build-operate projects.

The FT story lands a day after we tracked SK Hynix pledging $1.05B to shore up Korea's chip supplier base.
