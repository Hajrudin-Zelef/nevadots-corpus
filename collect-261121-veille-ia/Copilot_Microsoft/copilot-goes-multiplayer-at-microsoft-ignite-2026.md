Microsoft kicked off its annual Ignite conference yesterday, in San Francisco, California, with a sweeping overhaul of its enterprise AI stack, unveiling collaborative “multiplayer” AI workspaces, a landmark CRM partnership with Salesforce and a new industry-wide AI transparency initiative.

Addressing attendees during his opening keynote, Microsoft Chairman and CEO Satya Nadella framed the announcements around turning rapid AI advancements into measurable business value while maintaining organisational control and security.

“We need to parlay any technological advance in to real outcomes and new points of leverage for people, organisations and society at large,” he said. “For us, that comes down to three things: Keeping human ambition at the centre, ensuring you have ownership over your organisation’s intelligence and learning, and trust. Our goal is to build a stack that addresses these three architectural imperatives.

## Copilot goes multiplayer and Microsoft teams up with salesforce

At the centre of Microsoft’s productivity updates is a redesigned Copilot experience “designed to turn intelligence into tangible value,” according to Microsoft. While previous iterations focused on individual assistants, the newly announced Copilot Workspaces introduces a shared environment where multiple employees and AI agents can collaborate simultaneously.

“Copilot was about the single–player work but time has come for a true multi-player form factor. This is Copilot Workspaces,” Satya said. 

In a notable shift toward cross-platform interoperability, Microsoft also announced Copilotforce in partnership with Salesforce. Now in public preview, the integration connects Salesforce with Microsoft Copilot, Teams and Fabric.

Copilotforce launched with more than 38 prebuilt sales skills – such as pipeline review, renewal radar and automated call follow-ups – while preserving existing security permissions. According to Microsoft Source, the partnership also includes two-way Zero Copy data integrations between Salesforce Data 360 and Microsoft OneLake in Fabric so companies do not have to maintain duplicate datasets.

“Through Copilotforce, we’re bringing Salesforce to the largest surface in enterprise software today, embedding trusted Salesforce context and intelligent sales workflows directly where sellers work,” said Miguel Milano, President and Chief Operating Officer at Salesforce. “By bringing together the #1 CRM with the #1 productivity surface, we’re transforming AI from a standalone tool into a powerful driver of productivity, smarter decisions and customer success.”

## AI Transparency Initiative and autonomous cyber defence

Addressing growing enterprise concerns over AI governance and reliability, Satya unveiled the Microsoft AI Transparency Initiative alongside more than 40 partner organisations and expanded collaboration with the National Institute of Standards and Technology

“What a model tells us about itself… cannot be trusted,” he said. “You have these unacceptable nested set of black boxes which we now need to make transparent and verifiable, which is why today we are announcing the Microsoft AI Transparency Initiative.”

As detailed on the Official Microsoft Blog, the initiative aims to create open standards for independently auditing and verifying AI agent behaviour across different model providers.

On the cybersecurity front, Microsoft announced the general availability of Microsoft Perception. The platform orchestrates specialised red, blue and green team AI agents to continuously test, detects and fix security gaps while keeping human defenders in control:

- **Red team agents** simulate attacks from an adversary’s perspective before a breach occurs.
- **Blue team agents** detect and investigate active threats.
- **Green team agents** patch and remediate vulnerabilities.

“With the rise of AI tools, attackers can exploit a vulnerability in as little as 35 minutes after it was published, making the window to address new CVes and apply patches smaller than ever,” said Jason Rogers, Group Product Manager of Microsoft Defender for Office 365, in a Microsoft blog. “The existing approach to exposure management can’t keep up with addressing risk. To address this, we are bringing delivering a new set of agentic exposure management capabilities in Microsoft Perception, helping organisations reduce the risks that matter most today, while strengthening their security foundation.”

To help executives track the financial return on these deployments, Microsoft also introduced Agent 365 Business Analytics, offering visibility into the spend, usage and business impact of AI agents across an organisation.

## Microsoft IQ and seven new in-house AI models

To prevent AI agents from operating in data silos, Microsoft launched Microsoft IQ, an enterprise context management platform designed to help organisations capture, govern and reuse internal business knowledge. Supported by hundreds of new data connectors and reusable business plugins, Microsoft IQ is already being deployed by companies like Publicis Groupe, which is combining its proprietary data and sector expertise with the platform to build custom AI tools for client projects.

“How do we capitalise on all this new capability that’s growing exponentially and how do we built the capability inside of our organisations to deal with the consequences of all this capability?” Satya asked during the keynote.

Microsoft also expanded its Microsoft Foundry development platform, which now hosts Meta’s latest models alongside closed-source offerings, and introduced seven new in-house Microsoft AI (MAI) models spanning coding, cybersecurity, image, audio and decision-making, including MAI Cyber 2 Flash, Code 2 Flash and the generally available Microsoft-Decision-1.1.

“Foundry has all the popular closed and open models,” Satya said. “We’re introducing a new flagship foundry model called Code 2. Code 2 is at the frontier of cost performance for everyday enterprise coding tasks.”

## Overhauling Azure infrastructure, Cobalt 200 and HorizonDB

Underpinning the software releases is a major hardware and cloud infrastructure push designed for agentic workloads that require heavy compute beyond standard model inference, such as compiling code and executing external tools.

Referring to the recent Microsoft outage, Satya acknowledged the stakes of cloud reliability: “Last week was really a reminder of how critical infrastructure is really the dial of the world. In that context, I know we have work to do to provide the resilience you count on… We are also restructuring the core compute storage and networking primitive in Azure to execute, connect and share context at that agent level.”

Key infrastructure updates outlined in the Azure Ignite 2026 Blog include:

- **Azure Cobalt 200 Virtual Machines:** Now broadly available, Microsoft’s custom Arm-based VMs deliver up to 50% better CPU performance than the previous Cobalt 100 generation. Cobalt is slated to expand to 23 regions by late 2026 and account for a major share of Azure's new compute capacity in 2027.
- **HorizonDB:** Microsoft’s distributed PostgreSQL database is now generally available on Azure (and in public preview within Microsoft Fabric), delivering up to 8x faster performance than self-managed PostgreSQL. “This represents the complete unification of transactional and analytical data for the agentic era,” Microsoft noted.
- **Azure Catalyst:** A new unified platform combining Azure and GitHub agentic tools to help enterprises modernise legacy applications, code and databases for AI.
- **Windows Hybrid Intelligence:** A new capability enabling AI tasks, starting with GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Copilot, to dynamically execute across local devices and the cloud depending on the optimal environment.
