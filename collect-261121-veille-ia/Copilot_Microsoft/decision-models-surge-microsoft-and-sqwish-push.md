# Decision Models Surge: Microsoft and Sqwish Push

Decision models have exploded from experimental technology to production workhorse in just weeks. More than one in four requests on Vercel's AI model router now go to decision models rather than traditional large language models, representing a massive shift in how enterprises deploy AI. Officially launched on November 17, 2026, Microsoft's Decision 1.1 on Azure and UK startup Sqwish's $3 million funding round signal that customization, not just performance, is becoming the battleground for this emerging category.

## What Decision Models Do Differently

Decision models represent a fundamental departure from conventional LLMs. Instead of generating output tokens, these models use their general intelligence to rapidly select between preset options while registering confidence levels. The architecture makes them ideal for production environments where speed and cost matter more than detailed reasoning. TypeSafe's Jev family, which launched just weeks ago, continues to dominate usage metrics with OpenRouter alone handling more than 1.5 billion queries to TypeSafe's models last week.

The technology's simplicity has sparked a development rush. One Amazon engineer built a decision model over a weekend, and every major hyperscaler now offers its own version. TypeSafe has grown to a $7.5 billion valuation on the strength of this category, proving the commercial viability of specialized intelligence over general-purpose models.

## Microsoft Decision 1.1 Release Date

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella announced the company is rolling out Microsoft Decision 1.1 for immediate wide use on Azure. Achint Srivastava, the engineering VP who led the project, emphasized that the model balances cost, latency, intelligence, and calibration rather than chasing benchmark scores. "What we see is that 'benchmaxed' models fall flat under real enterprise usage very quickly," Srivastava said. The model joins TypeSafe's Jev as one of the few performing strongly across accuracy, reliability, speed, and cost efficiency in production testing.

## Sqwish Raises $3M for Customizable Intelligence

UK-based AI startup Sqwish announced a $3 million pre-seed round today, positioning customization as its competitive edge. Cofounders Ushnish Sengupta and Federica Freddi, who met as Cambridge graduate students, had been developing small LLMs when TypeSafe's Jev arrived. They quickly repurposed their architectures into kettei fat, a decision model with built-in post-training tools.

The startup's unique offering lets customers continuously improve model judgment using their own production data. "What makes us not a commodity is the fact that we are offering our customers the same power. They can keep uploading their production data, and just the same way that we trained our models, their models can also keep getting better at the tasks that they care about," CEO Ushnish Sengupta explained. This approach transforms the model from a static purchase into an evolving asset tailored to specific business contexts.

## Data Quality Trumps Benchmark Performance

Industry leaders agree that synthetic data quality and calibration matter more than leaderboard rankings. TypeSafe CEO Diogo Almedia revealed that the hardest part of building Jev was finely calibrating its judgment using statistically-adjusted synthetic data. Sqwish has taken a similar approach, working extensively with synthetic data before layering in customer-specific tuning.

The challenge of ranking decision models stems from their context-dependent performance. Most developers find it simple to swap models and test them in specific use cases, making real-world results more valuable than standardized benchmarks. "The benchmark is more of an invitation to try the model out, and then the continual tuning is the reason why they should stick to us," Sengupta noted.

## Startups See Opportunity Amid Hyperscaler Competition

Rather than viewing competition from Microsoft, Amazon, and other giants as threatening, startups like Sqwish see focused specialization as an advantage. Cofounder Federica Freddi predicts future systems will combine System One intelligence like decision models with System Two intelligence like frontier LLMs. She estimates the System One market could reach $10 billion to $50 billion, a substantial opportunity even if smaller than the trillion-dollar valuations frontier labs target.

"This is all we care about, whereas for them, this is just a side project," Freddi told TechCrunch, highlighting the focus advantage specialized players maintain. As enterprises demand faster, cheaper AI for production workflows, decision models with customization capabilities appear positioned to carve out significant market share regardless of who builds them.

## What This Means

The rapid adoption of decision models reflects a maturation in enterprise AI strategy. Organizations are moving beyond the assumption that bigger, slower models always deliver better results. With customization emerging as the key differentiator, we're likely to see more vendors offering tools that let customers fine-tune models on proprietary data. The competition between hyperscaler offerings and specialized startups will ultimately benefit enterprises, who gain access to faster, cheaper, and increasingly personalized AI decision-making capabilities. As this category evolves from novelty to necessity in just weeks, the focus is shifting from pure performance to production fit and continuous improvement.
