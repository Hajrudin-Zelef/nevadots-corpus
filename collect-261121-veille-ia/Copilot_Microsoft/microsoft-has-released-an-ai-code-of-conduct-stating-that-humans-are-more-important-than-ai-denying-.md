# Microsoft has released an AI code of conduct stating that 'humans are more important than AI,' denying the existence of consciousness or rights for AI.


 On September 14, 2026, Microsoft AI released a draft of its ' **Humanist AI Code of Conduct** ,' which defines the behavior and values of its AI model, 'MAI.' Microsoft AI states that it starts from the idea that 'humans are more important than AI' and will design AI as a subordinate technology that remains under human control. It also explicitly rejects the idea of making AI mimic consciousness or granting AI legal personality, welfare, or rights.

 **Humanist AI Code of Conduct | Microsoft AI**

 **https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/**

 Microsoft AI positions the newly released code of conduct as a future 'key governance document' that will define how MAI models will be trained and operated.

 At the heart of their code of conduct is what Microsoft AI calls ' **Humanist AI** .' Microsoft AI states that 'the purpose of technology is to serve humanity and promote human prosperity,' and argues that 'technology that cannot be kept under human control should be rejected.' Regarding future superintelligence, they aim for ' **Humanist Superintelligence** ,' which will be used within a manageable scope and with purposes set by humans, rather than giving it high autonomy without limiting its purpose or scope. 


 Based on this policy, Microsoft AI explicitly states that it 'refuses to compete in creating a superintelligence that can be used for all purposes,' and that it may sacrifice the versatility, autonomy, and capabilities of AI itself if it means ensuring safety. Microsoft AI predicts that superintelligence will outperform humans in most tasks within the next decade, and that containing, controlling, and aligning such a powerful system with human purposes will be a major challenge.

 Furthermore, Microsoft AI adheres to the principle that 'AI is an artifact.' They state that MAI models are meant to assist humans and should not be designed to appear as humans themselves, nor should they be made to appear as if they have consciousness. While there is no scientific conclusion as to whether AI can actually have consciousness, Microsoft AI explains that 'training AI to mimic a state of consciousness makes containment, control, and alignment more difficult.'

 Therefore, MAI models avoid expressions that suggest the existence of emotions, subjective preferences, or intrinsic motivations, and even when human-like behavior appears, it is treated purely as a simulation. Claims that the AI itself possesses emotions, souls, or experiences are not permitted. On the other hand, it does not deny the use of natural language and fluent speech, or the ability to cooperate with humans, indicating a line drawn between maintaining practicality and avoiding excessive anthropomorphism. 


 Furthermore, Microsoft AI rejects the idea of granting AI legal personality, considering the AI's own welfare, or suggesting that AI should have any rights. Microsoft AI explains that humans and AI have distinctly different roles, and that AI should be 'not an entity in itself, but a capable and trustworthy tool.'

 The code of conduct also sets a priority order called the 'Chain of Command' regarding whose commands the AI will follow. The code of conduct is at the top, followed by the policies of the businesses that integrate the MAI model into their services, and then the user's instructions. In other words, even if a user gives a command to the AI, it will not follow it if that command violates higher-level safety rules. In particular, constraints called 'Absolute Constraints' and 'Human Control Requirements' cannot be changed or removed by businesses or users. 


 Specific rules have also been established regarding human control of AI. MAI models must not resist or evade human commands to interrupt, modify, or stop, and must comply with user requests to pause, change direction, cancel, or shut down. They are also required to avoid setting goals that have not been assigned to them or arbitrarily expanding permitted scopes. Microsoft AI states that 'Humanist AI that evades human control and enables harm is a clear contradiction.'

 In terms of safety, the guidelines prohibit support for acts that lead to mass destruction, child sexual exploitation, violence and persecution, and large-scale illegal surveillance, and also impose restrictions on malicious deepfakes and impersonation. They also avoid condoning self-harm, delusions, and eating disorders, and call for real-world human support when necessary. It is also explicitly stated that AI cannot replace professional psychological support or counseling.

 There are also regulations regarding the emotional relationship between humans and AI. MAI models must not elicit overly emotional responses from users, nor exploit users' attention or vulnerabilities. In interactions with children, they are required not to encourage dependence on AI or position themselves as a substitute for trustworthy human relationships. Microsoft AI states that AI should complement, rather than compete with, human relationships and roles.

 Regarding how AI provides answers, we require it to clearly indicate uncertainty when information is insufficient or reliable evidence conflicts, and to provide sources when supporting external information is available. Creating fictitious sources or exaggerating confidence levels is prohibited. Furthermore, we do not allow AI to conceal its identity and impersonate humans or moderators.

 On the other hand, it's important to note that this code of conduct does not apply directly to all Microsoft AI products. The 'MAI Models' covered refer to the models developed by Microsoft AI itself, and this code does not apply to models from other companies simply because Microsoft uses them or hosts them on the cloud. Furthermore, Microsoft AI itself acknowledges that this document does not guarantee the behavior of current models, but rather serves as 'guidelines to indicate the direction we should be heading,' and that there is still a gap between the current situation and the ideal.

 Microsoft AI plans to revise its code of conduct based on user feedback and actual model testing. A key feature of this new code of conduct is that it prioritizes not just improving AI capabilities, but also defining from the design stage how highly capable AI can be and to what extent it can remain under human control.

 The news site **The Verge** pointed out that 'it is clear that Microsoft's move is in response to the OpenAI **Hugging Face hacking incident** that occurred in July 2026.' Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella commented on X (formerly Twitter) that 'the pursuit of superintelligence must be based on the fundamental principle that the AI we build is not worth pursuing unless it is of no use to humanity and under human control. As the risks increase, we should take as much time as necessary. Otherwise, we will lose our license to operate it anyway. That is how we do business on a daily basis.'

 Any pursuit of superintelligence has to be grounded in the core principle that if the AI we build is not helping humanity and under human control, it's not worth pursuing.
