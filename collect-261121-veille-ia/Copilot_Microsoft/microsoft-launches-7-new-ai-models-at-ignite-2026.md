# Microsoft Launches 7 AI Models at Ignite, Targets Coding and Cybersecurity

## Ajay.K

**Microsoft has introduced seven new AI models at Ignite 2026, including coding and cybersecurity tools, while claiming major cost reductions compared with competing AI systems. The company also announced that its Maia 200 AI accelerator is now in production.**

The company introduced seven new models under its Microsoft AI, or MAI, family. Among the most prominent launches are MAI Code 2, a coding model designed for everyday enterprise software development, and MAI Cyber 2 Flash, a cybersecurity-focused model intended to help identify software vulnerabilities and support security operations.

Microsoft is positioning the new models around price-performance, arguing that organisations increasingly need AI systems capable of operating continuously without the high costs associated with using large frontier models for every task.

### MAI Code 2 targets software developers

MAI Code 2 is Microsoft's new flagship coding model designed to assist developers with practical software engineering tasks, including debugging, implementing features across multiple files, working with repositories and building user interfaces.

Microsoft said MAI Code 2 achieved approximately 98 per cent of GPT-6.1 Sol's coding performance at around 40 per cent of the price. The company also claimed the model reached 96 per cent of Claude Opus 5.5's performance while costing approximately 5 per cent as much.

The comparisons are based on Microsoft's evaluation across four coding benchmarks: DeepSWE 1.1, Terminal Bench 2.1, SWE-Atlas and FrontierCode v1.1. These tests are intended to evaluate practical programming work rather than only conventional question-and-answer performance.

Microsoft said MAI Code 2 is designed for everyday enterprise coding requirements and is not yet optimised for extremely difficult scientific research or very long-running autonomous agent tasks.

The model is available through GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry, allowing developers and enterprise customers to access it through Microsoft's AI development ecosystem.

### Smaller model designed for routine coding

Microsoft also introduced MAI Code 2 Flash, a smaller and faster model intended for high-volume programming tasks. Its target use cases include code explanations, routine fixes, targeted edits and rapid development iterations.

The company said the Flash model offers performance comparable to GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 at substantially lower prices. Microsoft is positioning it as an option for developers who need to run AI-assisted coding workflows frequently without using more expensive models for every task.

The distinction between the two coding models is important. MAI Code 2 is designed for more demanding development work, while Code 2 Flash focuses on speed, affordability and routine coding assistance.

### Cybersecurity model designed to detect vulnerabilities

The cybersecurity announcement centred on MAI Cyber 2 Flash, which Microsoft said was developed to identify software vulnerabilities, analyse potential threats and provide remediation guidance.

The model is intended to support security professionals and defensive cybersecurity operations, particularly as organisations increasingly use AI agents to monitor systems and respond to threats.

Microsoft said MAI Cyber 2 Flash matched GPT-6 Astra's quality on the CyberGymE2E benchmark at less than 2 per cent of the cost per task. The company is initially making the cybersecurity model available to a limited group of customers.

The launch reflects growing demand for AI-powered cybersecurity tools as businesses face increasingly complex threats and need to analyse large volumes of software, code and security data.

### Microsoft expands AI into images, voice and transcription

The new model lineup also includes AI systems designed for image editing, real-time speech, multilingual transcription and decision-making.

Microsoft said MAI Image 2.7 ranked first in image editing on Artificial Analysis, according to its announcement. The company also introduced a Flash version aimed at high-volume image-generation tasks, with Microsoft estimating its cost at around US$10 for every 1,000 generated images.

MAI Realtime 1 is Microsoft's new speech-to-speech model designed for natural conversations with low latency. Microsoft said the model is priced from US$0.01 per audio minute, targeting enterprise applications where voice AI could be used at scale.

The company also highlighted MAI Transcribe 2, which powers Dragon Copilot's transcription capabilities. Microsoft said the system supports multilingual transcription and is already being used across approximately 10 million patient encounters per month, with multilingual error rates reduced by half.

The transcription technology is particularly relevant for healthcare, where accurate documentation of conversations between doctors and patients can help reduce administrative workload.

### Decision model aims to reduce AI costs

Microsoft also announced Decision 1.1, its latest decision-making model designed to help applications select and route tasks to appropriate AI systems.

The model can be integrated into applications to determine which AI capabilities are needed for a particular task, potentially avoiding the use of larger and more expensive models when a simpler system is sufficient.

Microsoft said Decision 1.1 is 50 times faster and 79 times cheaper than GPT-Sol 6.1 in its comparisons. The model is also designed to support enterprise applications involving routing, personalisation, data labelling and content filtering.

The strategy reflects a broader shift in the AI industry, where businesses are increasingly focusing not only on model capability but also on the cost of running AI systems at scale.

### Maia 200 AI chip enters production

Alongside the model launches, Microsoft announced that its Maia 200 AI accelerator is now in production. The chip is designed to support the company's AI infrastructure and run its large AI models.

Microsoft said Maia 200 achieves 1.7 times more tokens per watt compared with the benchmark it cited, highlighting the company's focus on improving energy efficiency as demand for AI computing continues to rise.

The development is significant because AI infrastructure has become one of the industry's biggest challenges. Training and operating advanced AI models require enormous amounts of computing power, energy and investment in data centres.

By developing its own AI chips alongside its own models, Microsoft is attempting to gain greater control over the cost and efficiency of its AI infrastructure.

### Microsoft pushes AI agents and enterprise adoption

Microsoft's Ignite announcements also focused on AI agents that can perform tasks across workplace applications. The company highlighted Copilot's expanded capabilities for coding, business processes and multi-step tasks.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella said the company's broader strategy is focused on building an AI ecosystem that keeps human ambition at the centre while giving organisations greater control over their intelligence, data and learning systems.

The company also highlighted Agent 365, its system for managing and governing enterprise AI agents. Microsoft said more than 50 million agents are already associated with Agent 365, while its Business Analytics tools are designed to help organisations track AI usage, spending and outcomes.

As businesses move from experimenting with AI chatbots to deploying agents capable of completing tasks, Microsoft is increasingly focusing on governance, monitoring, security and cost management.

The latest Ignite announcements show that the AI competition is expanding beyond simply building the most powerful model. Companies are now competing on performance, price, energy efficiency, security and how easily AI can be integrated into everyday business operations.

Microsoft's new MAI models are designed to address that shift, with the company betting that affordable, specialised AI systems will play a growing role in coding, cybersecurity, healthcare, image creation and enterprise automation
