Microsoft made Microsoft-Decision-1.1 generally available on November 17, 2026, as part of its Ignite announcements. The company describes it as a fast, low-cost model for specific decisions inside automated workflows, including routing requests, classifying inputs and checking results.

## What the model does

One potential use is in a support queue. An application could use the model to classify an incoming request and send it to the relevant team, then use a separate service to produce a reply. Microsoft also lists prioritization and workflow control among the model’s intended tasks.

These decisions can happen several times in one automated task. A workflow might first choose where a request goes, then decide whether the response needs another check. Each decision adds to the time and cost of completing the task, which helps explain why Microsoft is emphasizing speed and cost.

## What general availability tells developers

Microsoft says the model is ready for enterprise deployment. The company announced other models for tasks such as image and audio generation at Ignite, with availability varying by model. Decision-1.1’s release status applies to this specific model.

The announcement establishes availability and intended uses. It leaves open how the model performs on any given company’s workload, especially when requests are ambiguous or a wrong classification sends work to the wrong team.
