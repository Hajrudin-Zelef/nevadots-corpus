TL;DR

- Binance reopened withdrawals at 06:00 UTC on Nov. 1 with bitcoin; 15 networks were back by 12:36 UTC.
- Hours later, 531 of 552 listed coins were still suspended, including Cardano, Litecoin and Chainlink.
- Net outflows hit $3.64 billion in the first 9.5 hours, about 2.1% of Binance's $171 billion in assets.
- Binance says it has replaced 95% of the stolen assets; issuers and partners froze about $2.7B to $3B.
- The FBI blamed North Korea's TraderTraitor group; Binance hasn't published a full root cause yet.

**Binance withdrawals** came back on **Sunday, November 1, 2026**, but not all at once. The world’s largest crypto exchange restarted with bitcoin at **06:00 UTC**, three days after hackers drained about **$6.3 billion** from its wallets in the biggest crypto theft on record. By midday, 15 networks were open, while hundreds of other coins were still waiting.

If you’ve never used a crypto exchange, here’s the context. An exchange like Binance holds coins on behalf of millions of customers, much like a bank holds deposits. A **withdrawal** moves your coins from the exchange to a wallet you control. When withdrawals freeze, your balance still shows up, but you can’t take it out. Here’s what’s open, what isn’t, who pays for the loss and what to check before you move anything.

## **What Happened, in 60 Seconds**

| **When (UTC)** | **What happened** | 
| Oct. 29, about 20:05 | Attackers start draining Binance wallets; most funds leave within about 20 minutes | 
| Oct. 29, about 23:05 | A second wave hits Binance wallets on Avalanche and Polygon | 
| Oct. 30 | Binance pauses withdrawals and says it has readied $6.34 billion to cover losses | 
| Oct. 31 | The FBI attributes the attack to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group; Binance says 90% of assets are replaced | 
| Nov. 1, 06:00 | Phase one of withdrawals begins with bitcoin | 
| Nov. 1, 12:36 | All 15 phase-one networks are back online | 

The money came from Binance’s **hot wallets**, the online wallets an exchange uses for everyday withdrawals. By contrast, cold wallets are kept offline and are much harder to hack. According to TRM Labs’ analysis, the attackers took about **18,270 BTC**, **200,630 ETH**, **1.875 billion USDT** and **823 million USDC** on Ethereum, plus XRP, SOL and tokens on other blockchains. That’s about four times the $1.5 billion Bybit hack of February 2025.

## **What’s Open and What’s Still Frozen**

Binance didn’t publish one full list of the 15 phase-one networks. Based on its status updates, the first wave included **Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain, XRP, Solana, Tron**, plus layer-2 networks like **Base, Arbitrum and Optimism**, along with stablecoins such as **USDT and USDC**. A **layer-2** is a faster, cheaper network built on top of Ethereum.

The rest is still locked. At 11:22 a.m. ET on November 1, Binance’s status page showed withdrawals suspended for **531 of 552** listed coins, with 14 coins, including bitcoin and ether, partly restored. Networks still in testing included **Cardano, Chainlink, Hyperliquid, Litecoin and NEAR**.

Binance said later phases depend on each blockchain passing its own security checks, and it hasn’t given dates.

## **Why Bitcoin and Stablecoins Went First**

Binance said it reopened networks based on **user demand over the past week**, so the most-requested coins came back first. That makes sense for two reasons, in our view.

First, these coins cover most of what customers want to move. Second, they’re also the assets Binance could rebuild fastest. Of the stolen funds, the hardest to replace are the smaller tokens outside the top ten, because there isn’t enough of them for sale to buy back quickly. Binance says that last **5%** is still being bought through exchanges and **OTC** (over-the-counter) desks, which arrange large private trades.

## **Who’s Paying for the $6.3 Billion**

Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said the exchange “will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not our users.” Here’s where the money is coming from:

| **Source** | **What it is** | **Approximate amount** | 
| Frozen stolen funds | Stablecoin issuers and partners blocked the hacker’s coins | About $2.7B to $3B | 
| SAFU | Binance’s emergency fund for users, held in bitcoin | About $1B | 
| Company treasury | Binance’s own crypto and cash | Not disclosed | 
| Futures insurance fund | A reserve normally used to cover losses in leveraged trading | Not disclosed | 

The freezes worked because **stablecoins** like USDT and USDC are controlled by companies, Tether and Circle, that can block specific addresses. Bitcoin and Ether can’t be frozen that way. TRM estimates the attacker still holds about **$2.4 billion** that can’t be frozen, including more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC.

## **Was This a Bank Run? Our Math**

A **bank run** happens when so many customers withdraw at once that the institution can’t pay. Here’s how the first day stacks up:

| **Measure** | **Figure** | 
| Withdrawals in first 9.5 hours | $4.65B | 
| Deposits in the same window | $1.01B | 
| Net outflow | **$3.64B, or 2.1%** of $171B in assets (Oct. 28 snapshot) | 
| Total hack vs. those assets | $6.3B, or about **3.7%** | 
| Binance Bitcoin balance change, 24 hours | About **−19,289 BTC** (CoinGlass) | 

So far, the numbers point to nervous customers, not a collapse. A 2.1% net outflow on reopening day is meaningful, but Binance said withdrawals were *“stabilising as expected.”* For context, the bitcoin that left Binance in a day was about the same as the 18,270 BTC the hacker took. Some of that money moved to rivals. In the same 24 hours, OKX saw about **$317 million** in net inflows, and Coinbase and Kraken added roughly 1,000 and 1,400 BTC.

The bigger test is still ahead. Binance promised a fresh **proof of reserves**, a report showing it holds enough assets to cover every customer balance, based on an October 31 snapshot and checked with **zero-knowledge proofs**. That’s a math technique that confirms the totals without exposing individual accounts.

## **The Questions Binance Hasn’t Answered**

Binance first linked the breach to a DevOps engineer’s compromised laptop, then said it found more breach points. The attackers abused **EIP-7702**, an Ethereum feature that lets a normal wallet temporarily act like a smart contract, to take control of a hot wallet. Teng said *“this was not a single ‘one-click’ action,”* but Binance hasn’t released a full root-cause report.

There’s also a dispute over whose money was hit. Teng said the attack targeted Binance’s own hot wallet, not customer funds. However, a review of Binance’s reserve data found those wallets had been listed among the assets backing customer balances. Our take: that’s why the new proof-of-reserves report matters more than any statement.

Big institutions are trying to design this risk away. For example, Google’s new crypto custody system never lets a single computer hold a full key.

## **What to Do If You Have Funds on Binance**

- **Check the status page first.** Confirm that both your coin and the network you plan to use are open.
- **Pick the right network.** Many coins exist on several blockchains. Sending USDT on a network your wallet doesn’t support can lose the funds.
- **Expect delays.** Binance warned that some withdrawals may take longer than usual.
- **Ignore “recovery” messages.** Scammers target users after every major hack. Binance won’t ask for your password or recovery phrase.
- **Think about where you keep long-term coins.** If you don’t trade often, a hardware wallet for crypto storage keeps your keys offline.

Markets have stayed fairly calm. Bitcoin traded near **$78,064** and BNB, Binance’s own token, near **$713** as of 10:18 a.m. UTC on November 1. That’s about 3.5% below BNB’s $738.78 on October 27, before the hack. Bitcoin had already slipped below $80,000 in late October, and it fell further after the theft.

## **FAQs**

### **Has a hacker ever returned stolen bitcoin?**

Rarely, but it has happened. In one case, someone drained $320 million in bitcoin from Liquid Network, then offered to give most of it back.

### **Did other exchanges get hacked in 2026?**

Yes. In September, Bitget lost about $350 million, and its user protection fund turned out to be mostly a bet on bitcoin’s price.

### **What’s the safest crypto wallet for a beginner?**

It depends on how much you hold and how often you trade. This list of crypto wallets for beginners compares easy mobile apps with safer offline options.

### **What is a futures insurance fund?**

It’s a reserve exchanges keep to cover losses when leveraged traders’ positions collapse faster than they can be closed.

### **What’s the difference between spot and futures trading?**

Spot trading means buying the coin itself. Futures are contracts on its future price, often with borrowed money.
