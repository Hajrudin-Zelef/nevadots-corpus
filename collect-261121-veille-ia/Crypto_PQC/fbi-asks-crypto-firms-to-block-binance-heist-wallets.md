Federal investigators want the companies that route and process crypto payments to turn away any transfer involving 628 wallet addresses connected with the record theft at Binance. In an alert dated October 31, the FBI blamed North Korea for draining over $6 billion in virtual assets out of the exchange and published 608 Ethereum wallets and 19 Bitcoin wallets, plus one on Solana, that are holding or once held the proceeds.

Pyongyang’s operatives struck around October 29, according to the bureau, which tracks this strand of North Korean hacking under the name TraderTraitor and says the published addresses belong to those actors or sit close to them. Agents from IRS Criminal Investigation (IRS-CI) and the US Secret Service (USSS) worked alongside the FBI and saw the thieves convert most of the loot into Ether (ETH).

Investigators expect that Ether to be pushed next through instant swap services and decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols, where one token can be traded for another without opening an account at a conventional exchange.

DeFi platforms, instant exchanges and analytics companies that trace blockchain activity all appear among the recipients, along with operators of THORNodes, which run THORChain’s swap network, and RPC nodes, the servers apps query to read chain data and broadcast transactions. Every other business handling virtual assets is covered too, and each is asked to block transfers to or from the flagged wallets as well as funds traced back to them.

Nothing in the alert makes the blocking compulsory, since the agencies framed it as an appeal to private companies. Anyone holding information can file a report through ic3.gov, the bureau’s Internet Crime Complaint Center, or reach out to the nearest FBI field office.

Secret Service investigators teamed up with industry partners minutes after the Thursday afternoon attack to locate and freeze what the agency’s November 1 post on X described as “a significant portion of stolen cryptocurrency.” No dollar figure appeared in the post, which listed the FBI, IRS-CI, US Attorney’s offices in Washington, DC, and the Central District of California, and the Justice Department’s CCIPS and National Security Division as partners in the probe.

According to Binance, attackers compromised its hot wallets, which stay online so the exchange can process everyday withdrawals, at around 20:05 UTC on October 29 and took tokens worth roughly $6.3 billion. Partners had locked up about $3 billion of it on-chain by October 30, the exchange added. Company leaders promised customers would not absorb the loss and earmarked over $6.34 billion from the SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users) reserve and the corporate treasury to cover it.

First-phase withdrawals reopened November 1, during a 06:00 to 09:00 UTC window, by which time Binance had replaced about 95% of the losses. Another update, posted November 2, scheduled more networks and assets for November 3 and described the outstanding 5% as less liquid tokens that need more time to buy.

Chainalysis, a blockchain tracing company, posted on October 30 that Pyongyang carried out the theft, which it put at $6.4 billion, and cited its “high degree of confidence.” Its investigators traced Binance proceeds commingling on-chain with coins from a Bitget hack the previous month, a case the firm had already pinned on North Korea. That assessment came a day before the federal alert.

Bybit’s $1.5 billion loss in February 2025 prompted the same kind of response, when the FBI named TraderTraitor and asked exchanges, bridges, DeFi services, analytics firms and RPC node operators to block a published set of Ethereum addresses. Five days separated that theft, around February 21, from the bureau’s February 26 notice. Just two days passed before the Binance attribution, for a theft about four times the size.

North Korea dismissed the accusation in a statement from Kim Yo Jong, whose brother Kim Jong Un leads the country and who heads a department in the ruling Workers’ Party Central Committee. Dated November 2, it appeared a day later through KCNA, the state news agency. She called the FBI’s claim “absurd rhetoric and nothing new” and also disputed Washington’s link between Pyongyang and the Bybit hack.
