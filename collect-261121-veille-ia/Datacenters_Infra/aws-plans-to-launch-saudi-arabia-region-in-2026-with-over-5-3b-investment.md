- Amazon Web Services will launch its first dedicated cloud infrastructure region in Saudi Arabia by December 2026, marking a $5.3 billion investment.
- The new sovereign region features three availability zones designed to meet strict data residency requirements for financial and public sector entities.
- A parallel strategic collaboration with HUMAIN will establish a 50-megawatt AI Zone by 2028, integrating custom Trainium chips and Arabic large language models.

Amazon Web Services (AWS) confirmed that its first cloud infrastructure region in Saudi Arabia is scheduled for launch in December 2026. This development follows an initial announcement in March 2024 and represents a planned investment exceeding $5.3 billion. The new region will expand AWS’s global footprint to 40 regions and is designed with sovereignty controls under the AWS Digital Sovereignty Pledge. This allows data to reside within Saudi borders, addressing local regulatory requirements for public and private sector organizations.

The facility will feature three availability zones with independent power, cooling, and physical security to ensure high resilience. This infrastructure addresses a critical need for financial institutions and government entities required by the Saudi Arabian Monetary Authority (SAMA) to keep data locally hosted. Previously, enterprises faced latency and compliance complexities by using the nearby Bahrain region established in 2019. The new region eliminates these issues by providing low-latency connectivity and full data sovereignty within the Kingdom.

A core component of this expansion is a strategic collaboration with HUMAIN, a company owned by the Public Investment Fund (PIF). By 2028, this partnership will deliver up to 50 megawatts of capacity in Saudi Arabia’s first dedicated AI zone. The infrastructure will utilize AWS’s custom Trainium chips alongside NVIDIA hardware to support heavy training and inference workloads. Additionally, HUMAIN’s Arabic language model, ALLaM, will be integrated into AmazonAMZN-- Bedrock for local AI applications.

The initiative aims to accelerate Saudi Arabia’s economic diversification under Vision 2030. An IDC report commissioned by AWS estimates AI could contribute $130 billion to the local economy by 2030. Large-scale projects like NEOM require substantial compute capacity, driving demand for advanced digital infrastructure. Early adopters include major entities such as stc Group, Abdul Latif Jameel, and Almosafer.

## How Does the HUMAIN Partnership Enhance AI Capabilities?

AWS is designated as HUMAIN’s preferred AI partner, with an additional investment of over $5 billion directed toward AI infrastructure and talent development. HUMAIN Fabric will be available through the AWS Marketplace, enabling secure data handling for enterprise applications. This collaboration positions Saudi Arabia as a major buyer of digital infrastructure and a global computing partner. The AI Zone will support both large-scale training and inference workloads for local enterprises.

Beyond infrastructure, AWS is investing in workforce development through the Amazon Academy. The program aims to train over 100,000 Saudi citizens in cloud computing and generative AI skills by 2030. Innovation centers and research grants will support local startups and digital career trainees. This focus on human capital aligns with the Kingdom’s broader economic transformation goals.

## Why Is AWS Entering a Market Already Served by Azure and Google?

AWS is entering the Saudi market later than competitors; Microsoft Azure launched two regions in 2022, and Google Cloud has also established a presence. However, AWS’s entry in 2026 aims to capture the growing enterprise demand for sovereign cloud capabilities. The company previously established a CloudFront edge location in Jeddah in January 2025 as a precursor to this full regional rollout. This move positions AWS to compete directly for large-scale government and enterprise contracts.

The investment occurs against a backdrop of robust financial performance for AWS, which reported a 37% year-over-year increase in net sales in the second quarter of 2026. Globally, AWS is expanding its footprint with plans for 18 new availability zones and six new regions across Asia, Latin America, and Europe. The Saudi region represents a strategic push to anchor AI infrastructure in the Gulf. Key partners including stc Group, ZainTECH, and Accenture are already engaging with AWS to accelerate cloud adoption and security compliance.
