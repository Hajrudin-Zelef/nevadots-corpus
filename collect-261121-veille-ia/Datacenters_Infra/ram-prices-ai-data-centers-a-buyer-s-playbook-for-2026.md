## Quick Answer: Why Is RAM So Expensive Right Now?

RAM costs more because the three companies that make most of the world's DRAM (Samsung, SK hynix and Micron) have steered production toward high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the specialized memory AI data centers need. For the same capacity, HBM3E uses roughly three times the wafer area of DDR5, so every wafer sent to AI leaves fewer chips for laptops, phones and office computers.

Procurement cannot lower the global price of memory. It can reduce how much your organization pays above that price. That is what the rest of this guide covers.

## Key Takeaways

- TrendForce raised its Q1 2026 forecast for conventional DRAM contract prices to +90 to 95% quarter over quarter, a record, and later put Q2 at +58 to 63%.
- Gartner estimated in February that combined DRAM and SSD prices will be about 130% higher by the end of 2026 than in 2025, lifting average PC prices roughly 17%.
- Increases are slowing, not reversing. TrendForce's November 11 update puts Q4 PC DRAM contract prices at +13 to 18%, down from +18 to 23% in Q3.
- IDC does not expect meaningful relief before the end of 2027, and does not expect PC pricing to return to 2025 levels.
- The most effective buyer response is not panic buying. It is better timing, right-sized configurations, competing quotes and price-protected agreements.

## Why Are AI Data Centers Making RAM More Expensive?

Memory is a shared resource. The same factories, the same silicon wafers and the same three suppliers serve AI servers, office laptops and smartphones. When AI buyers pay more and commit to larger orders, business and consumer memory gets squeezed.

### Memory Makers Are Following the Money to AI

AI accelerators depend on HBM, which sits right beside the processor. Hyperscale data center operators are buying it in huge volumes, and it carries far higher margins than the DDR4 and DDR5 chips inside everyday computers. Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have responded by steering advanced capacity toward server and HBM products. TrendForce attributes the continuing price increases to persistent supply tightness as makers prioritize those products.

### One Gigabyte of AI Memory Costs Roughly Three Gigabytes of Capacity

This is the part most coverage skips. At Hot Chips 2026, Micron said that HBM3E consumes roughly three times the wafer area of DDR5 for the same capacity, and that the gap widens with each generation. Micron has not published an exact wafer ratio for HBM4. Tom's Hardware's report on the talk adds that HBM sells for about five times the price of DDR5 per bit, so each wafer a maker moves to HBM removes a disproportionate amount of ordinary memory from the market. Demand for ordinary RAM did not collapse. The factory capacity that used to make it was reassigned.

### AI Is the Main Driver, Not the Only One

Two other factors made the squeeze worse. TrendForce said PC shipments in Q4 2025 beat expectations as vendors stocked up before price hikes, which deepened the PC DRAM shortage going into 2026. IDC also noted that the Windows 10 end-of-support refresh cycle collided with the memory shortage. Neither explains the shortage itself, but both pulled demand forward into a market that had no spare supply.

### Micron's Exit From Crucial Showed How Far the Shift Goes

On December 3, 2025, Micron announced it would exit the Crucial consumer business, with consumer shipments ending in February 2026. Micron said the move would improve supply and support for larger, strategic customers in faster-growing segments, and pointed to AI-driven data center demand. One of the best-known retail memory brands left the shelf.

### New Capacity Will Not Fix This Quickly

Memory fabs take years to build, and analysts expect makers to favor premium server products when they do add capacity. IDC sees no relief before the end of 2027. In an August release, TrendForce said it expects contract prices to remain broadly elevated in 2027, with combined server DRAM and HBM bit supply projected to grow 27% that year. Taiwan's Nanya Technology has said the shortage could last through 2028.

Two takeaways matter for buyers. First, the rate of increase is cooling while the price level keeps climbing. Compounded, the Q1 and Q2 increases alone roughly tripled conventional DRAM contract prices in six months (our arithmetic from TrendForce's ranges). Second, memory now takes a much bigger bite of what a PC costs to build, which is why manufacturers cannot absorb it.

### Three Different 17% Figures

Three numbers near 17% circulate, and they are not the same thing:

- **Gartner, about 17%:** its forecast that average PC prices rise by the end of 2026 versus 2025, driven by memory costs.
- **IDC, 17 to 18.3%:** the rise in the average selling price of PCs sold in 2026. An average also reflects a shift in which models people buy, not only price changes on the same model.
- **Dell, 15 to 20%:** TrendForce's December 5, 2025 report, citing industry sources, that Dell planned list-price increases of at least that size on some products.

Use Gartner's figure as a budgeting anchor, not as a prediction for any single quote.

### Why Memory Is 23% of PC Cost in One Source and 35% in Another

Gartner estimates memory will peak at about 23% of the average PC's bill of materials in 2026, up from 16% in 2025. HP said on its February 2026 earnings call that DRAM was about 35% of its own PC build cost, up from 15 to 18% the quarter before. Gartner's number is an industry-wide estimate; HP's is one manufacturer's snapshot during a steep run-up. Treat Gartner's as the planning baseline and HP's as the high end of what individual vendors are seeing.

IDC also reported preliminary figures showing global PC shipments down 20.1% in Q3 2026, partly because vendors and distributors bought inventory earlier in the year ahead of price increases.

## How Higher Memory Prices Reach U.S. Businesses and Households

Most people never buy a stick of RAM. They feel the shortage when a device costs more or ships with less.

### Laptop Prices Are Rising in 2026, and Quotes Expire Fast

On December 5, 2025, TrendForce reported, citing industry sources, that Dell planned to raise prices by at least 15 to 20% from mid-December and that Lenovo had told customers all existing quotes would expire on January 1, 2026. Because every major brand buys from the same small group of memory suppliers, none is immune. For buyers, the lesson is that a quote is only as good as its expiry date. Our post on rising hardware prices covers how this has played out across vendors.

### Phones Get Pricier and Cheaper Models Thin Out

IDC expects the average smartphone to sell for about $581 in 2026, up 27.6% in a year, and says Android will absorb most of the shipment decline. Part of that jump reflects a shift toward more expensive models, not just higher prices on the same phone.

### Entry-Level Computers Are Disappearing

Gartner predicts the sub-$500 PC segment will disappear by 2028 because low-margin machines stop being viable when memory takes a larger share of the cost. That hits students, seniors and first-time business owners hardest.

### Other Hardware Is Affected Too

In February 2026, Valve said the Steam Deck OLED may be out of stock intermittently in some regions because of memory and storage shortages, and it delayed its Steam Machine and Steam Frame for the same reason.

### Older Devices Stay in Service Longer

Gartner expects PC lifespans to stretch about 15% for business buyers and 20% for consumers by the end of 2026. That saves money in the short term, but older machines carry security and management risks, so a longer life needs a plan.

### Small Businesses Feel It Most

Large buyers negotiate multi-year allocations. A ten-person company usually cannot. A purchase that sits in an approval queue for a week can end up costing more than the number on the quote.

## Can Procurement Solve a Global Memory Shortage?

No. No buying strategy can make Samsung, SK hynix or Micron build more consumer RAM. But procurement is not about controlling the market. It is about controlling what you pay relative to the market.

Technology procurement is the process of sourcing, comparing, negotiating, purchasing and managing the hardware and software an organization needs. In a shortage, five things are within your control:

- **Timing:** when you buy and how long a quote stays valid.
- **Configuration:** how much memory each role actually needs.
- **Vendors:** how many sellers you compare.
- **Contract terms:** price protection, delivery windows and substitution rules.
- **Lifecycle:** how long each device stays in service and how it is retired.

## 8 Procurement Strategies That Reduce What You Overpay for Memory-Heavy Hardware

### Inventory Every Device and Map Its Refresh Date

You cannot time purchases you have not planned. List each laptop, desktop and server with its age, memory size, warranty end date and role. This shows which devices must be replaced now and which can safely wait, and it replaces last-minute emergency buying.

### Right-Size Memory by Role, but Do Not Under-Buy Soldered Memory

A front-desk workstation and a finance analyst running large spreadsheets do not need the same configuration. Define two or three standard builds by role. Then check whether memory is soldered, because soldered memory cannot be upgraded later. Buying lean and upgrading later is not a safe plan right now: add-on memory and SSDs are inflated too, and analysts have warned upgrades could cost significantly more if the shortage runs into 2027. Price the upgrade module today before you count on it. For soldered machines, buy for the device's full service life.

### Consolidate Orders for Leverage

Separate purchases from different departments or locations give vendors no reason to discount. One consolidated request carries more weight, especially now that weak PC demand is leaving OEMs with more inventory. If you manage several locations, see our guide to bulk and multi-site technology ordering and bring the volume to the table once.

### Treat Every Quote as a Clock

Record the quote date, expiry date, exact configuration and whether pricing is locked for future delivery. A quote that cannot be held until delivery is a price estimate, not a price. Our vendor quote comparison checklist shows how to compare quotes on the same configuration, taxes, shipping, warranty and validity terms.

### Compare Multiple Vendors on Identical Specifications

Memory costs hit every brand, but brands pass them on differently. Dell, HP, Lenovo, Acer, ASUS and Apple price configurations, discounts and refresh models in different ways. Quote the same specification from at least three sources, including authorized resellers, and compare total cost, not the headline number.

### Negotiate Price Protection and Delivery Terms

When list prices are rising, the best protection is a written commitment. Ask suppliers whether they can hold a price for a defined window, and agree on what happens if the vendor substitutes components. Even if the unit price does not drop, terms that protect against mid-order increases have real value. For tactics, see our guide to negotiating with technology vendors and apply them to memory-heavy configurations.

### Extend the Life of Sound Devices, With Guardrails

Not every older machine needs replacing. Where a desktop supports upgradeable memory, a targeted upgrade can add useful years, as long as you have priced the module (see strategy 2). Certified refurbished programs from major manufacturers can supply business-grade devices at lower cost. Set a firm rule: keep devices only while they receive security updates and meet the role's needs.

### Budget for a Range, Not a Single Number

Build at least two scenarios: one assuming today's quotes hold, and one assuming a further increase before purchase (for example, today's quote plus 10 to 15%; the right buffer depends on your vendor and timeline). Gartner's roughly 17% PC price rise is a defensible planning anchor. A range gives finance leads a realistic ceiling and keeps approvals from stalling when a quote changes. ## Three U.S.-Specific Levers Worth Checking

- **Tax treatment.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) raised the Section 179 expensing cap to $2.5 million (indexed for inflation) and restored 100% bonus depreciation for qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025. Business computers and servers generally qualify, and equipment generally must be placed in service by December 31 to count for that tax year. That matters for a purchase made this quarter. Do not buy hardware you do not need for a tax benefit, and confirm the rules with your tax adviser.
- **Leasing and device-as-a-service.** Leasing spreads the cost and can shift refresh timing to the provider. Compare total cost over the term, and check whether the contract fixes the price or lets the lessor reprice at renewal. Ask the same price-protection questions you would ask a hardware vendor.
- **Windows 10 end of support.** Microsoft ended Windows 10 support in October 2025. If your fleet still includes machines that cannot run Windows 11, they are a security risk and a forced-refresh candidate. Prioritize them in your inventory.

A warning about grey-market memory. During shortages, counterfeit, remarked or unverified memory shows up on marketplaces and with unknown resellers. Buy from authorized channels, check part numbers, and treat prices far below market as a red flag.

## Should You Buy Now or Wait?

Waiting for prices to fall is a risky plan. IDC does not expect memory relief before the end of 2027, and expects PC prices to stay above 2025 levels even as capacity expands. Panic buying the wrong machine is also costly. Use your situation to decide:

| Your situation | Likely best move | Why | 
|---|---|---|
| Devices failing or out of support now | Buy now, with right-sized specs | Delay adds security and downtime risk, and no relief is expected soon | 
| Refresh planned in the next 6 to 12 months | Request quotes now and negotiate delivery windows and price protection | Quotes expire quickly and prices are still rising | 
| Devices working well and still supported | Defer, or upgrade memory where possible (after pricing the module) | Gartner expects longer lifespans; no need to pay peak prices early | 
| Large multi-site rollout | Consolidate into one negotiated order | Volume improves leverage |

## What Happens Next? The Outlook for RAM Prices

The consensus is cautious. IDC says there is no meaningful relief before the end of 2027. TrendForce expects Q4 2026 prices to keep rising at a slower pace and contract prices to stay broadly elevated in 2027. New fabrication capacity will favor AI and server products first. Plan on elevated prices, not a snap-back, and build procurement habits that hold up either way.

## If You Are Buying for Yourself: 3 Tips

- Buy the memory you will need for the whole time you own the device. Many laptops have soldered memory that cannot be added later, and add-on modules cost more than they did a year ago.
- Compare the same configuration at three retailers and check return windows. Prices and stock move quickly. Certified refurbished models from the manufacturer can cut the cost.
- Avoid too-good-to-be-true RAM deals. Buy from authorized sellers, not unknown marketplace listings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why are RAM prices so high in 2026?

Memory makers shifted production toward HBM for AI data centers, which uses roughly three times the wafer area of DDR5 for the same capacity. That reduced the supply of ordinary RAM while demand stayed strong.

### How much have RAM prices gone up?

TrendForce forecast conventional DRAM contract prices rising 90 to 95% quarter over quarter in Q1 2026 and 58 to 63% in Q2. Gartner estimates combined DRAM and SSD prices will be about 130% higher by the end of 2026 than in 2025. Device prices have risen by smaller percentages because memory is only part of the cost.

### Is AI the only reason RAM is expensive?

AI-driven demand is the main driver cited by TrendForce, Gartner and IDC. Pull-forward buying in late 2025 and the Windows 10 refresh cycle added pressure, but they do not explain the shortage itself.

### Will RAM prices go down in 2027?

Probably not back to 2025 levels. IDC sees no meaningful relief before the end of 2027 and expects PC prices to stay above 2025 levels even as new capacity arrives. Increases may slow; falling prices are less certain.

### Should my business buy laptops now or wait?

If devices are failing or out of support, buy now with right-sized specifications. If they are working and supported, deferring can make sense. Either way, get quotes now so you know the price and its expiry date.

### How can small businesses reduce hardware costs during the shortage?

Standardize configurations by role, compare at least three vendors on identical specs, consolidate orders, ask about price protection, consider certified refurbished devices, and budget for a range instead of one number.

### Does procurement lower the price of RAM?

No. Procurement cannot change the global market price. It reduces what you overpay through timing, configuration, vendor competition, contract terms and lifecycle planning.

### Does the shortage affect phones and other devices too?

Yes. IDC expects 2026 smartphone shipments to fall 16.7% as average selling prices rise 27.6%, and Valve said in February that the Steam Deck OLED may be intermittently out of stock in some regions because of memory and storage shortages.
