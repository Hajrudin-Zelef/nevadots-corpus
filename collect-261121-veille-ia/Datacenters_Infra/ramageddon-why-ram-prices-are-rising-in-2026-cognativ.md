RAMageddon is the nickname for the 2026 memory shortage: AI data centers are absorbing so much DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) that RAM prices for servers, PCs and phones have climbed faster than at any point in recent memory. According to TrendForce, conventional DRAM contract prices rose about 93% to 98% quarter over quarter in the first quarter of 2026 alone.

The shortage has not ended. By late October 2026, Samsung, SK hynix and Micron had all reported record results and said demand would keep running ahead of supply into 2027 and beyond. This guide explains why RAM prices are rising, how HBM differs from ordinary DRAM, what has happened through October 31, 2026, and how businesses and builders can plan hardware purchases.

**Key takeaways**

- AI servers get first call on new memory supply, which leaves less conventional DRAM for PCs, phones and general servers.
- Price increases are slowing quarter by quarter, but TrendForce still forecasts rises in the fourth quarter of 2026.
- SK hynix expects demand to exceed supply through 2030, so planning beats waiting for a quick price drop.

## What Is RAMageddon? The AI Memory Shortage Explained

RAMageddon describes a supply shock in dynamic random-access memory (DRAM), the working memory inside every server, laptop and smartphone, plus the HBM stacked next to AI accelerators. The cause is a demand shift: hyperscalers and AI labs are buying memory at a scale that the industry's existing fabs cannot match.

TrendForce's June 1, 2026 report on first-quarter DRAM revenue put the shift plainly. As AI moves from model training toward inference, cloud data centers are adding general-purpose servers as well as AI-specific ones, broadening memory demand. Supplier inventories were extremely low, and new output was prioritized for high-capacity server modules (RDIMMs), limiting what PC makers and smartphone brands could buy. Industry DRAM revenue rose 81% quarter over quarter to about $97 billion.

### Who Makes the World's DRAM

Three companies dominate supply. In TrendForce's second-quarter 2026 ranking, Samsung held 39.4% of DRAM revenue, SK hynix 24.9% and Micron 23.3%. When those three steer capacity toward AI products, the rest of the market feels it immediately, because there are few alternative sources at scale.

## Why RAM Prices Are Rising

Several forces stack on top of each other, and none is likely to reverse within a quarter. Four stand out: AI servers moving to the front of the queue, HBM consuming more wafer capacity, new fabs taking years and legacy memory being squeezed too.

### AI Servers Move to the Front of the Queue

Cloud providers have proven willing to pay higher prices and sign multi-year contracts to secure supply. TrendForce notes that other customers then follow just to protect their allocations. Suppliers favor higher-margin server products, leaving PC and phone makers to negotiate for what remains.

### HBM Consumes More Wafer Capacity

HBM is built from DRAM dies, so every wafer that becomes HBM is a wafer that does not become DDR5 or LPDDR5X. HBM also needs more silicon for the same capacity. Micron has said HBM uses roughly three times the silicon of DDR5, and that the gap is widening with each generation, according to Tom's Hardware's coverage of Micron at Hot Chips 2026. Treat that ratio as an industry estimate, not a fixed constant.

### New Fabs Take Years

Bit output can grow only through process migrations until new cleanrooms are built, and that construction takes time. SK hynix cites the technical difficulty of leading-edge processes and long lead times for new fabs as reasons the imbalance will persist. Its Yongin Phase 1 site is set to start full-scale operation in 2027.

### Legacy Memory Is Being Squeezed Too

Older products are not a safe harbor. TrendForce's fourth-quarter 2026 outlook says major suppliers are still cutting output of specialty and legacy DRAM, and some are scaling back GDDR6. Micron also announced its exit from the Crucial consumer business on December 3, 2025, shipping Crucial products through retail channels only until the end of February 2026 to focus on larger enterprise customers.

## HBM vs DRAM: What Is the Difference?

Both are DRAM at the cell level. The difference is packaging and purpose. Standard DRAM, such as DDR5 in servers and PCs or LPDDR5X in phones and laptops, sits on modules or beside the processor and trades bandwidth for capacity, low cost and easy replacement.

HBM stacks several DRAM dies vertically, connects them with through-silicon vias and places the stack on the same package as a GPU or AI accelerator. That short, extremely wide connection delivers far more bandwidth, which large language models need to keep accelerators busy. The price is complexity: more silicon per bit, advanced packaging and stacking yield losses.

For buyers, the practical point is that HBM is not something you purchase separately. It arrives inside accelerators. Yet its growth still raises what you pay for ordinary DRAM, because both compete for the same fabs. Samsung and SK hynix are now ramping HBM4, and SK hynix expected HBM4 to surpass HBM3E in both shipments and revenue in the fourth quarter of 2026.

## DRAM Price Timeline: 2026 Quarter by Quarter

TrendForce publishes the most widely cited contract price figures. Contract prices are what large buyers pay; retail prices follow with a lag. The 2026 pattern is steep increases that shrink each quarter, not a decline.

Why the slowdown? TrendForce points to a much higher comparison base, limited room for PC and smartphone buyers to absorb more increases, and long-term agreements that cap how fast some prices can rise. Server DRAM is still expected to remain undersupplied in the fourth quarter, and enterprise SSD prices are accelerating as cloud providers expand AI inference infrastructure.

## Where the Memory Shortage Stands (Through October 31, 2026)

Late-2026 earnings confirmed that suppliers see no near-term balance. Micron, SK hynix and Samsung each reported in turn, and their results point in the same direction.

### Micron, September 30, 2026

Micron reported record fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $54.23 billion and fiscal 2026 revenue of $133.19 billion, up from $37.38 billion a year earlier. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said the company expects "an even stronger fiscal 2027" and pointed to its strategic customer agreements.

### SK hynix, October 28, 2026

SK hynix posted third-quarter revenue of about KRW 104.6 trillion and an 80% operating margin. It said it expects demand to exceed supply through 2030. It has signed long-term agreements, typically five years, with 14 major customers, and those contracts cover more than half of its total production capacity for 2027 and 2028.

### Samsung, October 29, 2026

Samsung reported third-quarter revenue of KRW 195.3 trillion and operating profit of KRW 107.4 trillion, with record memory revenue and profit achieved "amid capacity constraints." It said AI-driven demand in 2027 "will far exceed the industry supply in comparison with 2026." According to Reuters, as reported by eWeek, Samsung also said its 2027 HBM supply is already sold out and tight conditions could last through 2028.

## When Will RAM Prices Go Down?

No supplier is forecasting a near-term drop. The more realistic expectation for 2027 is slower increases rather than falling prices, with relief tied to physical capacity coming online. Watch four signals.

First, new fab output, such as SK hynix's M15X ramp and its Yongin Phase 1 site. Second, HBM capacity growth, which determines how much wafer supply AI keeps pulling away. Third, the share of output locked into long-term agreements, which reduces what is left for spot and short-term buyers. Fourth, quarterly contract price reports from TrendForce, which show whether the run of smaller increases is turning flat.

Predictions of a return to 2024 price levels should be treated with skepticism. Multi-year contracts give suppliers less reason to cut prices quickly when capacity arrives.

## Strategies to Overcome the Memory Crisis

The memory shortage rewards planning more than timing, whether you are budgeting a server fleet or a single workstation. The advice differs for businesses planning hardware purchases, AI builders running self-hosted inference, and PC builders and upgraders.

### For Businesses Planning Hardware Purchases

Start with an inventory of what you will actually need over the next 12 to 24 months, by workload, then buy against that plan instead of waiting for a dip. Quote with more than one vendor, ask about allocation and lead times, and consider annual or multi-year supply commitments for predictable demand. Extending refresh cycles for machines that are not memory-bound frees budget for the systems that are. A broader technology strategy review can weigh owned hardware against cloud capacity, where the memory cost is bundled into the instance price.

### For AI Builders and Self-Hosted Inference

Memory is now often the largest cost driver in self-hosted AI. Size servers to the models you will run, not the largest model you might try. Quantized models need far less memory, and right-sizing context length and concurrency reduces how much RAM and VRAM a deployment needs. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why are RAM prices so high right now?

AI data centers are buying server DRAM and HBM faster than suppliers can expand output. Memory makers prioritize those higher-margin products, so less conventional DRAM is left for PCs and phones, and contract prices have risen every quarter of 2026.

### When will RAM prices go down?

Suppliers do not expect a near-term drop. Samsung expects 2027 demand to exceed supply by more than in 2026, and SK hynix expects demand to outpace supply through 2030. Slower increases are more likely than falling prices in 2027.

### What is the difference between HBM and DRAM?

HBM is DRAM stacked vertically and packaged beside a GPU or AI accelerator for very high bandwidth. Standard DRAM such as DDR5 sits on modules and favors capacity and cost. HBM uses more silicon per bit, which tightens DRAM supply.

### Is it a good time to buy RAM?

If you need memory for work in the next year, buying now is reasonable, because TrendForce still forecasts increases in late 2026. If a purchase is optional, set a budget and watch quarterly contract price reports rather than waiting indefinitely.

### Does the memory shortage affect SSDs?

Yes. NAND Flash contract prices are also rising, and TrendForce expects enterprise SSD prices to accelerate in the fourth quarter of 2026 as cloud providers expand AI inference storage.

### Who makes most of the world's DRAM?

Samsung, SK hynix and Micron. In the second quarter of 2026, TrendForce estimated their combined share of DRAM revenue at nearly 88%, with Samsung first.

## Conclusion: Plan for a Long Memory Shortage

RAMageddon is not a short spike. AI demand for HBM and server DRAM has reset how memory is allocated, and the three dominant suppliers have locked much of their future output into long-term contracts. Price increases are slowing, but supply is expected to stay tight into 2027 and, by SK hynix's estimate, through 2030.
