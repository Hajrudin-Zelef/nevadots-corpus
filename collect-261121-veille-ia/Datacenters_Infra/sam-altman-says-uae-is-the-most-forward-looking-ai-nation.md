OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has called the UAE “the most forward-looking country in the world for AI adoption”. He says it decided to go all in on the technology.

He made the remarks at the opening ceremony of ADIPEC 2026 in Abu Dhabi on 2 November. ADIPEC is the large energy conference hosted by ADNOC, Abu Dhabi’s state oil company, and this year’s edition runs until 5 November. ADNOC, OpenAI and G42, an Abu Dhabi AI company, have signed a partnership in Abu Dhabi to apply AI in the energy sector.

According to ADIPEC’s account of the speech and interview, Altman described the deal as a model for applying advanced AI to real-world industrial challenges. On the UAE, he said:

I think the UAE has consistently been the most forward-looking country in the world for AI adoption. Going back to the launch of ChatGPT, this is a country that has decided to go all in on AI — through its government and across its economy. It’s an incredible embrace, and it has come from the leadership.

Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI

## What the ADNOC partnership does

ADNOC says it is becoming the first energy company in the world to deploy OpenAI’s frontier technology, connecting AI agents across its business using G42’s UAE-based infrastructure. The systems will combine ADNOC’s operational information with OpenAI’s reasoning models, running on G42 computing infrastructure inside the UAE.

The first applications cover integrated field development plans, and commercial decisions on crude supply, demand, prices and margins. ADNOC says planning that takes months could shrink to hours. This hasn’t been independently demonstrated at scale.

The UAE is also a partner in the planned Stargate UAE computing project, which involves American technology companies.

## Altman’s other points

Fast Company Middle East reports that Altman told the audience AI is already changing how people work, with some users treating tools such as ChatGPT as a colleague. He said AI can already handle many tasks in some white-collar roles, and he’s confident societies will adapt. He stressed human creativity and judgement, and said people should keep control of decisions.

On energy, he said the energy used per query is falling as the technology becomes more efficient, but lower running costs are encouraging more use. Still, the rapid expansion of AI will require significantly more energy, even as AI makes energy systems more efficient.

He also said AI systems have recently begun solving mathematical problems that had stayed unresolved, and that similar capabilities could reach other sciences and energy production.

Altman has been vocal on compute lately: Tbreak covered his recent comments on PC RAM in late October.

ADIPEC 2026 is the largest edition in the event’s history, with 239,000 participants expected from more than 170 countries and 2,260 exhibitors. ADNOC chief Sultan Al Jaber said of the partnership: “And now, together, we will find out what AI can do for energy.”
