The companies building most of the world's AI capacity have just told the SEC what worries them. *November 3, 2026*
| Cyentia Institute — IRIS Xtreme 2026

Most cyber risk research counts attacks. The Cyentia Institute's IRIS Xtreme 2026 counts what the worst ones actually cost. It studies the costliest 5% of publicly reported cyber incidents over five years: 136 confirmed events and $26.4 billion in documented losses, plus 205 incidents companies reported to the SEC. It is hindsight evidence, and the newest events date from early 2025, because the full cost of an attack takes a long time to become known. That is exactly what makes it useful: it shows where the money was really lost, so you can check your own risk assessment against outcomes rather than headlines.

*October 27, 2026*
| OpenAI, Anthropic, Google & OWASP — AI agent security guidance

AI agents now read your email, browse the web, process invoices and change records with an employee's own access. The companies that build them, OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, have each published how agents should be secured, and OWASP's independent Top 10 lists cover the same ground. They agree on two things. First, you can't fully filter what goes into an agent: hidden instructions in a web page, an email or a document can steer it, and every lab says that problem won't be completely solved. Second, because of that, you have to limit what an agent is allowed to do. As OpenAI puts it, "a runtime credential is not task authority." Govern what goes in, limit what comes out: that makes agent governance a job for compliance, security and the executive team at the same time.

*October 20, 2026*
| Microsoft — Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026: Decoding emergence

Microsoft says AI is "changing the physics of cybersecurity": it is reducing the time, expertise and cost required to find and exploit weaknesses. Its 2026 Digital Defense Report shows the result, an exposure window opening at both ends. Attackers turn new flaws into working exploits faster than most organizations can patch, and once inside they are staying hidden longer before anyone notices. In the middle sits a new kind of actor, the AI agent, which often has access but no owner. The answer for boards isn't another tool or a stricter deadline. It's direction on two things: the clock, meaning whether defenses are resourced and automated to keep pace with machine-speed attackers, and the owner, meaning a named person who answers for every AI agent and every exposed system.

*October 13, 2026*
| Anthropic — Detecting and countering misuse of AI: September 2026

Anthropic's September 2026 threat intelligence report documents eight months of disrupted AI misuse, and its central finding is not a new technique. It's a new cost structure: the reconnaissance, exploitation, tooling and data processing that once set elite operations apart now run as AI agents, at machine speed and in parallel. You don't need exotic new controls. You need the familiar ones to run at the attacker's speed, and your likelihood scores and your assumption that you're too small to target need to change.
