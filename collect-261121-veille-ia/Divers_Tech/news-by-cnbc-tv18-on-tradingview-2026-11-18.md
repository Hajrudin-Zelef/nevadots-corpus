# Nvidia no longer selling US-approved Hopper chips to China, Interim CEO Kress says

Nvidia is no longer shipping its Hopper data center chips to China, even after the US government approved selling a version of the same last year, interim CEO Colette Kress told analysts during the company's third quarter earnings call.

During the third quarter, Nvidia shipped licensed Hopper data center chips worth $2.4 billion to China. However, its future guidance does not include any licensed Hopper data center products. Kress confirmed that the chips are no longer being sold to Chna.

Kress did not elaborate the reason behind stalling of these shipments. Yet, this does mark a shift in stance from Nvidia, whose former CEO Jensen Huang was the biggest advocate for resuming exports to China.

Huang, the co-founder of the company, resigned last week following allegations of multiple extra marital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. He is now under investigation for potentially aiding China's spy agency and help smuggle chips to China, an allegation that Beijing has gone on to deny.

Following the Huang scandal, the Trump administration has moved to apply more scrutiny to exports of processors to multiple countries and not just China, and also to limit any chip sales abroad in case the data center buying them plans to provide Chinese companies remote access to advanced AI chips and servers.

At one point, Nvidia had also pushed for the US government to approve the sale of its advanced Blackwell chips to China, a request that the Trump administration denied, but courtesy of Huang's personal relationship with US President Donald Trump, the company was allowed to sell H200 Hopper processors, which are two generations behind the current Rubin family.

During the earnings call, board chairman Harvey Jones made it clear that they have no intention to reinstate Huang as CEO, even as he came out in the open for the first time after his ouster that he will be open to returning as CEO in case "that is what the board wants."

Nvidia also announced on Tuesday that it was forming a board committee dedicated to national security and export controls.
