An AI development company delivers a working system that solves a business problem, not a model on its own. The work spans several layers, and a credible partner can explain which of them your project needs.

## Why AI projects stall after the demonstration, and what that means for your choice

AI projects stall after the demonstration for reasons that are largely predictable, and Gartner's 2024 forecast names four of them. It predicted that at least 30 percent of generative AI projects would be abandoned after proof of concept by the end of 2025 because of poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs or unclear business value. That is a prediction, not a measured failure rate, but the causes it names match what a buyer can test before signing anything.

| Cause named by Gartner | Question to put to any AI development company | 
|---|---|
| Poor data quality | How will you assess our data before building, and what will you tell us if it is not good enough? | 
| Inadequate risk controls | How do you test accuracy, handle errors and keep a human in the loop where mistakes are costly? |

The difference between a production-ready project and a demonstration is simple: a demonstration works on prepared examples, while a production system works on your real data, inside your real systems, with someone responsible for it afterwards.

## Data security and governance for AI development in Abu Dhabi

AI development in Abu Dhabi has to account for data rules that differ by sector and by where your company is registered, so ask your supplier which apply before design starts. This is awareness, not legal advice, and your legal adviser should confirm what applies to you.

- **Federal personal data law.** The UAE's Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 makes consent the default basis for processing personal data unless an exception applies. It does not apply in the DIFC and ADGM free zones, which run their own regimes.
- **ADGM companies.** Businesses registered in Abu Dhabi Global Market fall under the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021, which were enacted on 11 February 2021 and apply to registered entities.
- **Healthcare.** Health data is handled under a separate framework, and the Abu Dhabi Department of Health has published a policy on the use of AI in the healthcare sector. Any healthcare organisation, or supplier using Abu Dhabi patient data in AI, should check the current Department of Health policies before building.
- **Sector requirements.** Banking, energy, government-linked and other regulated organisations often have additional rules and internal policies on data location and supplier access.

Beyond law, two respected references help you judge a supplier's maturity. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology published its voluntary AI Risk Management Framework in January 2023, built around four functions: govern, map, measure and manage. For security of AI applications, the OWASP GenAI Security Project maintains a Top 10 list for large language model applications, and prompt injection, where crafted inputs manipulate the system, has headed the list in every edition since 2023. A supplier should be able to explain, in plain language, how they think about both.

You may also see suppliers cite ISO/IEC 42001, the AI management system standard published in December 2023. It is a certifiable standard, but it applies to an organisation's management system and not to the quality of a particular product, so treat it as one signal among several. Many capable teams do not hold it, and holding it does not prove a particular project will work.

## AI development process from business requirements to deployment

The AI development process runs from business requirements to deployment in seven stages, and a trustworthy supplier will tell you what you should see at the end of each one.

1. **Discovery.** Agree the business problem, who uses the solution and how success will be measured. You should leave with a written scope.
2. **Feasibility.** Check whether your data, systems and rules can support the idea. You should leave with an honest view of risks and what is not yet ready.
3. **Proof of concept.** Test the core idea on a small scale with real examples. You should see evidence on your own data, not generic demonstrations.
4. **Development.** Build the solution and its integrations in stages, with regular reviews.
5. **Testing.** Evaluate accuracy, handle edge cases and set human review points before real users rely on it.
6. **Launch.** Release to a controlled group first, with support in place.
7. **Monitoring and improvement.** Track results against the measures agreed at the start, and update as data and rules change.

The stage most often skipped is the first. Projects that begin with a tool rather than a measurable problem tend to produce impressive demonstrations and no business result. The stage most often neglected is the last: an AI system that nobody monitors will drift, and the people who notice first are your customers.

## When custom AI development is not justified

Custom AI development is not justified when a simpler option would solve the problem, and a good supplier will say so. Commissioning a build in these situations is a common and avoidable mistake.

- **An off-the-shelf tool already does the job.** If an existing product covers your need, buying it is faster and lower risk than building.
- **The process is not defined.** AI cannot fix a process nobody can describe. Define it first, and automate afterwards.
- **Your data is missing or unusable.** If the information does not exist, or sits in places you cannot access, the first project is data work, not AI.
- **A rule-based automation would do.** Many tasks need clear rules, not intelligence, and a simpler solution costs less to run and is easier to trust.
- **The risk of error is high and nobody can review it.** Where mistakes are costly, AI should assist a person, not replace one.
- **There is no measurable goal.** "We should be using AI" is not a business case.

This is why the first conversation should be an assessment, not a quotation.
