As concerns over rising investments in artificial intelligence (AI) and the monetisation of AI tools continue to influence the technology sector, industries are increasingly exploring practical applications that can deliver measurable economic benefits. One emerging area is Physical AI, which enables AI systems to interact with and operate in the real world rather than remaining confined to digital platforms.

Abu Dhabi's state-owned oil company, ADNOC, has recently signed an agreement with AI firms G42 and OpenAI to deploy advanced AI capabilities across its operations. The partnership aims to improve operational efficiency and address complex engineering challenges in the energy sector.

Speaking at the ADIPEC conference in Abu Dhabi on November 2, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman highlighted the growing relationship between the AI and energy industries. He noted that AI companies could now contribute to the energy sector by offering technologies that improve industrial processes and operations.

What is Physical AI?

Physical AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that can perceive, understand and perform tasks in the physical environment. Unlike conventional AI applications, such as chatbots, image-generation tools and coding assistants, physical AI combines AI models with sensors, actuators, robotics and control systems to interact with the real world.

These systems can observe their surroundings, process information, make decisions and take appropriate actions. Applications include autonomous robots, camera-equipped machines and self-driving vehicles.

Traditional industrial robots generally operate through predefined instructions and perform repetitive tasks in controlled environments. For example, robotic arms in automobile factories are programmed to carry out specific welding operations repeatedly.

Physical AI goes beyond this approach by enabling machines to adapt to changing surroundings and perform more complex tasks with greater autonomy. Its growing adoption could transform sectors such as energy, manufacturing, transportation and industrial engineering, opening new opportunities for the commercial application of artificial intelligence.
