Four agreements between Abu Dhabi-based entities and OpenAI, spanning government, energy and investment, were announced by the Abu Dhabi Media Office on 2 November. Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan met OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman and witnessed the signing.
By our count, G42 subsidiary Inception42 is a party to three of the four agreements, or 75%. The media office gave no dollar value for any of them.

The key insight: Four agreements between Abu Dhabi-based entities and OpenAI were announced on 2 November across government, energy and investment, and G42 subsidiary Inception42 is a party to three of them, 75% by our count, according to the Abu Dhabi Media Office.
The Four Agreements
In energy, ADNOC, G42 through Inception42, and OpenAI signed an agreement that the media office says makes ADNOC “the first company in the energy sector to deploy OpenAI’s frontier technology”. It is “Deployed on OpenAI’s GPT models using in-country inference infrastructure in the UAE”.
In government, Abu Dhabi Government and OpenAI launched what the release calls “the world’s first AI-native Machine-Readable Government initiative”.
In investment, Mubadala, Inception42 and OpenAI signed an agreement to apply AI to “investment analysis, opportunity identification and risk assessment”. Separately, Inception42 and OpenAI signed “a five-year agreement to enable the adoption of frontier AI across government, enterprise and education”.
What Was Posted on X
Wall St Engine summarised the four agreements on X: ADNOC and G42 deploying OpenAI’s Frontier technology across oil production, refining and trading; a government pilot converting regulations into machine-readable formats, which the post says is already live; Mubadala and Inception42 using AI for investment analysis; and a five-year Inception42 partnership. The post also refers to a planned 5 GW AI data center project in the UAE that OpenAI is involved in.
How the Government Pilot Works
The release says the initiative “translates a defined set of government policies and regulations into machine-readable form”, so AI systems can help officials with routine compliance checks, “including those related to issuing permits and certificates”.
It adds that “Each assessment is traceable to the governing rule, while government officials retain oversight and accountability for final decisions.” The first pilot “is already in production with the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority (ADAFSA), focused on food imports”, led by the Department of Government Enablement.
The processing runs on “OpenAI’s UAE inference residency, with real-time model computation running on GPUs within UAE data centres”, the release says.
What Altman Said
Altman was quoted as saying: “Abu Dhabi and the UAE are setting a bold, global standard for AI integration across both the public and private sectors to make sure the benefits of AI reach everyone.”
The release ties the deals to Abu Dhabi’s goal “to become the world’s first fully AI-native government”. A post on X from Kalshi framed the signing as a UAE deal; the media office describes the parties as Abu Dhabi-based entities.
Update, 2 Nov: ADNOC’s own release
ADNOC’s own release calls the energy deal a “landmark multi-year partnership”, announced at ADIPEC, and says ADNOC is the first energy company to deploy OpenAI’s platform “for connecting and coordination frontier AI agents across complex business operations”.
It names two first uses. One turns field master planning into “a single, integrated decision”. The other is meant “to value every barrel while it is still in the ground”, with agents that “continuously forecast crude oil supply, demand, prices and margins”. The system runs “on G42’s sovereign AI infrastructure in the UAE and with human experts retaining oversight of consequential decisions”.
Altman is quoted in it: “More capable AI can help us tackle some of the world’s hardest problems – particularly around energy.” The release states no contract value.
Altman also spoke on stage at ADIPEC with The National’s editor-in-chief, Mina Al-Oraibi, on AI and the energy needed to power it (video). The National had not published a transcript when we added this.
Who Is in Each Deal
Inception42 appears in the ADNOC agreement, the Mubadala agreement and the five-year adoption agreement. The government initiative is between Abu Dhabi Government and OpenAI alone, according to the release.
Two of the four, the ADNOC deal and the government pilot, name in-country inference as the way the models run. OpenAI’s UAE footprint already includes the $30B Stargate UAE build, and UAE investors led OpenAI’s latest funding talks.

CNBC reported on Monday that OpenAI signed the Abu Dhabi agreements as it works to secure funding from investors in the region for a new round of up to $20 billion, which CNBC reported on Saturday is co-led by investors from the United Arab Emirates.
