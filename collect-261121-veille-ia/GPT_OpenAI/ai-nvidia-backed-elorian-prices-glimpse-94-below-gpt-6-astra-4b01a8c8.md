Elorian, the Palo Alto visual-AI startup whose seed round included Nvidia, according to TechCrunch, released its first model, Glimpse, on 16 October 2026 and priced it at $0.60 per million input tokens and $3.00 per million output tokens. Elorian says “both rates are 94% lower than GPT-6 Astra’s, 85% lower than Claude Opus 5.5’s, 70% lower than GPT-6.1 Sol’s, and 20% lower than Gemini 3.8 Flash’s introductory rates.”
On its own benchmark table, Elorian says “Glimpse exceeds Astra on four evaluations spanning scientific chart reasoning, open-vocabulary counting, short-form video captioning, and manipulation-event understanding, and matches it on embodied reasoning.” The same table shows Glimpse below GPT-6 Astra on six other evaluations.
Business Pill · COST PER TASK
A one-minute explainer of cost per task: paying for the job done rather than for each word. It teaches the general idea only and says nothing about any company in this story.
The key insight: As we read it, Elorian is competing on price per task as much as on scores: it lists Glimpse at $0.60 per million input tokens against $10.00 for GPT-6 Astra, says its rates are 94% lower, and in one robot-planning example puts Glimpse’s cost at $0.035 for the episode against $0.249 for Astra.
What Elorian Launched
Glimpse is a research preview available through an API and a chat interface, Elorian says. Its post says “We developed Glimpse as a small team with only a few hundred GPUs, post-trained on an open weights base model.”
TechCrunch, which says it exclusively learned of the launch, says the chat interface is waitlist-only for now, while Elorian’s post says Glimpse “is available through API and our chat UI today.” The two accounts differ on chat access.
Elorian calls Glimpse “a new category of visual thinking model: a system that reasons about the physical world from what it sees.” Its post says Glimpse can zoom into a region, watch a short interval at a denser frame rate, or track one feature across time, which it calls the “visual scratch space”.
What Was Posted on X
Elorian’s own account announced Glimpse on 16 October 2026 as its first frontier visual reasoning model; the figures in this piece come from the launch post the announcement points to.
An investor account summarised the launch: Glimpse ahead of GPT-6 Astra on four benchmarks, a few hundred GPUs, API pricing of $0.60 and $3 per million tokens, and the $55 million seed with Nvidia and Jeff Dean. Its ‘about 17x cheaper’ figure is the poster’s arithmetic on Astra’s standard rates.
The Price Against Frontier Models
Elorian’s Table 1 lists uncached input prices per million tokens of $0.60 for Glimpse, $10.00 for GPT-6 Astra, $4.00 for Claude Opus 5.5, $2.00 for GPT-6.1 Sol and $0.75 introductory for Gemini 3.8 Flash. Output prices are $3.00, $50.00, $20.00, $10.00 and $3.75 introductory respectively.
In one robotics example in the post, Glimpse and GPT-6 Astra each act as the planner for the same robot policy on the same LIBERO task. Elorian says both complete it: “Glimpse’s planning costs $0.035 for the episode; Astra’s costs $0.249.”
What the Benchmarks Show
On WGO Bench, for end-to-end manipulation events, Elorian lists Glimpse at 42.70 against 39.42 for GPT-6 Astra. On CharXiv-R, for scientific chart reasoning, it lists 92.90 against 92.10, and on UGC-VideoCap, for short-form video captioning, 82.66 against 80.32.
On FSC-147, open-vocabulary counting, the table lists Glimpse at 83.36 against 82.02, and on ERQA, embodied spatial reasoning, both at 83.75. GPT-6 Astra is ahead on HSS (53.6 against 46.36), Video-MME v2 (64.06 against 56.68), CountQA, DREAM-1K, TOMATO and BabyVision.
Elorian notes the conditions: “Unless otherwise noted, all model results use the highest thinking effort and native agent harness”. HSS is a benchmark Elorian built with Scale AI and announced on 8 October, according to its blog.
Who Is Behind Elorian
TechCrunch reports that Elorian raised a $55 million seed round at a $300 million valuation before launching a product, in a round co-led by Striker Venture Partners, Menlo Ventures and Altimeter Capital, with participation from Nvidia and Google chief scientist Jeff Dean.
Its chief executive is Andrew Dai, a former Google DeepMind researcher, according to TechCrunch. “Humans can look at Ikea diagrams, with no text, and assemble Ikea furniture, right? Why can’t AI models?” Dai told TechCrunch.
Elorian says it is working toward direct action outputs for robots, and that “calibrated reconstruction and robot control remain work ahead.” Earlier we wrote about Nvidia RTX Spark laptops starting at $2,599 and about Claude Opus 5.5 fast mode leaving preview at $8/$40 per million tokens.
