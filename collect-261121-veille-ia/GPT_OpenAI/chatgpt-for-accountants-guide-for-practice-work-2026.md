## Where ChatGPT Helps in an Accounting Practice

ChatGPT helps in an accounting practice by drafting written communications, creating structured checklists, and generating spreadsheet formulas for staff review.

Language models process narrative patterns effectively. When you supply raw meeting notes, internal procedures, or regulatory updates, ChatGPT formats the information into readable drafts that your staff can refine.

- Drafting client emails and engagement-letter language for review by a senior accountant.
- Summarising a long IRS notice or accounting standard into plain language for a staff member.
- Turning a messy list of year-end questions into a structured client checklist.
- Explaining a variance in words once you provide the confirmed numerical figures.
- Drafting standard operating procedure (SOP) documents for internal firm onboarding and routine tasks.
- Writing complex formulas or scripts for spreadsheets to assist manual data analysis.
- Building a first-draft month-end task checklist.

## Where ChatGPT Falls Short

ChatGPT falls short on transactional execution and numerical computation because general-purpose language models can produce confident wrong numbers and cannot be relied on to calculate.

General-purpose language models can produce confident wrong numbers. A prompt asking ChatGPT to balance an account or compute complex tax liability can generate plausible narrative explanations alongside fabricated totals.

- Bank and credit-card reconciliation at volume, which requires exact transaction matching and ledger integrity.
- Tax return calculation, where tax brackets, phase-outs, and depreciation schedules demand deterministic computational precision.
- Anything where the output is filed with an authority or sent to a client without human review.
- Final tax-position research without checking primary source documents, court cases, and statutory codes directly.

## Client Confidentiality and Data Settings

Client confidentiality requires verifying OpenAI business data settings and removing identifying details before submitting prompts.

OpenAI states that for its business offerings (ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise, and the API) it does not train on customer business data by default. You can confirm these commitments on the OpenAI business data page.

Consumer plans operate under different data retention and training policies. You must check your account settings and confirm the data policy on the OpenAI business data page before pasting any firm information into the interface.

Do not paste client names, tax IDs, bank account numbers, or full ledgers into a tool whose data terms you have not checked. Anonymise prompts by replacing specific names with generic labels like 'Client A', and check your engagement letters and professional body guidance on client data and AI.

## A Safe Review Workflow

A structured review workflow requires draft generation, source document verification, and qualified accountant sign-off.

In the drafting phase, you provide anonymised background information and instructions to create an email, SOP, or checklist. Treat the output as preliminary working material that requires professional refinement.

In the verification phase, you check every claim and number against official primary records. If ChatGPT references a tax notice, regulatory standard, or variance figure, compare that output against primary source documents and approved working papers.

In the sign-off phase, a designated reviewer examines the final document before delivery. No client letter, policy document, or advisory memo should leave your practice without written approval from an accountant who takes professional responsibility for the work.
