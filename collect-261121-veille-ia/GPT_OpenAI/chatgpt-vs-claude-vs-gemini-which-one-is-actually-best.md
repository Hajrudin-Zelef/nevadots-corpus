# ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini: which one is actually best?

By MultiAI Team · · 22 min read

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are the three AI assistants most people choose between, and each company says theirs is the best. The honest answer is that each one wins something different. ChatGPT is the most used and has the widest set of tools. Claude scores highest on independent tests and is the one reviewers keep picking for writing and code. Gemini handles the longest files, makes images and video, and lives inside Gmail and Docs.

This is our full comparison: where each came from, every plan and price, the limits nobody puts on the pricing page, ten head-to-head rounds, accuracy, speed, apps, privacy, team plans, and which one suits which kind of person. Every price and limit was checked on each company's own pages on 23 October 2026, and every score comes from an independent leaderboard, a maker's published results, or our own testing.

## Who uses which

ChatGPT is still by far the biggest. OpenAI said at its developer day on 29 September that ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users. Google's Gemini app passed 1 billion monthly users in August. Anthropic doesn't publish a user count for Claude; in March it told TechCrunch that daily users had more than tripled since January.

The trend matters more than the totals. A year ago ChatGPT took almost three in every four visits to AI chatbot sites. Now it takes just over half, and Gemini and Claude have taken the rest. In America, a Pew survey this year found that 44% of adults had used ChatGPT, 24% Gemini and 6% Claude. People are spreading out, and a lot of them use more than one.

## Where each one came from

**ChatGPT** started it all on 30 November 2022 and has led on firsts: the
first mass-market chatbot, and the first "reasoning" model that thinks
before answering (o1, in September 2024). The
GPT-6 family arrived this September.

**Claude** came from Anthropic, founded by former OpenAI researchers, and
launched in March 2023. It built its name on writing and coding, and its
newest models lead the independent leaderboards this autumn.

**Gemini** began life as Bard in March 2023 and took the Gemini name in
February 2024. Google has since folded it into Android, Chrome, Gmail and
Docs, and on 13 October it brought Gemini 4 Argon to paying users.

Each company now sells a family of models rather than one. This is who's who:

## What you get for free, and for $20

**ChatGPT Free** runs GPT-6 Luna, OpenAI's light model. OpenAI's help centre
says free users have "unlimited everyday text chats, subject to
abuse-prevention safeguards"; uploads, images, voice and deep research have
their own lower caps, and free users get three file uploads a day. The free
plan shows ads, and so does the $8 Go plan. **Plus ($20)** moves you to
GPT-6 Sol, removes the ads and raises every limit.

**Claude Free** gives you Sonnet and Haiku, with web search, memory across
chats, voice, file creation and up to five projects. The catch is the limit,
which resets every five hours and arrives sooner on long chats. **Pro ($20,
or $17 billed yearly)** adds Opus 5.5, at least five times the free usage,
and Claude Code.

**Gemini's free plan** changed on 9 October: free users now get
only Flash-Lite,
Google's lightest model, though the default Auto setting may send a hard
question to a stronger one. It still includes image generation, Deep
Research and Canvas. **AI Pro ($19.99)** unlocks Gemini 4 Argon, Gemini
inside Gmail and Docs, and 5 TB of Google storage. If you already pay Google
$9.99 a month for 2 TB, the AI part costs you about $10.

## The limits nobody puts on the pricing page

None of the three publishes a message count for any plan. What they do publish is how much a chat can hold, how big a file can be, and when limits reset.

**The context window** is everything the AI can keep in mind at once: your
messages, its replies, and any files you add. When a chat outgrows it, the
earliest parts drop out, which is one reason
AI answers get worse in long conversations.
On the free plans the windows are small: about 40 pages in ChatGPT and 50 in
Gemini. Claude's paid plans and Gemini AI Pro both hold a million tokens,
roughly 1,500 pages. ChatGPT Plus holds 256,000 tokens when it reasons, and
only 54,000 in quick mode.

|  | ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini | 
|---|---|---|---|
| Biggest file | 512 MB (images 20 MB) | 500 MB | 100 MB (video 2 GB) | 
| Files at once | 80 every 3 hours; 3 a day on Free | 20 per chat | 10 per message |

Gemini is the only one of the three that takes video directly: up to five minutes on the free plan and an hour on AI Pro. Claude and Gemini both use a five-hour window, so a heavy morning can lock you out until the afternoon.

## How they score

Two leaderboards are worth knowing, and they measure different things.

**Artificial Analysis runs hard tests** on reasoning, knowledge and coding,
with the same settings for every model. Claude Opus 5.5 leads its index at
58. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is second at 56, and GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon tie
at 53. On this measure, Anthropic's models hold the top three places.

**Arena asks people to vote blind**: they see two answers to their own
question without knowing which model wrote which, and pick the better one.
On 22 October Gemini 4 Argon led with 1517 points, a rating Arena still
marks as preliminary, with Claude Opus 5.5 eight points behind, close to a
tie. OpenAI's best was its older GPT-5.6 Sol, in 21st place; GPT-6.1 Sol
was 27th.

Put together: Claude is the strongest at hard problems, Gemini writes the answers people like reading most, and ChatGPT's models trail both on these two boards. The gaps are smaller than they look in daily use, as the rounds below show.

## Head to head: ten rounds

Each round compares the best model each company offers for that job, using independent leaderboards first, the makers' own published results where nothing independent exists, and hands-on reviews. Makers run their own tests at settings they choose, so we say whose number it is.

### 1. Writing: Claude

Hands-on reviewers keep reaching the same verdict. In Tom's Guide's test of ChatGPT and Claude Opus 5.5 on five everyday prompts, Claude won four, including rewriting an announcement in a natural, human voice. A follow-up piece picked Claude for natural writing and ChatGPT for structured plans.

The leaderboards are more mixed. On EQ-Bench's creative writing test, where another AI judges short stories, GPT-6 Astra is first and Opus 5.5 seventh; Gemini 4 Argon hasn't been rated yet. For fiction, try ChatGPT too. For emails, posts and reports that sound like a person, Claude. Our guide on making AI writing sound like you covers how to get any of them closer to your voice.

### 2. Coding: Claude

On Terminal-Bench 4.0, which tests real coding work in a terminal, Anthropic reports 66.4% for Opus 5.5 against 57.9% for GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI's figure) and 57.4% for Gemini 4 Argon (Google's). Vals AI's independent run of the same test also put Opus first. Claude leads Arena's agent board too, and in DataCamp's hands-on coding test Opus 5.5 beat Argon, whose animation clipped through obstacles.

ChatGPT isn't far behind: GPT-6 Astra leads FrontierSWE, a test of large engineering tasks, and Codex is a strong coding tool. Gemini leads its own DeepSWE benchmark but has drawn the most criticism from people who tried it on real programming. For day-to-day coding all three are capable; for long, multi-step coding work, Claude.

### 3. Math and reasoning: ChatGPT

OpenAI's models lead the hardest reasoning tests. GPT-6 Astra scores 96.3% on GPQA Diamond, graduate-level science questions, the top score on Artificial Analysis's board. On ARC-AGI-2, a test of solving puzzles never seen before, GPT-6 scores 95.0%, Opus 5.5 93.3% and Argon 91.3%. On MathArena, which tests models on fresh competition problems, OpenAI's models hold the top three places among those tested.

Claude has one notable win: Opus 5.5 leads Humanity's Last Exam, a set of expert questions across every field, at 61.4% without tools. The margins at the top are narrow, but for math and logic puzzles, ChatGPT edges it.

### 4. Long documents and video: Gemini

Gemini 4 Argon holds up best when a document gets very long. On GraphWalks, which asks models to trace connections across 256,000 to a million tokens, Google reports 84.2% for Argon, against 71.8% for GPT-6 Astra and 66.8% for Opus 5.5. It's also the only one that watches video: it scores 91.7% on LVBench, a long-video test, against 87.5% for Astra and 83.7% for Opus, by Google's figures.

On shorter documents the gap closes. In our earlier flagship test, Opus and GPT-6.1 Sol read slightly better than Argon on Artificial Analysis's long-document test. If you work with whole contract bundles, a year of reports or recorded meetings, start with Gemini.

### 5. Professional analysis: Gemini and Claude, a tie

Two independent indexes of real work disagree. The Vals Index, built from finance, legal and tax tasks, puts Gemini 4 Argon first at 68.9%, with Opus 5.5 at 67.0%. Artificial Analysis's GDPval test, which grades finished work across dozens of professions, puts Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 first and second, well ahead of Argon, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra. DataCamp's reviewer came down the same way: Argon for legal, financial and long-document work, Opus where every requirement has to hold to the end.

### 6. Making images: ChatGPT

On Artificial Analysis's image arena, where people vote blind on pictures made from the same prompt, OpenAI's GPT Image 2.5 is first with 1222 points. Google's Nano Banana 2.1, the model in Gemini, scores 1176. Gemini makes up for it on price: image generation is free, and video starts on the $4.99 AI Plus plan. ChatGPT has made no video since OpenAI shut down Sora in April. Claude makes neither images nor video; it can draw charts and diagrams, but not pictures.

### 7. Reading images and charts: Claude and ChatGPT, a tie

On MMMU-Pro, which asks questions about diagrams, charts and photos across academic subjects, Artificial Analysis scores Opus 5.5 at 88% and GPT-6 Astra at 87%. Gemini 4 Argon isn't on that board yet. On chart reading, the makers' own figures conflict so much depending on settings that we wouldn't pick a winner from them. Screenshots, charts and handwritten notes all work well in both.

### 8. Voice: Gemini and ChatGPT, a tie

On Big Bench Audio, which asks spoken reasoning questions and scores the spoken answers, the model behind Gemini Live scores 92% (98% with its extended thinking on), and the model behind ChatGPT's voice mode 90%. ChatGPT's voice can see through your camera from the $8 Go plan up and works in CarPlay; Gemini Live works on Android phones, watches and in Android Auto. Claude has a voice mode on every plan, but it hasn't been tested on the same benchmark.

### 9. Hands-off tasks: Claude

All three now offer a mode that works through a task step by step while you do something else: ChatGPT Work, Gemini Spark (AI Pro and up), and Claude Duet, which launched on 22 October with a waitlist. On Arena's agent leaderboard, where people vote on how well models complete real multi-step tasks, Opus 5.5 is first with a clear lead (20.9% against 14.7% for GPT-6 Astra); Argon is sixth. ChatGPT Work is the one you can use today without waiting.

### 10. Learning and study: ChatGPT

Tom's Guide picked ChatGPT for learning something new and for quick questions, and suggested starting there if you're unsure, because its free plan allows more chatting. It's also the only one built for teenagers: ChatGPT is open from age 13 with a parent's permission, with dedicated teen accounts since August. Claude requires users to be 18. Gemini is open from 13, and US college students can get AI Pro free for a year.

## Which one makes things up least

Every AI sometimes states things that aren't true. Two independent tests measure different versions of the problem.

Vectara's leaderboard asks models to summarise documents and counts how often they add details that aren't in the source. OpenAI's models do best: 6.5% for GPT-6 Sol and 8.7% for GPT-6 Astra, against 12.1% for Gemini 4 Argon. Claude's 5.5 models haven't been added yet; the last Claude tested, Opus 4.7, scored 12.0%.

Artificial Analysis's Omniscience test asks thousands of hard factual questions, rewarding right answers and penalising wrong guesses. Opus 5.5 leads at 46, ahead of Astra at 44 and Argon at 42. Argon is the most cautious: it guessed on only 15% of questions it couldn't answer, against 54% for GPT-6.1 Sol.

So ChatGPT sticks closest to a document you give it, and Claude knows the most. Whichever you use, the best protection is checking important answers with a second model, which we explain in why AI makes things up.

## Speed and cost per answer: our own test

We gave each company's flagship the same eight everyday tasks three times: a billing calculation, a bug fix, pulling dates from meeting notes, a logic puzzle, an invoice total across three currencies, a date function, a sales table and a contract deadline. Every task had one right answer, checked by a script.

**All three got all 24 right.** On everyday work with a clear answer, there
was no quality difference to find. The differences were speed and price.
OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol was the fastest at 5.0 seconds for a whole answer and
the cheapest by far. Opus 5.5 took 5.7 seconds and cost five times as much,
because it writes longer answers. Gemini 4 Argon was slowest at 8.4
seconds, mostly on the puzzle and the contract, where it thought for twice
as long as the others. The full results are in our
three-way flagship test.

On a subscription you don't pay per answer, but the same pattern shows up as limits: a model that writes more uses up your allowance faster.

## What each app can do

The models are close. The apps around them are not.

- **Memory.** All three remember things about you across chats. Claude
includes memory on the free plan, and both Claude and Gemini can import
what ChatGPT has learned about you, so switching doesn't mean starting
over.
- **Projects and custom assistants.** ChatGPT has projects and custom GPTs.
Claude has projects and skills. Gemini launched skills on 30 September,
and they're replacing its Gems: personal Gems start moving over in
November.
- **Deep research.** All three can spend several minutes searching the web
and writing a report with sources. Gemini's is free; ChatGPT's is limited
on Free; Claude's needs Pro.
- **Coding tools.** Codex, Claude Code and Google's Antigravity all come
with the paid plans.

## Apps, devices and ecosystems

**Gemini is everywhere Google is.** It's replacing Google Assistant on
Android phones and watches this autumn, it's built into Chrome, and on AI Pro it
works inside Gmail, Docs and Drive. If your life runs on Google, that alone
can decide it.

**ChatGPT is everywhere else.** It's built into Siri on iPhone, runs in
CarPlay, has a browser extension for Chrome, Edge and others, and connects
to the most outside apps: more than 2,000 by one count, though OpenAI
doesn't publish a number.

**Claude is the best connected to work tools.** It lists 1,006 connectors,
including Microsoft 365, Slack and Notion, and has add-ins for Excel,
PowerPoint, Word and Outlook. Gemini's 83 connected apps don't include
Microsoft, Slack or Notion.

All three reply in almost any language you write in. Gemini's app is offered in more than 70 languages and over 230 countries; ChatGPT's interface comes in 59 languages, Claude's in 11.

## Which one fits you

**Students:** ChatGPT for explanations and practice, and it's the only one
open to under-18s with teen accounts. In the US, take Google's free year of
AI Pro before 31 December.

**Writers and marketers:** Claude. Its drafts need the least rewriting, and
paid plans hold a whole book or campaign in one chat.

**Developers:** Claude, with Claude Code. Keep ChatGPT's Codex as a second

## Switching, or using all three

**Moving your history.** ChatGPT and Claude both email you a download of
your chats (ChatGPT's can take up to seven days, and both links expire after
24 hours). Gemini's comes through Google Takeout. **Moving your memory** is
easier than it used to be: Claude and Gemini can both import what ChatGPT

import tool.

## Questions people ask

**Which is smartest: ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?**
On independent tests, Claude Opus 5.5 scores highest (58 on the Artificial
Analysis index, against 53 for the best from OpenAI and Google). In blind
votes, people prefer Gemini 4 Argon's answers by a whisker. OpenAI leads on
the hardest math and science tests.

**Which is best overall?**
There's no single winner. In our ten rounds, Claude and ChatGPT won five
each and Gemini three, with three ties. Claude for writing and coding,
ChatGPT for an all-round toolbox, Gemini for long files and Google apps.

**Which has the best free plan?**
Claude gives you the strongest model for free, but with limits that reset
every five hours. ChatGPT is the most generous with text chat. Gemini now
runs only its lightest model for free users, but includes free image
generation and Deep Research.

**Is Gemini better than ChatGPT?**
For long documents, video, Google apps and blind-vote preference, yes. For
math, image quality, learning, add-ons and short everyday answers, ChatGPT
is ahead.

**Is Claude better than ChatGPT?**
For writing, coding and hands-off tasks, yes, and it scores higher on the
main independent index. ChatGPT makes images, works for teenagers, has more
add-ons and lets free users chat more.

**Which is best for coding?**
Claude, especially with Claude Code, by most independent measures. ChatGPT's
Codex is a strong second.

**Which is best for students?**
ChatGPT for learning and explanations, and it's the only one open to
13-to-17-year-olds with teen accounts. US college students can get Google
AI Pro free for a year.

**Which one makes things up least?**
ChatGPT's models add the fewest details to summaries, and Claude Opus 5.5
scores best on hard factual questions. None is immune, so check anything
important with a second model.

**Which one is safest with my data?**
All three let you stop your chats being used for training. Claude asks you
to choose, keeps your chats for 30 days if you say no, and only lets a
safety team see flagged chats. ChatGPT and Gemini have training on unless
you turn it off.

**Can I move from ChatGPT to Claude or Gemini without losing everything?**
Yes. Export your ChatGPT history from Data controls, and use Claude's or
Gemini's memory import to bring over what ChatGPT knows about you.

**Can I use all three without paying three times?**
Yes. The free plans cover light use of each. For more, a multi-model app
gives you every model behind one login and one bill.

**Why is ChatGPT's model in our test GPT-6.1 Sol, not the one in ChatGPT?**
GPT-6.1 Sol is OpenAI's newest model, but in ChatGPT it only runs in Work
mode and Codex; the normal chat uses GPT-6 Sol. Apps like ours reach 6.1
Sol through OpenAI's API, which is how we tested it.
