## Anthropic’s Fable 5.2: A Leap in AI for Complex Tasks

Anthropic has introduced two new AI models, **Claude Fable 5.2** and Mythos 5.2, with Fable 5.2 available to the public. Touted as the most advanced AI model yet, Fable 5.2 excels in tackling complex scientific and technical problems. However, its utility for everyday tasks is limited. It is designed for ambitious applications in areas like theoretical physics, protein folding, robotics, and hardware engineering. For routine coding or general knowledge tasks, Anthropic suggests using Opus 5.5 instead.

## Performance and Benchmarks

Fable 5.2 has achieved new performance benchmarks across multiple domains. On the Agentic Scientific Research benchmark, it scored a leading **74.6%**, surpassing Opus 5.5 (58.7%), Astra (68.1%), and Gemini 4 Carbon (64.8%). It also leads in cybersecurity, protein design, agentic search, multidisciplinary reasoning, and visual chart recognition. However, Gemini Carbon narrowly outperformed it in business workflows by two percentage points.

On the Frontier Code 2.0 benchmark, Fable 5.2 showed diminishing returns at extended reasoning levels. While it excelled at “extra high” reasoning, its performance plateaued at “max” reasoning, with higher costs yielding no significant gains. At “high” reasoning, it matched Opus 5.5’s “max” performance for coding tasks but at a lower cost. On the original Frontier Code benchmark, Fable 5.2 scored 49.1%, outperforming Opus 5’s 45.5%.

## Capabilities and Use Cases

Fable 5.2 is designed for “really hard tasks. In testing, it demonstrated exceptional capabilities in hardware engineering, successfully designing and producing three functional electronic devices, including circuit boards, 3D-printed enclosures, and software, based on a single-paragraph prompt. It also coordinated over 1,000 sub-agents to develop a prototype operating system in eight days, complete with a core, drivers, file storage, and cloud functionality.

Additionally, it reportedly discovered a novel method to predict a complete capsid structure using two open source protein folding models, though this claim is unverified.

In game development, Fable 5.2 rapidly generated functional mechanics. For example, a prompt to create a game in the style of Mega Bonk resulted in “Shardstorm,” a 3D third-person horde survival roguelike. While the mechanics, such as sprinting and leveling up, worked well, the graphics were less refined than those produced by Opus 5.5. The game was generated in 16 minutes at medium reasoning.

Fable 5.2 avoids directly cloning existing games, instead creating original games within the same genre to avoid intellectual property conflicts.

The model also excels in multimedia creation. It generated a 20-second travel advertisement entirely in code, complete with music, art, animation, and scripting. Other projects include a “mirror Earth” game with real-time weather and destructible environments, though some glitches were noted. This game allows users to interact with a voxel-based environment, such as punching holes in walls or damaging cars.

## Cost and Accessibility

Fable 5.2 is available through a paid Claude plan, starting at **$17/month** for the Pro tier. API pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, making it slightly cheaper than GPT 6 Astra and its predecessor, Fable 5.1, which cost $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens.

Despite these reductions, its high costs make it impractical for most users, with an average task in max reasoning mode costing **$26.54**. To reduce expenses, Anthropic recommends using lower reasoning levels and delegating simpler tasks to smaller models like Haiku, Sonnet, and Nopus.

Fable 5.2 is more efficient in some scenarios, using fewer output tokens per task than Sonnet 5.5. For instance, on the Buccy Bench benchmark, it generated an SVG version of Gary Busey in 19 minutes and 50 seconds, consuming 122,248 tokens at a cost of nearly $5. It ranks sixth on the Buccy Bench leaderboard, with GPT 6.1 Soul in the top spot.

## Limitations and Ethical Guardrails

Fable 5.2 is not optimized for everyday tasks like research or small coding projects, where Opus 5.5 performs comparably. The model adheres to strict ethical guidelines, refusing to clone games or use real people’s likenesses. For example, when prompted to design a handheld desk gadget featuring Gary Busey, it instead created a robot character.

The output included specifications for a device featuring a pixel art character reacting to button presses, along with four STL files for 3D printing, character images, wiring instructions, assembly instructions, and firmware code. Bamboo Studio provides detailed instructions for assembly, including a shell design and a list of parts available on Amazon.

The assembly manual, generated in 15 minutes, offered step-by-step guidance with graphics. Tasks included printing the case, flashing the firmware, creating plug-on tails, pin mapping, bench testing the Pico board, and fitting the button. Required components include three nuts, a display clamp, a Pico board, a back plate, and a shell.

## Community Insights and Comparisons

Early access users have highlighted Fable 5.2’s versatility across diverse applications. One team created a Gears of War-inspired game with impressive graphics, while another demonstrated 23 projects, including a Zelda recreation, a Halo-like game, and clones of Photoshop and Adobe Premiere. Other users described Fable 5.2 as transformative for 3D motion videos, surpassing Opus in this area. Additional projects include an F-Zero-inspired racing game and a “mirror Earth” simulation with real-time weather and destructible environments.

The model’s performance varies with reasoning level and task complexity. It excels in mechanics and large-scale tasks but produces less detailed graphics at medium reasoning compared to Opus 5.5. Generation speed may also slow under high GPU demand.

Fable 5.2 represents a major step forward for tackling complex challenges like scientific research, hardware engineering, and large-scale simulations. However, its high costs and limited utility for simpler tasks make it less practical for everyday use. According to its creator, Fable 5.2 is best suited for ambitious projects where its advanced capabilities can be fully applied.
