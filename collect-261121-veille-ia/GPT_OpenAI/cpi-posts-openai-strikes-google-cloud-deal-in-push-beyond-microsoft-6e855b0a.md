OpenAI is expanding the distribution of its artificial intelligence technology through a new agreement with Google Cloud, giving businesses access to its advanced models across all three major U.S. cloud platforms as the ChatGPT developer reduces its historical dependence on Microsoft.
	
The agreement will make OpenAI’s leading AI models and Codex software development tools available through Google Cloud’s Model Garden and Marketplace, according to a CNBC report published Wednesday.
The expansion represents another step in OpenAI’s effort to reach corporate customers through multiple infrastructure providers rather than relying primarily on its longtime partner, Microsoft. OpenAI has also expanded distribution through Amazon Web Services, placing its technology within reach of enterprise customers using the three largest U.S. cloud platforms.
The partnership is particularly notable because Google and OpenAI compete directly in the rapidly growing market for generative AI technology. Google’s Gemini models challenge OpenAI’s products across consumer applications, software development and enterprise services.
Despite that rivalry, the agreement reflects the increasing importance of providing businesses with access to competing AI systems through existing cloud infrastructure.
Enterprise Customers Gain More Options
Under the arrangement, Google Cloud customers will be able to purchase OpenAI models through Google’s existing commercial infrastructure, according to CNBC and announcements from the two companies.
Businesses with previously negotiated Google Cloud spending commitments will be able to apply those commitments toward OpenAI products, potentially simplifying procurement and reducing the need for separate vendor agreements.
The initial offering includes GPT-Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, according to Google Cloud’s October 21 announcement. The models are designed to address different enterprise requirements, ranging from complex reasoning and software engineering to lower-cost, high-volume applications.
Google said the models are beginning to roll out in private preview to selected enterprise customers, with broader availability planned in the coming weeks.
OpenAI’s Codex tools will also be supported, allowing developers to connect software engineering workflows to Google’s cloud infrastructure.
The companies plan to extend access to additional Google development products, including Google Antigravity and Gemini Agent.
For enterprise customers, the integration could make it easier to compare models from multiple developers without moving applications and sensitive information between separate cloud environments.
Microsoft Relationship Enters a New Phase
The Google agreement underscores the evolution of OpenAI’s relationship with Microsoft, which provided critical financial and computing support during the ChatGPT maker’s early expansion.
Microsoft’s Azure cloud platform has served as a major distribution channel for OpenAI’s technology, but changes to the companies’ commercial arrangements have allowed OpenAI to pursue additional partnerships.

According to CNBC, the expansion to Google Cloud follows OpenAI’s earlier move to make its technology available through Amazon’s cloud services.
The developments point to a broader distribution strategy in which OpenAI seeks access to enterprise customers regardless of their preferred infrastructure provider.
The Google agreement does not end OpenAI’s relationship with Microsoft. OpenAI said in its announcement that Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner.
Nevertheless, the addition of Google provides another channel for commercializing OpenAI’s technology at a time when competition among AI developers is intensifying.
Google Strengthens Its Position as an AI Platform
The agreement also supports Google’s efforts to establish its cloud business as a destination for companies deploying AI systems from multiple developers.
Google Cloud already offers its proprietary Gemini technology alongside third-party models. Adding OpenAI’s products expands the range of tools available to corporate customers and software developers.
According to Google’s announcement, its platform now offers access to leading proprietary AI models from Google, OpenAI and Anthropic, alongside a broader catalog of other models.
That approach could help Google attract businesses seeking flexibility in choosing AI systems for different applications.
Rather than committing to a single developer, companies can evaluate models based on performance, operating costs, security requirements and the complexity of individual tasks.
Google is also emphasizing enterprise data protections, including access controls, auditing capabilities and infrastructure designed to support organizations handling sensitive information.
Such features are particularly important for businesses in regulated industries, where compliance and data governance can determine whether new AI technology is deployed at scale.
Cloud Providers Become Central to AI Competition
The partnership highlights a shift in the commercial structure of the AI industry, as major cloud providers increasingly serve as distribution platforms for competing technology developers.
For companies such as OpenAI, broader cloud availability offers a way to reach customers that have already committed substantial resources to established infrastructure providers.
For Google, Microsoft and Amazon, hosting advanced AI models can help attract and retain enterprise workloads, even when those models are developed by competitors.
The arrangement also creates a competitive challenge for OpenAI. Greater distribution makes its technology more accessible, but it also places the company’s models alongside rival products that customers can evaluate directly.
As a result, model performance, pricing, reliability and integration with existing business systems are likely to play an increasingly important role in enterprise purchasing decisions.
The Google partnership gives OpenAI another route into the corporate market while reinforcing the growing interdependence between AI developers and the cloud companies that provide access to their technology.
