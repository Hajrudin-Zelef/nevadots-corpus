OpenAI is introducing GPT‑6.2 Astra, its new frontier model for complex tasks and GPT‑6.2 Sol, which it bills as a more efficient model for everyday tasks.

Artificial Analysis highlights that OpenAI now trails only Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 in the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, taking the number two spot on the popular benchmark.

The independent research firm notes that OpenAI models now occupy most of the Cost per Task Pareto frontier, sharing the frontier with only Anthropic and Meta.

Matt Weaver, Head of Applied AI Architects at OpenAI, tells AI Magazine: “With GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, we’re helping businesses get better results on complex work while using fewer tokens.”

## GPT-6.2 Astra

In the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, GPT-6.2 Astra jumps 5 points from GPT-6 Astra, overtaking Gemini 4 Carbon (60) and Claude Opus 5.5 (59) at top effort settings. It scores 62, only behind Claude Fable 5.2 (max).

OpenAI notes that on Agents’ Last Exam, a benchmark that tests multistep professional work, GPT‑6.2 Astra sets a new high, scoring 5.2 points higher than GPT‑6 Astra at less than a third of the cost, and 5.5 points ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at just 5% of the cost.

Matt adds: “Take a financial model. Getting the numbers right is part of it, but you also need a workbook that someone else can pick up and use. Or a presentation that follows your company’s template, rather than something you spend hours reformatting.

“Astra has got better at producing work that meets those professional standards, as well as handling complex coding tasks.”

On OpenAI’s internal GDPval v2 benchmark that measures complex, long-horizon workflows from real projects in sectors like consulting, finance and design, GPT‑6.2 Astra leads the models the lab tested, up 6.2 percentage points from GPT‑6 Astra while using 22% fewer output tokens. GPT‑6.2 Sol improves 5.3 points from GPT‑6.1 Sol.

With GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, we’re helping businesses get better results on complex work while using fewer tokens


Cost efficiency has become a central battleground in AI, with enterprises routing workloads across models to maximise value and US labs competing more aggressively on price against Chinese rivals that have historically undercut them.

GPT‑6.2 Astra’s standard API input and output token prices are 20% lower than GPT‑6 Astra’s – dropping to US$8 and US$40 per million tokens, down from US$10 and US$50. But the key sell is across the Pareto frontier, which is a set of optimal outcomes where you cannot improve one goal without making at least one other goal worse.

Artificial Analysis writes in a post on X that OpenAI 'dominates' the Output Token per Task Pareto Frontier, with Claude Fable 5.2 being the only model from a non-OpenAI lab appearing on the frontier.

OpenAI says it believes GPT-6.2 Astra is “the best model in the world for scientists”. The model sets a new high on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, which tests how well models can carry out scientific work in a terminal.

In coding, GPT-6.2 Astra is flat with GPT-6 Astra according to Artificial Analysis, and GPT-6.2 Sol loses 3 points relative to GPT-6.1 Sol in the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index (Codex harness at max effort).

- GPT-6.2 Astra ranks second on the Intelligence Index, trailing only Claude Fable 5.2
- GPT-6.2 Sol scores 57 on the Intelligence Index, matching GPT-6 Astra at roughly 15% cost
- GPT-6.2 Astra drops 20% to US$8 input and US$40 output per million tokens
- GPT-6.2 Sol keeps GPT-6.1 Sol pricing at US$2 input and US$10 output per million
- OpenAI 'dominates' the Cost per Task frontier
- GPT-6.2 Astra leads AA-3D (1,318), ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 (1,271) and GPT-6.2 Sol (1,259)
- GPT-6.2 Astra (79) and GPT-6.2 Sol (78) top Claude Fable 5.2 (75) on AA-Robotics.

## GPT‑6.2 Sol

Matt notes that Astra is for the hardest projects, but Sol is the model you’d turn to throughout the day – whether you’re drafting a document, working through an analysis or tracking down a software bug.

“That efficiency matters when you start using AI at scale,” Matt says. “People can get more done within their usage limits. Astra’s standard API input and output token prices are also 20% lower than GPT-6 Astra’s – so businesses have more capability, with more room to put it to work.”

OpenAI notes it powers through multistep agentic workflows, writes clearly and naturally and is intuitive to collaborate with. OpenAI notes that on its recently released LifeVal evaluation, which measures performance on challenging everyday tasks, GPT‑6.2 Sol outscores GPT‑6 Astra at about one-seventh the cost.

In Cognition’s FrontierCode 2.0, GPT‑6.2 Sol comes within 0.5 points of GPT‑6.2 Astra at about one-fifth the cost (both at max).

The AI lab notes that many employees at OpenAI use it as their default model for both technical and non-technical tasks. GPT‑6.2 Sol carries forward GPT‑6.1 Sol’s rates of US$2 for input, US$0.10 for cached input, and US$10 for output per million tokens.

## Safety, RSI and power

Both models meet the Critical threshold for cybersecurity capabilities and the High threshold for biological and chemical capabilities under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, its process for tracking and preparing for advanced AI capabilities that could introduce new risks of severe harm.

GPT-6.2 Astra meets the High threshold for AI Self-Improvement capabilities, while GPT-6.2 Sol falls below it. In the system card, OpenAI explains it views this threshold as an early milestone for proactively preparing safeguards for any models that may in future be capable of fully automated, recursive self-improvement.

Speaking to Matthew Berman on the Forward Future podcast in October, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said: “[RSI] is a very dangerous thing. The world is going to need some speed limits on how quickly we let RSI go.”

Discussing the risks more broadly, Sam said: “Both of the ways that I have worried about things going very wrong, either as a loss of control accident to AI, or an intentional loss of control incident to AI – some lab says I’m going to push the button on RSI to make sure we have the power. …

“We could get too much power concentration in the hands of a few people that control too much of the AI and are making the decisions. I think we can avoid both of those.”

## Key partners for OpenAI

- Microsoft: As one of OpenAI’s most entrenched partners, Microsoft holds a 27% stake in the restructured OpenAI Group Public Benefit Corporation (PBC) following a historic US$13bn initial investment.
- AWS: AWS is a strategic cloud partner with OpenAI, collaborating to build a stateful runtime environment on Amazon Bedrock following a US$50bn investment in OpenAI.
- NVIDIA: NVIDIA has been OpenAI’s essential hardware supplier for a decade, providing the GPUs that have powered ChatGPT from the beginning.
- Broadcom: In an effort to reduce reliance on standard GPUs, OpenAI has strategically partnered with Broadcom to co-develop custom AI accelerators and Ethernet networking racks.
