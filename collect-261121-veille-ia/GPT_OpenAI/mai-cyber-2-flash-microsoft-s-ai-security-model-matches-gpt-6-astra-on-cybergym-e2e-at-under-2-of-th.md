Microsoft announced MAI Cyber 2 Flash on November 17, 2026: its AI security model scores 92.3% on CyberGym-E2E, matching GPT-6 Astra's quality at less than 2% of the cost per task in Microsoft's own evaluation, with a 1M-token context window and list prices of $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens.

## What changed with MAI Cyber 2 Flash

Microsoft announced MAI Cyber 2 Flash on November 17, 2026, at Ignite: its latest specialist AI security model, built on Microsoft's proprietary security data to find vulnerabilities, recommend fixes and investigate threats around the clock. It is coming exclusively to Microsoft security products, including MDASH (written as Codename MDASH in Microsoft's post), where it will automatically replace the current model in the MAI profile for eligible customers starting mid-December 2026. Microsoft says the MAI Cyber family already accounts for about 40% of scans in MDASH, and 90% of scans that started with MAI continued to use it.

In Microsoft's own CyberGym-E2E evaluation of real-world vulnerability discovery, MAI Cyber 2 Flash scored 92.3% against GPT-6 Astra's 91.5%, using half the reasoning budget, at less than 2% of the cost per task; on WebExploitBench it scored 40.4% against GPT-5.6 Cyber's 38.4% at the same reasoning budget (Microsoft's figures). Microsoft lists its price at $0.10 input, $0.01 cached input and $0.50 output per million tokens, with output tokens 86% cheaper than MAI Cyber 1.1 Flash, and a one-million-token context window, four times its predecessor's. Microsoft also disclosed that 92 of the benchmark's 905 tasks overlapped with projects in its training data, excluded them for every model, and reported results on the remaining 813. As of November 17, 2026, Artificial Analysis had not published a page for the model.

## Why MAI Cyber 2 Flash matters for businesses

Why it matters: always-on security scanning is only affordable if the model doing it is cheap enough to run continuously, and Microsoft is pricing MAI Cyber 2 Flash for exactly that — frontier-level vulnerability discovery at a small fraction of a general model's cost per task. If your organization runs Microsoft security products, the upgrade arrives inside MDASH automatically from mid-December, so your team gets stronger scans at a lower token cost without a procurement cycle; and specialist models like this are how smaller security teams cover more code, more often.
