Meta has introduced Muse Ember, its smallest and most efficient AI model, claiming it delivers better performance per dollar than OpenAI’s GPT-6 Luna and Anthropic’s Claude Haiku 5.5 across several reasoning and agent-based tasks.

The new model focuses on complex reasoning and repetitive AI agent operations, including coding, tool calls, task routing, information extraction, and processing lengthy documents.

According to Meta, Muse Ember delivers performance comparable to Claude Haiku 5.5 at roughly one-third the cost in certain configurations, while outperforming GPT-6 Luna across the tested reasoning settings.

## Performance and Benchmarks

Meta says Muse Ember sits on the efficiency frontier of the Artificial Analysis leaderboard, meaning no tested model achieves a higher intelligence score at a lower cost per task.

At maximum reasoning effort, Muse Ember scored 36 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, matching Claude Haiku 5.5 and exceeding GPT-6 Luna’s score of 28.

The model also performed well in agent-based tasks, scoring 37.8% on AutomationBench-AA, compared with 20.2% for GPT-6 Luna and 13.9% for Claude Haiku 5.5.

The following table compares the models across major benchmarks at their maximum reasoning settings.

| Benchmark | Muse Ember | GPT-6 Luna | Claude Haiku 5.5 | 
|---|---|---|---|
| Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index | 36 | 28 | 36 | 
| AutomationBench-AA | 37.8% | 20.2% | 13.9% | 
| Agents’ Last Exam | 61.6% | 57.1% | 57.0% | 
| Personal Superintelligence Index | 46.8% | 37.0% | 36.1% | 
| Terminal-Bench 4.0 | 40.9% | 16.4% | 39.2% | 
| Coding Agent Index-AA | 46 | 31 | 37 | 
| FrontierCode 2.0-AA | 27.2% | 25.2% | 34.9% | 
| GDP.pdf-AA | 22.8% | 22.8% | 20.8% | 
| OSWorld 2.1 | 69.5% | 50.5% | 68.9% | 
| PerceptionBench | 80.6% | 62.8% | 78.6% | 

Bold figures indicate the highest score in each benchmark. Results come from Artificial Analysis and Meta’s internal evaluations. Some tests use different evaluation setups.

Muse Ember scored 40.9% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, ahead of Claude Haiku 5.5 (39.2%) and GPT-6 Luna (16.4%).

However, Claude Haiku 5.5 performed better on FrontierCode 2.0-AA, scoring 34.9% compared with Muse Ember’s 27.2%.

## Features and Capabilities

Muse Ember supports text, images, audio, and video, allowing it to analyze screenshots, charts, scanned documents, recordings, and other content.

The model features a 1-million-token context window and supports outputs of up to 512,000 tokens, allowing it to process large codebases and document collections.

It can also perform computer-based tasks, review contracts, analyze documents, run coding tests, and complete workflows involving multiple connected applications.

Meta says developers can pair Muse Ember with Muse Wonder, using the larger model for complex planning while Ember handles repetitive tasks.

## Pricing and Availability

Muse Ember is available through Muse Code and the Meta Model API under the name `muse-ember-1.0`.

Meta has announced the following pricing:

| Pricing Category | Cost | 
|---|---|
| Input tokens (1 million) | $0.10 | 
| Cached input tokens (1 million) | $0.004 | 
| Output tokens (1 million) | $0.50 | 
| Estimated cost per Artificial Analysis task | $0.14 | 

According to Meta, Muse Ember costs approximately $0.14 per Artificial Analysis task, compared with $2.39 for Muse Spark 1.3.

Despite the difference in cost, the two models scored 36 and 37, respectively, on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.

## Muse Ember vs. Muse Spark 1.3

Muse Ember is designed for repetitive coding and agent-based tasks, while Muse Spark 1.3 targets more demanding coding and agentic workflows.

| Feature | Muse Ember | Muse Spark 1.3 | 
|---|---|---|
| Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index | 36 | 37 | 
| Cost per task | $0.14 | $2.39 | 
| Input price (1M tokens) | $0.10 | $1.25 | 
| Output price (1M tokens) | $0.50 | $4.25 | 

The figures show that Muse Ember achieves a similar intelligence score at a substantially lower estimated cost per task.

## Safety and Future Plans

Meta says it evaluated Muse Ember under its Superintelligence Scaling Framework, covering cybersecurity, chemical and biological risks, and potential loss-of-control scenarios.

The company reported that the model remained below its high-risk thresholds in the categories tested.

Meta also assessed whether Muse Ember follows user instructions, protects sensitive information, resists malicious instructions embedded in external content, and requests human approval before high-impact actions.

Muse Ember is the first model Meta trained using Muse Wonder Full. It joins Muse Spark and Muse Wonder in the company’s AI model lineup.

Meta says all three models are now available on Google Cloud and Oracle Cloud, with additional efficiency-focused models currently in development.
