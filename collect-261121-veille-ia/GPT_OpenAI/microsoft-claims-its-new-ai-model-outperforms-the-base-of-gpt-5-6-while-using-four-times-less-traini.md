Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman said the new base model MAI-Base-2 surpasses the model underlying GPT-5.6, with pre-training consuming only a quarter of the compute OpenAI needed.


Image by Gemini for abit.ee

The bold claim came at an all-hands meeting. According to Mustafa Suleyman, Microsoft now has a pre-trained base model more powerful than the one behind GPT-5.6, and it cost a quarter of the compute that OpenAI spent.

What is pre-training? It is the opening stage, in which a model hunts for patterns across huge volumes of text, images and other data. It demands enormous computing power and is expensive. If Microsoft has genuinely made the process more efficient, its costs should fall noticeably.

The new model is called MAI-Base-2. It was tested against OpenAI systems, including the one codenamed Spud, which powers GPT-5.6. Suleyman says MAI-Base-2 scored better on tests measuring how well an AI understands and predicts patterns in data. One caveat matters: the comparisons rely on technical information about OpenAI models available to Microsoft, not on an independent audit.

Chinese rivals got a mention too. Suleyman says the model is 12 times more economical than DeepSeek models, though he did not name a specific system. Microsoft's blog refers to DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base.

The awkward part lies elsewhere. Microsoft has put billions of dollars into OpenAI, and its stake in the maker of ChatGPT probably exceeds $100 billion. Criticising a partner could dent the value of that investment. Yet the two are closely tied: Microsoft gives Azure customers access to OpenAI models and uses them in Copilot. Over the past year, however, the relationship has cooled noticeably, and Microsoft increasingly bets on its own models. In March it merged its Copilot teams, and Suleyman took charge of AI development.
