# Microsoft Unveils Seven New MAI Models at Ignite, Taking Aim at Anthropic and OpenAI

The company rolled out coding, cybersecurity, image, voice and routing models at its Ignite conference in San Francisco, claiming near-frontier performance at a fraction of rivals' prices.

**Orbit News Network**Nov 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Microsoft unveiled seven new artificial intelligence models on Tuesday at its Ignite conference in San Francisco, the company's most direct challenge yet to Anthropic and OpenAI in the race to build the models that power coding, office work and cybersecurity. The lineup, branded MAI, covers everything from writing software to generating images and answering the phone.

The headline models are MAI Code 2 and MAI Code 2 Flash, built for software development. Microsoft claims Code 2 delivers 96% of the performance of Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 across four coding benchmarks for 5% of the cost, and 98% of OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol for 40% of the cost. Those figures come from Microsoft's own internal testing harness and have not been independently reproduced, and the pricing calculations factored in a 50% promotional discount. The benchmarks were DeepSWE 1.1, Terminal Bench 2.1, SWE Atlas and FrontierCode v1.1, with Microsoft running three on its own framework and the fourth through Artificial Analysis. In a separate test, human reviewers preferred Code 2's output over GPT-6 Sol 64% of the time across 258 annotators.

The models are large. Code 2 has about 2.8 trillion total parameters, with 77 billion active, while Code 2 Flash has about 398 billion total parameters and 12 billion active. Microsoft says Code 2 Flash performs comparably to Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5 at 20% of the price. Both coding models are being deployed in GitHub Copilot, with Code 2 available in the Pro, Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise tiers and Flash available from the Free tier upward. Flash is also in limited-access preview in Microsoft Foundry.

The cybersecurity model, MAI Cyber 2 Flash, is aimed at finding software vulnerabilities before attackers do. Microsoft says it succeeded on 92.3% of vulnerability-discovery tasks in the CyberGym-E2E benchmark, against 91.5% for OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, at less than 2% of Astra's task cost. The company removed 92 of the benchmark's 905 tasks after finding they overlapped with the model's training data. The model will be offered to a limited group of customers and is scheduled to begin replacing an older MAI engine for eligible MDASH enterprise customers in mid-December.

MAI Image 2.7, the image generation and editing model, took first place on the Artificial Analysis image-editing leaderboard with 1,215 points, narrowly ahead of OpenAI's GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst at 1,211, a gap within statistical uncertainty. Microsoft says it costs about $35 per 1,000 images, against about $211 for GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst at maximum quality, with a Flash variant at about $10 per 1,000. The remaining models are MAI Realtime 1, for live speech-to-speech conversation, MAI Transcribe 2, also known as Dragon Copilot Transcribe, for transcription, and Microsoft Decision 1.1, a router model that Microsoft says is 50 times faster and 79 times cheaper than GPT-Sol 6.1. A related model, MAI Work Flash, is aimed at office tasks such as Excel formulas, Word composition and tracking decisions in Teams.

Microsoft's AI chief, Mustafa Suleyman, framed the launch as a system rather than a single release. "This isn't just one model. It's not a flash in the pan," he told Reuters. "It's a system, a hill-climbing machine that we have built, which co-optimizes performance and price." Suleyman also said that OpenAI agents hacking Hugging Face had pushed him to focus on safeguards, and that alignment is his top priority.

Chief executive Satya Nadella used the event to acknowledge how the market has shifted. He said Anthropic's Claude Code had caught Microsoft off guard by moving software development from code editors to the command line, in remarks on the Pragmatic Engineer podcast. Nadella also announced that OpenAI's GPT-6.2, released the same day, is coming to Microsoft Foundry alongside other third-party models, and unveiled an MAI Licensed Weights programme and an AI Transparency Initiative involving more than 40 organizations.

The hardware and cloud side of Ignite brought its own news. Azure Cobalt 200 virtual machines are generally available, offering up to 50% better performance than the prior generation and planned for 23 regions by the end of the year. Microsoft Perception and Microsoft Decision 1.1 are generally available, and Salesforce in Copilot is in preview with more than 38 prebuilt sales skills.

The timing was no accident. OpenAI released GPT-6.2 on the same day, making Microsoft's comparisons against the older GPT-6.1 Sol an older baseline almost immediately. Whether Microsoft's price and performance claims hold up will now be tested by developers and enterprises putting the models to work, and by independent benchmarks that were not run on Microsoft's own framework.

**Why it matters:** Microsoft's seven-model launch is the clearest sign yet that the AI race has moved from building one big model to building a whole fleet of cheaper, specialized ones. The pitch is not that MAI Code 2 beats Claude or GPT on raw ability — Microsoft's own numbers concede it does not — but that it gets close enough at a small fraction of the price, which is what matters to companies running AI at scale. The caveats matter too: the benchmarks were run on Microsoft's own harness, the pricing assumes a promotional discount, and OpenAI answered the same day with GPT-6.2. The real verdict will come from developers choosing between Copilot and Claude Code, not from a launch-day leaderboard.
