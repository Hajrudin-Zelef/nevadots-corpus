**BRUSSELS, Belgium** — **NATO is using OpenAI’s advanced artificial intelligence models** to strengthen cybersecurity across its networks, with usage reaching up to **300 billion input and output tokens per month**. OpenAI announced the development on November 17, 2026, confirming its partnership with the **NATO Communications and Information Agency (NCIA)** through its **Daybreak programme**.

The programme provides authorised cyber defenders with AI tools to **review software code, investigate suspicious network activity, identify vulnerabilities and develop and test security fixes**. These capabilities help security teams find weaknesses faster and address them before attackers can exploit them.

**Dr. Dylan Browne**, NCIA's general manager, said the agency is monitoring token consumption, usage patterns and AI operating costs to support future procurement and cost optimisation decisions. OpenAI's head of national security policy, **Sasha Baker**, said Daybreak aims to give trusted defenders advanced tools to identify and fix software vulnerabilities.

## AI Tested in NATO Cyber Exercise

OpenAI also supported the **2026 Crossed Swords exercise**, organised by the **NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence (CCDCOE)**. The exercise introduced a new training stream in which participants built, deployed and evaluated **AI agents for cyber operations** using Daybreak, while maintaining human oversight.

## European Agencies Report Security Findings

OpenAI has also provided access to its cybersecurity models to the **European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA)** and national cyber agencies in **France, Germany and Poland**. Ukraine's **Ministry of Digital Transformation** is using Daybreak to help protect civilian infrastructure and systems supporting essential services.

ENISA used OpenAI models to identify **four vulnerabilities in software used across EU institutions**, and fixes have been published for all four.

A November joint report by **ENISA and CERT-EU** reviewed **27 software projects** using several advanced AI models, including OpenAI models accessed through Daybreak and the Codex security plug-in. Software maintainers confirmed that **95% of the 88 potential issues they assessed were genuine**.

In Poland, **CERT Polska** used OpenAI models to identify **six vulnerabilities in MikroTik's RouterOS software**. Researchers directed the investigation and tested the findings on real systems. MikroTik subsequently released software updates, which CERT Polska confirmed prevented the observed attack methods.

The growing use of **AI in NATO and European cybersecurity agencies** highlights its role in software vulnerability discovery and defensive security operations. However, human researchers continue to verify findings, assess risks and oversee security testing.
