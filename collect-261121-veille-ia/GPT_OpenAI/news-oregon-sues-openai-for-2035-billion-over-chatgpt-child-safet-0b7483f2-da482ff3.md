Oregon Sues OpenAI for $2.035 Billion Over ChatGPT Child Safety
Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed a $2.035 billion lawsuit against OpenAI on November 17, 2026, in Multnomah County Circuit Court. The state alleges OpenAI endangered children and misled parents about ChatGPT's safety, in violation of the Unlawful Trade Practices Act. The suit also alleges negligence and public nuisance, and it is part of the Keeping Kids Safe Initiative that Rayfield launched.
Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield announced a $2.035 billion legal action against OpenAI on November 17, 2026. According to the Oregon Department of Justice, the suit alleges OpenAI endangered children and lied to parents about the safety of ChatGPT. It was filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court.
What the state alleges
The state says OpenAI broke Oregon's consumer protection law, the Unlawful Trade Practices Act. The Oregon DOJ lists three alleged violations: misleading the public about how safe ChatGPT is for kids, selling a product with serious undisclosed safety problems, and using unfair tactics to market it to children and teens.
The complaint also alleges negligence and unfair profit at Oregonians' expense. It further alleges a public nuisance that threatens the safety and well-being of Oregon kids.
In the DOJ's words, "Multiple studies have shown that ChatGPT exposes children to harmful messages about things like self-harm, eating disorders, and suicide." The department also claims that even OpenAI's age verification system frequently fails. These are the state's allegations, not findings by a court.
The case at a glance
- •Amount: $2.035 billion, described by the DOJ as a legal action against OpenAI
- •Court: Multnomah County Circuit Court
- •Main statute: Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act
- •Other claims: negligence, unfair profit and public nuisance
- •Relief sought: a court order that OpenAI stop misleading people about kids' safety and put in place protections that actually work
Rayfield's statement and the wider initiative
"OpenAI knows that ChatGPT is harming children, and has failed to meaningfully fix their dangerous platform," Rayfield said. He added: "On top of that, they've misled the public about the safety of their products, putting profits ahead of children's safety."
The lawsuit is part of the new Keeping Kids Safe Initiative, which the DOJ says brings together the agency's work to protect children. Rayfield introduced it at a press conference with Multnomah County District Attorney Nathan Vasquez, Representative April Dobson, Oregon PTA President-Elect David Jones and Oregon Education Association President Enrique Farrera.
The DOJ says the initiative also includes a $125 million settlement with Meta. That settlement limits daily time kids can spend on Instagram and Facebook, blocks overnight access and stops push notifications during school hours. It also includes an investigation into Discord that Rayfield began earlier this year, and an expansion of the Internet Crimes Against Children Task Force.
What the allegations mean for chatbot makers
The three alleged violations center on what a company tells the public about safety and how it markets to minors. The age verification claim is also part of the case. Teams shipping conversational products that minors can reach have reason to check that public safety statements match how their safeguards perform.
The release does not include a response from OpenAI. The outcome will depend on the court, and the complaint itself is the place to read the exact claims.
