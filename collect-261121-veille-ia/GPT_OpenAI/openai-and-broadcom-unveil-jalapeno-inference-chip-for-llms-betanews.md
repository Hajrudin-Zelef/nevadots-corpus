OpenAI and Broadcom (NASDAQ: AVGO) unveiled Jalapeño on June 24, 2026, introducing OpenAI’s first Intelligence Processor, a custom accelerator built around large language model inference. The companies announced the chip from San Francisco and Palo Alto, California, calling it the first AI accelerator in a multi-generation compute platform they are building together.

Jalapeño is designed to make advanced AI faster, more reliable, and more accessible through hardware built for inference, the stage where models generate responses for users and applications. OpenAI said the chip expands its platform from products and models into silicon, adding hardware design to the systems behind ChatGPT, Codex, the API, and future agentic products.

The chip was delivered to OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and President Greg Brockman by Broadcom President and CEO Hock Tan and Semiconductor Solutions President Charlie Kawwas. The handoff introduced the first generation of a platform designed for initial deployment by the end of 2026 and expansion in the years that follow.

OpenAI designed Jalapeño from scratch around its understanding of LLM fundamentals, drawing from its model roadmap, kernels, serving systems, and product needs. Broadcom is contributing chip implementation, networking, and connectivity technologies, while Celestica is supporting board, rack, and system integration.

Engineering samples of Jalapeño are already running machine-learning workloads in the lab at production target frequency and power. OpenAI said those workloads include GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark, while final performance measurements are still underway.

Early testing shows Jalapeño will deliver performance per watt substantially better than current state-of-the-art, according to the companies. A detailed technical report on performance is expected in the coming months.

The architecture is built to reduce data movement while balancing compute, memory, and networking resources. OpenAI said the design is intended to bring realized utilization much closer to theoretical peak performance.

Broadcom’s silicon implementation and networking technologies, including Tomahawk networking silicon, are being used to help bring the platform to large-scale production. The work connects Jalapeño to the type of AI infrastructure needed for large model serving across data centers.

Greg Brockman, OpenAI president and co-founder, said, “The world is moving to a compute-powered economy.” He described Jalapeño as a long-term infrastructure project meant to make compute more abundant and AI faster, more reliable, and more affordable for people and businesses.

Richard Ho, who leads OpenAI’s hardware program, said, “Jalapeño was designed from the ground up for LLM inference using detailed insights from our close collaboration with OpenAI researchers.” He said the architecture was optimized around kernels, memory movement, networking, and serving patterns used by frontier AI models.

Hock Tan, Broadcom president and CEO, said, “This is just the beginning of a multi-generation roadmap.” He said the companies are co-developing silicon directly with OpenAI to enable gigawatt scale data centers with Microsoft and other partners beginning in 2026.

Jalapeño is described as a blank-slate design for modern LLM inference, not a general-purpose accelerator adapted from earlier AI workloads. The companies said it is built for current and future LLMs across the industry, using OpenAI’s views on inference needs for present and upcoming AI models.

The design goal is to combine the power and throughput of current leading AI accelerators with latency closer to the fastest specialized inference systems. OpenAI said that combination makes Jalapeño suited for interactive LLM products running at scale.

The chip was co-developed from initial design to manufacturing tape-out in nine months. OpenAI said the custom AI accelerator program may represent the fastest ASIC development cycle achieved in high-performance advanced semiconductors.

The nine-month timeline involved software-hardware co-development between OpenAI’s engineering teams and Broadcom’s silicon implementation teams. OpenAI also used its own models to accelerate parts of the design and optimization process.

The same models used by customers are now helping improve the infrastructure used to run future models. OpenAI said this loop could reduce compute costs across the industry if AI helps engineers design better chips faster.

Jalapeño follows the October 13, 2025, collaboration between OpenAI and Broadcom for 10 gigawatts of custom AI accelerators. Under that agreement, OpenAI designs the accelerators and systems, while Broadcom helps develop and deploy the hardware.

The 2025 agreement also covers accelerator and network systems for next-generation AI clusters. Broadcom said the racks use Ethernet and other connectivity technologies and are targeted to start deployment in the second half of 2026, with completion planned by the end of 2029.

Those racks are planned for OpenAI facilities and partner data centers. The companies said they have long-standing agreements on co-development and supply of the AI accelerators, along with a term sheet to deploy racks that include OpenAI-designed accelerators and Broadcom networking systems.

The racks include Broadcom’s Ethernet, PCIe, and optical connectivity technologies. Broadcom said the collaboration supports the use of custom accelerators and Ethernet for scale-up and scale-out networking in AI data centers.

OpenAI said it has grown to more than 800 million weekly active users, with adoption across global enterprises, small businesses, and developers. The company tied the custom chip work to demand for dependable compute behind products used by students, developers, businesses, researchers, and enterprises.

Jalapeño also arrives as AI companies continue to invest in custom chips, data center capacity, and supplier partnerships. Recent data center power demand deals show how large model providers and platform companies are seeking more compute capacity for training and inference workloads.

OpenAI and Broadcom have not released full benchmark results, pricing, or customer availability plans for Jalapeño. The companies said more technical performance details will be presented after final measurements are completed.
