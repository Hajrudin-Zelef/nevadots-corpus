OpenAI has formed a partnership with NATO to deploy advanced AI models for cyber defense, assisting in vulnerability detection, threat response, and operational readiness. The initiative aims to empower defenders with tools to identify vulnerabilities, verify patches, and protect critical systems in real time. As part of the partnership, OpenAI is collaborating with NATO’s Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) to provide access to its advanced cybersecurity models.

These tools are designed to enhance the protection of allied networks, which are essential for the security and operational capabilities of NATO member states. According to OpenAI, NATO has used up to 300 billion input and output tokens per month to support these efforts. Dr. Dylan Brown, director general of NCIA, highlighted the transformative potential of AI in cybersecurity, stating: “AI‑based cyber‑defense models represent a generational leap in cybersecurity. They accelerate vulnerability detection, identify new threats at machine speed, and enable defenders to out‑pace adversaries.” He also emphasized the importance of scalability to optimize costs and evaluate competing solutions.

OpenAI’s cybersecurity tools were showcased during the Crossed Swords 2026 exercise, organized by the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence (CCDCOE). This year’s exercise introduced a special training program in which participants created, deployed, and evaluated AI agents for cyber operations.

The training focused on human control in integrating AI agents into operational processes. More than 250 participants from over 40 countries took part in the exercise, which combined cyber operations with kinetic military scenarios. Saša Baker, head of OpenAI’s national security policy, said: “With Daybreak, we place more efficient tools in the hands of NATO defenders, helping them find and fix vulnerabilities before malicious actors can exploit them.”

Beyond NATO, OpenAI has provided access to its cybersecurity models to other European organizations, including the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and national cyber agencies in France, Germany, and Poland. These efforts align with NATO’s broader strategy to adopt responsible AI principles and improve interoperability among its member states.

By leveraging AI technologies, NATO and its allies aim to shift from reactive defense to proactive resilience, ensuring the security of critical digital infrastructure at unprecedented speed and scale.
