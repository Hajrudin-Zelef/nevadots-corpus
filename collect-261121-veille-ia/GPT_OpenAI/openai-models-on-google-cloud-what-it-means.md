OpenAI Models Are Coming to Google Cloud: Why Rivals Are Now Sharing a Shelf

A Nerd @ Net Nerds

Oct 21

3 min read

Two of the biggest rivals in artificial intelligence just agreed to share a shelf. On October 21, 2026, Google Cloud announced that it is launching OpenAI models in its Model Garden, the catalog where Google Cloud customers pick the AI models they want to build with. OpenAI published its own announcement the same day. It is a surprising move, and it tells us a lot about where business AI is heading.

What was announced

In a post on the Google Cloud blog, Michael Gerstenhaber, a VP of Product Management for Gemini at Google Cloud, said Google Cloud is now the only hyperscale cloud provider offering access to all three leading closed source language models. Alongside those, the platform offers more than 200 first party, third party and open models on one enterprise AI platform.

Three OpenAI models are involved. GPT-6 Astra is designed for demanding work across coding, computer use, professional tasks and scientific research. GPT-6.1 Sol offers near Astra intelligence at lower cost across agentic coding, computer use and professional work. GPT-6 Luna is a fast, cost efficient model for everyday work at scale.

Who can use them, and when

This is not a general release yet. Google says the OpenAI models are rolling out now in private preview to select enterprise customers, with more coming soon. Starting today, those customers can build with the models through Google's APIs and connect their local Codex command line and desktop workflows directly to Google's Gemini platform for inference. Google also says OpenAI models will soon be available in its Gemini for business product and in Google Antigravity.

If you are a small business owner who simply uses ChatGPT or Gemini in a browser, nothing changes for you today. This news is aimed at companies and developers who build their own tools on cloud platforms.

Why this matters: choice without lock-in

Google's pitch is choice. Modern enterprise AI, it argues, means matching each job to the right model instead of relying on a single model for everything. Customers can switch between models based on performance, speed and cost, without separate vendor contracts. Because the OpenAI models are sold through Google Cloud Marketplace, usage appears on a single Google Cloud invoice and counts toward existing cloud spending commitments.

Google also highlighted data protection. It says customer prompts, context and outputs stay inside the customer's designated data perimeter and are never used to train foundation models. It lists zero data retention options as coming soon, and says OpenAI models run on dedicated Google Cloud infrastructure, with Confidential Compute also coming soon.

The bigger picture

For years, each cloud company leaned on its favorite AI partner. This announcement shows that large customers are demanding flexibility, and the providers are responding. A company that wants Gemini for one task and a GPT model for another can now do that in one place. Even competitors are willing to cooperate when customers insist on choice.

It also shows how quickly the model landscape moves. Names like Astra, Sol and Luna did not exist in most conversations a few months ago, and the pecking order changes with every release.

What this means for small business owners

You may never log into a cloud model catalog, but the effects reach you. More competition tends to push prices down and quality up. Software vendors you already use, such as accounting tools, customer support platforms and marketing apps, will be able to switch to better models faster. That means the AI features inside your everyday software can improve without you doing anything.

Keep one practical habit: ask your software vendors which AI models they use and where your data goes. Good vendors can answer clearly. Also resist the urge to chase every new model name. For most small businesses, a reliable assistant used consistently beats the newest model used rarely.
