OpenAI has struck a partnership with NATO’s communications arm to supply frontier cybersecurity models to the alliance’s core institutions, an arrangement under which NATO has already consumed as much as 300 billion raw input and output tokens a month.

The company said it is working with the NATO Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) to give defenders access to its advanced models for authorized security work, including reviewing software, investigating suspicious activity, validating vulnerabilities and building and testing fixes. The tools are meant to protect Allied networks that underpin member states’ security and operational capabilities.

Dylan Browne, the NCIA’s general manager, framed the shift in sweeping terms. “AI-powered cyber defence models represent a generational leap in cyber security,” he said, adding that they speed up vulnerability discovery, spot emerging threats at machine speed and let defenders stay ahead of adversaries.

Browne also pointed to the procurement math behind the program, saying the alliance intends to deepen its grasp of token consumption, usage patterns and the underlying economics of the models. That insight, he said, will help NATO optimize costs, compare competing offerings and make informed purchasing decisions.

OpenAI’s national security policy chief, Sasha Baker, said the defenders of democratic societies ought to have the most advanced technology available. “Through Daybreak, we’re putting more capable tools in [NATO defenders’] hands to help them find and fix vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them,” she said.

Daybreak is the name OpenAI uses for the program that channels its models to government and national cyber agency defenders.

AI agents join the drills

The tools also featured in the 2026 Crossed Swords exercise run by the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence (CCDCOE). This year’s edition added a training stream in which participants built, deployed and evaluated AI agents for cyber operations inside the technical environment, with operators working alongside the agents and an emphasis on keeping humans in the loop. More than 250 participants from over 40 countries took part, in an exercise that blended cyber operations with kinetic military scenarios.

Beyond the alliance itself, OpenAI said it has extended model access to European defenders, including the EU Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and national agencies in France (ANSSI), Germany and Poland. Ukraine’s Ministry of Digital Transformation is using Daybreak to harden the cyber defense of civilian infrastructure, aiming to help teams find software flaws and develop and test fixes faster while essential services face sustained attacks.

Some of the early results are concrete. ENISA used the models to identify four vulnerabilities in software used across EU institutions, and fixes were published for all four. A November joint report from ENISA and CERT-EU described a review of 27 software projects using frontier models, including OpenAI models accessed through Daybreak and tools such as the Codex security plug-in. Software maintainers confirmed 95% of the 88 potential issues assessed as genuine, according to the report.

CERT Polska, meanwhile, used OpenAI models to surface six vulnerabilities in MikroTik RouterOS and coordinated disclosure with the vendor. Researchers directed the work, tested findings on live systems and verified impact, with AI speeding up analysis and the exploration of potential weaknesses. MikroTik released fixes that CERT Polska confirmed blocked the attacks it had observed.

From reacting to pre-empting

The arrangement fits NATO’s broader push to adopt responsible AI principles and improve interoperability among member states. OpenAI said it remains committed to those principles and to delivering safe, secure tools.

The alliance’s ambition is to move from reactive defense toward proactive resilience, securing critical digital infrastructure at a scale and speed that manual review cannot match. For AI vendors, the program offers a template for government procurement of frontier models, with token economics and head-to-head evaluation now part of the buying decision. For network operators, the disclosed vulnerability finds and vendor fixes give a concrete record of where AI-assisted review is already paying off.

OpenAI said it will keep working with governments, national cyber agencies and international partners to convert frontier capability into more resilient systems.
