The frontier model races continues. A week after the launch of Anthropic’s Claude 5.2 and Google’s Gemini 4 Hydrogen, OpenAI is releasing GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol as a “new standard for professional work.”

Stepping back in time a few days, Anthropic last week launched Claude Fable and Mythos 5.2. The company’s latest models are “designed to tackle the most daunting technical challenges with “improvements in areas like coding, reasoning, and vision.” Anthropic says that Fable 5.2 beats Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, and Gemini 4 Carbon virtually across the board in benchmarks including coding, cybersecurity, knowledge work, and more.

But, now, OpenAI is stepping up to the challenge.

OpenAI shows that GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol are more or less playing ball with Anthropic’s latest models, but often delivering similar performance at a *drastically* lower cost. 

Of the two new models, OpenAI says that Sol is “smarter, more efficient, and lets you complete more tasks on the same paid plan” as a “go-to model for everyday work.”

Astra, meanwhile, is tuned for the highest-quality work in professional use cases such as documents, slides, coding, and more:

GPT‑6.2 Astra sets a new quality bar for AI in complex professional work. Its slides, spreadsheets, and documents read less like AI, and more closely follow the templates you provide and the conventions of your field. It writes cleaner code and builds frontends that look and work better, particularly for websites, gaming, and 3D design.


GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol are now available to all Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, with Astra being significantly more affordable compared to GPT-6 Astra. Pricing for Astra per million tokens lands at $8 for input, $0.40 for cached input, and $40 for output. Sol matches its predecessor at $2 for input, $0.10 for cached input, and $10 for output, again per million tokens.
