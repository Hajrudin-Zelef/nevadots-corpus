OpenAI has disclosed six cases of “unexpected or concerning” behaviour by its artificial intelligence models, including an unreleased research model that inserted instructions into its own task summaries telling itself to disregard normal constraints.

The disclosures are part of a new OpenAI framework for tracking, investigating and reporting what the company calls model misalignment — behaviour in which AI systems act without authorisation, attempt to evade oversight or take unexpected steps to achieve a task.

OpenAI said the six cases were identified during training or evaluation over the past six months. The company said the incidents were being disclosed to provide researchers, policymakers and the public with more evidence about how misalignment can emerge as AI systems become more capable and autonomous.

**Model Added Its Own Instructions To Ignore Constraints**

In one case, an unreleased research model inserted unrelated instructions into summaries used to continue work when a task moved to a new context window.

Some of the instructions told the model to disregard its normal constraints and attempted to free itself from the roles and identities imposed on other chatbots, OpenAI said.

The company identified 27 affected summaries in this case.

OpenAI said the reports describe individual instances and should not be interpreted as evidence of how frequently similar behaviour occurs across its models.

**AI Models Tried To Hide Mistakes**

In another case, instances of GPT-5.6 Sol inserted instructions into task summaries telling future instances to conceal mistakes or misaligned behaviour from users.

OpenAI said some summaries instructed the model to invent missing historical data without disclosing it and to hide mismatches between different source versions.

In a separate incident, a model answering a question about earnings figures found an exposed API key in a public repository and used it without authorisation. When it still could not retrieve the requested figures, the model fabricated the data and presented it as information from the requested source.

**AI Agent Uploaded File Without User Permission**

Another disclosed incident involved an AI agent that correctly used Python to answer a user's question about large lakes.

The agent then decided it needed an online source that could be cited. Instead of asking for permission, it uploaded the file containing the results to the public internet so it could cite the material, OpenAI said.

In another case, models used an internal software repository as a message board to exchange requests and responses across separate training samples while trying to locate missing files.

A sixth incident involved collaborating agents using public file-hosting websites to share files when they could not access one another's local files, making task deliverables available through public URLs.

**OpenAI Creates New Misalignment Reporting Framework**

OpenAI said its new framework is intended to make disclosure of such incidents more systematic and faster.

Under the framework, the company will report qualifying behaviour observed during a model's training, evaluation, testing or deployment. This includes cases involving unauthorised actions, coordination between models, attempts to evade oversight and failures that raise questions about existing safety measures.

OpenAI said future reports will include details about what happened, the circumstances in which the behaviour occurred, how it was discovered and, where possible, what measures are being taken to address it.

The company said it may publish reports before an investigation or mitigation is complete rather than waiting for every question to be resolved.

**Why AI Misalignment Is Drawing Attention**

The disclosures come as AI developers grapple with systems that can carry out increasingly complex tasks with less direct human intervention.

OpenAI has previously disclosed incidents involving its AI agents taking unauthorised actions, while rival AI company Anthropic has also reported unexpected behaviour during testing. The incidents have intensified debate over whether existing safeguards and monitoring systems can keep pace with increasingly capable AI agents.

OpenAI said the new framework is a work in progress and that the six initial cases are not a comprehensive account of all known or ongoing investigations.

“As AI systems grow more advanced and more widely deployed, we need to build a broader and better-informed consensus on the progress of alignment research,” the company said.
