# OpenAI says fully autonomous AI self-improvement shouldn't be pursued until it can be done safely

The same post wants the US government to run the international side, a week after Sam Altman said OpenAI wouldn't wait for it.

OpenAI published Building standards for the next phase of AI on September 21, 2026, under its Global Affairs heading. It asks the United States to lead an effort with other countries to write global technical standards for frontier AI, and it wants those standards to cover AI that builds AI.

Fully autonomous recursive self-improvement isn't happening today, the post says, and shouldn't be pursued until it can be done safely. The first of the three goals at the top of the same page is building an automated AI researcher, and the two sit about a screen apart (OpenAI shortens the mouthful to RSI, and so does everything below).

- What
- Global technical standards for frontier AI, including for RSI
- Who should lead
- The United States
- Through
- The emerging network of AI safety institutes, the CAISI and national industry bodies
- Not
- Licenses, mandatory prerelease review, or approval requirements for AI models

## What OpenAI wants written down

The mechanism runs through AI safety institutes that governments have already built, with the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation and national industry bodies doing the standard setting. Ten countries get named as having one: Australia, Canada, Germany, France, Kenya, Japan, Korea, Singapore, India and the United Kingdom. China isn't among them.

The standards themselves would measure how much of a lab's AI research is being done autonomously, and would say when an automated research process has to stop for a human to look at it. A third would set common severity levels and reporting thresholds for alignment incidents. OpenAI offers its own misalignment reporting framework as an early contribution to that one (it came with six reports of misaligned behavior on September 16, 2026, and we went through them).

## Where the phrase came from

Pacing the frontier is Dario Amodei's phrase. It comes from an essay on his own site that asks the labs to slow down and to give outside evaluators desks and badges inside their companies, and we wrote that one up when it went out. OpenAI keeps the verb and hands it a definition of its own: keeping alignment research, and the deployment of that research, ahead of capabilities.

The international half is the piece Sam Altman had already marked as his government's job, a week before this. He posted it on September 14, 2026.

## What OpenAI leaves open

OpenAI doesn't give a date for any of it. It doesn't say what should happen to a lab that skips the standards once they exist either, and the closest it comes is a line about how any lab doing automated AI research "must take accountability for doing so safely, in accordance with basic principles of self-responsibility and existing laws."

The only thing here that sits on a calendar belongs to somebody else. OpenAI says dialogue between the United States and China on these questions would be a positive step, and that upcoming talks are happening at an opportune time.
