NATO has used up to 300 billion raw tokens monthly across inputs and outputs in cybersecurity work with OpenAI, the company said on November 17. OpenAI works with NATO’s Communications and Information Agency (NCIA) to give the alliance’s core institutions access to models for defending their networks. European agencies have also used its models to uncover software vulnerabilities for which fixes have been released.

OpenAI’s Daybreak initiative supports authorized defensive tasks, including examining software, investigating suspicious behavior and checking whether suspected vulnerabilities are real. It also supports creating fixes and testing whether they work. The NATO usage figure measures raw input and output tokens, rather than a count of vulnerabilities found or attacks prevented.

Among the concrete results, OpenAI says ENISA identified four vulnerabilities affecting software used by EU institutions with help from its models. All four now have published fixes. Separately, CERT Polska used OpenAI models while finding six vulnerabilities affecting MikroTik RouterOS, then worked with the vendor on disclosure.

Researchers guided the RouterOS investigation, checked findings against actual systems and established their impact, according to OpenAI. MikroTik issued fixes that CERT Polska verified blocked the attacks observed during that work. OpenAI says AI helped speed the researchers’ analysis and investigation of possible flaws.

A joint November report by ENISA and CERT-EU describes examining 27 software projects with frontier models, including OpenAI models provided through Daybreak, alongside tools such as the Codex security plug-in. Across all models evaluated, maintainers confirmed 95% of 88 assessed potential issues as genuine. OpenAI has also given cyber model access to national agencies in Germany and Poland, as well as France’s ANSSI.

The work follows OpenAI’s June release of GPT-5.5-Cyber, which was designed to validate vulnerabilities and produce patches for human review. That earlier expansion also introduced Patch the Planet with Trail of Bits and HackerOne to support open-source software maintainers.

Ukraine’s Ministry of Digital Transformation is using Daybreak for civilian infrastructure defense, with the aim of helping teams find flaws and prepare fixes faster amid continuing cyber attacks. OpenAI also supported the 2026 Crossed Swords exercise, organized by the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence (CCDCOE). A new training stream had participants build, deploy and evaluate agents for cyber operations using Daybreak, while emphasizing human oversight.
