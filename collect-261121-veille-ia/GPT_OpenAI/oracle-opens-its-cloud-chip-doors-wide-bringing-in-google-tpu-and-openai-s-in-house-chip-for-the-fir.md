# Oracle Opens Its Cloud Chip Doors Wide, Bringing In Google TPU and OpenAI's In-House Chip for the First Time

Oracle announced Tuesday at its Oracle AI World conference in Las Vegas that its cloud infrastructure unit (OCI) will for the first time include Google's tensor processing units (TPUs), while also bringing in OpenAI's self-developed Jalapeño chip and hardware from Cerebras Systems, giving customers more accelerator choices beyond Nvidia GPUs. It marks the first time Google's custom chips will run on another hyperscale cloud provider's platform.

Mahesh Thiagarajan, president of Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, said during the conference that tight chip supply remains a challenge, and letting customers choose from different accelerators is key to expanding AI computing capacity. He stressed that this does not mean Oracle is turning away from Nvidia, whose chips remain the "best and most general-purpose" option, and that the AI market is large enough to accommodate multiple architectures.

At the same conference, Oracle also announced an expansion of its partnership with OpenAI, integrating ChatGPT and Codex directly into its own applications. Thiagarajan said that through ChatGPT, "anyone can build an application, deploy it, and manage it." Oracle's database has long supported the data-record and security needs of the healthcare and financial industries, and the partnership effectively opens a new channel for OpenAI into the enterprise market. OpenAI President and co-founder Greg Brockman said in a news release that as the company makes AI easier to deploy through the tools and systems enterprises already use, more and more businesses are choosing OpenAI.

Notably, Thiagarajan disclosed that his team witnessed firsthand OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra model completing training at the Stargate data center campus in Abilene, Texas, and was able to see the benefits in real time.

On Cerebras, the two companies said in a joint statement the same day that OCI will begin deploying cloud services based on Cerebras computers in the first half of 2027. Cerebras's design approach differs from traditional GPUs, replacing multiple processors with a giant chip that occupies an entire silicon wafer and touts the ability to produce AI query results faster. After the announcement, Cerebras shares rose about 10% at one point, while Oracle shares also climbed nearly 5% that day. However, Cerebras has still fallen 41% cumulatively since its listing in May through Monday's close.

A Cerebras spokesperson called it a major partnership but declined to disclose its scale due to a quiet period. Oracle likewise did not say how many Cerebras products it will use. Seven months ago, Amazon's AWS became the first to announce it would bring Cerebras hardware into its data centers, a move seen at the time as important validation for the challenger.

Here is the lineup of AI hardware Oracle is adding or expanding on OCI:

| Chip/Platform | Supplier | Status | 
|---|---|---|
| Google TPU | Google, under Alphabet | First entry onto a non-Google hyperscale cloud platform | 
| Jalapeño chip | OpenAI | Being brought onto OCI | 
| Cerebras computers | Cerebras Systems | Deployment from the first half of 2027 | 
| Vera Rubin NVL72, Vera CPU | Nvidia | Now generally available on OCI | 
| HGX Rubin NVL8 | Nvidia | Planned for introduction, x86 architecture with eight-GPU configuration | 

Note: Vera Rubin-related systems will scale to millions of kilowatts in the form of OCI Superclusters; the HGX Rubin NVL8 carries two 64-core Intel Xeon 6 CPUs, 4TB of DDR5 memory and a 6.4 Tbps high-speed RDMA network.

On the Nvidia partnership, Oracle and Nvidia plan to scale OCI Superclusters equipped with Vera Rubin NVL72 systems to millions of kilowatts to meet rising demand for AI training, inference and agentic applications. Each rack connects 72 GPUs via NVLink and then scales horizontally to hundreds of thousands of GPUs through OCI's multi-plane network. Oracle said customers can scale CPU and GPU resources separately according to workload, reducing idle computing power and improving infrastructure efficiency.

Amid the infrastructure expansion, Oracle's multibillion-dollar Project Jupiter data center in Sunland Park, New Mexico, still faces local opposition. Thiagarajan noted that the project is just one of 30 to 40 data center projects the company has under development each quarter. "We're actually not too worried, because we build global infrastructure," he said. The campus, already under construction, is scheduled to hold an air-quality permit hearing on December 7; political headwinds prompted Oracle last month to send a force majeure notice to financial backer Blue Owl Capital, but the company still maintains that construction is proceeding as planned.

Market attention now turns to Oracle's investor day. Investors will examine whether the company can deliver on its fiscal 2030 revenue target and whether rising capital costs are eroding cloud contract margins. Bank of America analysts said in a note to clients that they look forward to further details on the progress of major data center projects. Oracle shares are down more than 30% this year, partly reflecting market concerns over how it will finance its massive digital infrastructure investment.

Overall, Oracle is bringing multiple chip suppliers onto its cloud platform at once, responding to customers' hunger for computing power while positioning itself as an AI infrastructure provider that is "not tied to a single chip architecture." For Google, the first external supply of TPUs is a milestone validating the strength of its custom chips; for OpenAI and Cerebras, it means gaining another large outlet in a market dominated by Nvidia.
