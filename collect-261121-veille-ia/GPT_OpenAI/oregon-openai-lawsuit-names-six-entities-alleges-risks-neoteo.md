Oregon filed a lawsuit against six OpenAI entities in Multnomah County Circuit Court on November 17, 2026. The state alleges that ChatGPT’s design, marketing and operation put minors at risk and mislead parents. Those claims are allegations, not court findings.

## Which OpenAI entities did Oregon name?

The State of Oregon, acting through Attorney General Dan Rayfield, named six defendants in its complaint:

- OpenAI Global, LLC
- OpenAI Foundation
- OpenAI OpCo, LLC
- OpenAI Group PBC
- OpenAI Holdings, LLC
- OpenAI, LLC

ChatGPT is the product at the center of Oregon’s allegations; the complaint names these six entities as defendants.

The complaint’s six defendants are separate legal entities; ChatGPT is the product at the center of the state’s allegations.

## What does Oregon allege, and what does it ask for?

The complaint lists claims under Oregon’s Unlawful Trade Practices Act, negligence, unjust enrichment and public nuisance. Oregon alleges that ChatGPT’s design and marketing endanger minors and mislead parents. The state also says the case concerns safety problems and marketing to children and teens.

Oregon’s Department of Justice says the state is seeking effective protections for children and an end to misleading claims about safety. The department described the lawsuit as part of its Keeping Kids Safe Initiative, announced alongside the filing. These claims and requests are the state’s position in the case.

## What amount did Oregon state, and how did OpenAI respond?

The Oregon Department of Justice described the legal action as $2.035 billion. That is the state’s stated figure for the action.

OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the company had protections for teenagers and was working with experts to strengthen them. He also said OpenAI supported practical AI policies that apply across the industry, according to The Oregonian/OregonLive.
