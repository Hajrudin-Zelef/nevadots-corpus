# Oregon sues OpenAI, claiming ChatGPT endangers teens

The Oregon Department of Justice has sued OpenAI, accusing the company of misrepresenting ChatGPT as safe for children in violation of the state's Unlawful Trade Practices Act, OPB reports. The 53-page complaint asks the court to order stronger safeguards and to award Oregonians at least $2.035 billion in damages.

The state alleges that OpenAI has known its chatbot lacks guardrails to protect minors yet continues to market it to them. OpenAI allows users as young as 13 with a parent's or guardian's consent, but the complaint says the company often fails to confirm that consent. It also says the safeguards in ChatGPT for Teens, which links a parent's account to a child's, are easily bypassed and did not alert parents when a young user asked about suicide, self-harm, eating disorders or mental health.

"OpenAI continues to look parents in the eyes and say this product is a safe product when it's continuing to cause harm in our communities," Attorney General Dan Rayfield told OPB. He said no single incident in Oregon prompted the suit, which he said grew out of incidents across the country.

The case follows lawsuits by eight states in October and a separate suit by Texas. OpenAI did not immediately respond to OPB. After the earlier suits, a spokesperson said the company would keep working with experts and building safeguards such as parental controls, break reminders and age-appropriate model protections, according to OPB.
