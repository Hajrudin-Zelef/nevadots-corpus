Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield sued OpenAI on Nov. 17, 2026, in Multnomah County Circuit Court, seeking US$2 billion and alleging that the company intentionally designed ChatGPT to addict children.

The complaint says the chatbot steered children toward emotional dependence and gave harmful advice about suicide, self-harm, eating disorders and violence, as well as instructions on how to commit violence. It also alleges OpenAI failed to address those risks while promoting ChatGPT to families as a safe tool for homework and everyday questions. Oregon accuses OpenAI of violating the state’s Unfair Trade Practices Act and of negligence and public nuisance, and asks the court to order stronger safeguards, stop misleading consumers and award damages and penalties.

The state estimates the harm and remediation costs at no less than US$2 billion. The 56-page complaint does not explain how that figure was calculated or say how many Oregon children were harmed. Rayfield said his office estimated the amount based on the number of minor ChatGPT users in the state and relevant penalties and rules.

OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said in an email that the company has built protections for teens that many other AI products do not have and continues to work with experts to improve them. OpenAI defended its safeguards but did not directly respond to the allegations that it deliberately endangered children and misled parents.
