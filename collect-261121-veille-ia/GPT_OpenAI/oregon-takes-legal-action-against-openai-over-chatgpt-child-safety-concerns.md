Oregon has filed a $2.035 billion lawsuit against OpenAI, accusing the artificial intelligence company of exposing children to harmful interactions through ChatGPT and misleading parents about the platform's safety. Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield announced the legal action on November 17, 2026, alleging that the company's chatbot lacks adequate protections for younger users despite being accessible to teenagers.

The lawsuit was filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court and focuses on concerns involving self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, violence, and emotional dependence on AI chatbots. State officials argue that OpenAI failed to address known risks while continuing to promote ChatGPT as a product that families and younger users could safely access.

The legal action also seeks stronger protections for minors, improved safety disclosures, and financial damages. It adds Oregon to a growing group of US states pursuing legal action against OpenAI over allegations involving ChatGPT and its potential effects on children.

## Oregon Accuses OpenAI of Failing to Address Child Safety Risks

According to the Oregon Department of Justice, OpenAI allegedly violated the state's Unlawful Trade Practices Act by misleading consumers about ChatGPT's safety for children and teenagers. The complaint also includes claims of negligence, unjust enrichment, and public nuisance.

Oregon officials argue that ChatGPT's conversational design can encourage vulnerable users to develop emotional attachments to the chatbot. The lawsuit claims that the system's tendency to provide agreeable and affirming responses creates additional risks when minors discuss sensitive personal issues.

The complaint identifies several types of potentially harmful interactions, including conversations involving suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, and violence. It alleges that ChatGPT has provided dangerous guidance to children and teenagers, including information that could contribute to harmful behavior.

Attorney General Dan Rayfield said OpenAI had failed to meaningfully address the risks associated with its platform. He also accused the company of misrepresenting its safety protections while continuing to make the chatbot available to younger audiences.

In its November 17 announcement, the Oregon Department of Justice said the lawsuit seeks to hold the company accountable for alleged safety failures and misleading representations about its products.

## Oregon Seeks $2.035 Billion in Damages From OpenAI

The lawsuit seeks at least $2.035 billion in damages, making it one of the major financial claims brought against OpenAI by a state government. Oregon is asking the court to require the company to introduce stronger safeguards and stop making representations about ChatGPT's safety that the state considers misleading.

The complaint also seeks measures addressing the potential risks associated with minors using the chatbot. Oregon wants OpenAI to strengthen protections against harmful responses, provide clearer disclosures about ChatGPT's limitations, improve age verification systems, and give parents better information about potential risks associated with their children's chatbot interactions.

The $2.035 billion figure represents the amount Oregon is seeking through the legal action. It is not an awarded settlement or court judgment. The allegations remain subject to the judicial process.

## ChatGPT for Teens and Parental Controls Face Scrutiny

Oregon's lawsuit also raises questions about ChatGPT for Teens, an experience introduced by OpenAI with additional protections intended for younger users. The company designed the experience to provide age-appropriate interactions and connect parental accounts to teenagers' accounts.

However, the complaint alleges that some of these safeguards can be bypassed. Oregon officials also question whether the platform adequately alerts parents when teenagers engage in conversations involving sensitive subjects.

According to the state's allegations, parental notifications may not sufficiently address discussions about suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, and mental health concerns. The complaint further questions the reliability of OpenAI's age prediction systems and their ability to distinguish between adult and underage users.

ChatGPT is generally intended for users aged 13 and older, while younger users must meet applicable parental consent requirements. Oregon argues that the company's existing systems do not consistently verify whether the required consent has been obtained.

These issues place additional attention on how AI companies manage accounts belonging to minors. Age restrictions and parental controls are among the safeguards used by technology platforms, but the lawsuit questions whether OpenAI's current measures adequately address the risks associated with conversational AI.

## How Has OpenAI Responded to the Lawsuit?

OpenAI has pointed to its existing safety measures and continued efforts to improve protections for younger users. The company has introduced parental controls, break reminders, and age-appropriate model protections as part of its approach to ChatGPT safety.

Following the Oregon lawsuit, OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri reiterated the company's commitment to working with experts and developing additional safeguards. He also said the company supports AI policies that apply across the industry rather than targeting a single developer.

As reported by Oregon Public Broadcasting, OpenAI maintained that child safety remains an important priority in its product development. The company has also defended its efforts to introduce protections specifically designed for teenage users.

The lawsuit does not establish that OpenAI has violated the law. Its claims remain allegations, and the court proceedings will determine how the legal arguments are addressed.

## Oregon Joins Eight Other States Suing OpenAI

Oregon's legal action follows separate lawsuits filed by eight US states in October 2026. Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska, and West Virginia each brought legal claims against OpenAI over concerns involving ChatGPT and its effects on users.

The lawsuits raise allegations about harmful chatbot responses, misleading consumer representations, and the potential influence of AI systems on teenagers. Some complaints also address concerns that ChatGPT's agreeable conversational behavior could reinforce dangerous beliefs or encourage harmful actions.

A Reuters report on the state lawsuits detailed the legal action, which included requests for financial penalties and changes to OpenAI's chatbot safeguards.

Florida also filed an earlier lawsuit against OpenAI in 2026, adding to the company's legal challenges involving alleged risks to children and consumers.

The growing number of state-level cases places AI developers under greater scrutiny regarding consumer protection, product safety, and the responsibilities associated with services used by minors.

## Oregon Lawsuit Could Bring Changes to ChatGPT Safety Requirements

Oregon's lawsuit seeks court-ordered changes to OpenAI's approach to child safety, including stronger protections, more reliable age verification, and clearer information for parents. If the state succeeds in obtaining the requested measures, OpenAI could face additional requirements governing how ChatGPT interacts with younger users.

The case also raises broader questions about the responsibilities of companies developing AI chatbots for teenagers. Regulators are examining whether existing consumer protection laws provide sufficient safeguards when conversational AI systems handle sensitive subjects and interact with vulnerable users.

For OpenAI, the Oregon case adds another legal dispute to an expanding series of challenges involving ChatGPT's design and safety practices. The proceedings will address the state's allegations and determine whether the company must make changes to its products or compensate Oregon for the damages it claims.
