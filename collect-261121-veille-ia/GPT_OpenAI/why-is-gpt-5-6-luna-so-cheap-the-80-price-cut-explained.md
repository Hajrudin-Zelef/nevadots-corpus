# GPT-5.6 Luna Price Cut: What OpenAI's 80% Drop Means

OpenAI slashed Luna by 80% and Terra by 20% on July 30, 2026. Here is what changed and who should act.

On July 30, 2026, OpenAI slashed the price of GPT-5.6 Luna by about 80% and Terra by 20%. Luna input pricing dropped from $1.00 to $0.20 per million tokens, while output pricing fell from $6.00 to $1.20. Pricing for the flagship model, Sol, stayed the same that day. OpenAI has since lowered GPT-5.6 Sol to $4.00 input and $20.00 output.

OpenAI described the move as pushing the price-performance frontier forward. According to the company, Luna now matches models that were considered frontier-class a year ago, at roughly 6 cents on the dollar per task and nearly nine times the speed.

This page breaks down what changed, why it happened, and which high-volume workloads are now more affordable. It's designed to help buyers decide what to do next.

## GPT Luna Pricing and Model Choice

OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna's price by about 80% on July 30, 2026, bringing it to $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens. Our reading is that the cut was meant to compete with low-cost models such as Gemini Flash, Claude Haiku, GLM and DeepSeek; OpenAI has not stated that reason.

Cached input for GPT-5.6 Luna costs $0.02 per million tokens. GPT-6 Luna is cheaper still, at $0.10 for input and $0.50 for output per million tokens. See GPT-5.6 API pricing for the other GPT-5.6 rates.

GPT-5.6 Luna costs about one-tenth as much per token as GPT-5.6 Terra, which runs $2.00 for input and $12.00 for output per million tokens. Pick Luna for summarization, drafting, classification, routine automation or cheap multi-step agents. Terra is the balanced tier. For the hardest reasoning within GPT-5.6, pick Sol, priced at $4.00 for input and $20.00 for output per million tokens after its September 10 cut. GPT-6 Sol is cheaper still at $2.00 for input and $10.00 for output.

## What Changed on July 30, 2026

OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna by about 80% and GPT-5.6 Terra by about 20%, effective July 30, 2026. The flagship Sol tier was left unchanged.

Luna input dropped from $1.00 to $0.20 per million tokens. Luna output dropped from $6.00 to $1.20. That is the largest single price move in the GPT-5.6 family so far.

Terra input fell from $2.50 to $2.00, and Terra output fell from $15.00 to $12.00. Sol held at $5.00 input and $30.00 output on July 30, and OpenAI has since lowered GPT-5.6 Sol to $4.00 input and $20.00 output.

- Luna: the fastest, cheapest tier — now 80% cheaper.
- Terra: the balanced tier — now 20% cheaper.
- Sol: the flagship tier. Not cut on July 30, and lowered to $4.00 input and $20.00 output on September 10.

## Why OpenAI Cut the Price

OpenAI cut Luna and Terra to stay ahead in a fast-moving price war for cheap, capable models. Rivals have pushed low-cost frontier tiers hard all year.

The pressure comes from cheap tiers like Gemini Flash and Claude Haiku, plus open-weight models such as GLM and DeepSeek. These options made high-volume work cheaper than Luna was.

OpenAI calls this advancing the price-performance frontier. Its argument is that a task on Luna now costs a small fraction of what the same quality cost a year ago.

- Competitive pressure from Gemini Flash and Claude Haiku.
- Open-weight models like GLM and DeepSeek pushing prices down.
- OpenAI's goal: keep the cheapest quality tier on its own platform.

## OpenAI's Price-Performance Claims

OpenAI says Luna now delivers year-ago frontier quality for roughly 6 cents on the dollar per task, at nearly 9x the speed. These are OpenAI's own figures.

On Agents' Last Exam, OpenAI reports that Luna outperforms Claude Fable 5 at an estimated cost per task nearly 99% lower. That is a vendor benchmark, so read it with care.

We treat these as claims from the maker, not independent results. They are useful for direction, but you should test Luna on your own tasks before trusting the numbers.

- ~6 cents on the dollar per task versus year-ago frontier models.
- ~9x faster than that older frontier class.
- Beats Claude Fable 5 on Agents' Last Exam at ~99% lower cost per task (OpenAI's figure).

## Which Workloads This Unlocks

The cut makes high-volume, repetitive AI work economical that was borderline before. When each call costs less, you can run far more of them.

Luna is built for speed and scale: summarization, drafting, classification, and routine automation. At $0.20 input and $1.20 output, these jobs get much cheaper at volume.

It also lowers the cost of cheap agents that make many small model calls. Multi-step agents add up fast, so a lower per-token price changes what is affordable.

- High-volume text classification and tagging.
- Bulk drafting of emails, product copy, and replies.
- Summarizing large document sets and transcripts.
- Routine automation and cheap multi-step agents.

## The Honest Caveat: Sol Was Not Cut

The flagship Sol tier did not get cheaper on July 30. It held at $5.00 input and $30.00 output per million tokens, and has since dropped to $4.00 input and $20.00 output.

So the headline savings apply to lighter work, not the hardest reasoning. If your task needs Sol's max or ultra reasoning modes, the July 30 cut did not change your cost.

This matters for planning. Do not assume the whole GPT-5.6 family got cheaper on July 30. Only Luna and Terra did.

- Sol pricing was unchanged on July 30, 2026, and was lowered to $4.00 input and $20.00 output on September 10, 2026.
- Complex coding, science, and deep reasoning did not get cheaper on Sol in the July 30 cut.
- Savings are concentrated in high-volume, lower-complexity work.
