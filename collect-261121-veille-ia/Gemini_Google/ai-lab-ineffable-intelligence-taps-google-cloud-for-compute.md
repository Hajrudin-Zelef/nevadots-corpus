AI lab Ineffable Intelligence has selected Google Cloud as its preferred cloud partner.

Under the agreement, Ineffable will use Google Cloud compute capacity to train its "superlearner" - an artificial intelligence system that can theoretically learn continuously from its own experience.

Ineffable will deploy "one of the largest" clusters of A5X bare-metal instances, which are powered by the Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 GPUs.

Nvidia's Vera Rubin is the chip designer's latest generation of AI compute, with deployments beginning to roll out. Microsoft and CoreWeave have both claimed to be the first to deploy the system.

According to the company, the requirements of its superlearner differ significantly from AI models using static datasets, and the systems need to generate, evaluate, and then learn from experience in real time, which requires large-scale compute and high-performance networking.

The exact number of GPUs set to be deployed on Google Cloud by Ineffable has not been disclosed.

"Training frontier models requires more than just raw compute; it requires a sophisticated orchestration of hardware and software," said David Silver, CEO and founder of Ineffable Intelligence.

"We evaluated the space and chose Google Cloud as the best fit for our reinforcement learning infrastructure. We aren't just looking for processors; we are building a resilient and scalable environment to make 'first contact' with superintelligence, AI that transcends human limitations in science, mathematics, and technology."

"We are honored that the Ineffable Intelligence team has chosen Google Cloud to power its mission," added Thomas Kurian, CEO of Google Cloud.

"Ineffable is leveraging our full-stack AI Hypercomputer, from Jupiter networking to our optimized storage, to ensure its researchers can focus on breakthroughs, not bottlenecks. This partnership reflects how leading AI innovators are choosing Google Cloud to move faster and focus on revolutionary research and real-world impact."

Ineffable Intelligence is a UK-based AI company. In April 2026, it received backing from the UK government's Sovereign AI fund. The company raised $1.1 billion in its seed funding round.
