## Google DeepMind’s Gemini 4 Carbon: A New Frontier Model

**Gemini 4 Carbon**, Google DeepMind’s latest and most advanced model, is now live. Positioned as the company’s most capable offering to date, it builds on the Gemini 4 lineup, which also includes the earlier-released Gemini 4 Argon. While Carbon surpasses Argon in capability, Argon retains an advantage in speed. Carbon is designed for complex, multi-domain tasks, emphasizing precision, iterative improvement, and cost-effectiveness.

## Performance and Benchmarks

Gemini 4 Carbon has delivered strong results in key benchmarks. On **terminal bench 4.0**, which measures agentic coding in a terminal environment, Carbon achieved a score of 65.2%, outperforming both Opus 5.5 and GPT6 Astra, which scored around 59%. It also excelled in the automation bench for knowledge work, scoring approximately 54%, compared to the low 40s achieved by comparable models.

However, these achievements come with trade-offs. On the artificial analysis intelligence index, Carbon requires **26 minutes per task**, significantly longer than the median of 13 minutes. It also generates approximately 205 million tokens per task, nearly three times the median of 72 million tokens. These metrics suggest Carbon is better suited for tasks that can run autonomously rather than those requiring frequent interaction. Most benchmarks referenced were created prior to the release of Fable 5.2, which may influence direct comparisons.

## Capabilities and Applications

Google has identified four key areas where Gemini 4 Carbon excels:

- Multi-day agentic engineering: Tasks involving deeper reasoning, surgical code edits, verification loops, and cyber defense.
- High-precision knowledge work: Applications in finance, legal, healthcare, and exhaustive retrieval, with a claim of zero fabricated citations.
- Frontier discovery tasks: Fields such as physics, quantum computing, genomics, and drug discovery.
- Full-stack front-end and 3D web development: Realistic physics, interactive game mechanics, and self-verifying UIs.

In testing, the model demonstrated notable strengths in software development. It successfully created a native Mac Pomodoro app with features that went beyond the explicit requests, such as a menu bar controller and project tracking. The app was developed entirely in Swift and included a compiled version ready to run. While the testing environment had Xcode installed, it remains unclear whether the model used it for signing.

Similarly, Carbon developed a mashup app combining features from Bear, a note-taking app, and Obsidian, a “thinking linking” app. These projects highlighted Carbon’s ability to research, design, and deliver polished software solutions.

Carbon also showcased its capabilities in 3D rendering. It created a detailed interactive model of Marina Bay Sands in Singapore, including surrounding landmarks like the Apple store sphere and Louis Vuitton store pyramid. The task required minimal additional prompts and demonstrated the model’s iterative improvement, as it added details over time. Observations suggest Carbon self-verifies and refines its outputs during such tasks.

## Cost and Pricing

One of Carbon’s standout features is its affordability. On the artificial analysis intelligence index, it costs **just under $7.80 per task**, compared to $26.50 for Anthropic’s Fable 5.2, the current leader on the index. While Carbon scores five points lower than Fable 5.2, its cost is roughly a third, making it an appealing option for cost-conscious users.

Google has introduced an introductory price for Carbon at **$2 in and $10 out per million tokens**, matching Argon’s launch pricing. This represents a significant discount from the standard rate of $4 in and $20 out per million tokens. The introductory pricing is valid through November 30, 2026. Speculation suggests this pricing may be extended or that a newer model with similar pricing could be introduced.

## Comparisons and Limitations

Despite its strengths, Carbon is no longer the top model on the artificial analysis intelligence index. It initially held the number one spot with 60 points but was overtaken by Fable 5.2, which now leads with 65 points. While Fable 5.2 is considered the cutting edge for tasks Carbon can perform, Carbon’s affordability makes it a compelling alternative for many users.

Carbon’s slower processing time and higher token generation may limit its suitability for interactive tasks. However, its ability to self-verify and iteratively improve outputs offsets these drawbacks for long-running tasks. The model’s API has some restrictions, such as preset values for temperature, top P, and top K, though users can adjust the reasoning effort level. These preset parameters cannot be modified, which may limit customization for certain use cases.

Gemini 4 Argon, while faster, has notable weaknesses compared to Carbon. Testing revealed that Argon struggles with tool calling and tends to overthink tasks instead of using search effectively. Although Carbon is slower, it is described as newer and more capable. Both models are the same size, but Carbon’s introductory pricing and enhanced capabilities make it a more attractive option for many users.

## Implications for Software Development

Gemini 4 Carbon’s capabilities could influence software development practices. Developers at small and medium businesses may increasingly use such tools to replace high-cost SaaS solutions. The ability to research and replicate app features could also encourage more users to create and open source their own software. Models capable of researching apps via videos, screenshots, and descriptions may further support users in developing custom applications.

At the same time, the rise of models like Carbon may prompt software businesses to adapt. Developers might shift toward using “dumb” front-end interfaces with intelligence or complexity hosted in the cloud to prevent cloning. This approach could become a standard strategy for protecting proprietary software. Small software houses are expected to continue adding value by building polished apps and addressing user needs effectively, keeping software businesses relevant.

Gemini 4 Carbon represents a significant advancement in AI capabilities, particularly for software development and high-precision tasks. While it no longer holds the top spot on the artificial analysis intelligence index, its affordability and strengths in iterative improvement make it a valuable tool for many users. For those prioritizing cost over peak performance, Carbon offers a compelling alternative to higher-priced models like Fable 5.2.
