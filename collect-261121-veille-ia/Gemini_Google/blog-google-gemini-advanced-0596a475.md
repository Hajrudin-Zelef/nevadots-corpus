Google now delivers it through two plans, Google AI Pro and Google AI Ultra. The free version already handles a lot. So the real question is what an upgrade adds for your work.

What is Google Gemini Advanced?
Let’s clear up the naming first, because most write-ups get it wrong. Gemini Advanced launched in February 2024 as Google’s paid Gemini tier.
At Google I/O in May 2025, Google renamed and replaced it with Google AI Pro. You can read the details in Google’s I/O 2025 announcement. Today, Google Gemini Advanced means the paid Gemini experience sold through Google AI Pro and Google AI Ultra.
That paid experience runs on Google’s current flagship model, Gemini 3.1 Pro. It offers a 1M-token context window for long documents and large projects. Here is the short history: Bard became Gemini, then Gemini Advanced and now Google AI Pro.
The paid tier mainly helps by reducing interruptions during longer tasks. On the free version, the assistant can stop mid-task, drop to a lighter model or cap how many files you add at once.
So who actually pays? People who hit the free-tier limits regularly are more likely to benefit. That includes heavy researchers, people working across long documents and daily users of Gemini in Gmail, Docs and Sheets.
If you use Gemini once or twice a week, you probably do not need to pay. If it sits at the center of your workflow, the upgrade removes the walls you keep hitting.
The free tier is more capable than many people expect. It runs a fast model, handles everyday questions and now includes Deep Research and Gemini Live. So the upgrade question is really about volume and depth, not access to a locked-off product.
Google Gemini Advanced features and capabilities
Paying for Google Gemini Advanced changes what the assistant can do. It also raises how much you can do before you hit a limit. These are the features you would use day to day.
We have grouped them into three areas: research and long-context work, interactive help and assistance inside your Google apps. Each behaves differently on the free and paid tiers, so we flag that as we go.
1. Deep Research and the 1M-token context window
These two capabilities do the heavy lifting for most professional users. Deep Research browses the web for you. It then writes a structured report with citations you can check, and you can turn it into an audio overview.
Here is a concrete example: You could ask Deep Research to compare four project management tools on price, integrations and team size. It works through the sources, then hands you a cited brief with links you can open. That saves the hours you would otherwise spend opening tabs and stitching notes together.
Deep Research suits work you would otherwise dread. Think of a market scan, a literature review or a vendor shortlist. You get a first draft with sources in minutes, then refine it yourself.
The 1M-token context window is the amount of text Gemini can hold in a single prompt. With Google AI Pro, it can work across long documents, large codebases and multi-file prompts without losing earlier details.
In plain terms, that window holds a few hundred pages, or a large batch of files, at once. You could paste a 200-page policy document and ask about a clause on page 180. Gemini still has the earlier pages in view.
This matters most for long, connected work. A researcher can hold a full report in one thread. A developer can share a large codebase and ask about how the pieces fit.
Smaller windows force you to break work into chunks and re-explain the background each time. The larger window removes that extra chunking and repeated re-explaining.
The free tier includes Deep Research at lower usage limits. A paid plan mainly gives you more of it plus the larger context window.
2. Gemini Live, Canvas and multimodal help
Beyond research, Gemini has features built for back-and-forth work. Gemini Live is a conversational mode. You talk to Gemini using voice, your camera or a shared screen.
It helps when you are working through a problem out loud. For instance, you could point your phone camera at a broken appliance. Then ask Gemini Live what the part is called and how to reset it.
Canvas is a side-by-side workspace for drafting and editing writing or code. You revise in place instead of copying text back and forth.
Say you are drafting a landing page. You keep the copy on one side and ask Gemini to shorten the headline or change the tone. The draft updates without losing your place.
Gemini is also multimodal. You can give it text, images, audio and video. It can also generate images and video with credits on paid tiers.
A multimodal prompt can be simple. You could upload a photo of a nutrition label and ask Gemini to flag anything high in sodium. You could also upload a PDF invoice and ask for the total and due date.
These features shine on quick, real-world tasks. You can talk through a recipe, draft a reply in Canvas or check a contract clause from a photo. Each one saves a step you would normally do by hand.
Paid plans also add the Jules coding agent in beta. It can take on coding tasks in the background. Like Deep Research, Gemini Live is on the free tier at lower limits, so paying mainly raises how much you use it.
3. Gemini across your Google apps
For many people, the biggest practical gain is help inside the apps they already use. Paid plans put Gemini inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Meet and Drive. It can draft emails, summarize threads, analyze data and pull together slides.
A few everyday examples show how this lands. In Gmail, you can ask Gemini to summarize a 30-message thread into three bullets before a meeting.
In Docs, you can ask it to draft a first pass of a proposal from a few notes. Then you edit from there instead of staring at a blank page. In Sheets, you can point it at a column of sales figures and ask for a chart.
The same help reaches your other apps. In Slides, Gemini can draft a deck outline or generate images for a layout. In Meet, it can take notes and write a recap so you stay in the conversation.

Gemini Advanced vs free Gemini: what upgrading adds
The gap between free and paid is smaller than it used to be. So it helps to compare the parts that affect real work. The main criteria are the model, the context size, how often you hit limits and storage.
Here is how the free tier lines up against Google Gemini Advanced, delivered through Google AI Pro.
| Criterion | Free Gemini | Gemini Advanced (Google AI Pro) | 
|---|---|---|
| Default model | Gemini 3.6 Flash, with varying access to 3.1 Pro | Gemini 3.1 Pro | 
| Context window | Smaller | Up to 1M tokens | 
| Usage limits | Baseline, compute-based | Higher, compute-based | 
| Deep Research and Gemini Live | Included at lower limits | Included at higher limits | 
| Storage | 15 GB | 5 TB | 
| Extras | Core chat and file uploads | Jules coding agent (beta), Google Flow Credits and full Gemini in Google apps | 
The takeaway: Upgrading gives you the stronger default model, a much larger context window, higher limits and Gemini inside your Google apps. The free tier still includes many of the same core features.
Storage is easy to overlook. The free 15 GB fills up fast if you use Gmail, Drive and Photos. Paid plans jump to 5 TB or more, which removes that worry for most users.
The people who feel the limits first are heavy daily users. That includes researchers running back-to-back reports and writers feeding in long drafts. If Gemini switches to a lighter model or asks you to wait, you have found the ceiling the paid tier lifts.
A simple test helps. Watch for the moments Gemini slows down, shortens answers or asks you to try later. If that happens often in a week, the paid limits are likely worth it.
Many people also mix tiers over time. You might start free, upgrade during a busy project and reassess later. Google lets you change plans, so the choice is not permanent.

Pricing is where old articles trip people up. Google offers four US consumer tiers, and you can confirm current figures on Google’s official plans page.
| Plan | Monthly price (US) | Storage | Best for | 
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 15 GB | Trying Gemini and light everyday use | 
| Google AI Plus | $4.99 | 400 GB | Casual users who want more room and higher limits | 
| Google AI Pro | $19.99 | 5 TB | Professionals and students who want the full paid experience | 
| Google AI Ultra | From $99.99 | From 20 TB | Power users, heavy creators and developers | 
In this guide, we use Gemini Advanced to refer mainly to Google AI Pro, which costs $19.99 per month. AI Plus is a lower-cost step up, and AI Ultra targets the heaviest workloads.
Each tier targets a different need. AI Plus adds room and higher limits for casual users. AI Pro gives professionals the full model and app integration.
AI Ultra is built for the heaviest work. It suits frequent video generation, large coding jobs and big data analysis.
Usage limits are compute-based rather than a fixed daily message count. Your allowance refreshes roughly every five hours toward a weekly cap. Heavier features like video generation use it up faster.
Google AI Pro vs Google AI Ultra: which plan fits?
If you have decided to pay, the choice comes down to how hard you push the tools. It is less about named features and more about headroom.
- Storage: Google AI Pro includes 5 TB, while Google AI Ultra starts at 20 TB.
- Usage limits: AI Ultra starts at $99.99 per month for 5x limits, or $199.99 per month for 20x limits.
- Google Flow Credits: AI Pro includes 1,000 credits for video generation. AI Ultra includes 10,000 to 25,000.
- Ultra-only tools: AI Ultra adds Deep Think for harder reasoning and Gemini Spark, available in select countries.
For many individuals, students and professionals, Google AI Pro can cover typical research, writing and spreadsheet work comfortably. Picture a graduate student on a thesis, or a solo consultant drafting proposals.
Google AI Ultra is a different profile. A creator making short videos every week uses Flow Credits fast. A developer running large jobs also keeps hitting the AI Pro ceiling.
Many people may not need Ultra, especially if they do not hit AI Pro limits regularly. Its limits and credits are aimed at daily creators and technical teams. If you are unsure, start on AI Pro and move up only if you keep hitting the ceiling.
Limits, accuracy and privacy to check first
Before you enter a card, a few points shape your experience and budget. They matter more than the feature list, so weigh them first.
- Usage is metered by compute, not a fixed daily message count, and your allowance refreshes roughly every five hours toward a weekly cap.
- Answers can be wrong or dated. Review Gemini’s output before you rely on it, especially for high-stakes decisions.
- Conversations may be retained. Avoid sharing confidential or sensitive information, and check Google’s privacy policy for how your data is used.
- Paid plans auto-renew. For a one-off project, set a reminder to cancel before the next charge.

FAQs
Is Gemini Advanced still called Gemini Advanced?
No, Google retired the Gemini Advanced name at Google I/O in May 2025. It now sells that paid experience as Google AI Pro and Google AI Ultra, though many people still search for the old name.
What are the different versions of Gemini?
Gemini comes in faster and more capable models. The free tier runs a Flash model built for speed, while paid plans use the Gemini 3.1 Pro model for harder reasoning. Google updates these models often, so the exact version names can change over time.
What is the context window difference between free Gemini and Gemini Advanced?
Paid Gemini through Google AI Pro runs on Gemini 3.1 Pro with a 1M-token context window. It can hold long documents and large projects in one prompt. The free tier defaults to Gemini 3.6 Flash with a smaller window and varying access to 3.1 Pro.
Can I get Gemini Advanced for free?
Partly. The free Gemini tier includes Deep Research and Gemini Live at lower usage limits. Eligible US college students can also get 12 months of Google AI Pro free, though we did not verify any broader free trial here.
Does Gemini Advanced work on desktop and offline?
Yes on desktop, no offline. You can use Gemini on the web, on Android and iOS or through the macOS and Windows desktop apps. All of these need an internet connection, because Gemini runs your prompts on Google’s servers.
