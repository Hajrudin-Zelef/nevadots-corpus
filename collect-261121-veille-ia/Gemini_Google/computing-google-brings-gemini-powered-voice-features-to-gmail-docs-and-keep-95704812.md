Google is making its Workspace apps a little more conversational. The company has officially launched three new voice-powered features for Gmail, Docs, and Keep, using its Gemini Audio models to let users search, write, and organize information simply by speaking. Google says the features are designed to make everyday tasks easier when typing isn’t practical.
The announcement comes from Yulie Kwon Kim, vice president of Product at Google Workspace, who said Google is bringing the Gemini Audio models to its Workspace products after first previewing them at Google I/O. The new tools are now rolling out this week, turning voice interaction from a way to dictate text into something closer to a conversation with an AI assistant.
Gmail can find answers while Docs helps you write
The most immediately useful addition could be Gmail Live, which lets users have a conversational search experience with their inbox. Instead of manually searching through long email threads, users can ask questions such as “What’s my flight’s gate number?” or “What is happening at my child’s school during the week?” Gmail Live then scans the inbox and provides an answer.
Google is also introducing Docs Live, which works more like a voice-based writing partner. Users can talk through an idea in real time, with the feature organizing those thoughts and helping turn them into a structured document. With the user’s permission, Docs Live can also pull relevant information from Gmail, Drive, Chat, and the wider web. Google says it can help with everything from brainstorming and outlining to refining a document’s tone.
That could be particularly useful for users who have ideas faster than they can type them. Rather than starting with a blank document, they can essentially talk their way toward a first draft.
Keep turns voice rambling into usable notes
Google Keep is getting a similar treatment with Keep Live. The feature is aimed at those moments when an idea arrives suddenly and typing it out feels like too much work.
Users can simply speak their thoughts, including a stream-of-consciousness “brain dump,” and Keep Live processes the input in the background, turning it into structured lists and actionable notes. Google says users won’t need to type a single word.
The rollout is already beginning. Gmail Live and Keep Live are available to Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers, while Docs Live is available to Pro and Ultra subscribers. Google says the features are also coming soon to Workspace business customers.
The bigger change isn’t simply that Google Workspace can now understand your voice. It is that Google is trying to make interacting with its apps feel less like operating software and more like having a conversation.
