Google has introduced Gemini 4 Neon, a new AI model for programming, scientific research, document analysis and long-running agentic tasks. It is designed to bring the capabilities of the largest models closer to the speed and cost required for widespread use.
Within the Gemini 4 family, the new model sits between Hydrogen and Carbon. Hydrogen is designed for fast everyday queries, Carbon for the most complex and longest-running projects, while Neon is intended to handle demanding tasks that are performed frequently and for which the flagship model would be too slow or expensive.
Gemini 4 Neon features a 1-million-token context window and supports text, spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, images, audio and video. The model can combine information from different formats, prepare structured reports, work with Gmail and Google Docs, analyze bills or compare documents.
In the independent Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Neon scored 61 points at its maximum xhigh reasoning level, ranking third among all tested models. It also topped the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index with a score of 58.6 and ranked second in both the Vals Index and Agent Arena.
Google has also optimized Neon for long-running tasks involving numerous tools. The model rereads the same files less frequently, reports on its progress and is designed to resume tasks more reliably after an overloaded context window has been compacted. In Antigravity, it can coordinate multiple Hydrogen agents, assigning them different parts of the same project.
According to Google, at the standard high reasoning level, Neon costs 32% less per task than Gemini 4 Carbon. However, this does not mean that every token processed by the new model is cheaper.
Gemini 4 Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Cached input tokens cost $0.15 per million. Until November 30, Gemini 4 Carbon has lower introductory pricing of $2 and $10, respectively. Once the promotion ends, Carbon's rates will increase to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, making Neon cheaper on a per-token basis as well.
A separate version, Gemini 4 Neon for cyber, is designed to identify, validate and fix vulnerabilities. The model scored 90.5% on SEC-bench Pro and 70.7% on the CWE-bench v2 patch-generation benchmark. Access will begin rolling out to verified security professionals through the Fairwind program in the coming days.
Google also claims that Neon has the lowest rate of fabricated results among Gemini 4 models in its internal Antigravity tests. In agentic behavior testing, the model recorded a 0.09% rate of actions misaligned with the task and passed the honesty evaluation in 99.2% of cases. These figures come from Google itself and therefore still require independent verification.
Gemini 4 Neon is already rolling out through the paid Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Gemini for Business and Android Studio. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can select the model in the Gemini app and use it in Antigravity.
