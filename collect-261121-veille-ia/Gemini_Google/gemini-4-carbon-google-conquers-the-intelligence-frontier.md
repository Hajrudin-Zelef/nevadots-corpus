Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon is the new number one on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5. Though there is some nuance, it is considered the most intelligent model.

Behind it on the intelligence benchmark now sit Anthropic’s most powerful model, Claude Opus 5.5 (max, with fallback), and OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra (max).

Before AI research, deployment, and interest exploded, Google was ahead of the curve in AI, with its efforts led by former Google DeepMind CEO and current Alphabet Chief Scientist, Demis Hassabis.

Its leading position in the field was overtaken when OpenAI and Anthropic continued to release ever more powerful models. Now, Google is staging a comeback at the frontier with its new efforts headed by Koray Kavukcuoglu, Senior Vice President of Google DeepMind.

Carbon shows the pace at which we’re pushing the frontier


## Gemini 4 Carbon

Artificial Analysis highlights that Gemini 4 Carbon scores 59 on Intelligence Index v5, driven by leading performance in complex reasoning, knowledge and scientific tasks.

The research firm notes that it makes large gains in coding and knowledge work, particularly when compared to other Gemini models, but it is not leading in all these areas.

Koray highlights that the model is designed to take on much harder, long-horizon engineering problems and sustained agentic workflows.

“Internally, our teams have seen Carbon run for days to persist through dead ends to tackle complex, multi-step engineering tasks like root-causing and patching subtle bugs across large codebases and low-level systems,” he adds.

Speaking on the Google for Developers podcast a month ago, Koray explained: “Our quality of current models [is] a little bit below the frontier and that’s fair enough.

“To put it very bluntly, there’s nothing other than being at the frontier that’s important for us.”

The pace at which it has pushed the intelligence benchmark is arguably unprecedented. But it doesn’t top every benchmark.

For a model designed for complex agentic workflows, the AA-Briefcase knowledge-work benchmark is a good indicator: Gemini 4 Carbon reached 1,609 Elo (+126 over Argon), but still trails SpaceXAI’s Grok 4.7, Meta’s Muse Wonder, and Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro.

Artificial Analysis adds that the model is somewhat expensive when compared to other models of similar capability.

The independent firm notes that the model is also faster than average, but very verbose. The model supports text, image, speech, and video input, outputs text and has a 1 million token context window.

Writing on LinkedIn, Alphabet and Google CEO Sundar Pichai said the new model “shows the pace at which we’re pushing the frontier”.

Gemini 4 Carbon is being rolled out to selected users and is not yet publicly available. Sundar added: “We'll be expanding it to more partners through our Fairwind Program, ahead of wider availability soon, so hang tight!”

- Gemini 4 Carbon tops the Intelligence Index v5 with a score of 59
- It ranks second on coding agents, trailing only Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5
- Carbon leads AA-Omniscience v1.1 while maintaining a low 11% hallucination rate
- Standard task costs run 23% cheaper than Claude Opus 5.5 at US$7.76
- It offers a 1M-token context window, multimodal inputs, and 50% launch discounts.

## Gemini 4 Argon

Google’s new model builds on the release of Gemini 4 Argon, which was announced only two weeks ago.

Benchmarking data from Artificial Analysis suggests that Gemini 4 Argon (High) is amongst the leading models in intelligence and reasonably priced when compared to other models of similar price.

It scored a 53 on the Intelligence Index, meaning it tied with OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra (running max, which also scores 53) and several Anthropic iterations such as Claude Fable 5.1 (xhigh with fallback, which also scores 53).

The only lab with models ahead on the specific benchmark is Anthropic; only various iterations of Anthropic’s Claude are a cut above. Now, Google’s latest model has taken the lead.

Gemini 4 Carbon is pushing cyber capabilities on Google’s previous release. On Google’s internal benchmarks, Carbon achieves more than 2x uplift in success rate for generating secure code over Argon.

As another example, the Chrome team reported that Carbon performed more than 10x better than "another lab's large frontier model" when generating high-quality patches.
