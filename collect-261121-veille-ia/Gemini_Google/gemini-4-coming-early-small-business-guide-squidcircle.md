This week, Google DeepMind’s new chief Koray Kavukcuoglu did something Google almost never does: he put a rough public timeline on an unreleased flagship model. Speaking at The Information’s AI Agenda Live Summit on September 23, he said Gemini 4 has entered early post-training and that he wants it shipped “much earlier” than the end of the year — as an early version that improves in public rather than a polished, stage-managed launch.

The Verge and The Decoder both reported the same framing, which is about as confirmed as anything gets before Google makes it official. No date, no benchmarks, no pricing. Just urgency.

For a small business, the immediate question isn’t “is Gemini 4 good?” It’s this: September alone has already delivered GPT-6 Astra, Claude updates, Gemini 3.8 Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, and a stack of Flash-and-compressed variants from every lab with a GPU budget. Enterprise buyers are openly talking about “model fatigue.” Another flagship landing ahead of schedule is one more thing to evaluate — and most small businesses have zero spare capacity to evaluate it.

Here’s the plan we run when a major model lands, distilled into something you can copy.

## Don’t Re-Platform for a Model. Re-Platform for a Workflow.

The single biggest mistake we see small businesses make is rebuilding their whole AI stack every time a new model tops a leaderboard. Gemini 4 may well leapfrog GPT-6 and Claude on some benchmarks — that’s exactly what the current price war is producing: a rotating champion every few weeks.

The fix is architectural. Keep your prompts, data, and business logic in a layer you own — a simple routing script, an automation platform, or an agent framework — and treat the model as a swappable component. When Gemini 4 ships, switching to it (or testing it) should be a config change, not a rebuild.

## Evaluate on Your Tasks, Not Their Benchmarks

Benchmarks measure what labs want measured. Your receptionist script, your proposal generator, and your lead-triage flow measure what actually pays you.

Before Gemini 4 arrives, do this now:

- **Build a 20-item eval set.** Real inputs from your business, with the outputs you’d consider good. It takes an afternoon.
- **Score your current model on it.** That’s your baseline — not a leaderboard.
- **When Gemini 4 drops, run the same 20 items.** Cost, speed, and quality side by side.

This is how we decide every model migration, and it has saved us from at least three “obvious upgrades” that turned out to be downgrades on our actual workloads.

## Early Release Means Rough Edges — Plan for Iteration

The most interesting part of Kavukcuoglu’s statement is the shape of the launch: ship an early post-training output, then iterate in public. That’s a departure from Google’s usual polished-launch playbook, and it means the Gemini 4 you can access in week one may be meaningfully worse than the one available in month two.

Practical implication: don’t migrate anything production-critical on day one. Run your eval set, watch early adopter reports for two weeks, and let the post-training iterations settle. The early-adopter tax is real — you pay in bugs and churn for access you didn’t need yet.

## Model Fatigue Is Now a Strategy Question

Most small businesses say AI is finally paying off — but the ones getting returns aren’t the ones trying every model. They’re the ones who picked two or three workflows, automated them well, and only change models when their own evals say it’s worth it.

The industry’s release cadence is now part of the story — arguably the story, given that half the lab leaders are simultaneously asking for a slowdown while shipping faster than ever. You can’t control the cadence. You can control whether it costs you anything.

## The Takeaway

Gemini 4 arriving early is good news: more competition, better prices, faster capability gains. But the winning small business move in late 2026 is boring on purpose — own your workflow layer, evaluate on your own data, and let the labs race each other for your business.
