Leading lab Google DeepMind recently introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen, which is amongst the leading models in intelligence while being reasonably priced compared to similar offerings.

It follows the release of Gemini 4 Carbon just a few weeks ago and Gemini 4 Argon, which came just a few weeks before that. While those models excel at deep, long-horizon reasoning on difficult problems, Hydrogen is built for rapid execution across diverse agentic workflows.

The lab is pumping out new models at a rate comparable to its competitors OpenAI and Anthropic, and is arguably at ‘the frontier’ together with its rival labs.

Gemini 4 Carbon held pole position on the Artificial Analysis Index for Intelligence for some time, before being knocked back off the top spot by Anthropic’s latest model, Claude Fable 5.2.

- 14.9 - Time per Intelligence Index task (minutes)
- 54 - Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index
- US$3.16 - Cost per Intelligence Index task
- 304mn - Output tokens from Intelligence Index
- 1mn - Context window.

## How does it hold up?

Speaking on the Google for Developers podcast two months ago about models, Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP of Google DeepMind and Google’s Chief AI Architect, said: “If I had a magic wand, I would make them more intelligent. When the models get more intelligent, they do everything better."

Koray, who heads up Google's AI work following former Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis' transition to Chair of Google DeepMind and Alphabet Chief Scientist, seems to have found his magic wand. Hydrogen surpasses Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default high thinking level (51 vs. 50, and reaching 54 at its highest xhigh thinking setting), while often completing tasks faster than models in its class. 

Gemini 4 Hydrogen is priced flat across its entire 1 million-token context window at US$1.50 per million input tokens and US$7.50 per million output tokens.

Artificial Analysis highlights that at 14.9 minutes per Intelligence Index task, Gemini 4 Hydrogen (Xhigh) is slower than average compared with the median of 13.3 minutes for comparable models. It also notes it’s very verbose.

Koray highlights that: “Beyond multi-model workflows, Hydrogen is a major standalone upgrade for teams migrating from Gemini 3.8 Flash.

“It delivers significant leaps in both speed and intelligence – finishing tasks 2.5 times faster at its high default setting while more than tripling Terminal-Bench 4.1 scores (57% vs. 18.2%), cutting fabricated claims by more than 5x, and adapting cleanly when a tool call fails or a search returns empty.

## Enterprise customers

Enterprise customers report positive results. Yashodha Bhavnani, VP of AI Products at Box, which connects enterprise files, documents, and workflows to LLMs, says: “In Box’s evaluations, Gemini 4 Hydrogen’s accuracy significantly improved when compared to Gemini 3.8 Flash, while running about twice as fast and using 12% fewer tokens.

“We saw the strongest gains in data-heavy workflows, where accurately reading tables and performing complex calculations is essential to producing reliable reports. These results demonstrate meaningful progress in the model’s ability to handle complex enterprise content with greater accuracy and efficiency.”

Hydrogen is available across the free Gemini app and Google Search, alongside Google AI Studio, Vertex AI, the Gemini API and Google Antigravity. Integration with Gemini in Google Sheets, however, requires a paid Google AI Pro or Ultra subscription.

In domain evaluations, Hydrogen ranks #1 overall on the Vals Tax Agent Bench (52.3%) and leads its class on HLE-Diamond (58.8%) and AutomationBench-AA.

It strengthens enterprise guardrails by cutting misaligned actions by 12x and boosting resilience to indirect prompt injections by more than 2.3x on the Gray Swan IPI benchmark.

## Multi-model collaboration

Google is also introducing Orchestrator in Google Antigravity. Rather than using a single model from start to finish, Orchestrator switches between models like Carbon and Hydrogen within the same session.

Google highlights that on Terminal-Bench 4.0, Orchestrator reached near-Carbon task success rates while, on average, consuming 24% less quota and completing 19% faster.

For workflows that require parallel agents, Carbon can act as the coordinator while Hydrogen can power worker and verifier agents to build and test changes simultaneously across isolated workspaces.
