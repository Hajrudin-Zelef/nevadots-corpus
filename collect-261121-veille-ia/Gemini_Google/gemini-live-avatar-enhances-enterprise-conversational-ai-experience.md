Google’s **Gemini** conversational AI just got a face. The company has rolled out **Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar**, a feature that layers near real-time video generation on top of its existing live dialogue models, giving enterprise chatbots something they’ve never had before: a visible, expressive presence that talks, listens, and reacts on screen as the conversation happens. The move builds directly on last week’s Gemini 3.8 Live launch and pushes Google’s assistant technology further into the realm of lifelike digital agents.

Summary

- Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar pairs streaming video with live dialogue to create a visible, animated presence during conversations.
- The feature supports 97 languages, adapting lip-sync and facial expressions on the fly without losing video quality.
- Live Avatar can run background tool calls, like fetching data, while dialogue keeps flowing uninterrupted.
- Enterprises can build custom avatars that preserve brand identity or likeness, though this option is currently limited to allowlisted accounts.
- Every AI-generated audio and video output carries a SynthID watermark, and the feature is live now inside Gemini Enterprise.

Live Avatar gives Gemini’s dialogue system a visual body, not just a voice. According to Google, the feature works by natively coupling the company’s existing live dialogue capabilities with low-latency streaming video, so the avatar generates near real-time facial movement and expression in sync with what it’s saying and hearing.

The result, Google says, is a conversational experience that “listens, sees, and speaks with a dynamic visual persona.” In practical terms, that means precise lip-syncing, natural facial expressions, and smooth turn-taking between the avatar and the person it’s talking to. For businesses, that translates into virtual agents that feel closer to a real interaction than a scripted chatbot exchange.

Shuo-yiin Chang, a Research Scientist on the project, and CJ Zheng, a Software Engineer speaking on behalf of the Gemini Audio Team, are credited with the launch. Google frames the underlying idea simply: conversation is inherently multimodal, built on listening, looking, and speaking at once, and Live Avatar is designed to bring all three into an enterprise setting simultaneously rather than handling audio and visuals as separate tracks.

This matters for any company weighing how customers interact with automated systems. A visual, expressive agent changes the emotional register of a digital interaction, whether it’s answering a support ticket or walking someone through a product demo. Google points to customer service and interactive walkthroughs as two use cases where that shift could be felt most directly.

One of the more striking capabilities is language range: Live Avatar works across **97 languages**, adjusting lip-sync and expressions in real time as a conversation shifts between them. Google says the multilingual speech-to-speech synchronization is native to the system, meaning the avatar can transition mid-conversation across languages without degrading video fidelity or introducing visual drift.

For global enterprises, that’s a meaningful detail. Deploying a single avatar that holds its visual quality across nearly a hundred languages removes a common bottleneck in scaling customer-facing AI internationally, rather than requiring separate builds or region-specific tuning.

Live Avatar is also built on Gemini’s broader reasoning capabilities, not just its visual layer. Through what Google calls **asynchronous tool calling**, the avatar can trigger tool calls and fetch data in the background while the dialogue with a user keeps running. Google’s own example involves checking in a hotel guest: the avatar continues talking naturally while retrieving the information it needs behind the scenes, rather than pausing the exchange to process the request.

That combination of visual presence and background task execution is where the feature moves beyond a novelty. An avatar that can multitask without breaking conversational flow is closer to a functional digital employee than a scripted animation, which is likely the distinction Google is aiming for with enterprise buyers.

Beyond a library of preset avatars, organizations can build their own. Starting from a high-quality reference image, developers can generate a fully animated, responsive avatar that preserves the reference likeness, brand styling, or character identity, as Google describes in its announcement. That opens the door for companies to deploy avatars that visually match an existing brand mascot, spokesperson, or character rather than relying on a generic digital face.

Custom avatar creation, however, is not open to everyone yet. Google specifies that this option is currently available only through **enterprise allowlisting**, meaning access is gated to approved organizational accounts rather than offered broadly at launch.

Every piece of audio and video the system generates carries a **SynthID watermark**. Google says this imperceptible marker is woven directly into both the audio and video output, helping keep AI-generated content detectable and reducing the risk of misinformation or misattribution. The company describes Live Avatar as built with strict safeguards designed to respect identity while keeping generated content transparent, and points to its model card for a fuller account of its safety approach.

Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar is available now inside **Gemini Enterprise**, with API documentation published for developers who want to start building with it. That places the feature squarely in the enterprise tier rather than a general consumer rollout, at least for now.

Why this matters for the wider AI landscape: Live Avatar signals a shift in how conversational agents are expected to present themselves. Text and voice interfaces have dominated enterprise AI deployment so far, but a visible, multilingual, brand-customizable avatar layered on top of Gemini’s reasoning stack pushes toward a more human-like default for customer-facing automation. Whether competitors follow with comparable video-dialogue integration, and how quickly custom avatar access expands beyond allowlisted enterprises, will shape how fast this format moves from a Gemini Enterprise feature into a broader industry standard.
