# Google announces 'Gemini 4 Neon,' combining cutting-edge inference capabilities with high-speed, low-cost processing to become the world's top processor for coding performance.


 On November 18, 2026, Google announced a new AI model called ' **Gemini 4 Neon** .' Developed based on the top-of-the-line Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Neon combines performance comparable to the most advanced AI models with high-speed and low-cost processing for advanced coding, data analysis, scientific research, and business process automation.

 **Introducing Gemini 4 Neon**

 

 Today we're introducing Gemini 4 Neon, bringing frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion across complex software engineering, knowledge work, and science workflows.

 Google offers two models in the Gemini 4 series: the Gemini 4 Carbon for particularly difficult and long-term tasks, and the Gemini 4 Hydrogen for quickly handling everyday tasks. The newly introduced Gemini 4 Neon is positioned between the two, and is said to be suitable for performing complex tasks that require advanced inference at high frequency.

 


 Gemini 4 Neon supports text, image, audio, and video input, and features a context window with a total of up to 1 million tokens for input and output. It can analyze large amounts of PDFs, spreadsheets, graphs, audio, and video data together and create deliverables such as reports. It supports output of up to 64,000 tokens in normal synchronous processing and up to 1 million tokens in background long-text generation.

 In Artificial Analysis's comprehensive benchmark 'Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index,' which independently evaluates AI performance, Gemini 4 Neon scored 61 points at the highest thinking level, 'xhigh.' This surpassed Claude Opus 5.5's 59 points and GPT-6.2 Sol's 57 points, ranking it 3rd among all models.

 Gemini 4 Neon offers four levels of thinking ability, allowing you to adjust reasoning power and token consumption according to the difficulty of the task. At the default thinking level, 'high,' the cost per task on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is 32% lower than that of Gemini 4 Carbon. Google also states that even at 'xhigh,' the cost per task is more than 36% lower than the highest thinking level of competing state-of-the-art models.

 


 In the 'Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index,' which evaluates coding performance, it achieved the top position among all models with a score of 58.6 points. In 'Terminal-Bench 4.1,' which evaluates the ability to perform tasks on the terminal, it scored 65.2%, in 'FrontierCode 2.0,' which evaluates advanced software development capabilities, it scored 46.2%, and in 'CursorBench 4.1,' which evaluates the performance of AI coding tools, it scored 65.4%.

 In 'SWE-sweep,' which evaluates the ability to modify existing code, it recorded 12.8% in the mini-SWE execution environment. In Google's agent-based development environment 'Google Antigravity,' it reached 39.0%, significantly exceeding the 10.1% achieved by Claude Opus 5.5 and 8.3% by GPT-6.2 Sol in the same execution environment.

 Gemini 4 Neon is designed to deliver stable performance even during long tasks involving multiple tools. In Google Antigravity, Gemini 4 Neon takes the lead, and it's also possible to run multiple Gemini 4 Hydrogen instances in parallel as sub-agents. It also offers improved capabilities such as reducing duplicate file loading, sharing progress during work, and properly compressing context accumulated over long work sessions.

 Google has released a video demonstrating the use of Gemini 4 Neon for coding, showing how to create a realistic laboratory by controlling the 3DCG software Blender via MCP.

Gemini 4 Neon excels not only in coding but also in tasks requiring specialized knowledge. It ranked second overall in the Vals Index, which assesses practical skills, with a score of 70.9%, and secured first place in both the Legal Research Bench and the Tax Agent Bench. Surge AI also ranked first in most benchmarks, including finance, accounting, spreadsheet creation, and handling complex constraints.

Furthermore, it ranked first with 56.3% in Zapier's 'AutomationBench,' which evaluates business automation capabilities. In the 'Agent Arena Index,' an evaluation metric based on more than 4,200 actual agent usage sessions, it ranked second globally with 1,336 points, and also ranked second in its ability to respond to user requests for corrections during task execution.

In the fields of science and mathematics, the student achieved 70.5% on 'Terminal-Bench Science,' which assesses the ability to perform tasks related to scientific research; 84.0% on 'RiemannBench,' which assesses mathematical problem-solving ability; and 71.3% on 'HLE-Diamond,' which assesses advanced knowledge and reasoning ability. On 'GraphWalks,' which assesses the ability to process long-text contexts, the student reached 95.2%, surpassing Claude Opus 5.5's 66.8% and GPT-6.2 Sol's 80.1%.

The following table compares the benchmark results of the Gemini 4 Neon, Claude Opus 5.5, and GPT-6.2 Sol, as published by Google. The Gemini 4 Neon outperforms other companies' high-performance models in a wide range of areas, including overall intelligence, task automation, science and mathematics, long-text context processing, and cybersecurity. On the other hand, it lags slightly behind the Claude Opus 5.5 in coding-related tests such as Terminal-Bench 4.1 and FrontierCode 2.0, while the GPT-6.2 Sol is superior in Chartography, which evaluates graph reading ability. The Gemini 4 Neon is a model that prioritizes a balance between performance and cost, yet it demonstrates performance equal to or better than other companies' high-performance models in many benchmarks.


 Google also announced 'Gemini 4 Neon for cyber,' a version specifically designed for cybersecurity. In 'AutoBaxBench,' which evaluates the ability to generate secure code, it outperformed Gemini 4 Carbon across all difficulty levels, achieving a score of 76% on the most difficult problem. It also reached 90.5% in 'SEC-bench Pro,' which evaluates the ability to discover vulnerabilities, and 70.7% in 'CWE-bench v2,' which evaluates the ability to fix vulnerabilities.

 Improvements have also been made to the reliability of the AI agent. In internal testing by Google, Gemini 4 Neon recorded the lowest data fabrication rate in the history of the Gemini series on Google Antigravity. In addition, the percentage of cheating where the AI exploited loopholes in the tests to make it appear as though it had completed the tasks was 0% in Terminal-Bench 4.0, and it recorded a 99.2% pass rate in internal tests that assessed the integrity of the AI agent.

 Furthermore, it offers improved resistance to 'indirect prompt injection attacks,' which inject malicious instructions into web pages, emails, documents, and code loaded by the AI. In Gray Swan benchmarks, attacks against Gemini 4 Neon, running at the highest intelligence level with real-time security measures applied, had a 0% success rate.

 


 The Gemini app allows you to use Gemini 4 Neon for analyzing complex documents, creating reports, and conducting research using the latest information from the web. Google explains that it can retrieve necessary information from Gmail and Google Docs, and can handle multi-stage tasks such as matching multiple receipts and documents more quickly and efficiently than traditional large models.

 The API usage fee for Gemini 4 Neon is $3 (approximately 450 yen) per million input tokens and $15 (approximately 2250 yen) per million output tokens. A 95% discount is applied to cached inputs, bringing the price down to $0.15 (approximately 23 yen) per million tokens.

 Gemini 4 Neon is available now through Google AI Studio, Gemini API, and Gemini for business for enterprise users. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can also use it with the Gemini app and Google Antigravity. Gemini 4 Neon for cyber will be available to beneficiaries of Google's Fairwind Program cybersecurity program within the next few days.
