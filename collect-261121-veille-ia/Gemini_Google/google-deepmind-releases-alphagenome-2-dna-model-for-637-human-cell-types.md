Google DeepMind has released AlphaGenome 2, a DNA sequence model that predicts how DNA variants affect gene activity across 637 human cell types and 311 mouse cell types.

The model was trained on gene activity and other molecular data from 68 tissues and more than 100 million single cells. Unlike AlphaGenome 1, which struggled to determine which cell type a variant acted on, the new model can distinguish molecular features of different cells more precisely. Researchers can also input new single-cell gene activity data, including data from disease-state cells, to predict variant effects without retraining the model.

In a retrospective analysis, the DeepMind team used AlphaGenome 2 to reanalyze an ETS2 gene variant linked to inflammatory bowel disease. The model reproduced part of earlier findings in 30 minutes. In 2024, researchers at the Francis Crick Institute in the UK used gene editing and other experiments to show that the variant sits in an enhancer regulatory sequence and can boost ETS2 expression in specific immune cells; that work took 1.5 years.
