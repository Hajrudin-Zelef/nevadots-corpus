Following last week’s release of Carbon and Hydrogen, Google today is rolling out Gemini 4 Neon. The model offers “frontier-level reasoning for complex, high-frequency workflows faster and more cost efficiently.”

Before today, Google’s Gemini 4 family consisted of:

- **Hydrogen** — Everyday help: Flash replacement
- **Argon** — Code & analysis: “…ideal choice for the*majority* of software engineering and knowledge work”
- **Carbon** — Most complex work: “…built for tasks that test the limits of a model’s intelligence”

Neon has performance comparable to, if not surpassing, Carbon across coding, reasoning, and agentic tasks, but at Flash speeds and a lower cost.

As such, Neon is available in the Gemini app for AI Pro (and AI Ultra) subscribers, with 3.1 Pro finally going away. Neon also replaces Argon. Meanwhile, 3.5 Flash-Lite is now “Auto” in the model picker for paid users.

With this launch, the model picker is:

- **Auto** : Adapts to your needs
- **Everyday help** : 4 Hydrogen
- **Deeper analysis** : 4 Neon
- **Code & complex work** : 4 Carbon

In the Gemini app, Google says to expect:

- **Higher-quality writing and knowledge work:** Neon writes clearly, with well-structured responses and a natural, direct tone. It reads uploads like spreadsheets, PDFs, and photos more precisely, turns dense material into helpful reports and summaries, and pulls in relevant details from Google apps like Gmail and Docs.
- **Reliable research and analysis:** Neon frequently incorporates details from sources across the web to keep responses current for fast-moving topics like comparing pricing across vendors before a major purchase or pulling together a complex brief with the latest market data.
- **Faster help for tougher tasks:** Tasks in Gemini can now run on Neon, working through complex, multi-step jobs more quickly and efficiently than with larger models. Get more done while stretching usage limits further when handing off tedious to-dos, like analyzing billing statements to spot why a utility charge spiked or cross-checking multiple receipts and documents to negotiate a refund.

Neon is built for complex coding, analytical reasoning, and production-grade enterprise workflows. While Carbon tackles your hardest, long-horizon projects and Hydrogen handles fast everyday execution, Neon balances depth and efficiency.

Neon is priced at $3/1M input tokens and $15/1M output tokens, or twice as expensive as Hydrogen. Argon and Carbon cost $2/1M input and $10/1M output until November 30. After the promotional pricing, it will be $4/1M input and $20/1M output, with Neon 25% cheaper as a result.

The model is also available in Google Antigravity for paid AI Pro or Ultra subscribers.
