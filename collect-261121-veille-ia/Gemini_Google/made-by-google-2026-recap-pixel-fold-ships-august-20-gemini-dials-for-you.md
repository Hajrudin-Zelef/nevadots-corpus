Google's Made by Google 2026 keynote wrapped Wednesday evening in New York City, and every major product it unveiled is already available to pre-order — with phones expected to ship around August 20 for a price ranging from $899 to $1,899. The event confirmed a surprise reversal on the Pixel 11 Pro Fold's timeline, locked in the Pixel Tag at $29 (well below the ~$34 European pre-release estimate), and deployed Gemini as an autonomous agent capable of calling businesses and placing orders entirely on its own.

Comedian Trevor Noah hosted the late-night-style production at a New York venue decked out with a live audience of journalists and influencers, a DJ, and — closing the night — a live performance from UK pop artist Pink Pantheress filmed on a Pixel 11 Pro. Rick Osterloh, Google's SVP of Devices and Services, noted that more than one billion people now use Gemini, a figure that framed the show's consistent argument: this is not a hardware year so much as an AI-era year, with each piece of silicon in service of on-device intelligence.

Pre-orders are live now at the Google Store phones section. Devices will ship around August 20 for the four Pixel 11 phones and the Pixel Watch 5. The Pixel Tag will go on sale November 11.

### Tensor G6: First Android Chip on TSMC's 2nm Process

The engine under every Pixel 11 model is the Tensor G6, built on TSMC's N2 (2nm) manufacturing node — the first major Android smartphone chip to use Gate-All-Around (GAA) transistor architecture in commercial production.

The distinction from prior silicon is structural. In FinFET transistors — the design used through the 5nm node and in most 3nm chips including the Tensor G5 — the gate material wraps around three sides of the conducting silicon channel. GAA wraps all four sides simultaneously, tightening electrostatic control and suppressing the current leakage that accelerates at sub-7nm gate lengths. TSMC's N2 implementation uses stacked nanosheet structures. The practical result, per Google: 50% more TPU compute, 2x memory bandwidth, and on-device AI tasks running up to 3.5 times faster while consuming up to 3.5 times less energy compared to the G5. CPU performance gains are more modest — 15% faster app loading, 25% faster web browsing — but the AI efficiency advantage is real for the sustained Gemini inference workloads these phones are designed around.

The G6's 1+4+2 CPU layout places a single Cortex C1-Ultra prime core at 4.11 GHz, four C1-Pro performance cores at 3.38 GHz, and two efficiency cores at 2.65 GHz — a configuration Google's "Santafe" TPU and "Metis" image signal processor handle in parallel. The G6 also marks the end of Google's long-running Exynos modem dependency: every prior Tensor chip shipped with a Samsung Exynos modem. The G6 switches to MediaTek's M90 (MT6986D), which adds support for satellite-based emergency communication alongside standard 5G bands and delivers potential peak speeds up to 12 Gbps.

One caveat for gaming buyers: analysts at multiple outlets have noted the G6's GPU is a PowerVR C-Series architecture considered older than the PowerVR DXT in the Tensor G5. Google's hardware philosophy has consistently prioritized AI processing efficiency over peak graphics performance. That is the right tradeoff for most Pixel users — but it is worth factoring in if sustained high-frame-rate gaming is a primary use case.

A new Titan M3 security chip rounds out the silicon, designed with quantum computing threat models in mind.

### Pixel 11: $899, Thinner Camera Bar, 25W Wireless Charging

The base Pixel 11 starts at $899 for 256GB of storage — a $100 increase over the Pixel 10's launch price — and ships with 12GB of RAM. Google has eliminated the 128GB tier entirely across all four models.

The most visible hardware change on the standard model is the redesigned all-glass Camera Bar, now 40% thinner than the Pixel 10's and described by Google as something that "practically disappears" when a case is applied. The main camera is a new 48MP sensor with 56% better light sensitivity than its predecessor; Tensor G6's Metis ISP enables 30x SuperRes Zoom via the 5x telephoto. Wireless charging jumps from 15W to 25W Qi2.2 — a meaningful real-world improvement — and battery life is rated at 30-plus hours.

Available colors are Frost, Obsidian, Hibiscus, and Pistachio.

### Pixel 11 Pro and Pro XL: HiLight LED Ring, $1,099 and $1,299

The Pro models carry the keynote's most distinctive hardware feature: HiLight, an RGB LED ring built into the Camera Bar that replaces the temperature sensor that appeared on the Pixel 8 Pro, 9 Pro, and 10 Pro. HiLight serves as a hardware-level transparency indicator for on-device AI — when the phone is face-down, different pulse patterns signal whether Gemini is listening, processing, or composing a response. Users can also assign custom colors to favorite contacts, currently supported in Phone by Google and WhatsApp; a specific color pulsing face-down tells you exactly who is calling without picking up the phone.

Camera hardware is substantially upgraded on the Pro tier. A new 50MP wide sensor and 48MP telephoto with a larger image sensor deliver 30% more light sensitivity than the Pro 10, enabling Portrait Mode at 5x and 120x Pro Zoom. Instant Night Sight is up to 4.5 times faster than on the Pixel 10 Pro. The Super Actua display reaches 3,600 nits of peak brightness, and Google says the surface is twice as scratch-resistant thanks to a new coating.

Both Pro models ship with 12GB RAM at the 256GB base configuration — a reduction from the Pixel 10 Pro's standard 16GB — stepping up to 16GB in the 512GB and 1TB configurations. The Pro XL adds 75% charge in approximately 30 minutes via wired charging and rated battery life of 30-plus hours with 25W Qi2.2 wireless. The Pixel 11 Pro (6.3-inch) starts at $1,099; the Pro XL (6.8-inch) at $1,299. Google now bundles six months of AI Pro with Pro purchases, down from twelve months with the previous generation.

Colors: Canyon, Fog, Olive, and matte Obsidian — the first time the matte finish arrives on Obsidian.

### Pixel 11 Pro Fold: $1,899, Ships August 20 — Not October

The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is the keynote's clearest surprise: Google is shipping it August 20 alongside every other phone in the lineup, abandoning its historical pattern of launching foldables six to eight weeks after slab models. Pre-keynote TechTimes coverage predicted an October ship date.

The hardware is the lightest and thinnest Pro Fold yet. The new chassis weighs approximately 239 grams (8.4 oz), down from 258 grams (9.1 oz) on the prior generation — nearly 10% lighter. The thinner profile comes from a redesigned hinge and a new glass fiber composite matte back cover that Google describes as "nearly impossible to crack." The hinge was tested over 80 million open-close cycles per Google's own claims.

The outer 6.5-inch display graduates to Super Actua (20% brighter), protected by glass ceramic. The inner Super Actua Flex display reaches 3,600 nits peak brightness with thinner bezels and a new dual-layer anti-impact film that Google says improves the crease appearance. HiLight also arrives on the Fold.

The camera suite adds a 48MP wide sensor with 56% improved light sensitivity and 30x Super Zoom. The Tensor G6 is paired with 16GB RAM and 256GB of storage as standard. The battery shrinks from 5,015 to 4,750 mAh — a deliberate tradeoff for the lighter and thinner chassis — while rated battery life holds at 24-plus hours with 25W Qi2.2 wireless charging.

An accessibility addition debuted on stage: Live Transcribe in Gboard now supports American Sign Language. The Sign-to-Text feature lets users sign into the front camera while viewers read a transcription on the outer display in real time — demonstrated by deaf actor Daniel Durant.

Available in Olive and Obsidian. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is priced at $1,899 — $100 more than the prior generation.

### Camera Software: Looks, Magic Capture, and Creator Suite

Google used a dedicated camera segment to introduce software capabilities that significantly change the capture workflow.

Camera Looks moves aesthetic control to the point of capture rather than post-processing. Google rewrote its camera pipeline to merge fewer frames — producing a less-processed, more film-like result — and rebuilt color lookup tables at the sensor level to emulate classic film chemistry. Options include Natural (relaxed details), Shadows (deeper contrast), Vanilla (warmer tone), plus stylistic presets such as Digi, Black Tie, Minimal, Editorial, Classic, and Velvet, all further adjustable post-capture.

Magic Capture is a passive session mode conceptually inspired by Google Clips, the discontinued ambient camera product. Users activate it, and the system selects optimal HDR+ photos and video from what the camera sees, with light editing tools available afterward including Unblur and cropping.

A Creator Suite is aimed at phone-based content creation: Teleprompter mode scrolls and adapts to the speaker's pacing; Project Folders organizes clips; an Audio Visualizer shows microphone levels in-viewfinder; a Storyboard tool enables trimming and rearranging clips via Google Photos; and Social Media Grid Lines appear when framing shots. The 120x zoom camera visible at the keynote was used to film Pink Pantheress's closing performance on stage — a live product demonstration at scale.

Pro Stable Video — arriving on the Pixel 11 Pro in bright, shaky conditions — and refined on-device noise reduction for video round out the camera software story.

### Gemini Intelligence: Agentic AI on Your Phone

The Gemini Intelligence segment drew the evening's loudest reactions — and for good reason. What Google is describing is a fundamental shift in what a smartphone is for.

Agentic AI systems differ from conventional AI assistants by pursuing goals rather than answering individual questions. Where a standard Gemini query asks for information, Gemini Intelligence on the Pixel 11 can take a goal — "book me a restaurant for Saturday night" — and execute multi-step tasks autonomously: opening the app, finding availability, selecting a time, and submitting the reservation, handing off only at the payment step.

Task Automation extends this across more third-party apps than last year's version, covering orders, bookings, form submissions, and live phone calls made on the user's behalf. Gboard Rambler handles the voice-input side: a new mode that strips filler words in real time, converting natural speech into clean, properly formatted text. A waveform animation in Gboard's suggestion strip shows when Rambler is active. This processing runs on-device via Tensor G6's Santafe TPU — not a cloud round-trip.

Proactive Assistance (formerly called Magic Cue) surfaces richer contextual cards: flight status with live links, Google Maps listings, Calendar previews, one-tap restaurant bookings, and product comparisons. Gemini can suggest saving events to Calendar or details to Google Wallet.

Circle to Search gains tighter integration with the camera, allowing users to identify objects, translate text, or search things at a distance without leaving the camera app. Live Translate for audio — previously limited to phone calls — expands to podcasts and video in YouTube and other supported apps, with real-time dubbing powered by Tensor G6.

### Pixel Watch 5: New Health Tools, Gemini on Your Wrist

The Pixel Watch 5 retains the circular glass dome design and upgrades the inside substantially. The Snapdragon W5 Gen 2 Accelerated — a tuned variant of the chip that has powered Pixel Watch hardware since 2023 — delivers a 12% CPU performance boost, while RAM doubles from 2GB to 3GB and storage doubles to 64GB. Combined with the M55 co-processor, Google claims 20% overall performance improvement. Route tracking accuracy improves by 2x in dense urban environments.

The health story is the watch's headline. Pixel Watch 5 introduces monthly insulin resistance trend summaries and monthly blood pressure summaries through the Google Health app — designed to surface metabolic patterns over time, not individual readings. Independent GPS reviewer DC Rainmaker, who traveled to New York specifically to evaluate the watch, compared the cloud-corrected GPS improvement in magnitude to Garmin's introduction of multi-band GNSS on the Fenix 7. A new Breathing Emergency Detection feature and improvements to strength and HIIT workout tracking round out the Health Guardian suite. The watch also gains Loss of Pulse Detection with automatic SOS — if the sensor registers an absent pulse, it contacts emergency services.

Gemini on the wrist brings At a Glance to the watch face bottom, surfacing flight details, boarding passes, and transit stops. Offline Gemini commands let users control timers, alarms, and workouts without a network connection. New Bedtime Automations pause music and silence notifications when the watch detects the user has fallen asleep.

One critical clarification for buyers: Gemini Intelligence — the agentic, multi-step AI capability in Google's marketing — will not be available on the watch at launch. "Later in 2026" is the official timeline, with no specific date. The structural reason is hardware: the W5 Gen 2 Accelerated carries no dedicated NPU (neural processing unit). Samsung's competing Galaxy Watch 9 uses the Snapdragon Wear Elite chip with a dedicated Hexagon NPU that runs AI models locally at roughly 10 tokens per second. The Pixel Watch 5 runs any local AI model on general-purpose CPU cores — a less efficient approach for AI workloads — though Google argues that the Watch 5's battery life advantage offsets this. Whether that is true in real-world use remains to be confirmed in post-launch testing.

Stephen Curry joined the stage to introduce a limited Stephen Curry Edition Pixel Watch 5 designed around workout use, priced at $579.

Pricing for the standard Pixel Watch 5 begins at $399 for the 41mm model (a $50 increase from last year) and $429 for the 45mm (+$30), with cellular adding $100 across both sizes. Two years of Satellite SOS service are included at no additional cost.

### Pixel Tag: Google Answers AirTag at $29

Google's first first-party item tracker, the Pixel Tag, is confirmed at $29 for a single unit or $99 for a four-pack — $6 less than Apple's AirTag 2 and significantly below the ~$34 pre-release European estimate TechTimes flagged before the event. The pill-shaped accessory carries a removable coin-cell battery, integrates with Android's Find Hub network, and is operable from a Pixel Watch via voice command through Pixel Buds.

The tracker's key technical differentiator is that it includes both UWB and Bluetooth Channel Sounding — two distinct ranging radios with different addressable audiences. UWB enables directional Precision Finding (guiding you to within inches of a lost item) on Pixel 9 Pro and later phones and comparable Samsung Galaxy S-series devices. But Channel Sounding extends sub-meter precision to any Android phone running Android 16 with a Bluetooth 6.0 chip, regardless of whether that phone has a UWB radio.

Channel Sounding achieves this by replacing RSSI-based proximity estimation (signal strength, accurate only to meters) with Phase-Based Ranging across 72 RF channels in the 2.4 GHz spectrum. The initiator and reflector exchange signals alternately across frequencies, resolving the multipath reflections that defeat RSSI in real-world indoor environments. No dedicated second radio chip is required — the 2.4 GHz Bluetooth 6.0 hardware already present in modern Android phones is sufficient. The practical result: the majority of Android users whose phones lack UWB can still track down a Pixel Tag to within roughly half a meter (about 20 inches) using Channel Sounding alone.

One caveat: the Pixel Tag will not ship until November 11, making it the only product announced at Made by Google 2026 with a significantly delayed release. Buyers who need a tracker before then should consider the Apple AirTag 2 ($29), Motorola Moto Tag ($39), or existing Find Hub-compatible Chipolo trackers.

### What Did Gemini Intelligence Actually Confirm?

The broadest claim of the night is also the one that requires the most scrutiny after launch. Google's Gemini Intelligence — the agentic AI layer that can execute multi-step tasks across apps without user intervention at each step — represents a genuine architectural shift in how the company positions the Pixel platform. Rick Osterloh's framing was deliberate: Gemini is no longer a feature inside a phone. The phone is a body for Gemini.

The practical limitation is hardware gatekeeping. Gemini Intelligence requires a flagship SoC, at least 12GB of RAM, and Gemini Nano v3. The Pixel 11 Pro, Pro XL, and Pro Fold meet these requirements at all storage configurations. The base Pixel 11 ships with 12GB RAM — meeting the minimum — though the full on-device capability depends on Google's software rollout timeline. The Pixel Watch 5's Gemini Intelligence remains "later in 2026" with no committed date.

The data access question — what information Gemini can read, across which apps, when it acts as an autonomous agent — is one that hands-on post-launch reporting will need to examine in detail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When will Pixel 11 phones and the Pixel Watch 5 ship?

All four Pixel 11 models and both Pixel Watch 5 sizes will ship around August 20. Pre-orders opened August 12 at 10 AM ET and are live at the Google Store and major retailers now. The Pixel Tag is the only Made by Google 2026 product with a significantly later ship date — November 11.

### What is HiLight, and is it on the base Pixel 11?

HiLight is an RGB LED ring built into the horizontal Camera Bar on the Pixel 11's back. It pulses different color patterns to indicate Gemini's AI state when the phone is face-down — listening, processing, or composing — and lets users assign custom colors to favorite contacts in Phone by Google and WhatsApp. HiLight is exclusive to the Pixel 11 Pro, Pro XL, and Pro Fold; it does not appear on the base Pixel 11.

### Does the Pixel Tag require UWB to achieve precise location tracking?

No. The Pixel Tag includes two ranging radios: UWB (which works with Pixel 9 Pro and later, and select Samsung Galaxy S-series phones) and Bluetooth Channel Sounding (which works with any Android phone running Android 16 with a Bluetooth 6.0 chip). Channel Sounding achieves sub-half-meter accuracy using phase measurements across the standard Bluetooth 2.4 GHz spectrum — no dedicated UWB hardware in your phone is required. This means most Android users can use Precision Finding with the Pixel Tag, not only those with premium flagships.

### Will Gemini Intelligence work on day one for Pixel Watch 5 buyers?

No. Google has confirmed that Gemini Intelligence — the autonomous, multi-step task AI — is deferred to "later in 2026" for the Pixel Watch 5, with no specific date given. Buyers who purchase on August 20 will receive Wear OS 7 with the standard Gemini assistant (which relays to the phone or cloud), live health updates, loss of pulse detection, satellite SOS, and the other hardware features — but not the fully on-device agentic Gemini Intelligence Google's marketing emphasizes.
