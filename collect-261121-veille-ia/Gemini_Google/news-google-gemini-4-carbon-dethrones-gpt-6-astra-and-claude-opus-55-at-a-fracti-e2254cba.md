Just two weeks after making Gemini 4 Argon generally available, Google has announced Gemini 4 Carbon, its most capable AI model yet. According to Google's benchmarks, the new model beats OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 to become the highest-scoring model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.
Gemini 4 Carbon scored 59 points on the recently updated Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 with 58 points and GPT-6 Astra with 57 points. For comparison, Gemini 4 Argon scored just 50 points on the same benchmark. This is a significant improvement in just a few weeks.
Google highlighted that it focused heavily on coding, long-running agentic tasks, and cybersecurity capabilities with this new Carbon model. Teams at Google have already used the model to work autonomously for days, investigating complex bugs in large codebases.
In the SEC-bench Pro benchmark, which measures the capability to identify real-world software vulnerabilities, Carbon scored 88.2%. In ExploitGym, Carbon scored 54.8%, well ahead of GPT-6 Astra and Opus 5.5. In CWE-bench, it scored 71.0%.
Google has also improved the model's reliability and safety. In internal testing, Carbon generated approximately half as many unsupported claims as Argon, while high-severity misalignment alerts dropped by nearly 80%. The model also achieved a 0% attack success rate on Gray Swan's Indirect Prompt Injection benchmark.
Despite these improvements, Google is maintaining its aggressive pricing strategy. Gemini 4 Carbon will initially cost $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with a 95% discount on cached input tokens. This is five times cheaper than GPT-6 Astra's standard API pricing of $10/$50 and half the price of Claude Opus 5.5's $4/$20.
After the promotional period, Carbon's API pricing will increase to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens.
Unfortunately, developers and consumers cannot try Carbon immediately. Google is initially making the model available to a select group of cybersecurity defenders through its Fairwind Program.
