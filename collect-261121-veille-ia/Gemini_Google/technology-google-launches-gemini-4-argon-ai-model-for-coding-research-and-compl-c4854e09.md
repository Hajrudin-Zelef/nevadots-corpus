Google has launched Gemini 4 Argon, its latest AI model designed to handle complex tasks across software development, cybersecurity, finance, legal work, scientific research and robotics. The company says the model offers improvements in multi-step reasoning, visual understanding and autonomous task execution.
Gemini 4 Argon is available globally to developers and enterprises through the Gemini API. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can also access it through the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, subject to subscription eligibility. The model is also available through Google Antigravity for eligible users.
Gemini 4 Argon price and availability
Google has introduced Gemini 4 Argon at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Cached input tokens are available at a 95% discount. From December 1, 2026, the pricing will increase to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens.
Developers can access the model through Google AI Studio and supported development tools, while enterprise customers can use it through eligible business subscriptions and the API.
Gemini 4 Argon features
Google says Gemini 4 Argon is designed for tasks that require extended reasoning and multiple steps. It can help developers refactor large codebases, identify software vulnerabilities and optimise applications. Google also reported a 35% increase in agentic code submissions and a nearly one-third reduction in code rollbacks among its internal engineering teams using the model with Antigravity.
The company cited examples of the model improving the performance of its open-source Agent Development Kit, optimising scientific computing software and helping researchers analyse complex engineering problems.
Gemini 4 Argon also brings improvements to robotics development. According to Google, the model scored 87% on the Embodied Reasoning benchmark, which evaluates the ability to understand and solve spatial problems in three-dimensional environments. In an internal demonstration, it used a video of a person playing cornhole to develop a simulation before transferring the learned task to an Apollo humanoid robot.
Google has also highlighted applications in scientific research, including quantum computing, nuclear fusion and enzyme design. The company says researchers used the model to develop enzyme candidates that subsequently demonstrated catalytic activity during laboratory testing.
Safety and security
Google says Gemini 4 Argon includes safeguards designed to reduce harmful outputs, resist prompt injection attacks and prevent AI agents from taking unauthorised actions. The company has also introduced monitoring mechanisms intended to identify potentially unsafe behaviour during autonomous task execution.
The model supports a one-million-token input-output limit through the Gemini API, allowing developers to work with larger tasks and extended workflows.
