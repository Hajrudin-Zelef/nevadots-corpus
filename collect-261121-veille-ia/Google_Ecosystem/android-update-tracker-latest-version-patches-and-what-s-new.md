## How Android updates work

Android ships in three layers. A major version, such as Android 17, arrives once a year. Quarterly Platform Releases (QPR) add features between major versions, and a monthly security bulletin lists the fixes that phone makers pick up.

Pixel phones get these first. Other brands, such as Samsung, add their own skin on top and roll updates out later, so your phone can lag behind the dates on this page. Our Pixel update tracker follows the Pixel side of it.

## Android 17 stable (rolling out from June 16, 2026)

- What it is: the first stable release of Android 17. It reached Pixel 6 and newer phones alongside the June Pixel Drop.
- Released: June 16, 2026 in most coverage, with some outlets dating it June 17 because of time zones.
- What’s new: Continue On cross-device handoff, App Bubbles for multitasking and tighter privacy controls, according to coverage of Google’s release. Gemini Intelligence was not part of the stable release, per Gizchina.
- Before it: Android 17 Beta 4.1 on June 1, 2026 was the last beta, per Google’s release notes.

Source: Android 17 release notes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the latest Android version?

Android 17 is the latest stable version. It began rolling out to Pixel phones in mid-June 2026.

### What is the latest Android security patch level?

The latest bulletin is October 2026, with a 2026-10-01 patch level, published on October 5, 2026.

### Why does my phone not have the latest patch?

Google publishes the bulletin first. Phone makers then test the fixes on their own devices, so most non-Pixel phones get them days or weeks later.
