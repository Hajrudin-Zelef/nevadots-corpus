Google’s AI Search bundling policy for schools limits educators’ control and prompts employee opposition and consideration of alternate search engines by major districts.

Google adopted a policy requiring schools to enable certain AI features to use Google Search in student settings. The company said Search and AI are deeply integrated, arguing that banning AI would make Search unavailable. Alphabet Workers Union CWA Local 9009 criticized the approach, saying educators need freedom to choose AI use in curricula. The union warned that cutting off Search would push users away from Google products. More than 500 employees signed a petition asking leadership to allow AI features in Search to be turned off without disrupting Search access. New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago school districts reportedly considered switching to alternate search engines to retain control over student AI use.

 "“Any opportunity to drive positive change with AI in education will rely on educators having the freedom to choose how to implement it in their curriculum,” the statement reads. “Cutting off access to Search for schools that don't wish to use AI will not help persuade our users, and will more likely result in them shutting out Google products altogether.”"
