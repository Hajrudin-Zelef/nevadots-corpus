# Google books Blue Origin heavy-lift launch for Project Suncatcher satellites

The dedicated New Glenn flight, targeted for Q1 2029, would place Google satellites carrying roughly the compute of a data center rack each into sun-synchronous orbit, the company says.

Google has signed Blue Origin to fly the first heavy-lift launch for Project Suncatcher, its effort to put AI compute in orbit. The announcement was posted on blog.google on November 19, 2026, by Travis Beals, senior director of the company's Paradigms of Intelligence group, who described the project as a long-term moonshot aimed at tapping the abundant solar power available in space.[1]

Under the plan Google outlined, Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket would deploy Google satellites into sun-synchronous orbit, with each satellite carrying roughly the capacity of a single data center rack. The launch is targeted for Q1 2029, which leaves more than two years between the announcement and any hardware reaching orbit.[1]

## What's next

Google described the flight as a dedicated launch that builds on existing work with other satellite and launch partners, and called it a milestone on a longer roadmap rather than an operational deployment. The company did not name those other partners, disclose the number of satellites involved, or detail the terms of the Blue Origin agreement.[1]
