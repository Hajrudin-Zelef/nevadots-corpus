# Google Launches Fairwind Program for Proactive Cyber Defense

Google has launched the Fairwind Program, a limited-access initiative giving governments and trusted partners access to advanced AI-powered cyber defense tools to find, verify, and fix vulnerabilities.

Xcademia Team

Google has launched the **Fairwind Program**, a limited-access initiative that gives governments, trusted Google Cloud customers, and cybersecurity partners access to advanced cyber defense capabilities.

The program is designed to help defenders proactively identify and address cyber risks using advanced AI. As a first step, participants will receive access to **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber** to help autonomously find and fix vulnerabilities.

Google says the program is focused on protecting critical infrastructure, public services, and national security while giving trusted defenders earlier access to capabilities that can help them strengthen their systems.

## 

**Combining Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber with CodeMender**

A central part of the Fairwind Program combines **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber** with Google's **CodeMender** harness.

Together, the tools are designed to help defenders find, verify, and fix software vulnerabilities at agentic scale.

Google says Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber provides specialized reasoning for writing and validating code fixes, while CodeMender provides the harness for vulnerability discovery and remediation.

According to Google, the combination can generate verified, deployment-ready patches in minutes within an organization's secure cloud environment, compared with vulnerabilities that can otherwise take weeks to fix manually.

## **Why Google is offering early access**

Google says defenders using advanced AI have faced a difficult choice.

They could use large frontier models that may be expensive to deploy and difficult to control across enterprise codebases, or smaller open-weight models that may have limitations with complex vulnerability remediation and require organizations to build their own tooling and infrastructure.

The Fairwind Program is intended to provide a trusted group of organizations with access to Google's advanced AI and cyber defense capabilities.

Google describes this early access as an opportunity for participating defenders to strengthen their systems before malicious actors can exploit new capabilities.

## 

**Who can participate?**

Google is initially focusing access on organizations considered critical to society's resilience.

The program includes:

- **Governments and national cyber authorities:** To help harden public-sector networks and citizen services against targeted intrusions.
- **Critical infrastructure operators:** To help protect essential services across healthcare, telecommunications, energy, and financial networks from operational disruption.
- **Core technology platforms:** To help secure software foundations used by large numbers of downstream users.

Google says the Fairwind Program already has **more than 650 participating partners globally**.

## 

**Operational requirements for participants**

Access to these capabilities comes with operational requirements.

Participating organizations agree to strict standards designed to support responsible use of the AI-powered cyber defense tools.

These include limiting access to employees working within internal cybersecurity, incident response, or penetration testing teams.

Participants must also deploy protections such as **multi-factor authentication**.

These requirements are intended to restrict access to appropriate security personnel and add safeguards around the use of advanced cyber defense capabilities.

## **Partners are already using the capabilities**

Google says trusted defenders and industry leaders are already putting **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber** into practice through the Fairwind Program.

The announcement includes testimonials from organizations including **Armadin, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Snowflake, and Wiz**.

Google does not provide detailed technical results or performance measurements from these organizations in the announcement.

**Additional details were not disclosed in the announcement.**

## 

**Fairwind will evolve with its partners**

Google says the Fairwind Program will evolve alongside the needs of its partners and users.

The company plans to adapt its product offerings and expand partner access while working with industry organizations, governments, and open-weight community leaders.

Google says the objective is to find an appropriate balance between broader access to advanced cyber capabilities and robust security.

The company did not provide specific details about future access expansion in the announcement.

## 

**CodeMender remains available to Google Cloud customers**

While the Fairwind Program prioritizes access to Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber for participating organizations, Google says its broader customer base can also use CodeMender for proactive code security.

Google Cloud customers can use CodeMender with publicly available models hosted on the **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform**, alongside security solutions offered through **AI Threat Defense**.

This provides an additional path for organizations that are not part of the limited-access Fairwind Program.

## 

**Google's broader cybersecurity effort**

Google positions Fairwind as part of its wider work on cyber resilience.

The company points to its experience with zero-trust architecture, AI defenses, and built-in security as part of the foundation behind its cybersecurity approach.

Google also highlighted its work through **Google.org**.

The company says its latest commitment brings its total global cybersecurity funding to **more than $100 million**.

Google's 2026 Google.org US Cybersecurity Impact Report also states that **$36 million** has been provided to **35 cyber clinics** to date.

According to Google, those clinics have provided free, hands-on security support to more than **1,250 hospitals, public school districts, and municipal utilities in the United States**.

## **The focus: shortening the path from discovery to remediation**

Google's Fairwind announcement centers on one core objective: helping defenders reduce the time between identifying a vulnerability and addressing it.

By combining advanced AI models with vulnerability remediation tooling, Google says the program can give trusted government and enterprise partners access to autonomous capabilities for finding and fixing vulnerabilities.

The initiative also reflects Google's focus on giving selected defenders access to advanced cyber capabilities under controlled conditions.

For organizations involved in critical infrastructure, public services, government systems, and widely used technology platforms, the program is positioned as a way to strengthen cyber defenses against increasingly capable threats.

## 

**Conclusion**

The **Fairwind Program** represents Google's latest effort to apply advanced AI to proactive cybersecurity.

The initial program combines **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber** with **CodeMender** to help trusted defenders find, verify, and fix software vulnerabilities.

Access is currently limited to governments, trusted Google Cloud customers, and cybersecurity partners, with participating organizations required to follow operational security standards.

Google says more than **650 partners globally** are participating, while the program will continue to evolve with its partners and users.

The broader initiative also connects Google's AI-based cyber defense work with its wider cybersecurity efforts, including Google.org funding for cybersecurity clinics in the United States.

For defenders, the central idea is straightforward: reducing the time between discovering a vulnerability and applying a verified fix can provide a practical security advantage.
