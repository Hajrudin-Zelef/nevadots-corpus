Bloomberg reported on Nov. 19, 2026, that more than 540 Google employees and contractors had signed an Alphabet Workers Union petition asking Google to let schools use Google Search with its AI features turned off. The union spokesperson supplied the signatory count cited in the report.

## The request is about choice within Search

The petition, titled “Let Students Search,” argues that educators should decide how and when AI enters classrooms. Its signers warn that tying access to Search to AI use could lead schools to reject Google products more broadly, according to Gizmodo’s account of the petition.

The requested control is specific: schools would keep Search while turning off its AI features. That is different from deciding whether students can use a separate AI product.

## Separate product switches leave a key distinction

Gizmodo reported that Google said schools could toggle Search, Gemini and Gemini Notebook separately. That addresses control over those products; the account said it did not address whether AI features built into Search could be switched off independently. A separate Gemini setting, by itself, does not answer whether schools can retain Search without its integrated AI features.

NeoTeo’s earlier explanation of Google’s school-account Search and Gemini controls provides background on the product-level distinction.

For schools, control over Search and control over the AI built into Search are separate questions.

## Three districts were considering alternatives

Gizmodo, citing The New York Times, reported that school districts in New York City, Los Angeles and Chicago were considering other search engines. The reported discussions give the petition a practical institutional dimension: the question is not only which school tools administrators can toggle, but whether districts see alternatives as preferable to Google’s arrangement.
