Google has confirmed that it’s entered into a partnership with aerospace and spaceflight company Blue Origin towards its first heavy-lift launch for Project Suncatcher. For those unfamiliar, Google’s Project Suncatcher serves as a long-term moonshot to scale AI technology in space by harnessing solar power.

According to Google, the project is set to be finalized by Q1 2029, and will use Blue Origin’s heavy-lift New Glenn rocket to deploy satellites which will essentially carry data center racks into sun-synchronous orbit. Travis Beals, Senior Director of Project Suncatcher at Google comments:

Project Suncatcher is a long-term moonshot to scale AI infrastructure in space by tapping into the abundant solar power in orbit… This dedicated launch with Blue Origin represents a crucial milestone on our roadmap as we expand the boundaries of what’s possible for the future of compute.


Back in October, Suncatcher reached a key milestone by generating the first-ever AI response from an orbit-based, data-center-class TPU. During a ground-station pass over Svalbard, Norway, operators uplinked a prompt to the onboard Gemma 4 open model, commanding it to generate a space-themed haiku. Gemma 4 then responded with the phrase, “Blue marble below, Data streams in silent grace, Starlight feeds my core.”
