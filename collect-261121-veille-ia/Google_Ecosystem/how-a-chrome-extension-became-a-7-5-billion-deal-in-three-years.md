# **How a Chrome extension became a $7.5 billion deal in three years**

*In Atelier Of Mind by Devyanshi Bansal*

Why Stripe, a payments company, paid billions for OpenRouter, an AI startup that owns no AI models. The money, the tech and the risks, explained simplyThe buyer wasn't Google. It wasn't OpenAI. It was a payments company.

This is the story of why Stripe bought OpenRouter, and why it's really a story about money, not AI.

*How to read the numbers.* *OpenRouter is private and has never published its revenue. Stripe doesn't publish revenue either. So in this piece:* *confirmed* *= said by the companies,* *reported* *= a news outlet citing anonymous sources,* *estimated* *= an analyst's model or my maths.*


## The quick version

- **What happened:** On**19 August 2026** , Stripe signed a deal to buy OpenRouter (confirmed). Neither side shared the price. The New York Times reported**$7.5B** ; other reports range from $7B to $10B.
- **What OpenRouter does:** one account, one bill, one piece of code to use 400+ AI models from 80+ providers. It keeps a fee of about 5% on the money developers load in.
- **Why it's interesting:** Stripe paid roughly**5.8x** what OpenRouter was valued at just**12 weeks earlier** , for a company earning maybe**~2%** of what Stripe earns.
- **The twist:** Stripe was already OpenRouter's payment processor. It watched the money grow before it made the offer.

## 1. The problem nobody wanted to solve

Imagine you're building an AI app.

You want Claude for writing, GPT for reasoning, and something cheap like DeepSeek for simple tasks.

That means three accounts. Three sets of keys. Three bills. Three different ways of writing code. And when one of them goes down at 2 am, your app goes down with it.

In April 2023, Alex Atallah, co-founder of the NFT marketplace OpenSea, launched a small Chrome extension called **Window AI** to make switching between models easier. By **August 2023** it had a new name: **OpenRouter**.

The idea was simple. **One account. One prepaid balance. Every model.**

Think of it like a **travel booking site for AI**. You still pick your flight (your model). But you book everything in one place, pay once, and if one airline cancels, you get put on another.

Atallah's background matters here. At OpenSea he learned the marketplace playbook: gather scattered supply in one place, take a small cut of everything that flows through, and win by having the most buyers and sellers. He ran the same play on AI.

## 2. How OpenRouter actually makes money

Here's the part most people get wrong.

OpenRouter **doesn't mark up model prices.** If a request to Claude costs $0.05 directly from Anthropic, it costs $0.05 on OpenRouter too.

**But** it charges a fee when you load money in.

- Load **$100** of credits by card and you pay about**$105.50** (a 5.5% fee). By crypto, 5%.
- That $100 becomes one balance, usable on **any** model. Like a metro card that works on every line, with each ride costing a different fare.
- As you use models, OpenRouter pays the providers. It keeps the fee.

Two terms worth knowing, because they'll come up again:

- **GMV (gross merchandise value):** the total money flowing*through* a marketplace. For OpenRouter, that's all the money developers spend on models. Estimated at**~$2.5B a year** (derived from its fee, not disclosed).
- **Revenue:** the slice it keeps, about 5% of that. Estimated at**~$140M a year** by July 2026.

Same idea as Zomato: the total value of food ordered is GMV; the commission Zomato keeps is revenue.

One more correction to a popular myth. **Developers choose their own models.** OpenRouter mostly decides *which company serves* that model. Claude, for example, is served by Anthropic, Amazon and Google, so OpenRouter can pick the fastest or most available of the three. There's an optional "Auto" mode that picks the model for you, but most people don't use it.

Even OpenRouter's own investor, Menlo Ventures, says the product is **being the best gateway**, not clever routing. Convenience, uptime and one bill.

## 3. The rocket ship

*A token is the unit AI models charge in, roughly a chunk of a word.*

Token volume grew about **33% a month for three years** (Menlo Ventures, an investor source). Estimated yearly revenue went from about **$5M** in mid-2025 to about **$140M** by July 2026.

A quick note on that revenue number. It's a **run-rate**: take what a company earns right now and multiply it to a full year. Run-rates change every month, which is why you'll see wildly different figures. More on that in section 6.

**But** OpenRouter also built something more valuable than revenue: **data.**

Its public leaderboards show which AI models developers actually use. Journalists cite them. AI labs launch new models there first, sometimes anonymously, just to see how they perform. OpenRouter became the closest thing the AI world has to a live scoreboard.

## 4. Enter Stripe

A mouse next to an elephant.

**But** look at one row: the cut. OpenRouter keeps about **15 times more** of every dollar that passes through it. And it was growing far faster than Stripe's roughly 33% a year.

## 5. Why Stripe wanted it

Stripe had been quietly building an AI money machine for two years.

- **Oct 2024:** bought**Bridge** (stablecoins) for $1.1B.
- **Jan 2026:** closed**Metronome** , the usage-billing company that bills customers for OpenAI, Anthropic and NVIDIA (reported ~$1B).
- **Mar 2026:** launched**Token Billing** , which tracks an AI startup's model costs and adds its markup when billing that startup's own customers.

Stripe already worked with **88% of the Forbes AI 50** (Stripe's own letter, Aug 2026).

So Stripe was already touching AI money on both sides. It processed the subscriptions users paid to AI apps. And it processed many of the bills those apps paid to the AI labs.

**But** it was only the **cashier**. It moved the money, took a small processing fee, and never saw the full picture.

**Therefore** OpenRouter was the missing piece. Not because it decides where money goes, but because of three things Stripe couldn't get any other way:

1. **A ~5% cut of AI spending.** Every dollar developers load in, across every model, earns a fee. More AI usage means more money, no matter which model wins.
2. **A live view of the AI market.** Which models developers pick, how much they spend, how fast it's growing. That's a goldmine for selling Stripe's other products to AI companies.
3. **The wallet AI agents already use.** In October 2026,**76%** of OpenRouter's tokens came from AI agents, not humans typing. If software agents start paying for things on their own, Stripe wants to be the system they already pay through.

Patrick Collison, Stripe's CEO, put it in one line: **"Tokens are the central currency for companies building with AI."**

Stripe sells the pipes money flows through. Tokens are becoming a new kind of money. **Therefore** it wanted the biggest pipe for tokens.

## 6. The price, and the fight over it

This is where the story gets dramatic.

- **28 May 2026:** OpenRouter valued at about**$1.3B** .
- **~17 July:** The Information reports that bigger companies are interested in buying it.
- **23 July:** The Wall Street Journal says Stripe is in talks at around**$10B** .
- **16 August:** Bloomberg reports a deal at**more than $7B** .
- **19 August:** Both companies announce it. The NYT reports**$7.5B** .

From $1.3B to $7.5B in **83 days.**

Is that crazy? It depends which revenue number you divide by.

- $7.5B ÷ ~$140M (July run-rate) ≈ **54x revenue** . Expensive, but explainable for something growing this fast.
- $7.5B ÷ ~$50M (April estimate) ≈ **150x revenue** . Sounds absurd.

Same deal. Different month. Critics quoting "150x" are using the older number. That gap itself tells you how fast OpenRouter was growing.

**But** here's the detail most coverage missed.

Stripe was **already OpenRouter's payment processor**, since at least January 2026. It could see the money coming in, month after month, before it made a single offer. As fintech writer Simon Taylor put it, Stripe was "bidding with the target's revenue trend in plain view."

To outsiders, the price looked like bubble madness. To Stripe, it was a number it had watched grow with its own eyes.

And it wasn't alone. Stripe reportedly **outbid Databricks** and others. The price came from an auction, not just Stripe's own enthusiasm.

**One more way to see it:** $7.5B is less than **5%** of Stripe's own $159B value. That summer, Stripe and a partner had also offered about **$53B** for PayPal, an offer PayPal rejected. Next to that, OpenRouter was a medium-sized bet.

## 7. Why OpenRouter said yes

The founders' stated reasons: Stripe's payments infrastructure, its network of customers, and its fraud expertise. That last one is real. Stripe's data shows **one in six** attempted sign-ups to AI services comes from a bad actor.

The financial reason is simpler: a **~5.8x jump in value in 12 weeks**, with every investor making money.

**But** there's a quieter reason too. OpenRouter's business had cracks.

- **Its revenue per token was falling.** Developers moved to cheap Chinese open models, and token prices kept crashing. Sacra estimates revenue per token fell about**60% between March and August 2026** . A 5% fee on a cheaper token is a smaller fee.
- **Free rivals were appearing.** Vercel's AI gateway charges 0%. Cloud providers charge no gateway fee at all.
- **Big customers were building their own.** More than ten companies, including Cursor and Ramp, built their own routers.

**Therefore** selling at the peak, to the one buyer with the payments and fraud tools to make the fee stickier, was smart defence as well as a big payday.

## 8. The case against

Not everyone is convinced.

**The price.** "A near-8x markup in two months reads like classic bubble behavior," wrote Simon Taylor. Decrypt said Stripe was "buying position, not technology." The top comment on Hacker News asked: "Why is an API middleman worth this much money?"

**The trust problem.** OpenRouter's value comes from being **neutral**: every model and every provider gets a fair shot. It promised to stay that way, saying its routing "doesn't bend to any model, any provider, or any parent company."

**But** that's a promise, not a contract. And Stripe processes payments for many of the same labs whose traffic OpenRouter routes. Stripe doesn't force anyone's model choice, but it now owns the defaults, the rankings and the decision about which provider serves open models. Those are quiet levers, hard to notice if they ever tilt.

## 9. What the market is saying so far

Three months in, the early signals:

- **Developers didn’t leave.** October 2026 was OpenRouter’s biggest month ever: 790 trillion tokens, up 37%. No reported customer losses or fee changes.
- **Prices kept falling.** Since January, the average price per million tokens dropped from $1.21 to $0.52 for closed models, and from $0.28 to $0.084 for open ones. Usage soars, revenue per token shrinks.
- **A rival showed up within 24 hours.** On**20 August** , the day after the announcement, Ramp launched its own router,**free** for the rest of 2026. Competitors clearly see routing as land worth grabbing, and they’re fighting on price.
- **The deal is reported closed.** Two smaller outlets say it completed on**10–11 November 2026** . I couldn’t find an official confirmation from Stripe or OpenRouter yet.

## 10. The verdict

**Was it a good decision for Stripe?** On balance, yes.

The strategy fits perfectly. Stripe now has the full chain for AI money: route it (OpenRouter), measure it (Metronome), bill it (Token Billing), pay it (Stripe, stablecoins). It gets a live view of AI spending across every major lab. And it owns the wallet that AI agents already pay through.

The price is rich by any measure. **But** the downside is limited: less than 5% of Stripe’s value, paid by a buyer who saw the numbers first.

The real risk isn’t overpaying by a few billion. It’s the asset losing its power:

- labs or big spenders decide a Stripe-owned router isn’t neutral and go direct;
- free rivals like Ramp push the ~5% fee toward zero;
- the most valuable traffic moves to in-house routers.

**Who profits more?** Right now, clearly the sellers. Their win is already in hand. CapitalG’s May investment multiplied roughly 5.8x in twelve weeks. Dealroom estimates a $700K early angel cheque turned into about $94M. Stripe’s win will take years to prove.

**Three signs to watch:**

1. Does OpenRouter’s ~5% fee hold?
2. Do AI labs keep launching new models there first?
3. Does Stripe publish how routing works, as critics are asking?

## The last word

In every gold rush, the people who got rich weren’t always the ones digging. They were the ones selling the shovels, and running the bank where the gold got weighed.

OpenAI, Anthropic and Google are digging.

Stripe just bought the scale.
