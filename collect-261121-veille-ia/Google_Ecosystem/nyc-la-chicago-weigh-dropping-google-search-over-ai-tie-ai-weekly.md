## TL;DR

- NYC, LA and Chicago public schools, serving 1.57 million students combined, are weighing dropping Google Search after Google refused an AI-free version.
- Google VP of education Chris Phillips told the Times 'Search and AI are deeply integrated,' meaning any school AI opt-out also loses access to Search.
- LAUSD has already disabled Google's AI Overviews and says it could switch default search engines 'literally overnight'; DuckDuckGo and Microsoft Bing are the alternatives on the table.

Three of the largest school systems in the United States, with a combined 1.57 million students, are weighing whether to drop Google Search after the company declined to sell schools an AI-free version of Search, The New York Times reports. New York City, Los Angeles and Chicago public schools have each asked Google for a Search product with AI stripped out. Google said no.

"Search and AI are deeply integrated," Chris Phillips, Google's vice president and general manager for education, told the Times. Beginning next year, Google will turn its AI products off by default for schools and let administrators choose what to re-enable, but switching off AI will also switch off Search.

The districts are already testing their exits. Los Angeles Unified has disabled Google's AI Overviews for students, and at a recent meeting of the LA school board's AI committee, Chief Information Officer Jack Kelanic said the district could change its default search engine "literally overnight." NYC Schools Chancellor Kamar Samuels told the Times, "If Google doesn't reconsider their position, we will explore other search options." Chicago has told Google that if it cannot provide an AI-free Search, "the district will look to other search solutions." DuckDuckGo and Microsoft's Bing, both of which let users turn off AI-generated answers, are the alternatives on the table.

The standoff follows a visible run of education-AI pushback stories we've tracked this week, from campus petition drives to district-level moratoria on generative AI.
