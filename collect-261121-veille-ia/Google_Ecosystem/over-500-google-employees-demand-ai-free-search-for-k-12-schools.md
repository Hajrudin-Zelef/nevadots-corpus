More than 500 Google employees and contractors have signed a petition urging the company to offer Google Search without AI features to K-12 schools.

According to Bloomberg, the petition targets Google CEO Sundar Pichai, Search head Elizabeth Reid, and education chief Chris Phillips.

The Alphabet Workers Union organized the petition, raising concerns about Google’s growing use of generative AI in education.

## Google employees want traditional Search back in schools

The petition follows Google’s decision to expand access to Gemini and AI-powered Search features for K-12 students.

The move sparked criticism because schools that wanted to avoid AI features could face restrictions on access to Google’s traditional search tools.

Google subsequently announced plans to disable AI features for students by default. However, the company has not committed to providing a completely AI-free version of Google Search.

Employees argue that disabling AI features by default does not go far enough. They want schools to have the option to remove generative AI from Search entirely.

### Google could lose schools over its AI strategy

Employees warn that Google’s approach could push schools toward competing search engines and educational platforms.

Google leadership has reportedly defended its AI strategy by arguing that students will encounter similar technology from competitors regardless of Google’s decisions.

However, some employees question whether creating a separate, AI-free Search experience would present significant technical challenges.

Switching away from Google could also prove difficult for schools that depend on Chromebooks, Google Workspace for Education, and other Google services.

Clever co-founder Dan Carroll believes Google faces a difficult decision. The company must balance its push toward AI-powered products with schools’ demands for traditional search tools.

For now, Google’s decision to disable AI features by default has not resolved the disagreement. The petition shows that opposition to mandatory AI integration extends beyond educators and parents to Google’s own workforce.
