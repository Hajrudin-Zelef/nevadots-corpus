New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago public schools are looking at other search engines following Google’s refusal last month to provide students with a version of Search that didn’t incorporate AI, according to The New York Times.

In a blog post earlier this month, Google said that in early 2027, Search, Gemini, Gemini Notebook, and Gemini in Classroom will be off by default for K-12 student accounts until administrators turn them on. Schools can still enable Search without the stand-alone Gemini tools, as we reported a few days ago. However, they still cannot get Search without AI Overviews or AI Mode. Google has said those features are so “deeply integrated” that it cannot provide a chatbot-free search engine.

## The biggest districts are pushing back

“If Google doesn’t change their position we’re going to look at other search options,” said NYC Schools Chancellor Kamar H. Samuels in a statement, per NYT. “We will not accept the false binary that Google has presented us with.” The city already banned generative AI for younger students this fall. Chicago Public Schools told families last week that it wants Search without the AI component, and “if such an arrangement cannot be reached with Google, CPS will look to other search solutions.”

Los Angeles Unified has turned off Google’s AI Overviews for students and is now looking at whether Search should be in the classroom at all. A district spokesperson confirmed this to NBC LA. Jack Kelanic, the district’s CIO, said the district could change its default search engine “literally overnight.” To make things easy, both Google Search alternatives—DuckDuckGo and Bing—already allow schools to turn off AI-generated answers.

## What this could mean at home

Parents protested last week at Google offices in New York, Chicago, and LA. Teachers are concerned that students will be using the chatbots in Search to cheat, or will be reading AI-generated summaries rather than the original sources. A recent survey even showed that many parents worry their children are becoming too reliant on AI.

Google has said Search and Gemini will remain off next year until the administrators turn them on. NYC has declined Google’s Guided Learning pilot and is currently evaluating five other AI tools for high school. Some parents in the city have reported that their students are still able to access AI Mode at home on their school accounts. If Google doesn’t provide a chatbot-free Search, then students in those districts could lose the search tool on their Chromebooks entirely or continue to get AI answers.
