## API access and pricing

Google says Genie 4 is generally available on the Interactions API for real-time interactive world generation.[5] Its listed pricing is $1.50 per million input tokens, $9 per million text output tokens and $17.50 per million video output tokens.[5] Google estimates video output costs about 10 cents per second, with stereo audio included.[5] These are distinct measures: teams evaluating a workflow should consider the token rates as well as the estimated per-second video cost, rather than treating one figure as the full cost of every use case.

## What developers can generate

Google says developers can experiment with Genie 4 from a text prompt, a reference image or both.[4] The model can generate continuous 720p worlds at 24 frames per second with synchronized native audio.[4] Google describes the audio as including music-like sounds, ambient noise and interactive dialogue generated alongside a world.[1] The combination offers developers a way to prototype a moving, audible environment from different starting inputs; the evidence does not specify how output quality varies with those inputs.

## Real-time scene control

Google says users can navigate worlds with keyboard controls and use live prompts to change weather, lighting, objects or events.[4] The company also says users can prompt changes to their environments in real time while navigating.[2] Google DeepMind describes Genie 4 as simulating how a world responds and sounds, and how people in it move, react and speak.[1] These controls make interactive exploration part of the offering, not just generation from a static prompt, while Google identifies intricate physical interactions as an ongoing challenge.[3]

## Potential and limitations

Google DeepMind says Genie 4 is designed to improve realism and real-time interactive world simulation.[1] Google says Gemini Omni grounds simulations in real-world appearance and behavior, while other Gemini models help with reasoning, coherence and continuity.[1] The company points to training environments for robots and agents, as well as new interactive experiences, as potential uses.[1] It also identifies camera latency, real-time audio precision and long-term scene permanence among the challenges.[3] Teams considering it for a workflow should test those limitations against their specific requirements.

For teams exploring simulation or interactive content, general API availability creates a route to test Genie 4 in developer workflows. The listed rates, output format and stated limitations give professionals concrete variables to assess before committing a use case.
