## The problem

Residents of Southaven, Mississippi, say xAI's subsidiary MZX offered to buy their homes only if they stay out of a class-action noise suit over the datacenter's 69 gas turbines; MZX had bought five homes since 9 October, according to DeSoto county records.

## In brief

Real estate agents acting for xAI have gone door to door in the Colonial Hills neighborhood of Southaven, Mississippi, offering to buy homes provided owners agree not to join a lawsuit over noise from the company's gas-turbine power plant. A realtor told one resident the target is 418 homes, with 18 owners already agreeing to sell, according to a recording heard by the Guardian.

The plant, a former Duke Energy site with 69 temporary gas turbines, powers datacenters that xAI has built in the Memphis and Southaven area since July 2024. Neighbors describe continuous noise like an airplane engine, and three residents filed a lawsuit in June over 'near-constant noise, vibrations and other nuisance-level harms'.

xAI did not respond to questions about the numbers and has said it wants to be a good neighbor and denies the lawsuit's claims. The homes would house xAI staff, according to the realtor and the local mayor. The turbines also face scrutiny over missing permits and estimated health costs from emissions.

## Main points

1. A realtor said the target is 418 homes in Colonial Hills, with 18 owners already agreeing to sell, according to a recording heard by the Guardian; xAI did not respond to questions about those numbers.
2. Since 9 October, five neighborhood homes have been bought by MZX, the xAI subsidiary, according to DeSoto county records.
3. The average house price in the area is about $180,000, and the buyout is offered at market value.
4. Three residents filed a lawsuit in June over noise and vibrations; one plaintiff, Jason Haley, lives about half a mile from the turbines.
5. The Environmental Protection Agency determined that dozens of turbines ran without permits required by federal law, though it said enforcement was a state matter.
6. The Southern Environmental Law Center estimated toxic emissions from the turbines have caused $44m in annual health costs; no on-site air monitoring has been done.
7. In July, xAI agreed with state regulators to replace the temporary turbines with a permanent gas-fired facility by next summer, which should lessen the noise.

## The full story

MZX, the xAI subsidiary that operates the site, handed Colonial Hills residents a letter saying it 'requires property for its business purposes' and will buy nearby houses on the condition that owners do not join the class-action lawsuit. The realtor and the local mayor say the homes will house xAI staff as the company expands.

Residents describe the noise as constant. Piano teacher Elizabeth Rose, who lives about two miles away, said she has been woken at 2am and 3am. Haley, who has owned his home for 20 years, said he cannot sleep. Paramedic Gavin Foust said he will refuse the offer because of the no-sue requirement, though his elderly neighbors agreed to sell.

The plant began operating in August last year to feed three xAI datacenters: Colossus I, Colossus II and Macrohardrr. Environmental groups and the NAACP sued in federal court in April to stop the turbines; the Department of Justice unsuccessfully tried to block the suit, arguing xAI technology is needed for national security.

xAI says it has varied its power output and built sound-dampening walls, and denies all claims in the noise lawsuit. Musk said during a SpaceX earnings call this month that Grok is seeing the fastest growth he has ever seen.
