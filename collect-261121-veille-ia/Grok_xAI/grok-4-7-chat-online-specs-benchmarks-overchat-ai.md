I want to power a 12 V LED strip that draws 2 A from a 24 V supply. Should I use a resistor, a linear regulator or a buck converter? Explain with the numbers.

**Use a buck converter.** A resistor or linear regulator will each waste 24 W as heat (50% efficiency); a buck converter wastes only a few watts.

#### The load

- LED strip: 12 V × 2 A = **24 W** .
- Supply: 24 V. You must drop 12 V while supplying 2 A.

#### Resistor

- Drop 12 V at 2 A → R = 12/2 = 6 Ω.
- Dissipation: 12 V × 2 A = 24 W (or I²R = 24 W).
- Supply delivers 24 V × 2 A = 48 W; efficiency = 50%.
- A 24 W resistor is large, runs very hot, and gives poor regulation if the strip current changes with temperature or brightness.

#### Linear regulator (e.g. a 12 V LDO or 7812-style part rated for ≥2 A)

- Same electrical result: it drops (24-12) V × 2 A = 24 W as heat.
- Input power still 48 W, efficiency 50%.
- You need a substantial heatsink; the regulator will be hot and the supply is loaded at 2 A continuously.

#### Buck converter

- A typical synchronous buck is ~90% efficient (often 85–95% depending on the module).
- Input power ≈ 24 W/0.9 ≈ 27 W.
- Input current ≈ 27 W/24 V ≈ 1.1 A.
- Waste heat ≈ 2–4 W instead of 24 W — small heatsinking or none, far less stress on the 24 V supply, and stable 12 V output.

**Bottom line:** At 2 A the resistor and linear regulator each throw away as much power as the LEDs use. A buck converter is the only practical choice.

Write a bash script that backs up a folder to a dated .tar.gz file, keeps only the last 7 backups and logs each run. Then show how to run it every night at 2 am with cron.

```
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# backup-folder.sh — archive a folder to a dated .tar.gz, keep the last 7, log each run.
set -euo pipefail
# --- config ---
SOURCE="${1:-/path/to/folder}"          # folder to back up (no trailing slash needed)
DEST="${2:-/path/to/backups}"           # directory that holds the archives
KEEP=7
LOG="${DEST}/backup.log"
# --- prep ---
if [[ ! -d "$SOURCE" ]]; then
  echo "Source is not a directory: $SOURCE" >&2
  exit 1
fi
mkdir -p "$DEST"
STAMP="$(date +%Y-%m-%d_%H%M%S)"
NAME="$(basename "$SOURCE")"
ARCHIVE="${DEST}/${NAME}_${STAMP}.tar.gz"
log() {
  # timestamped line to the log and to stdout
  printf '%s %s\n' "$(date '+%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S')" "$*" | tee -a "$LOG"
}
log "START backup of ${SOURCE} -> ${ARCHIVE}"
if tar -czf "$ARCHIVE" -C "$(dirname "$SOURCE")" "$NAME"; then
  log "OK wrote $(du -h "$ARCHIVE" | awk '{print $1}') ${ARCHIVE}"
else
  log "FAIL tar exited $?"
  exit 1
fi
# Keep only the newest KEEP archives for this folder name; delete the rest.
# shellcheck disable=SC2012
mapfile -t OLD < <(ls -1t "${DEST}/${NAME}_"*.tar.gz 2>/dev/null | tail -n +"$((KEEP + 1))" || true)
if ((${#OLD[@]})); then
  for f in "${OLD[@]}"; do
    rm -f -- "$f"
    log "PRUNE removed ${f}"
  done
else
  log "PRUNE nothing to remove (keeping up to ${KEEP})"
fi
log "DONE"
```
Make it executable and try a run:

```
chmod +x backup-folder.sh
./backup-folder.sh /path/to/folder /path/to/backups
```
Nightly at 2:00 am with cron (use absolute paths; cron’s environment is minimal):

`crontab -e`
Add:

`0 2 * * * /path/to/backup-folder.sh /path/to/folder /path/to/backups`
That runs every day at 02:00. Output is also appended to `/path/to/backups/backup.log`. If you want cron’s own mail/stderr captured as well, you can append `>> /path/to/backups/cron.log 2>&1` to the crontab line.
