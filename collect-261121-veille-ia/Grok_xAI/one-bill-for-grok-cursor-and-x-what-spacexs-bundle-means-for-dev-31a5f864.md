SpaceX wants to be the single line item for your AI coding tool, your chatbot, and your social media account.
On October 12, SpaceX Super Intelligence (SpaceXSI), the AI unit formerly known as xAI, introduced The X Subscription. It folds Grok, the Grok Bot agent, the Cursor AI code editor, and X Premium into one plan family. The rollout is happening gradually, so not every account sees it yet.
For developers and the people who pay for their tools, this is more than a pricing change. It changes how AI coding spend gets measured, governed, and tied to identity.
How the Tiers Break Down
There are five plans: Free, Plus ($8/month), Premium ($30), Super ($100), and Ultra ($200). Usage limits rise with each tier.
Plus covers Grok Chat, Voice, Imagine, Build, the Grok API, and Cursor for coding. It also includes X Premium perks like the checkmark and fewer ads. Premium adds the newest Grok models, Grok Bot, and Cursor features such as cloud agents, code hosting, and automations. Super adds priority access to models during peak hours.
SpaceXSI says the $30 Premium tier replaces SuperGrok, Cursor Pro, and X Premium+, which together ran about $90 a month. Teams can get Premium, Super, and Ultra. Enterprises can negotiate custom plans.
The Deal Behind the Bundle
This bundle follows a series of acquisitions. SpaceX absorbed xAI in February. In June, it agreed to buy Anysphere, the company behind Cursor, for $60 billion in stock, and the deal closed in August.
The reasoning was simple. xAI has trailed OpenAI and Anthropic on coding tools. Cursor brought one of the most widely used AI editors and a large developer base. And SpaceX owns X, which gives Grok a distribution channel competitors don’t have. Now Cursor rides along with it.
That pricing choice says a lot.
“SpaceX is pricing Cursor like a perk on a social media plan,” said Mitch Ashley, vice president and practice lead for CIO & Technology Buyers and Software Lifecycle Engineering at The Futurum Group. “When a leading AI IDE is just one part of a $30 bundle, SpaceX is betting others sold on their own can’t hold a premium price. Moving an engineering team onto The X Subscription is a platform commitment made through a billing decision.”
The Shared Pool Is the Detail to Watch
The biggest change for engineering teams is how usage is counted. Under the new plans, one weekly usage pool covers both Grok and Cursor. Inside Grok, that same pool covers chat, image generation, voice, and Grok Bot.
That’s convenient for an individual. It’s harder for a team lead. A developer who spends the afternoon generating images or chatting with Grok draws down the same allowance that powers their Cursor agents.
There’s also a model question. Third-party models in Cursor stay available on Premium and above, but they’re billed separately at API list prices. Plenty of teams run Cursor with models from other providers today. Under the bundle, the included allowance favors SpaceXSI’s own models. Anything else runs on a second meter.
Questions to Ask Before You Switch
Switching is one-way. Once accounts are unified, you can’t go back, and new sign-ups can’t use the old plans. Existing SuperGrok, Cursor, and X Premium subscribers can keep what they have for now.
Before anyone moves a team over, a few questions are worth asking:
- Identity. The plan links Grok, Cursor, and X accounts. Does your organization want developer tooling tied to social media identities? How do SSO and offboarding work on team plans?
- Cost visibility. Can you separate coding usage from chat and image generation inside the shared pool? If not, chargeback gets messy.
- Model choice. How much of your team’s Cursor work runs on third-party models? Price that separately, since the bundle doesn’t cover it.
- Lock-in. Cursor competes with GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, and OpenAI Codex. A cheaper bundle today makes it harder to swap one piece out tomorrow.
Ashley sees metering as the core issue. “A shared usage pool makes AI coding spend harder to attribute, and a team that can’t meter its coding agents can’t govern them at scale,” he said. “Judge it on how much lock-in your organization can tolerate, because the bundle will win feature-per-dollar comparisons. Watch whether the team plans give platform leaders per-developer usage data and enterprise identity controls.”
The Bigger Picture
Bundling is an old playbook. Microsoft did it with Office. Google did it with Workspace. It works because it lowers the price of each piece and raises the cost of leaving.
For individual developers who already use X and want Grok and Cursor, the $30 tier is a real discount. For teams, the potential savings look attractive on paper. But they come with a shared meter, a tilt toward in-house models, and a permanent account merge.
So treat The X Subscription like any other platform decision. Pilot it with a small group. Measure where the usage goes. And don’t commit until the team plans show you who is using what.
