Real estate agents in Southaven, Miss. have gone door to door offering to buy houses if owners agree not to join a lawsuit over noise from the company’s power plant.

According to a recording heard by The Guardian, a realtor told one resident that 18 homeowners agreed to sell out of a target of 418 homes, however, xAI has not confirmed these numbers.

A private company is not the government. It should not be able to empty a neighborhood by pairing a check with a legal waiver.

xAI’s offer to buy the homes is unethical, and residents are not getting a fair deal.

The waiver is the problem. Sellers are told they will be “unable to recover any damages or other relief from the Haley lawsuit,” yet the offers rest on Zillow value estimates and the letter listed no prices.

A house gets a price while a legal claim for months of noise gets none. Every signature also shrinks the group of neighbors who can sue.Renters show how some residents have limited choices.

Danielle Dillard was given 60 days to leave after her landlord took the buyout, one month after she renewed her lease, and she said, “It was never optional.”

Southaven’s mayor Darren Musselwhite said, “Nobody is being forced to be bought out,” and called the process “totally voluntary.”

Census estimates show renters live in one-third to two-thirds of homes in the class-action neighborhoods, so voluntary for an owner can mean eviction for a tenant.The noise behind the lawsuit is real. Resident Elizabeth Rose said the plant, “sounds like an airplane engine but it’s continuous, it’s incredibly loud.”

It was also reported that the turbines ran without required federal permits. Musselwhite said that city monitors found no breach of the 70-decibel limit, but critics point to evidence suggesting that persistent noise of 50 decibels or more may harm health.


xAI said it intends to be a good neighbor. Resident Gavin Foust, who refused to take the offer because of the no-sue condition, called the situation “reckless and shameful.”

Good neighbors do not make people choose between a payout and their legal rights.

The real contest is far bigger than Southaven. xAI is racing to build the next frontier of artificial intelligence, with general AI as the prize and other frontier AI companies as competitors.

Elon Musk wants the world’s largest cluster of AI supercomputers, and his AI unit posted $7.6 billion in third quarter revenue, up 830%.

Mississippi’s governor Tate Reeves has even claimed that limits on data centers mean “surrendering the future of technology to China.” In this race, buying all 418 homes would cost roughly $75 million, less than 1% of SpaceX’s first half revenue.

The buyout is a rounding error for xAI but are the homes for the people who live there.

Fair treatment means paying for homes and legal claims separately and never tying to buyout people to silence them.
