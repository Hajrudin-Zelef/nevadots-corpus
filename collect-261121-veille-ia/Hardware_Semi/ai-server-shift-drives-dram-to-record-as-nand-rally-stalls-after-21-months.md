PC DRAM prices hit a record high again in Oct. on supply shortages driven by expanded production of AI server memory. As Korea's memory makers, including Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, focus capacity on server DRAM, supply of PC products has declined. NAND flash, by contrast, showed a mixed trend by market, with gains in some products stalling as higher prices dampened demand.

According to DRAMeXchange under market research firm TrendForce on the 30th, the average fixed transaction price in Oct. for DDR4 8-gigabit (Gb) 1Gx8, a mainstream PC DRAM product, was tallied at $27.5. That was up 5.77% from the prior month ($26) and marked a record high since the survey began in Jun. 2016. Compared with the price at that time, $2.9, it is about 9.5 times higher.

Prices for memory modules supplied to PC makers also rose. In Oct., the average contract price for DDR4 8-gigabyte (GB) modules was $170, up 14.86% from the prior month ($148). DDR5 16GB modules climbed 11.34% to $275 from $247, and DDR5 8GB modules rose 24.06% from the prior month to $165.

The main reason for the price rise is cited as the reallocation of production capacity by memory makers. Major DRAM manufacturers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are focusing on production of AI Server Memory, reducing supply of PC DRAM. As production lines are prioritized for higher-revenue server products, supply constraints for PC memory continue.

TrendForce projected that next year's PC DRAM output by Korean DRAM manufacturers will decrease by more than 20% from this year. Considering reduced purchases by PC makers and a U.S. memory company securing long-term contract volumes, it also raised the possibility that the decline could exceed 40%.

Even as PC demand slows, supply shortages are supporting higher prices. TrendForce maintained its prior outlook that PC DRAM contract prices in the fourth quarter will rise 13%–18% from the previous quarter. Although the pace would ease from the third quarter's 18%–23% increase, it said the upward price trend will continue.

However, PC makers' burden from higher prices is also growing. As clients pushed back against suppliers' proposed hikes of around 15%–20%, actual transactions have concentrated in the 10%–15% increase range. TrendForce expected most fourth-quarter contract price negotiations to wrap up in Nov.

NAND flash prices diverged by product. The average fixed transaction price in Oct. for 128Gb 16Gx8 multi-level cell (MLC), a mainstream NAND product for memory cards and USBs, was $30.61, unchanged from the prior month. The 21-month streak of gains has halted.

MLC NAND faced a brake on price increases as clients cut purchases amid high prices and supply constraints. TrendForce said demand for MLC is declining as clients switch to the relatively cheaper single-level cell (SLC) NAND or replace it with higher-density triple-level cell (TLC) products.

SLC NAND, by contrast, extended its gains. In Oct., SLC NAND prices rose about 5%–10% from the prior month. Prices for higher-capacity products of 4Gb or more, used in automobiles, industrial controllers, and low Earth orbit satellites, climbed 9.0%–9.9%. The shift of semiconductor makers' production facilities toward advanced three-dimensional (3D) NAND also played a role by reducing supply of legacy-node products.

TrendForce forecast that the price uptrend for high-capacity SLC products will continue in the fourth quarter. MLC, however, could stagnate at elevated levels or slip slightly as clients' product transitions reduce demand.
