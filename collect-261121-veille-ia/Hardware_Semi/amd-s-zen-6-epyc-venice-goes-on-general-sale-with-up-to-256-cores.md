# AMD's Zen 6 EPYC "Venice" goes on general sale with up to 256 cores

The EPYC 9006 series is made on TSMC's 2 nm process, and its top chip, the 256-core EPYC 9996, costs $14,904. AMD hasn't said when Zen 6 comes to Ryzen.

AMD made its 6th-generation EPYC server processors, code-named Venice and the first built on its Zen 6 cores, generally available on November 10. AMD launched them in July and began shipping them to customers between July and September; cloud servers running them are planned for early 2027. Venice is made on TSMC's 2 nm process, AMD said in May.

## The EPYC 9996

The top model, the EPYC 9996, has 256 cores and 512 threads, a 2.55 GHz base clock, a boost of up to 4.1 GHz and 1,024 MB of L3 cache. Its default power is 600 W, adjustable down to 400 W; AMD now calls this default CPU power instead of TDP. It costs $14,904 each in batches of 1,000, against $11,988 for the 5th-generation EPYC 9965.

Each socket takes 16 channels of DDR5 at up to 8,000 MT/s, or MRDIMMs at up to 12,800 MT/s. AMD says these are the first processors to support JEDEC-standard MRDIMMs at that speed. The 9996 has 96 lanes of PCIe 6.0 and goes in one- and two-socket servers.

Below it sit a second 256-core chip, the EPYC 9956 (1.70 GHz base, 400 W), then 192, 168, 128 and 96 cores, and high-frequency models that boost to 5.3 GHz. The cheapest is the 8-core EPYC 9016 on the smaller SP8 platform, at $700.

AMD claims up to 2.2 times the performance of a server with Intel's Xeon 6980P and up to 3.7 times one built on NVIDIA's Vera CPU. Those are AMD's own tests.

## And Ryzen?

Not yet. The announcement doesn't mention Ryzen, and AMD's remarks with its third-quarter results say only that it expects to "outperform the market as we deliver our Ryzen roadmap". AMD has given no date for Zen 6 desktop or laptop processors.
