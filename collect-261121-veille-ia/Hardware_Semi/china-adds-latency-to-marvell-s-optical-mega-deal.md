NEW YORK, Nov 2 (Reuters Breakingviews) - Marvell Technology (MRVL.O) boss Matt Murphy picked a precarious moment for one of the semiconductor industry's biggest takeovers. The company said on Monday it plans to buy data-center laser maker Lumentum (LITE.O) for an implied $115 billion, including debt. The strategic logic is clear, but the economic payoff is distant. Regulatory approval is also a wild card, adding nearly two years of murkiness, with Beijing potentially prolonging the pain.

It's an industrially sound deal. Marvell provides custom machine-learning chips for tech giants like Alphabet(GOOGL.O). Lumentum contributes scarce photonic components. Together, they could offer a more complete package for moving data within and between server farms, challenging rival Broadcom (AVGO.O). The optical transceiver market is expected to grow roughly five-fold from this year to nearly $300 billion by 2030, according to Bernstein analysts.

The all-stock agreement is already showing strains. Marvell's shares fell approximately 11% on Monday afternoon, while Lumentum's dropped nearly a fifth. The equity swap values Lumentum shares at $778 apiece versus the $1,132 implied offer, and about 12% above where the target is trading now.

More than $1 billion in annual cost savings hardly justifies the hefty price. Taxed at 17% and capitalized on a multiple of 10, they create more than $8 billion of extra value before one-off integration costs. Existing Marvell shareholders would capture just two-thirds of the synergies, barely 2.5% of its undisturbed $220 billion equity value. Lumentum shareholders get the rest. Other inducements, including a touted $60 billion of additional addressable markets, to arrive at a $550 billion opportunity in four years, are questionable.

Government clearance adds complexity. Marvell expects to close the deal in late 2027, potentially extending the process three times until August 2028. Failure to secure specified approvals could trigger a $3.2 billion termination fee. China is a tricky spot. Despite limited product overlap between the two companies, Beijing could scrutinize the duo's control over critical optical components. An outright block may be less attractive given Chinese manufacturers' reliance on advanced lasers from Lumentum, but they could demand supply guarantees that dilute Marvell's strategic advantage.

Another potential thorn is Broadcom, which could theoretically intervene. For Marvell, the idea is that a stronger AI networking empire will eventually outweigh the cost of waiting. In a fast-moving technology cycle, however, nearly two years is an eternity.
