Piper Sandler’s research note, as reported by The News International and TechSpot, says Amazon, Apple, AMD, Google, Tesla, Microsoft, Nvidia and Qualcomm have been examining Intel’s 14A process and that no one has committed to a deal yet. Intel’s own third-quarter 2026 results release confirms one concrete step: the 14A 0.9 process design kit (PDK) was released in October. As of November 13, 2026, that release is the official record; the eight names come from an analyst’s note.

## One Piper Sandler note named eight companies, and none has signed

Piper gives three reasons for the interest, as relayed by The News International: Intel’s technological progress, TSMC’s full capacity until 2028, and chip producers’ wish for alternatives outside Taiwan. The same report supplies the caveat that matters: “Evaluating a process is a long way from committing a product line to it.” TechSpot adds the timing, that Piper says Intel needs to sign an anchor customer in late 2026 or early 2027 before a planned 2028 production ramp.

Tesla is the one name with a documented public tie, and it runs through Terafab. TechTimes reported on October 22, 2026 that the joint Tesla-SpaceX-xAI-Intel Terafab venture, announced in April 2026, has signaled intent to use both 18A and 14A. That is a venture’s stated intent, so the table records it as intent; our report on Intel’s Terafab foundry deal covers the arrangement itself. For the other companies, the reporting traces back to the Piper note.

## Intel’s own record since July: a 0.9 kit, certified EDA flows, tools at Fab 62

What Intel and its partners have put on record describes a node and its ecosystem getting ready, and it calls the customers “potential”. The Q3 release, dated October 29, 2026, lists “Released Intel 14A 0.9 process design kit (PDK) in October” under foundry momentum. Intel Foundry VP Ben Sell told Herald Business that PDK 0.9 “gives them the confidence to design products based on the Intel 14A process,” and Herald reports that PDK 1.0 is planned for the first quarter of 2027.

The design-tool side is also dated. Synopsys announced on July 27, 2026 the certification of AI-powered EDA flows on Intel 14A. On the physical side, TechTimes reported on October 22, 2026 that production tools are being installed at Fab 62, Intel’s factory at the Ocotillo campus in Chandler, Arizona, and that Intel confirmed High-NA EUV will support volume manufacturing from 2028.

On customers, Intel’s language is careful. On the Q3 call, CEO Lip-Bu Tan said Intel was “making progress with multiple potential customers on the 14A process,” per Herald Business. CFO Zinsner said at a Deutsche Bank investor conference, as reported by Bits&Chips, that engagements with external foundry customers “have significantly increased” and that customers are “moving away from just looking at data” toward asking how much capacity they can get. That is a step beyond evaluation, and still short of a contract.

## The anchor-customer deadline falls in the same window as PDK 1.0

Piper’s condition is specific: Intel needs at least one anchor customer by the end of 2026 or the beginning of 2027 to reach production in 2028, per The News International. Herald Business gives the schedule it must fit: PDK 1.0 in the first quarter of 2027, risk production in the second half of 2027, a broader ramp in 2028. The deadline lands in the same window as PDK 1.0 and ahead of risk production, so an anchor customer who signs early in that window would be signing on the 0.9 kit. That is consistent with Sell’s description of 0.9 as the version that gives designers confidence.

Intel’s own statements make the deadline binding. TechTimes reports that Intel has repeatedly told investors it will not make the capital investments needed to bring 14A to high-volume manufacturing without a significant external foundry customer. The News International quotes Tan warning that “it will not be economical to develop and manufacture Intel 14A and successor leading-edge nodes on a go-forward basis” without one. Meanwhile Bits&Chips notes that, given long lead times for semiconductor equipment, Intel already needs to start placing orders and preparing production capacity (see our ASML tool-demand report). Intel has to find its customer on the same timeline it needs to order tools, which is why we read the date, not the list of names, as the pressure point.

Two money figures circulate and they differ. Piper’s note, as reported by The News International, tied a $20 billion share offering to a possible 14A fab of 20,000 wafer starts per month at a tooling cost of roughly $10 billion. Intel’s Q3 release says it completed a $23 billion public equity offering. We use Intel’s $23 billion for the offering; the 20,000 wafer starts and $10 billion are Piper’s estimate for a fab.

## What Intel’s 14A targets and the 5% line claim against TSMC

Intel’s SEC filing describes 14A as “the next generation node beyond Intel 18A and Intel 18A-P.” The name is a generation label: 14A is a 14-angstrom-class name, about 1.4 nm in the old naming scheme, and node names no longer measure a literal transistor dimension. Intel’s newsroom says high-volume manufacturing of its 18A generation began in 2025. 14A is the generation after 18A, and it is not in any chip on sale now.

Intel’s foundry process page states the 14A targets against Intel 18A: 15 to 20% higher performance at the same power, or 25 to 35% lower power at the same performance, and up to 30% higher chip density. It says the node uses RibbonFET 2 gate-all-around transistors and PowerDirect backside power delivery. These are Intel’s projections, not third-party measurements.

Naga Chandrasekaran of Intel told KeyBanc that Intel expects 14A to land within 5% of TSMC’s A14 on performance. TechTimes calls the statement intentionally ambiguous: it means the gap will be no wider than 5%, without saying which process leads. Read it as a claim of parity, not of a lead. The News International adds that Intel’s 18A is believed to be equivalent to TSMC’s 3-nanometre class, and that by 2028 14A could be up against TSMC’s N2 or A16. TSMC’s technology page describes A16 as integrating “leading nanosheet transistors with innovative backside power rail solution.” A16 follows N2, TSMC’s first major gate-all-around nanosheet generation, and A14 is TSMC’s next 1.4nm-class node, in roughly the same late-decade window as 14A. Herald Business reports that Samsung has moved its 1.4nm mass-production target from 2027 to 2029.

On yield, TrendForce’s July 24, 2026 report quotes Tan: “I see the yield for 256 SRAM and the defect density, the performance, ahead of the schedule that I put a very tough schedule for my team.” It is a statement about progress against his own schedule and contains no defect-density number, so there is nothing in it a designer can plan a die size around.

## Why Intel gets a look: TSMC’s order book and a quarterly loss at Intel Foundry

The pull is TSMC’s capacity, which Piper says is full until 2028. TSMC’s own monthly revenue page shows the demand. For August 2026 it lists monthly revenue of NT$514.806 billion, up 53.3%. As of November 13, 2026, the same page lists October 2026 at NT$586.89 billion, up 59.7%. TSMC’s third-quarter 2026 revenue was US$46.72 billion.

The other side of the ledger is Intel’s. Its Q3 release, dated October 29, 2026, shows Intel Foundry revenue of $6.7 billion, up 58% year over year, and an operating loss of $1,853 million (about $1.9 billion). A customer base that TSMC cannot fully serve gives Intel its opening, and a foundry still losing money at the operating line is what an anchor customer would have to change.

## What 14A changes before 2028, and what a signed deal would have to show

For chip designers, the gate is the kit: Intel’s Q3 release says 0.9 was released in October, and Intel’s newsroom page on the kit calls 1.0 “the next major milestone,” expected in the first quarter of 2027. Planning around Intel’s performance, power and density figures means planning around projections until a customer publishes silicon results.

For PC buyers, 14A is not close. Intel’s schedule puts risk production for internal products in the second half of 2027 and volume manufacturing in 2028, so no chip on sale now uses it.

Our assessment is that the interest is credible and the commitment is unproven. Intel’s released kit (listed in its Q3 results release), the Synopsys certified flows, the tool installation at Fab 62 and the CFO’s capacity remarks, as reported by Bits&Chips, all point the same way, but Intel’s CEO still says “potential customers” and the Piper note says no one has committed. The anchor-customer deadline at the end of 2026 or early 2027 matters more than the eight names, and only a statement from Intel or the customer, not an analyst note, would confirm a signed deal.
