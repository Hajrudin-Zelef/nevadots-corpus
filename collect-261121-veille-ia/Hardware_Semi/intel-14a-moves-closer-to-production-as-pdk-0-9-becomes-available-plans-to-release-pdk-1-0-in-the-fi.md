# Intel 14A moves closer to production as PDK 0.9 becomes available — plans to release PDK 1.0 in the first quarter of 2027

Intel says its next-generation process technology is reaching expected performance and yield maturity.

Intel Foundry recently released Intel 14A process design kit (PDK) version 0.9, marking a significant step towards the production of this next-generation manufacturing technology. The company asserts that the production node is reaching the expected performance and yield maturity at this stage of development, with silicon validation supporting these findings. Intel intends to unveil PDK 1.0 in the first quarter of 2027, which will confirm the process's readiness for high-volume manufacturing.

PDK 0.9 provides essential information, such as transistor models and design rules, allowing chip designers to develop products for the Intel 14A process using electronic design automation (EDA) tools. The release of PDK 0.9 is particularly crucial as it offers a representation of the Intel 14A process based on actual silicon measurements, rather than theoretical models or simulations.

Consequently, customers can proceed with product development with higher confidence that the technology will meet their performance and power consumption requirements.

While PDK 0.9 does not indicate Intel 14A's readiness for mass production, it does allow customers to design products on the technology. Intel expects to validate high-volume manufacturing readiness with PDK 1.0, scheduled for Q1 2027. It is essential to note that mass production using the 14A process technology is not anticipated until 2028. During this period, Intel will utilize its 14A process in risk production for its own products during the second half of 2027.

Intel 14A builds upon the architectural foundations established with Intel 18A, incorporating several notable enhancements, including 2nd Generation RibbonFET 2-gate-all-around (GAA) transistors, PowerDirect backside power delivery, and Turbo Cells. These advancements aim to enhance performance, optimize power efficiency, and accelerate the development cycle.

Although Intel has not disclosed specific figures regarding transistor performance, power consumption, or transistor density for Intel 14A, the company is leveraging its experience gained from 12 months of Intel 18A production to expedite the maturation of the 14A process.

Moreover, Intel has revised its collaboration model with equipment and materials suppliers to engage technology development teams and external suppliers earlier in the process development cycle. This strategic shift aims to address manufacturing defects and improve equipment capabilities before they hinder yield improvements after the technology enters mass production.

By involving suppliers in the development process, Intel seeks to maintain its historical influence over semiconductor manufacturing technologies while fostering stronger partnerships and potentially gaining an advantage over competitors.
