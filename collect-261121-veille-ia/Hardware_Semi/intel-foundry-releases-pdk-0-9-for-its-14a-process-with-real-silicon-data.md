Intel Foundry has released the PDK 0.9 for its Intel 14A process, a step that offers chip designers transistor models and design rules based on real silicon measurements rather than theoretical simulations. The node builds on the foundation of Intel 18A and incorporates second-generation RibbonFET transistors, PowerDirect backside power delivery, and Turbo Cells to accelerate critical paths in CPU and GPU.

## Second-generation RibbonFET and PowerDirect in the 14A node ð

The Intel 14A process rests on three technical pillars. Second-generation RibbonFET transistors tune the channel to improve current control. PowerDirect backside power delivery separates the power network from the signal network, which reduces resistance and frees up routing area. Turbo Cells allow critical paths in CPU and GPU to be accelerated without penalizing the rest of the design. Version 1.0 of the PDK will arrive in the first quarter of 2027 and will validate the volume production planned for 2028. For a professional in the sector, this means having reliable models earlier to reduce silicon revisions and plan products with greater certainty about power consumption and frequencies.

### A PDK that promises fewer scares and more certainty ð§

For years, designing a chip has had something of a gamble to it: the design was sent to manufacturing and fingers were crossed that the silicon wouldn't return expensive surprises. With the Intel 14A PDK 0.9, the company suggests changing the ritual for something akin to planning. It remains to be seen whether the silicon-measured models deliver what they promise, because for now it's a matter of waiting for version 1.0 in 2027 and volume production in 2028. Meanwhile, design teams can keep arguing with their spreadsheets.
