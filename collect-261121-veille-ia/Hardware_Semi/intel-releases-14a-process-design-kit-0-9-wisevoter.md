# Intel Releases 14A Process Design Kit 0.9

Intel Foundry has made version 0.9 of its Intel 14A process design kit available to customers, with version 1.0 planned for early 2027.

Updated on Nov. 3, 2026 in Semiconductors

Intel Foundry made Intel 14A process design kit (PDK) version 0.9 available last week, providing a representation of the process based on actual silicon measurements. The release lets customers begin designing chips intended to enter risk production around the time the process is certified.

## Why it matters

The PDK release gives chip designers an early look at the 14A process before final certification, which can shorten the time needed to bring new designs to production. The release also reflects Intel's push to apply manufacturing lessons from its Intel 18A production to accelerate 14A maturation.

The 14A process includes 2nd Generation RibbonFET 2 gate-all-around transistors and PowerDirect backside power delivery, with Turbo Cells standard cells. Intel's stated figure of 12 months of 18A production experience is being applied to accelerate 14A, though final yield results have not been disclosed.

## The players

Intel Foundry

Intel Foundry is the contract chip manufacturing division of Intel Corporation, which offers advanced semiconductor fabrication services to external customers.

Cadence

Cadence Design Systems is a major electronic design automation company whose software is widely used by chip designers to create and verify integrated circuits.

Synopsys

Synopsys is a leading electronic design automation company known for chip design and verification software used across the semiconductor industry.

Siemens

Siemens is a global industrial conglomerate that includes an electronic design automation business serving semiconductor designers.

Keysight

Keysight Technologies is a test and measurement company whose tools are used to validate electronic and semiconductor designs.

## The details

Intel 14A builds on architectural foundations introduced with Intel 18A and adds Turbo Cells standard cells along with inline defect detection, modeling, and simulation investments. Intel has also revised its collaboration model to involve external suppliers earlier, and Cadence has established a design-technology co-optimization collaboration while Keysight, Siemens, and Synopsys announced design-tool certifications for the process.

## Timeline

1. Last week: Intel Foundry made PDK 0.9 available to customers.
2. First quarter of 2027: Intel plans to release PDK 1.0.
3. Second half of 2027: Intel expects risk production for its own 14A products.
4. 2028: Intel expects to begin mass production using 14A.

## The Tech Race

Intel's 14A release follows directly from its 18A production run, with the company applying 12 months of 18A manufacturing experience to accelerate the next node. The move positions Intel to compete more directly with rival foundries as it seeks to win external customers for its advanced process technology.

Chip designers working with Intel Foundry can begin building designs against the 14A process now, using the PDK 0.9 measurements to plan for future production. The eventual availability of 14A-based chips in consumer devices depends on the mass production timeline Intel has set for 2028.

## The takeaway

Early design kits let chip companies start work before a process is fully certified, which can compress the path from research to products. Intel's 14A timeline shows how manufacturing lessons from one node can speed up the next.

## What happens next

Intel expects to release PDK 1.0 in the first quarter of 2027, validate HVM readiness at that point, and begin risk production for its own 14A products in the second half of 2027.
