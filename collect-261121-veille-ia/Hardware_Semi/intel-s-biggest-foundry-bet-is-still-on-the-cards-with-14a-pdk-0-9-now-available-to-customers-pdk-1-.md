Intel Foundry has a milestone in its 14A process development as PDK 0.9 is now available and PDK 1.0 is on track for high-volume manufacturing.

## Intel 14A PDK 0.9 Rolls Out To Customers With PDK 1.0 On-Track For The First Quarter of 2027

The PDK 0.9 is an important milestone for the 14A process node. It was meant to be available later this year, and Intel has fulfilled its promise and now looks to deliver the final PDK 1.0 by Q1 2027.

The Intel 14A PDK 0.9 gives customers a more mature, silicon-backed representation of the process technology for product design and production planning. It is an important milestone on the path toward the PDK 1.0 and high-volume manufacturing readiness.


Right now, Intel has announced general availability of 14A PDK 0.9 to its customers. This gives the Foundry division and its customers the ability to design products on the 14A process node. PDK 0.9 brings 14A into its maturity phase, which is backed by silicon validation. Another important thing is that 0.9 PDK allows customers to create designs that can go into the fab for production.

This means that customers can have their chip going through the production line while the node is achieving final certification, and with PDK 1.0 approaching, these designs will be on track for full high-volume production.

The 14A process node will offer over 15% performance-per-watt advantage over the 18A node while enabling a 20% increase in the chip integration process. In addition, 14A is building upon proven backside power delivery architecture with PowerDirect & gate-all-around transistor technology with RibbonFET 2. 14A also leverages Turbo Cells, which further optimizes efficiency & performance.

Besides 14A, Intel is also pushing for high-volume manufacturing of its 18A-P for 2027, while 14A Gen2 is expected to bring advanced features.

I’m looking forward to sustaining this level of quality across future releases and continuing to earn our customers’ trust by delivering on our commitments with a customer-first mindset. Our next major milestone is the Intel 14A PDK 1.0, expected in the first quarter of 2027.

*Ali Farhang is Corporate Vice President and General Manager of the Foundry Design Technology Platform (DTP) organization at Intel Foundry*


Intel is also working with EDA providers such as Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens, and Keysight, who have already started offering validated tools for the next-gen process technology.

The end goal for all of this is customers. If Intel Foundry needs to win big, it needs to secure some big customers. We have been hearing a lot of potential customers with Elon Musk's TeraFab lined up as one of the early adopters of this process technology.

With Intel's recent earnings, it looks like a lot is riding on the back of 14A being a success, after all that has been promised. The company has been testing 14A for internal products too, but it will need a lot of volume and a promising yield if it needs to grab hold of customers and deliver a competitive face against TSMC.
