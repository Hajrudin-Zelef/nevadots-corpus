Intel’s release of version 0.9 of its 14A process design kit marks a new milestone for the company’s next-generation manufacturing technology and highlights the importance of electronic design automation tools in preparing advanced chips for production.

Tom’s Hardware reported on November 3 that the updated process design kit gives chip designers access to additional transistor models, design rules and other process information needed to develop products for Intel’s 14A node. Intel expects the production-ready version 1.0 kit in 2027.

## Why the development matters to Cadence

A process design kit, or PDK, connects a semiconductor manufacturer’s process technology with the software used by chip designers. The availability of a more mature PDK allows EDA vendors and their customers to test design flows, optimize layouts and prepare intellectual property for a specific manufacturing process.

Cadence Design Systems has an established design-technology co-optimization collaboration with Intel covering next-generation AI-driven design tools and semiconductor intellectual property. The relationship places Cadence among the EDA suppliers working to support designs for Intel’s future process technologies, although the PDK milestone itself was announced by Intel rather than Cadence.

For Cadence, the development is relevant because advanced-node adoption typically requires close coordination among chip designers, foundries and EDA providers. As process geometries become more complex, customers increasingly depend on certified tools, accurate models and validated design flows before committing products to manufacturing.

## CDNS shares remain separate from the Intel announcement

Cadence shares closed at **$319.92** on November 6, 2026, up **1.4%**. The available reporting does not establish that Intel’s 14A PDK update caused that move, so the stock performance should be viewed separately from the process-technology announcement.

The Intel milestone nevertheless provides an important industry signal for Cadence investors. Successful support for advanced manufacturing nodes can strengthen the value of EDA platforms and related semiconductor IP, while delays in process readiness or customer adoption could slow the timing of design activity.

Investors will likely watch for further disclosures about Intel’s 14A production schedule, the availability of certified EDA tools and evidence that major chip customers are beginning designs on the process. Those developments would offer a clearer indication of how the 14A ecosystem could translate into commercial opportunities for Cadence.
