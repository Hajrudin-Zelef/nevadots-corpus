# Lumentum CEO Says Marvell Deal Is ‘Even More Powerful’ Despite Stock Selloff

**Lumentum Holdings Inc.** LITE and **Marvell Technology Inc.** MRVL are trying to reassure investors about their planned $115 billion all-stock combination after both stocks suffered steep declines amid deal concerns and a broader selloff across AI semiconductor names.

**Hurlston Defends The Combination**

Lumentum CEO **Michael Hurlston** defended the transaction, arguing that both companies continue to perform well and that the combined business would be stronger.

"Their numbers are phenomenal. Our numbers are phenomenal, and we feel the combination is even more powerful," Hurlston told CNBC’s Jim Cramer.

He also acknowledged the negative shareholder reaction. "Shareholders are not happy … It’s been a tough couple of weeks," Hurlston said.

Marvell and Lumentum shares have fallen 45% and 42%, respectively, since the Oct. 28 close, when Bloomberg reported that Marvell was nearing an acquisition of Lumentum.

The stocks have also lost roughly 30%-40% over the past month amid broader weakness across AI-related semiconductor stocks.

The companies formally announced the transaction on Nov. 2. The all-stock deal implied an enterprise value of $115 billion for Lumentum and is expected to close in the first half of 2028.

**Fiber Shift Underpins Deal Strategy**

Hurlston said the combination brings together Marvell’s semiconductor and networking technology with Lumentum’s optical components as AI data centers increasingly move from copper connections toward fiber optics.

He said that transition, rather than simply an increase in the number of data centers, could become an important growth driver for the combined company.

**Earnings And S-4 Could Reassure Investors**

Hurlston acknowledged that management must do more to convince shareholders. "We’ve got work to do with our shareholders," he said.

He pointed to Marvell’s Dec. 1 earnings report, Lumentum’s upcoming results and the merger-related S-4 filing as opportunities to show investors how both businesses are performing.

Hurlston said those disclosures could show that "there’s nothing wrong here," adding that the companies are "even better together."

Marvell is expected to report EPS of $1.09, up from 76 cents a year earlier, on revenue of $3.16 billion, compared with $2.08 billion in the prior-year period.

**Lumentum Results Show Demand Remains Strong**

Hurlston said Lumentum has not seen a slowdown in demand despite broader questions around AI infrastructure spending.

On Nov. 2, Lumentum reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 adjusted earnings of $4.57 per share, above the $4.19 estimate, while revenue of $1.263 billion topped expectations of $1.249 billion.

Hurlston said stronger financial performance from both companies could eventually help restore their valuation multiples.

**Regulatory Risk Remains**

Separately, Bloomberg analysts previously expressed skepticism about whether the transaction would close, citing uncertainty around China’s regulatory review.
