In early 2026, takeover talks between SambaNova Systems and Intel had hit a standstill. Six months later, SambaNova emerged with an US$11bn valuation, backed by Intel as a strategic partner and investor rather than an owner.

This turnaround highlights where the AI chip market is heading. While training giant models made NVIDIA the world’s most me-valued chipmaker, running those models for millions of users presents a different challenge. Speed and cost per token now decide which company wins.

Consequently, SambaNova has pivoted towards the inference market. Its new SN50 chip is pitched squarely at the agentic AI era.

SambaNova’s US$11bn valuation highlights the central role that fast inference now plays in the enterprise AI stack


## What is SambaNova’s tech ecosystem?

Founded in 2017 and based in San Jose, US, SambaNova designs its own processors, which it terms Reconfigurable Dataflow Units. Rather than selling chips in isolation, the company packages them into full systems and services.

These services include SambaCloud for developers and SambaStack and SambaManaged for operators who want to run their own inference clouds. It also offers SambaRack hardware for on-premises deployment.

The SN50, unveiled in February 2026, serves as the centrepiece of this technology ecosystem. SambaNova claims it runs up to five times faster than competing chips at up to three times lower total cost of ownership.

The chip links up to 256 accelerators and supports models with more than 10 trillion parameters. These performance figures have helped the business win serious customers across multiple sectors.

Rodrigo Liang, Co-Founder and CEO at SambaNova Systems, says: “SambaNova’s US$11bn valuation highlights the central role that fast inference now plays in the enterprise AI stack.”

## Enterprise clients deploying SambaNova hardware

JPMorganChase selected SambaNova as an inference infrastructure partner and is deploying SN40 and SN50 systems on premises. SoftBank Corp. will be the first company to deploy the SN50 in its next-generation data centres in Japan.

In addition, TEPCO Systems will distribute the technology across Japan’s power sector. In Europe, OVHcloud and Infercom have built inference services on SambaNova hardware.

The relationship with Intel has evolved into a strategic partnership rather than a corporate acquisition. A multi-year collaboration covers an Intel Xeon-based AI cloud and joint selling initiatives across global markets.

It also includes a blueprint for heterogeneous inference that uses GPUs for prefill, SambaNova chips for decode and Xeon 6 processors for agentic tools. Intel Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan also chairs SambaNova’s board.

## Backing SambaNova’s growth

Investors have followed this market momentum. A Series E funding round of more than US$350m led by Vista Equity Partners and Cambium Capital closed in February 2026.

General Atlantic led the first US$1bn close of a Series F in July 2026. The company also beat its sales plan for the fiscal year ending January 2026.

Its European wins matter to this growth strategy. OVHcloud, which runs more than 500,000 servers in 46 data centres, chose SambaNova for its AI Endpoints service.

Meanwhile, Infercom keeps all data and model execution inside EU borders for customers worried about data sovereignty.

Darrin Alves, CIO of Infrastructure Platforms at JPMorganChase, says: “At JPMorganChase, AI infrastructure has to meet a very high bar for performance, control and reliability.”

SambaNova has gone from a possible acquisition target to one of the best funded challengers in AI hardware in less than a year. Its bet relies on a simple premise.

As AI moves from training to everyday use, the market will reward the fastest and cheapest way to serve tokens.

The next 12 months will show whether the SN50 lives up to its performance claims at scale. Production proof will soon arrive with implementations for JPMorganChase, SoftBank and Intel’s heterogeneous blueprint all due to go live.
