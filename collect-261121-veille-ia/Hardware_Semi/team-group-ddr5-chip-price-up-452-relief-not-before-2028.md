Team Group CEO Gerry Chen (Chen Ching-wen) said the most severe stretch of the DDR5 shortage runs through the end of the second quarter of 2027, that he sees “no downward price trend whatsoever” in 2027, and that he does not expect supply and demand to balance until the second half of 2028 at the earliest, Fortune reported on November 10, 2026. The price cited on the call, a 16Gb DDR5 chip going from $8.70 in October 2025 to $48 in September 2026, is a 452% increase. It is a contract price for a chip, not the price of a retail kit.

Our assessment is that the direction holds up. Checked against four other price series, the chip move sits inside a band of 4.4 to 5.5 times earlier levels, and the forecasters who give a date, from AMD and Jefferies to SK hynix, put relief in 2028 or later. What the 452% cannot tell you is the price of a kit, and Team Group sells into this shortage, so it is not a neutral witness on how long it lasts.

## Chen’s call: worst through the second quarter of 2027, no price fall in 2027, balance in the second half of 2028 at the earliest

The procurement detail explains why a module brand is worried. Chen said, “We have contracts but can’t get enough volume, so we have to go to the spot market and pay a premium to grab supply,” as Fortune quotes him, citing Tom’s Hardware. Even with contracts in place, Team Group is paying a premium to secure chips: Fortune reports that Chen put the gap between spot and contract prices at 30% to 50%.

Team Group is selling well into the squeeze. Fortune reports record third-quarter revenue of NT$10.14 billion (about $312 million), up nearly 34% on the prior quarter. Business-to-business sales were 64.6% of revenue over the first three quarters of 2026, against 24.6% in the same period of 2025. The company plans to raise production capacity by about 50% with a new facility, which Crypto Briefing notes addresses assembly, not the underlying chip supply.

These are management statements from a module brand that buys its chips from fabs, and a seller benefits when scarcity lifts prices: the same Crypto Briefing report makes that point about the CEO’s forecast. That does not make the forecast wrong, and the forecast comparison below shows it matches most others, but it is why the number deserves a check before anyone repeats it.

On the product side, Team Group announced on October 15, 2026 that its T-CREATE EXPERT Ai CQDIMM DDR5 desktop memory won the GOOD DESIGN AWARD 2026, and the announcement calls it the company’s first DDR5 CQDIMM desktop memory with a capacity of 128GB per module.

## $8.70 to $48 is a 452% rise, 5.5x and “more than fivefold” at once, and it is a chip contract price

Fortune says contract prices for a 16Gb DDR5 chip rose from $8.70 in October 2025 to $48 in September 2026, according to figures cited on the call, and describes the move as more than five times the earlier price. TechRadar, citing Fortune, writes an increase of more than 450%. Both descriptions fit the same move.

| Phrasing | Value | How it is derived | 
|---|---|---|
| Contract price, October 2025 | $8.70 | Figure cited on the call, per Fortune | 
| Contract price, September 2026 | $48 | Figure cited on the call, per Fortune | 
| Change | $39.30 | $48 minus $8.70 | 
| Increase | 452% | Change divided by the October 2025 price | 
| Multiple | 5.5x | September 2026 price divided by the October 2025 price | 
| Level against the original | 552% | The multiple written as a percentage | 

The 552% figure describes a level. Reading it as the increase would overstate the rise by 100 points.

The unit is 16Gb, gigabits, not 16GB. Sixteen gigabits is 2GB, so a 32GB kit carries 16 of these dies. Both prices are contract points from October 2025 and September 2026, neither is a November 2026 quote, and because Fortune attributes them to figures cited on the call, they are Team Group’s presentation of contract pricing rather than an independent index.

The 16-die count also limits what the series can do. At $8.70 a die, 16 dies cost $139; at $48 they cost $768. Both exceed the $108 and $572 that a mainstream US 32GB DDR5-6000 kit averaged in August 2025 and August 2026, on PCPartPicker figures compiled by Tom’s Hardware and reported by ram-index. The chip series is therefore a signal for direction and ratio, not a bill of materials for a kit.

## At checkout the same shortage reads 4.4x to 5.3x: kits from $108 to $572, then $429 to $600 on November 3

Chips reach a buyer inside modules, so the retail record is the real test of the claim. Each series below has its own measure and window, which is why the multiples differ.

| Series | Measure and source | Earlier | Later | Multiple | 
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US mainstream 32GB DDR5-6000 kit | August average, PCPartPicker figures compiled by Tom’s Hardware, via ram-index | $108 (August 2025) | $572 (August 2026) | 5.3x | 
| German retail DDR5 | Retail data tracked by eteknix | July 2025 as the base | 419% above that base | 5.2x | 
| Corsair Vengeance RGB 32GB DDR5-6000 kit | One product’s price, per TweakTown | About $100 (late 2025) | About $440 (June 2026) | 4.4x | 

The cheapest US 32GB DDR5-6000 kit reached $449.97 on October 14, up from about $400 in August, Tom’s Hardware reported (via ShiftDelete). That sits below the $572 August average because one figure is the lowest listing and the other a mean across mainstream kits, so they do not conflict. As of November 3, 2026, capitalandcompute.net puts a US 32GB DDR5 kit at about $429 to $600, or $13.41 to $18.75 per gigabyte, and describes retail kits as up roughly four to five times from mid-2025 to mid-2026.

Placed on one scale with the chip price and with TrendForce’s compounded DRAM contract figures, which ram-index puts at about five times their third-quarter 2025 level by the third quarter of 2026, the series fall between 4.4x and 5.5x. The windows differ, so the match is approximate, but the chip price sits at the top of the band and the Corsair kit at the bottom, the order you would expect if kit prices trail chip costs.

## Price, not volume: 4% more bits, 369% more DRAM revenue and 20% higher phone prices

The shortage also shows up in numbers that do not depend on Team Group. Counterpoint Research reports that global DRAM revenue rose 369% year over year in the third quarter of 2026 to just under $200 billion, while bit shipments rose only about 4%, so price rather than volume drove the gain.

SK hynix’s third-quarter results, announced October 28, 2026, show the same pull from the supplier side: revenue of 104.6155 trillion won, operating profit of 83.3338 trillion won and net profit of 39.7756 trillion won, with quarterly sales passing 100 trillion won for the first time. The release credits a significant increase in server DDR5 sales for the DRAM result, and says the company plans to raise the share of its 10nm-class 6th generation (1c) process and expand sales of DDR5 and LPDDR5 SOCAMM for AI servers. ram-index adds that a deficit of 1% to 2% of bits produced roughly a fivefold rise in contract prices, which shows how little missing supply it takes.

Readers who see softer spot quotes may wonder whether the squeeze is easing. TrendForce said consumer DRAM spot prices “have retreated from their peaks” while contract shortages persist, and it expects conventional DRAM contract prices to rise another 10% to 15% quarter over quarter in the fourth quarter of 2026 (ShiftDelete, TechRadar). Spot and contract are different markets, so a softer spot quote does not contradict a contract price that is still rising, and the $48 chip figure is a contract number.

Downstream, Counterpoint says global smartphone shipments fell 20% year over year in the third quarter and that manufacturers passing on higher component costs pushed retail smartphone prices up 20% so far in 2026.

## Forecasters cluster at 2028, SK hynix says 2030, and each speaks from a position

Chen’s date is the one most forecasters share. ram-index says the earliest year any published model puts DRAM supply back above demand is 2028 (its buying guide), and that new fabs ramp meaningfully only from the second half of 2027.

| Speaker | Claim | Reported by | 
|---|---|---|
| Team Group, Gerry Chen | Balance in the second half of 2028 at the earliest; no downward price trend in 2027 | Fortune | 
| AMD, David McAfee | DDR5 prices stay high until around 2028, with AI demand shifting supply toward HBM and DDR4 production reduced | TweakTown | 
| Micron, Sanjay Mehrotra | Conditions “much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026” (prepared remarks, September 30) | ram-index | 
| SK hynix | Long-term agreements secured for more than 50% of 2027 and 2028 production capacity; a section headed “Memory supply shortage to persist through 2030” (own results release, checked November 13, 2026) | SK hynix newsroom; Fortune | 
| TrendForce | Conventional DRAM contract prices up another 10% to 15% in the fourth quarter of 2026; contract shortages support gradual increases in 2027 | TechRadar; ShiftDelete | 
| Jefferies | RAM prices up another 40% to 50% in the third quarter and 30% to 40% in the fourth, with little relief before 2028 | eteknix | 

Read together, Chen’s timeline is the shared earliest bound, not an outlier. The 2030 outlook is SK hynix’s own: its October 28, 2026 results release carries a section headed “Memory supply shortage to persist through 2030” (checked November 13, 2026), and Fortune repeats the date. It comes from a company that already has more than half of its 2027 and 2028 capacity under long-term agreements, so we would weight it as the forecast most tied to the speaker’s own order book. The forecasters differ on the fourth quarter: Jefferies expects 30% to 40% and TrendForce 10% to 15%, although the two may be measuring different price sets. They agree on direction, and the disagreement affects how fast prices rise, not whether.

The suppliers’ own pages show where DDR5 capacity is being aimed. SK hynix announced mass production of its 192GB SOCAMM2 on April 20, 2026, and after the OCP Global Summit (October 16, 2026) it showed a 256GB 3DS RDIMM, a 128GB DDR5 MRDIMM and the 192GB SOCAMM2. Micron’s DDR5 page lists server RDIMMs up to 9,200 MT/s and MRDIMMs up to 8,800 MT/s in capacities from 32GB to 256GB, aimed at data centers. For reference, SK hynix’s DDR5 page describes twice the speed of DDR4 at an operating voltage of 1.1V and 20% less power, and Micron’s page lists 1.1V against 1.2V for DDR4.

## Buying through mid-2027: the non-business share of Team Group’s revenue fell from about 75% to about 35%

Team Group’s mix shift shows where the squeeze may land. Business-to-business sales rose from 24.6% to 64.6% of its revenue, so everything else, which includes retail, fell from about 75% to about 35%. That is consistent with retail kits being served after business customers, though it does not prove it, and the planned 50% capacity expansion adds assembly, not chips, so it does not change that.

### Buying a kit

The prices to plan around are about $429 to $600 for a US 32GB DDR5 kit as of November 3, and $449.97 for the cheapest 32GB DDR5-6000 kit on October 14. Against that, TrendForce expects conventional DRAM contract prices up another 10% to 15% in the fourth quarter of 2026, and Chen sees no decline in 2027. The earliest balance date on the table is the second half of 2028, which starts in July 2028, about 20 months after November 2026, so waiting for a cheaper kit is a long bet. A softer spot quote is not a lower contract cost. Buy the capacity the machine will use: our comparison of 32GB and 64GB DDR5 configurations is the place to weigh the step up, and if you already own a DDR5 kit, our guide to overclocking DDR5 RAM is the place to look before buying again.

DDR4 is not a clean escape. AMD expects DDR5 prices to stay high until around 2028 because of AI-driven demand shifting supply toward HBM and reduced DDR4 production, per TweakTown. Tech Insider’s page on the AM4 lifeline and the DDR5 price spike is the one to read for the platform side of that choice; its headline percentage uses its own baseline and measure, so it will not match the 452% chip figure here.

### Ordering for an office or lab

SK hynix says long-term agreements cover more than 50% of its 2027 and 2028 production capacity, and Chen says even Team Group’s contracts do not deliver enough volume. A buyer without an agreement sits behind both. Ask suppliers for the validity period of every quote and for written allocation commitments on quantities, and expect server-class parts, such as the RDIMMs and MRDIMMs Micron lists for data centers, to be where chipmakers aim their capacity.

### Repeating the 452% figure

Write it as “a reported contract price for a 16Gb DDR5 chip, as cited on a Team Group call reported by Fortune on November 10, 2026, up 452% from October 2025 to September 2026,” not as “RAM is up 452%.” For what a buyer pays, use a dated kit price instead, such as $429 to $600 for 32GB on November 3 or $449.97 for the cheapest US kit on October 14.
