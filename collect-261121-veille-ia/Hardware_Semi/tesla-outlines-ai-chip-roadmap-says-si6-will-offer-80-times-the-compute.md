# Tesla outlines AI chip roadmap, says SI6 will offer 80 times the compute

The company now brands its accelerators as super intelligence chips, while its D3 training chip moves to a multi-chiplet design with stacked HBM.

Tesla has unveiled an updated roadmap for its artificial intelligence accelerators, giving a clearer picture of how the company plans to develop its custom chips, Tom's Hardware reports.

According to the report, Tesla expects its sixth-generation inference accelerator to deliver 80 times the compute performance of its current fourth-generation chip. The company's D3 training accelerator will also undergo a major architectural change, abandoning an SRAM-centric approach in favour of a multi-chiplet design with stacked HBM memory.

Alongside the technical plans, Tesla has started describing its accelerators as "super intelligence" rather than "artificial intelligence" hardware. As a result, the chips have been renamed with an "SI" prefix, making the sixth-generation inference part SI6.

The rebranding follows a similar move announced by Elon Musk, who recently confirmed that SpaceXAI will be renamed SpaceXSI for the same reason. Tesla has for years designed its own chips to power its Autopilot driver-assistance systems and to train its artificial intelligence models, reducing its reliance on off-the-shelf hardware from outside suppliers.
