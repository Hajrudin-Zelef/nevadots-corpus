# Trump Launches Super Intelligence Force (SIF): Who Leads It, What It Does and Why It Matters

President Donald J. Trump on Sunday, 4 October 2026, announced the formation of the Super Intelligence Force (SIF), a new federal task force charged with coordinating Washington’s policy on advanced artificial intelligence.

Posting on Truth Social, the president framed the body as the next step after last week’s White House summit with leading technology chief executives: “I am announcing the formation of the Super Intelligence Force (SIF). The Super Intelligence Force is tasked with coordinating the effort of the Federal Government to ensure that America continues to lead the World in Super Intelligence, which many say is bigger than the Industrial Revolution, and the Internet, and will protect the interests, and improve the lives, of all Americans.”

The announcement caps a week in which the administration rewrote the federal vocabulary of AI, secured a voluntary safety pledge from six of the world’s most valuable technology companies and placed national security officials at the centre of AI governance.

## What Is the Super Intelligence Force Announced by President Trump?

According to the president’s post, the SIF will coordinate the federal government’s engagement with consumers, public interest groups, religious organisations, critical infrastructure providers and the companies developing what the administration now calls Super Intelligence. In practice, that makes it a single coordinating point inside the executive branch rather than a new regulator. The announcement did not describe new rule-making powers, a dedicated budget or a statutory footing.

The choice of the word “Force” echoes Trump’s creation of the Space Force during his first term, and the framing is deliberately competitive. The stated objective is to keep the United States ahead of global rivals, with China the implicit benchmark, while protecting American consumers.

## Who Will Lead the Super Intelligence Force?

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will lead the task force. He will be joined by Federal Trade Commission Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Emil Michael, the Chief Technology Officer and Under Secretary of War for Research and Engineering, and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor. The group will report directly to the president and to White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.

The line-up is revealing. Placing the nation’s top intelligence official in charge signals that the White House views AI primarily through a national security lens, a point Clayton emphasised after last week’s summit when he described American AI leadership as a central security priority. Ferguson brings the government’s chief consumer protection enforcer into the room, Michael the Pentagon’s research machinery and Kupor the federal workforce expected to adopt the technology.

## How Does the SIF Fit With the “Era of Super Intelligence” Executive Order?

The SIF builds on an executive order signed on 29 September 2026 titled “Inaugurating The Era of Super Intelligence.” The order directs executive branch agencies to replace the terms “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI” with “Super Intelligence” and “SI.” It states that Super Intelligence refers to technologies already covered by the existing statutory definition of artificial intelligence, and gives White House officials 60 days to draft legislative language formally defining the term and its abbreviation in federal law.

The rebranding has drawn mockery from critics, but the 60-day deadline means proposed statutory language could reach Congress before the end of November.

## What Did the White House Accord on Super Intelligence Commit Tech Companies To?

On the same day as the executive order, Sundar Pichai of Google, Dario Amodei of Anthropic, Mark Zuckerberg of Meta, Greg Brockman of OpenAI, Elon Musk of xAI and Jensen Huang of Nvidia signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence. The one-page document asks signatories to maintain internal controls over their most capable models, set up an internal team to oversee those controls, work with independent outside auditors and create an independent board committee to review the results.

Executives presented the pledge as a confidence-building exercise. Zuckerberg said the aim was to give Americans and customers confidence that the technology works as intended, while Amodei stressed that the industry needed to work together so that America could “win safely.”

## Why Are Critics Calling the Accord Toothless?

The central criticism is enforcement. The accord is voluntary and not legally binding. It sets no penalties for non-compliance, does not require companies to publish audit results and gives the government no enforcement role. At least one US senator has argued the pledge will not hold technology companies accountable, and lawmakers are promoting the AI Systems Transparency Act, which would require developers to disclose the data they collect and the safeguards designed to prevent misuse.

That debate gives the SIF’s composition added significance. With the FTC chairman sitting on the task force, the administration retains a route to consumer protection enforcement even though the accord itself contains none. Reports published alongside the accord suggested the FTC is preparing to examine several AI companies over potential consumer protection concerns, although the scope of any inquiry has not been officially confirmed.

## How Does Trump’s Approach Compare With Biden’s 2023 AI Commitments?

Voluntary industry pledges are not new. In July 2023, the Biden White House secured voluntary commitments from seven companies, including Google, Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, OpenAI, Anthropic and Inflection, covering testing, information sharing and transparency.

The 2026 approach differs in emphasis. It has a narrower signatory list, with Microsoft and Amazon absent, a more explicitly competitive tone and leadership anchored in the intelligence community rather than a dedicated safety institute.

## What Does the Super Intelligence Force Mean for Investors and Markets?

For markets, the SIF matters less for what it does immediately than for what it signals. The accord’s signatories include several of the largest constituents of the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq, and a coordinated, industry-friendly federal posture lowers the near-term risk of binding regulation. That backdrop follows a volatile September for AI-exposed stocks, when a mid-month essay from Amodei urging laboratories to slow frontier development helped trigger a sharp global technology and semiconductor sell-off.

Investors will nonetheless watch three pressure points: the content of the statutory definition due within 60 days, any FTC action against AI developers, and whether Congress advances binding transparency legislation. With US markets closed for the weekend when the announcement landed, Monday’s session will provide the first read on how traders price the news.

## What Happens Next for the Super Intelligence Force?

The most immediate milestone is the end-November deadline for draft legislative language defining Super Intelligence. Beyond that, the White House has not yet published a charter, budget or meeting schedule for the SIF.

How quickly those details emerge will determine whether the Super Intelligence Force becomes a genuine coordinating engine for American AI policy or remains primarily a statement of intent in an intensifying global technology race.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Super Intelligence Force

### What is the Super Intelligence Force (SIF)?

The SIF is a federal task force announced by President Trump on 4 October 2026 to coordinate government policy and engagement on advanced artificial intelligence, which the administration now officially calls Super Intelligence.

### Who leads the Super Intelligence Force?

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton leads the SIF alongside FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon Chief Technology Officer Emil Michael and OPM Director Scott Kupor, reporting to the president and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.

### Is the White House Accord on Super Intelligence legally binding?

No. The accord is a voluntary pledge with no penalties, no mandatory public disclosure of audit results and no government enforcement role.
