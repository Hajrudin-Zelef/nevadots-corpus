## What Happened

Several big U.S. banks are now saying that if you let an AI agent use your account, you are responsible for what it does. That includes losses if hackers get in through the AI tool. TechCrunch reported on November 18, 2026 that Citi is the latest to say so.

An AI agent is a program that acts for you, such as logging into a website and clicking through tasks. Citi updated its Online User Agreement this week. A Citi spokesperson said the bank wants customers to understand “the risks associated with sharing banking credentials” with third-party AI tools. Under the update, customers who give outside AI agents access to an account are responsible for what those agents do. They are also liable for what hackers do if credentials leak or the AI tool is hacked. Citi’s terms say AI agents that Citi itself provides can be used without that added liability.

Citi is not alone, according to TechCrunch:

- JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies in October. This builds on our earlier alert about Bank of America.
- PNC changed its terms the same day the article ran.
- Citizens emailed customers this week, warning that they are responsible for anything their AI assistant does.

## What It Means for Your Business

If you connect an AI tool to a bank or card account, the bank may treat everything that happens next as your doing. A mistaken payment the agent makes counts. So does a loss after the tool is hacked or your password leaks.

This matters for owners who hand off bookkeeping, bill paying or expense tracking to AI helpers. A staff member who connects a tool to a company account can create the same exposure without you knowing.

The safest approach is simple. Keep AI tools away from your bank and card logins. If you want help understanding your spending, use tools that only read data and cannot move money. TechCrunch notes that Anthropic’s personal finance tool is described as read-only, meaning it “can’t move, manage, or invest your money.” Check any tool’s own description before you rely on it.

Terms differ by bank, and business accounts may have their own agreements. Read the one that applies to you rather than assuming.

## FAQ

The reporting covers the banks’ online user agreements and customer notices. We could not confirm how each bank’s business account terms read, so check yours or ask your banker.

The reporting is about giving AI agents your credentials or account access. Using AI on exported reports or statements, without handing over your login, avoids that problem.
