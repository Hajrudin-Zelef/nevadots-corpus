# Citi now holds you liable for what AI agents do in your account. What other banks say

- **What the agent does is yours.** If you give an agent your login âor otherwise permit such agent access to your accounts,â then âCiti will consider any access and actions taken by the agent as authorized by You.â
- **So is what others do with a leaked login.** If the login you gave an agent is âfurther shared, leaked, or otherwise distributed to other persons or AI agents, Citi will consider any access and actions taken with the Online Credentials as authorized by You.â
- **Citi wonât check.** It takes âno responsibility or other obligation to investigate the authority or validity of any such actions even if the actions taken by the agent or other person or AI agent with your Online Credentials conflict with any prompts or other instructionsâ you gave.

- **Citiâs privacy notice stops at the agent.** Letting an agent in authorizes Citi to disclose your information to it, âincluding sensitive personal information,â and âthe Citi Privacy Notice does not apply to the third-party AI Agent.â

## Whoâs responsible if an agent moves money, or gets hacked

You are, by the terms of every bank that has addressed it. Wells Fargo makes you âsolely responsibleâ for âunintended actions performed by the AI Agent, actions resulting from incomplete, inaccurate, or manipulated inputs, or the AI Agent exceeding the authority granted by you.â U.S. Bankâs terms cover errors âbased on inaccurate/manipulated inputs,â which is what a prompt injection supplies. But an agreement canât take those protections away. The Electronic Fund Transfer Act says, âNo writing or other agreement between a consumer and any other person may contain any provision which constitutes a waiver of any right conferred or cause of action created by this subchapter.â The banksâ terms leave room for that: Citiâs sentence on agent mistakes ends âexcept as otherwise permitted under applicable law,â Wells Fargoâs clauses begin âUnless otherwise provided by law or regulation,â and U.S. Bankâs say âexcept as otherwise required by applicable law.â We havenât found a regulator or court saying how the law treats an AI agent you gave your login to, or a thief who took it from the agent. The safe assumption is that a transfer made with a login you handed over is yours, until you tell the bank otherwise.
