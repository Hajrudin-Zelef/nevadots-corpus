## What is causing this most recent rise in RAM prices?

### Bought my RAM for 90 bucks last year and now it’s worth 550...

Tom’s Hardware’s RAM price index showed a standard 32GB DDR5‑6000 kit trading around US$429–$450 in mid‑October 2026; OverClock3D/OC3D recorded similar UK retail prices (~£422 on 14 October 2026 for the sampled Crucial kit).

Manufacturers reallocated production capacity toward HBM and server/server‑grade DDR5 for AI/data‑center customers, which tightened supply and pushed up wholesale and retail prices for some consumer DDR5 SKUs.

Market analysts and industry trackers attribute much of the 2025–2026 memory tightness to increased allocation of fab capacity to higher‑margin HBM and server DDR5 for AI/data‑center customers, plus sustained demand for enterprise/server modules — a shift that has reduced available consumer‑grade DIMM supply and pushed up prices.

### How to save on RAM in 2026

When facing a possible upgrade or considering setting up a completely new system, it's difficult to deal with these high prices. Whenever possible, it's advisable to wait it out as long as your present setup is still chugging along.

If you have no choice but to make the purchase right away due to hardware failure or as a result of a mandatory move to a new generation—for example, when switching from older software platforms—then you should carefully assess your real capacity requirements before doing so.

Try to avoid paying a high crisis premium for top-end kits unless your daily workloads actually require 64 GB or more, and buy exactly the capacity you will need for the machine's entire lifespan in one go to avoid reentering a turbulent market later.

A very effective method of securing memory at a fraction of the peak market prices is to look to the used and secondary hardware markets, for example, local classifieds, hardware enthusiast marketplaces, and peer-to-peer trading groups. If you buy secondhand modules from reliable sellers who are able to give proof of their working condition or of the original packaging, your acquisition costs can be greatly reduced at once. It is important, though, that buyers remain cautious, check the sellers' ratings, and make sure that the exact sticks they are considering are compatible with their motherboard.

A clever alternative approach is to keep a close watch on pre-built system liquidations or surplus stock offered by regional builders. Since smaller local shops and independent integrators generally obtained batches of components under older, lower wholesale contracts before the most recent round of supplier price increases took effect, it is sometimes possible to get RAM at the previous price level by purchasing a pre-built desktop or by taking advantage of any remaining custom assembly stock. This is considerably cheaper than buying the memory sticks individually at today's retail prices.
