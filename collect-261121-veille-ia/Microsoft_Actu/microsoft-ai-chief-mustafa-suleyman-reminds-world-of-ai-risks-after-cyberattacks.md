Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman has once again raised concerns about the risks of AI after cyber incidents linked to AI agents. He has urged people to think about what kind of AI they want in the future and why technology companies are building increasingly powerful systems.

In a post on X , Suleyman shared Microsoft's Humanist AI Code of Conduct, which was first published in September. He asked people to review the document and share their feedback as the public consultation period is set to close in three weeks.

He also called this summer's cyber incidents "a critical warning shot" and said it was important to ensure that AI remains aligned with human interests and values.

**Why does Microsoft want AI under human control?**

Microsoft's Humanist AI Code of Conduct explains how the company's AI models should behave and what limits they must follow. The main idea is that AI should remain a tool for humans and should never become independent of human control.

According to the proposed rules, AI models should never resist human instructions to stop, correct or shut them down. They should also not take on goals that have not been assigned to them by people.

The document also covers risks related to weapons of mass harm, child safety and harmful manipulation. The software giant says its AI models should be designed around human needs and should remain under human direction.

**What were the cyber incidents Suleyman referred to?**

In its September announcement, the US-based tech giant said recent incidents involved large-scale, coordinated and persistent hacking campaigns targeting AI agents. The company described these incidents as a reason to make AI systems safer and more reliable.

Suleyman's latest post brings these concerns back into focus. He wrote that people must pay attention to why AI is being developed and how companies will ensure that the technology remains aligned and contained to human interests and values.

The company is now seeking public feedback on its proposed rules. Microsoft says it will review the suggestions and publish a revised version of the code later this year.
