Microsoft has announced its third-generation custom artificial intelligence (AI) chip and inference platform, the Maia 300, marking an aggressive push to run more of its AI infrastructure on homegrown hardware after years of heavy reliance on Nvidia’s market-dominant processors. Executive leadership described the rollout as the company’s “biggest bet yet on our own silicon,” positioning the processor as the future operational backbone for its consumer, developer, and enterprise services. Production of the Maia 300 is scheduled to begin in 2027, and Microsoft plans to use the platform as its primary inference engine to process incoming AI queries across its ecosystem, powering tools such as Copilot, Microsoft Teams, GitHub, Foundry and Dragon Copilot. 

 Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella calls ‘Maia 300’ company’s biggest bet on silicon*Today we are announcing our biggest bet yet on our own silicon.**Starting next calendar year, we will scale our Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments, making it the primary inference system for our own product and services.**And we’re introducing Maia 300, which delivers 40% more tokens per watt compared to leading GPU-based systems.**We are already deploying Maia 200 across the US, Europe, and Australia, delivering up to 1.7x more tokens per watt across experiences in Teams and Copilot.**Our goal is to build on the full-stack efficiency gains we’ve already seen, while continuing to bring the latest innovations from across the ecosystem into our fleet.*

 Hardware gains: Density and energy efficiencyEngineered specifically for complex modern computing, including advanced reasoning, conversational bots and autonomous agentic workflows, the Maia 300 introduces notable efficiency and density leaps over its predecessors. 

 Microsoft plans to install 96 Maia 300 chips into every server rack, effectively doubling the 48-chip density of the Maia 200. The platform achieves up to seven times higher token throughput per rack compared to the Maia 200. Moreover, the hardware is claimed to produce 40% more tokens per megawatt, delivering notable power savings compared to standard GPUs. 

 While Nvidia graphics processing units remain the default industry standard for model training and fill Microsoft’s existing cloud data centers, high market demand and soaring infrastructure costs have pushed major cloud providers toward hardware diversification. 

 Looking ahead, Microsoft plans to scale Azure Maia platforms across multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027, according to an announcement from Executive Vice President Scott Guthrie. General Manager Alistair Speirs declined to provide a more specific timeline for the broader gigawatt expansion, but the trajectory signals Microsoft's clear intent to control its own hardware fate as enterprise AI demand accelerates.
