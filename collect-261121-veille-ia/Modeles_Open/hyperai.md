# Elorian Launches Glimpse, AI Model That Reasons With Images, Not About Them

Palo Alto AI startup Elorian has released Glimpse, its first model, launching as a research preview with API access and a waitlisted chat interface. The launch follows a $55 million seed round at a $300 million valuation and a stealth exit six months ago, with the round co-led by Striker Venture Partners, Menlo Ventures and Altimeter Capital and participation from Nvidia and Google chief scientist Jeff Dean. Elorian positions Glimpse around the argument that many current multimodal models can describe images but cannot truly inspect them. Rather than converting an image once into tokens and reasoning from that summary, Glimpse is designed to return to the visual source during reasoning. It can crop into a region, pull specific frames from a video or run code to measure and compare details, a capability Elorian calls a visual scratch space. CEO Andrew Dai, a former Google DeepMind researcher, cited tasks such as interpreting board games or text-free IKEA diagrams as examples of visual understanding that remains difficult for frontier models. The company says Glimpse was trained by a small team on a few hundred GPUs and is substantially smaller than frontier systems. Pricing is set at $0.60 per million input tokens and $3 per million output tokens, placing it between lightweight and mid-level models. Based on Elorian evaluations, the model outperforms GPT-6 Astra on scientific chart reasoning and short-form video captioning, while posting competitive results on other visual reasoning benchmarks. Its initial commercial focus is on organizations with large volumes of visual data, including robotics firms, scientific labs, media companies and creative studios. Elorian’s longer-term roadmap extends beyond visual reasoning into action. The startup aims to build a model of a scene that can be used to select and execute tasks, including direct robot control. Dai said the company plans to build the world’s best visual reasoning model within a year, aiming to lead across major visual reasoning tests.
