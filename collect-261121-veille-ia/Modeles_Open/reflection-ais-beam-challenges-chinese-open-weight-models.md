Despite raising billions, signing compute deals worth up to US$7bn and becoming the AI model provider for the US Department of Energy (DOE), Reflection AI had not released a public Model until October 2026.

That changed on 5 October 2026, when the Brooklyn-based lab announced Beam, a 501-billion parameter, open-weight model it says will compete with larger open models at the fraction of the inference compute.

Reflection AI has released Beam under the premise that governments, scientists and enterprises are looking for frontier AI they can own, inspect and run themselves.

## Reflection AI's origins

Reflection AI was founded in March 2024 by former Google DeepMind researchers, Misha Laskin and Ioannis Antonoglou.

Seeking an US-native alternative to Chinese-owned open-weight labs such as DeepSeek, Misha and Ioannis pitched frontier-class models whose weights are published so users can fine-tune and deploy them on their own infrastructure.

“You can’t do scientific discovery on a closed model,” Misha said in an interview for Axios.

“It would be like doing rocket science, but you only get to look at the rocket, as opposed to taking it apart and examining the engine.”

## How Beam offers a sovereign alternative to Chinese models

The answer to this pitch is Beam, a sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) model with 501bn total and 23bn active parameters, pretrained on 23.8tn tokens in under four weeks.

The model was trained over four weeks on 6,144 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs, with reinforcement learning generating more than 100 million rollouts on 10,500 of the same GPUs.

Reflection says Beam matches GLM 5.2 on advanced reasoning benchmarks while using three to four times less inference compute.

The weights for Beam were released under an open-source Apache 2.0 license, with tools for running and fine-tuning the model available on Hugging Face.

## NVIDIA and SpaceX backing the company

Money and compute have arrived ahead of Beam’s revenue, with Reflection AI boasting support amongst the likes of NVIDIA and SpaceX.

NVIDIA backed the company’s Series B and C funding rounds, the former of which saw US$800m, and the latter closing at a US$25bn pre-money valuation.

Supporting the company’s computing needs, a SpaceX agreement worth up to US$6.3bn provides GB300 capacity for Reflection AI at the Colossus 2 data centre near Memphis, while a Nebius contract worth more than US$1bn adds even more GPUs.

Such huge backers likely comes from the need for government and sovereign AI, which are expected to be the early markets for Beam.

Reflection AI provides its open-weight models to the DOE's Genesis Mission, which serves the 17 US National Laboratories.

Separately, it is building a 250MW sovereign AI data centre in South Korea with Shinsegae.

Announcing the Nebius deal in July 2026, Ioannis told Reuters: “The need for open models is clear, and this additional compute capacity will allow Reflection to continue to build and train frontier AI models at scale.”

## Key partners for Reflection AI

- **NVIDIA** is Reflection's anchor partner. NVIDIA led its Series B with $800m, supplies the GB300 systems used to train Beam, and named Reflection a founding member of its Nemotron Coalition of open frontier labs in March 2026.
- **SpaceX** offers Reflection’s multi-year compute agreement worth up to $6.3bn, giving the company NVIDIA GB300 capacity at the Colossus 2 data centre near Memphis, with payments of $150m a month running through 2029.
- **Nebius** has given Reflection a capacity contract worth more than $1bn, disclosed in July 2026, so the company has access to NVIDIA GPUs through the Nebius cloud platform to keep building and training frontier models at scale.
