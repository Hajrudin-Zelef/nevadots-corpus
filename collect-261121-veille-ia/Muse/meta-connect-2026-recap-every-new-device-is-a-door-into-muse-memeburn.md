TL;DR

- Meta Connect 2026 introduced Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 ($449), Ray-Ban Meta Audio ($349), Meta VR Glasses ($1,299) and Muse Charm (price TBA, December target).
- Muse Charm is the first Meta device built purely for the Muse agent.
- Meta plans to earn a merchant-paid fee on transactions Muse completes, which puts it in direct conflict with Amazon's ad business.

Meta Connect 2026 opened in Menlo Park on September 23 with four new pieces of hardware, a round of upgrades for the Muse personal AI agent, and the clearest explanation Meta has yet given of how it expects to make money from AI. Although most of the keynote was spent on glasses, the idea holding it together was not really eyewear, because Mark Zuckerberg framed nearly every announcement as another route into Muse, the agent Meta released on September 8, which he described as the centerpiece of Meta’s vision. This recap sorts every announcement by how much each product actually depends on Muse.

## **Meta Connect 2026 Announcements at a Glance**

| **Product** | **Price** | **Availability** | **What Muse does on it** | **Status** | 
| Ray-Ban Meta (Gen 3) | From $449 | On sale now | Voice access to the agent | Confirmed | 
| Ray-Ban Meta Audio | $349 | Ships October 13 | Voice access, no camera | Confirmed | 
| Meta VR Glasses | $1,299 | Spring 2027 | Not detailed in the keynote | Confirmed | 
| Muse Charm | Not announced | Targeting December 2026 | The device exists for Muse | Unveiled, components not final | 

Reading down the fourth column shows where Meta is heading, since the products that got the most stage time are the ones in which Muse stops being a feature and becomes the reason the device exists. Zuckerberg put it plainly when he told the audience, according to a Reuters report, that “we have shifted our focus to building the best devices for personal superintelligence.”

## **Muse Charm Was the Surprise of the Keynote**

The hardware nobody expected was Muse Charm, a palm-sized gadget that came out of Meta’s new design lab led by former Apple interface chief Alan Dye, as Bloomberg reported. It carries a small touchscreen, front and rear cameras, 5G and a fingerprint sensor, so that people who do not wear glasses can talk to Muse and show it what is in front of them without unlocking a phone. Zuckerberg gave no price and said Meta still needs to finalise the internal components before shipping in December, according to CNBC.

The timing is notable because only a few months ago a leaked memo put Meta’s AI pendant in testing no earlier than 2027, which suggests Muse’s fast start pulled a dedicated device forward on the roadmap.

## **Camera-Free Ray-Bans Answer the Privacy Backlash**

The Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 is a conventional refresh with longer battery life and new frames, but the more interesting pair is Ray-Ban Meta Audio, the first Meta glasses without a camera. CNN notes that the audio-only frames are lighter and last longer, and they arrive while Meta’s camera glasses face a hostile climate, with European regulators weighing bans and the “pervert glasses” label still following the product online. Removing the camera concedes that the lens, rather than the AI, is what many people object to, and because Muse is driven mostly by voice, a camera-free pair now gives up less than it would have a year ago. Buyers comparing frames can still start with our guide to the best smart glasses.

## **Meta VR Glasses Keep the Metaverse on the Roadmap**

Meta VR Glasses cost $1,299 and weigh roughly 100 grams, which Ubergizmo puts at about five times lighter than the Quest 3, because the battery and storage move into a tethered puck. Aimed at entertainment and productivity, it shows Meta has not abandoned virtual reality even as it tells investors that AI agents come first. Meta did not explain what role Muse will play on this headset, which is why the table above leaves that cell open.

## **What Muse Gained at Connect**

On the software side, Meta announced shopping integrations with retailers including Walmart and Instacart, and it now lets users customise their Muse avatar’s look, name and voice. Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang also said an encrypted Muse mode that walls off sensitive data is targeted for December 2026, TheStreet reported. That date matters because, as our walkthrough of the Muse web app found, Meta can still access data inside the agent’s virtual machine until that confidential mode ships, which means early users are handing over their accounts about three months before the strongest privacy protection exists.

## **How Meta Plans to Earn a Fee on What Muse Buys**

The announcement with the biggest financial consequences was not a device. Zuckerberg said Muse would stay free for a large number of tokens because Meta expects to profit over time by taking a small fee from the transactions Muse completes, a fee that TheStreet says will be charged to merchants rather than users. The free tier currently includes 100 million tokens a week, with paid tiers at $20 and $100 a month, so Meta earns when Muse buys something rather than when someone sees an ad.

That plan has already hit a wall. Amazon blocked Muse from shopping on Amazon.com, saying Meta never told it the agent would use the store, that Muse does not identify itself while browsing, and that it appears to capture customer credentials, GeekWire reported, while Meta has said Muse has no visibility into passwords or payment methods. The dispute is less one-sided than it looks, because Meta itself changed WhatsApp’s business terms last year to bar general-purpose AI chatbots such as ChatGPT from the platform. Both companies are guarding the customer relationship on their own turf while asking for access to everyone else’s, and a transaction-fee business turns that tension into the main event.

## **The Price Tags and Deadlines Meta Left Blank**

Connect left several practical questions open, including the price of Muse Charm, whether its 5G connection needs a separate data plan, whether the encrypted mode actually lands in December, and whether Amazon and Meta reach terms before the holiday season in which Meta wants Charm on shelves. Until those answers arrive, the safest reading of Connect 2026 is that Meta has committed its hardware roadmap to Muse, and that the devices will only be as useful as the services Muse is allowed to reach.

## **FAQ**

### **What did Meta announce at Connect 2026?**

Meta announced Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 glasses from $449, camera-free Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses at $349, Meta VR Glasses at $1,299, and Muse Charm, a pocket device for its Muse AI agent, along with new Muse shopping integrations.

### **When is Muse Charm coming out, and how much does it cost?**

Meta is targeting December 2026 for Muse Charm but has not announced a price.

### **Why did Amazon block Muse?**

Amazon says Muse used its store without permission, does not identify itself while browsing and appears to store customer credentials. Meta disputes the credentials claim.

### **How will Meta make money from Muse?**

Beyond paid tiers at $20 and $100 a month, Meta plans to take a small fee, paid by merchants, on transactions Muse completes for users.
