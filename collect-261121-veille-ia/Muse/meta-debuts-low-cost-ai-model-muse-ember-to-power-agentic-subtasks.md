Meta Superintelligence Labs has released Muse Ember, its smallest and most cost-efficient AI model so far, aimed at the repetitive jobs that AI agents run all day. These include tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, data extraction and parsing long documents. The model launched on 9 November.

Ember is available through the Meta Model API and in Muse Code. It costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens, the same list price as Anthropic’s Claude Haiku 5.5 and OpenAI’s GPT-6 Luna. It has a one-million-token context window and takes text, images and audio as inputs.

Meta says Ember delivers more intelligence per dollar than both rivals at every reasoning setting. On Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index, it scored 36 at maximum effort, matching Haiku 5.5 and ahead of Luna’s 28. The cost per task was about $0.14, against roughly $0.41 for Haiku and $0.15 for Luna. Artificial Analysis says Ember’s cached input is 60% cheaper than its rivals’.

The results are mixed. Ember led Meta’s benchmark table on tests such as AutomationBench-AA and Terminal-Bench 4.0, but Haiku 5.5 stays ahead on FrontierCode 2.0, a test of real engineering tasks. Artificial Analysis also noted that Ember’s factual accuracy was the lowest among comparably capable models it tested.

Meta designed Ember to work alongside larger models. “Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume,” the company said, referring to its frontier model released in October. Ember is the fourth model in Meta’s Muse family, after Spark, Glimmer and Wonder.
