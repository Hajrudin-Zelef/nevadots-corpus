*Technology, digital innovation, artificial intelligence, gadget, cybersecurity, and emerging tech trends.*

Menlo Park, California - Meta Platforms on Monday unveiled a new family of artificial intelligence models that it says can compete at the technological frontier, marking a significant milestone in the company's effort to catch up with rivals OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google after nearly a year of trailing in the race to develop the world's most capable AI systems. The two new models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, were previously known by the code-name Watermelon and are the company's most capable models to date, designed for personal and professional AI agents. Meta said the models can perform long-running tasks, use computers, and work in specialized areas including law, finance, and health, while also handling complex coding projects, delegating work to smaller AI agents, and recovering from errors without abandoning the original goal.

Meta has spent heavily to assemble a top AI research team and the computing infrastructure needed to compete, and the new models represent an important test of those investments. The company trained the Wonder family using its gigawatt-scale Prometheus data center, reflecting the enormous computational resources required to develop frontier-class AI systems. The launch forms part of Meta's broader push toward what Chief Executive Officer Mark Zuckerberg has described as "personal superintelligence," AI systems capable of understanding users' goals, preferences, and long-term needs. Unlike some earlier Meta models released with downloadable weights, the company did not announce plans to make the Wonder models open source, though Meta Superintelligence Labs head Alexandr Wang noted that Meta remains committed to open source and recently released the weights for Muse Glitter.

One of the models' primary features, Active Memory, allows the AI to retain important information about a user across multiple conversations and retrieve it when relevant to a new task. Another feature, Active Focus, is intended to keep an AI agent working when it encounters difficulties by trying alternative approaches, adapting to new information, and seeking clarification when necessary. Wang emphasized the significance of memory for personal agents, stating that it means the system carries what matters about a user across conversations over days, weeks, and months so that nobody is re-explaining themselves every time. Meta says the models also perform strongly on coding tasks and specialized professional work, with the company positioning them as evidence that it can compete with the leading AI labs rather than simply distribute AI technology through its enormous consumer platforms.

Muse Wonder is now available to developers through the Meta Model API and the company's Muse Code programming tool, while the more capable Muse Wonder Full is available through paid Muse Code subscriptions with plans starting at fifty dollars per month for access to Meta's most advanced models. Muse Wonder will later be incorporated into Meta's Muse personal assistant, initially for subscribers. Meta published benchmark results that it said showed the models matching or outperforming some systems from OpenAI and Anthropic on selected coding, memory, and professional tasks, though the broader performance of the models will still need to be assessed through independent testing. The company says the models perform especially well on unfamiliar tasks, suggesting that their capabilities extend beyond the benchmarks and tasks emphasized during training.

The competitive context surrounding the launch is significant. Anthropic and OpenAI have recently called for the industry to slow down the development of frontier AI models, citing safety concerns, yet Meta has proceeded with a launch that directly challenges their most advanced systems. Meta claims the Muse Wonder models are competitive with GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, and Claude Fable 5.1, pointing to results from internal testing on several industry benchmarks. The launch follows a fourteen-month period during which Zuckerberg overhauled his AI operation through an aggressive hiring push that put new talent at the helm, and the new models will be a key test for Meta's expensive and rebuilt superintelligence team.

On the safety front, Meta said it trained the Wonder family to protect private information, refuse assistance with harmful activity, and strengthen defenses against cyberattacks. The company says it has strengthened safeguards against cyber and biological misuse, as well as protections for personal information, and a model card with some details is set to be released on Monday, with a fuller safety report forthcoming. However, agents that remember personal information and act on a user's behalf also introduce new privacy and security challenges, raising questions about how Meta will balance the utility of persistent memory with the need to protect user data in an era of increasing scrutiny over AI privacy practices.

The launch represents a critical moment for Meta's AI ambitions. Having spent billions of dollars to build a competitive research organization and the computing infrastructure necessary to train frontier models, the company now faces the challenge of converting those investments into products and services that can compete with the offerings of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. Meta's long-term ambition is what Zuckerberg has called "personal superintelligence," AI that understands an individual's goals and preferences, and the company says the new models are a step toward that vision, with future specialized models intended for broader deployment across its products. Whether Meta's claimed performance advantages hold up in independent testing and how quickly the new technology reaches the billions of people using its apps will determine whether the Muse Wonder family marks a genuine turning point in the company's AI strategy or merely another incremental step in an increasingly crowded field.
