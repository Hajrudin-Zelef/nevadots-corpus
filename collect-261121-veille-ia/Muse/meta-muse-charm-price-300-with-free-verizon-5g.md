## Meta Muse Charm Pricing

Meta appears to be borrowing a trick from the phone business to sell its first pocket AI gadget: bundle the data. The company plans to price its Muse Charm device at $300 and give buyers up to 12 months of free 5G data from Verizon, according to a report published Thursday that cited a person familiar with the matter.

### **Key Takeaways**

- The Muse Charm will reportedly cost $300 and include up to a year of free Verizon 5G data.
- The keychain-sized device has a roughly 2-inch screen, a fingerprint reader and a built-in 5G modem, but it cannot make calls.
- Meta says it will “have more to share soon,” and the Charm is expected in time for the December holidays.

## What $300 Buys

Mark Zuckerberg unveiled the Muse Charm at Meta Connect in September. It is about the size of an AirPods case. Its screen, roughly 2 inches across and navigable with a thumb, shows a customizable Muse character in the middle.

A fingerprint reader confirms the owner’s identity before the device accepts commands. A built-in 5G modem lets it reach Muse without Wi-Fi, though it cannot place calls. At Connect, Zuckerberg said the device packs “the whole Muse experience, including the real-time voice and avatar stack” into something small enough to hang on a keychain.

Meta did not confirm the price. A spokesperson said: “we’re charmed by the interest in our AI devices, and we’ll have more to share soon.” Verizon did not respond to requests for comment.

## Why Meta Wants Its Own Device

The Charm gives users direct access to the Muse agent without opening a smartphone app. Meta has a clear reason to want that: it is trying to depend less on the operating systems Apple and Google control.

Muse has gained traction. Its agent can shop, book travel and fill in forms, and it has more than 10 million users. The agent itself can now place phone calls for paying US users, even though the Charm hardware cannot. Meta is also building commerce tools that would let it charge businesses a fee when people buy through the agent, and Zuckerberg has said Muse could eventually earn more per user than Meta’s existing apps.

A smartwatch-style device has reportedly slipped to 2027 so the company can focus on the Charm.

Dedicated AI gadgets have a rocky record. Humane’s AI Pin cost $700 and lasted less than a year on the market before HP bought the startup’s assets for $116 million and the pins stopped working. Meta starts from a stronger position: it already has an agent with millions of users, so the Charm does not need to create demand from scratch.

The bigger contest is over where people talk to AI. Meta already sells AI glasses, and at Connect it showed $1,299 VR Glasses set to launch in spring 2027. Google, meanwhile, has opened preorders for its first Gemini glasses. A $300 price with free data puts the Charm near impulse-buy territory for the holidays, as long as the agent proves useful enough to justify a second device in the pocket.
