Meta, led by Mark Zuckerberg, is reportedly preparing to launch its first dedicated AI hardware device, the Muse Charm, in December 2026, with a possible price tag of $300 (approximately Rs 28,000) in the US. According to a report by The Information, the company is also considering offering buyers up to 12 months of free 5G data through US telecom operator Verizon as part of the package. Meta has not officially confirmed the pricing or the proposed data offer.

The Muse Charm is a compact, pendant-like handheld device designed to provide direct access to Meta’s Muse personal AI assistant without requiring users to open a smartphone. Approximately the size of an AirPods case, the gadget is expected to feature a two-inch touchscreen, built-in speakers, microphones and a fingerprint sensor. It is designed to be carried in a pocket or attached to a keychain, making it a portable interface for interacting with the company’s AI technology.

One of the device’s key features is its reported built-in 5G modem, which could allow the AI assistant to function independently of a smartphone’s internet connection. However, this capability has not been officially confirmed. The device is also expected to display a customisable digital character called Jolly, which resembles a virtual pet and can appear on its screen. Users will be able to interact with the character while accessing the assistant’s wider range of capabilities.

Meta’s Muse assistant is designed to perform practical tasks such as online shopping, booking flights and filling out forms through voice-based interactions. Zuckerberg first demonstrated the Muse Charm at Meta Connect in September, describing it as a convenient way to communicate with the AI assistant and share information about one’s surroundings. The company is also positioning Muse as the primary interface for its smart glasses, allowing users to access the assistant through wearable devices and smartphones.

The reported $300 price would place the Muse Charm above some competing AI wearables in the US market. Friend’s AI wearable is priced at $250 (approximately Rs 23,000), while Amazon’s Bee Frontier is listed at $75 (around Rs 7,000), with an optional annual subscription costing $99 (approximately Rs 9,200). Meta is also working on a smartwatch, although its launch has reportedly been postponed until 2027 as the company prioritises the new AI device. The company has separately released open-source code to enable users to build their own Muse gadgets.

Meta is expected to introduce the Muse Charm in December, although an exact launch date and availability outside the US remain unclear. The launch comes as several technology companies explore dedicated AI hardware, with Nothing reportedly developing a voice recorder and OpenAI working on a smart speaker. However, earlier products such as the Humane AI Pin and Rabbit R1 have struggled to gain widespread acceptance, leaving questions about whether standalone AI wearables can establish a sustainable consumer market.
