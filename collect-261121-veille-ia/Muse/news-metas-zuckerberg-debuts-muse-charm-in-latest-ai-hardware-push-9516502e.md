Meta Platforms META -4.68% ▼ CEO Mark Zuckerberg unveiled a new handheld AI device, Muse Charm, at the company’s Meta Connect 2026 event on September 23. The announcement highlights Meta’s efforts to expand its AI ecosystem beyond smartphones and smart glasses. Muse is Meta’s AI assistant that answers questions, talks with users, helps with everyday tasks, and provides real-time assistance through voice and video interactions.
Zuckerberg shared few details about Muse Charm’s features, saying users will be able to interact with Meta AI agents “without having to unlock a phone or open an app.” He said Muse Charm will let users quickly talk to Muse and show it what’s happening around them, even without smart glasses.

What Is Muse Charm?
Muse Charm is a palm-sized, keychain-style device designed to provide quick access to Meta’s Muse AI agent. It features a small screen and 5G connectivity, allowing users to interact with the assistant on the go. Meta plans to make Muse Charm available by December, although pricing and sales targets have not been disclosed.
The announcement comes as Meta expands Muse’s capabilities, including real-time voice conversations and live video interactions. The company also plans to bring Muse to its AI glasses in the coming months.
Meta Expands Its AI Hardware Lineup
Beyond Muse Charm, Meta introduced several new hardware products at Connect 2026. These include its first camera-free Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses, priced at $349, and updated Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses starting at $449.
Meta also unveiled lightweight Meta VR Glasses, priced at $1,299, with shipments expected to begin in spring 2027. The new products reflect Meta’s broader push to integrate AI into everyday consumer devices.
Anthropic ANT -10.79% ▼ stock has dropped 22% from its opening trades after raising a record $81.3 billion in its Nasdaq debut, and nobody watching SpaceX SPCX -6.74% ▼ and Rivian RIVN -6.47% ▼ should be surprised. Big Wall Street listings tend to burn retail investors who rush in at the opening bell.
Shares of Anthropic opened 34% higher at $111.50 on Thursday and quickly climbed to $115.10, briefly valuing the company above $2 trillion. Sellers soon stepped in as the wider U.S. stock market tumbled. Anthropic closed its first day at $97.17, up 17.07% from the $83 IPO price yet down nearly 13% from its opening trade.
The selling continued on Friday morning as global AI stocks slid and Bloomberg reported that Apple AAPL -1.38% ▼ is building a rival AI agent. Anthropic stock fell another 10.68%, or $10.38, to $86.79 in live trading, TipRanks data shows. This drop puts investors who chased Thursday’s $111.50 opening price down 22.16% in two days.
“The Anthropic IPO gave us some brief respite from the cloud of negativity hanging over AI stocks. This pause is well and truly over,” Morningstar strategist Michael Field said on Friday.
SpaceX Shows How Fast Big IPO Pops Fade
Elon Musk’s SpaceX offers the closest blueprint for a listing of this size. SpaceX went public in June at $135 a share, raising $75 billion in what then stood as the biggest IPO in history.
SpaceX opened at $150 on June 12, closed its first day up 19.2% at $160.95, and surged to $225.64 four trading days later. Those early gains vanished within weeks. SpaceX fell back to $135.27 by mid-July and dropped near $104.83 in early August, down more than 30% from its first-day close.
Shares of SpaceX recently traded near $138, leaving day-one closing investors down about 14% ahead of a December 8 lockup expiration covering up to $110 billion in stock.
Rivian and Past Tech Debuts Have Burned Early Investors
Longer market history points to similar pain for investors who buy big tech listings right after the bell. Among the 15 largest U.S. IPOs since 2006, the average stock fell 50% from its IPO price at some point during its first year and still sat 33% below that mark a year later, Bloomberg data shows.
Electric truck maker Rivian serves as Wall Street’s clearest warning. Rivian priced its November 2021 IPO at $78, closed day one up 29% at $100.73, and peaked days later before large cash burn wiped out more than 77% of its IPO value, YCharts data shows.
Other big debuts tested investor patience for years. Alibaba dropped more than half from its post-IPO peak within roughly a year of its $25 billion listing in 2014, while Meta lost more than 50% in the 18 weeks after its 2012 debut before turning around.
Anthropic Weighs Fast Sales Growth Against Large AI Bills
Strong current sales separate Anthropic from past busts like Rivian, which booked zero revenue the year before going public. Anthropic grew revenue more than 12-fold to $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, according to its SEC prospectus. Its annual sales pace hit $81 billion in late October.
Still, giant computing bills and large losses leave little room for error. Anthropic reported $104 billion in net losses over the first nine months of 2026, driven largely by $94.3 billion in debt accounting charges, according to its prospectus.
The Claude AI maker has also locked in more than $900 billion in long-term data center commitments through 2045, including over $70 billion due in the next 12 months.
“At this valuation, the growth plan is largely priced in,” PitchBook analyst Harrison Rolfes told Reuters.
