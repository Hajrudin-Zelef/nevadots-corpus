Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Saudi Arabia's Humain have expanded their partnership to include the deployment of some 150,000 AI chips.

The chips will be deployed at an "AI Zone" in Riyadh, the development of which AWS and Humain agreed upon in May 2025, pledging around $5bn in investment.

The AI chips will include Nvidia GB300s and AWS' Trainium AI chips, and will be used for both training and inference workloads. The proportion of each has not been stated.

In addition to the deployment, Humain has made AWS its "preferred AI partner" globally.

“This marks a pivotal moment in our commitment to our partnership with Humain,” said Tanuja Randery, managing director EMEA at AWS. “By combining Humain's local expertise and investment with AWS AI solutions—including our advanced infrastructure, hardware partnerships with Nvidia, the transformative AI platform Amazon Bedrock, and AI solutions for business users, including Amazon Quick Suite—we're establishing a world-class innovation hub that will serve customers across Saudi Arabia and around the globe."

“The AI Zone represents the beginning of a multi-gigawatt journey for Humain and AWS. From inception, this infrastructure has been engineered to serve both national priorities and the world’s accelerating demand for AI compute,” added Tareq Amin, CEO of Humain. “What truly sets this partnership apart is the scale of our ambition and the innovation in how we work together. Through a groundbreaking commercial model and a shared commitment to global market expansion, we are creating an ecosystem that will shape the future of how AI ideas can be built, deployed, and scaled for the whole world.”

It has been a busy week for Humain. The company has also revealed plans to build a 500MW data center in Saudi Arabia in partnership with xAI, and has teamed up with AMD and Cisco for a joint venture that will establish 1GW of AI infrastructure in the kingdom.

AWS launched a CloudFront Edge location in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, at the start of this year. In March 2024, it revealed that it hoped to have a cloud region launched in the country in 2026, along with plans to invest more than $5.3 billion over the longer term.
