A New Era of Full-Stack AI Infrastructure

NVIDIA and Microsoft are deepening their strategic partnership, expanding collaboration across AI infrastructure, enterprise models, industrial computing and Windows devices. Announced at Microsoft Ignite, the expanded agreement aims to help businesses build, customize and deploy increasingly sophisticated AI applications using NVIDIA hardware and software integrated with Microsoft’s Azure and Foundry platforms.

The announcement signals a broader shift in enterprise AI strategy. Rather than concentrating exclusively on cloud-based access to large language models, the two companies are building an ecosystem that spans hyperscale data centers, private infrastructure, industrial edge devices and individual PCs.

The partnership includes Azure infrastructure powered by NVIDIA’s next-generation Vera Rubin architecture, expanded access to NVIDIA Nemotron models through Microsoft Foundry, new confidential computing capabilities and additional support for physical AI applications.

NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 Arrives in Azure Data Centers

news.lavx.hu

One of the biggest announcements is the arrival of NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems in Microsoft Azure data centers. The infrastructure is designed for demanding AI workloads, including advanced reasoning, autonomous AI agents, large-scale model training and high-performance inference.

Vera Rubin represents the next generation of NVIDIA’s accelerated computing platform, targeting increasingly complex AI applications that require substantial computing resources.

Microsoft and NVIDIA are also extending confidential computing protections to bare-metal and rack-scale AI infrastructure. This is particularly important for organizations handling proprietary models, sensitive business information and regulated datasets.

NVIDIA Confidential Computing and attestation technologies allow customers to verify the integrity of the computing environment before releasing sensitive models, encryption keys or data. The companies want to extend these protections across interconnected accelerators and entire AI factory environments.

Power efficiency is another priority. NVIDIA and Microsoft are collaborating around NVIDIA MaxLPS to help data centers extract more performance from their available electrical capacity.

Azure Demonstrates Massive NVIDIA GPU Scaling

Microsoft is highlighting its infrastructure performance through recent MLPerf benchmark results, demonstrating the scale of its collaboration with NVIDIA.

In MLPerf Training v6.1, Azure scaled to 10,240 NVIDIA GPUs using GB200 NVL72 systems to train the DeepSeek-V3 model. This represented the largest scale achieved in that benchmark round.

For MLPerf Inference v6.1, Azure’s NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems led cloud submissions in DeepSeek-R1 interactive throughput by more than 10%, according to Signal65.

These results demonstrate Microsoft’s ambition to compete at the infrastructure level, where GPU availability, networking, power efficiency and software optimization increasingly determine the economics of AI services.

However, benchmark leadership does not automatically translate into identical performance across every enterprise workload. Real-world results will depend on model architecture, inference patterns, networking and deployment configurations.

Microsoft Foundry Expands Access to NVIDIA Nemotron Models

getthegist.beehiiv.com

Another major part of the partnership focuses on enterprise AI customization.

NVIDIA and Microsoft are expanding the availability of NVIDIA open models and associated development capabilities through Microsoft Foundry. The objective is to make it easier for businesses to adapt AI models to specialized tasks without relying entirely on proprietary frontier models.

Microsoft’s serverless Programmed Training offering gives developers control over post-training workflows while Foundry manages the underlying infrastructure.

Enterprises can use their own business data to evaluate model responses, adjust behavior and improve performance for specific applications.

Foundry Managed Compute is also gaining support for custom model weights, connecting model customization with deployment through Microsoft’s managed infrastructure.

This approach could make specialized AI systems more practical for organizations that need predictable behavior, greater control over their data and lower operating costs.

EY Reports 55% Accuracy Improvement and 15x Lower Costs

One of the most interesting examples comes from professional services company EY.

EY used Microsoft Foundry’s Programmed Training capabilities to adapt NVIDIA Nemotron open models for classifying corporate purchasing records.

The resulting customized model improved accuracy on EY’s evaluation dataset by 55% while reducing costs by 15 times compared with proprietary frontier models.

The example illustrates why open models are becoming increasingly attractive to large enterprises.

Instead of paying for expensive general-purpose models to perform repetitive business tasks, companies can customize smaller or specialized models around their internal requirements.

The reported improvements are specific to EY’s evaluation workload, but they demonstrate the potential economic advantages of enterprise model customization.

NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Expands Enterprise AI Capabilities

The expanded NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 lineup on Foundry Managed Compute is designed to support different enterprise workloads.

Nemotron 3.5 Super targets long-running AI agents that handle coding, research and complex business workflows. Nemotron 3.5 Lightning focuses on applications requiring fast responses and tool execution.

The ecosystem also includes complementary models for different stages of enterprise AI operations:

Nemotron Embed: Helps AI applications retrieve relevant information.

Nemotron Rerank: Improves the relevance of retrieved information.

Nemotron Parse: Extracts structured information from business documents.

Nemotron Content Safety: Helps identify harmful or unsafe content.

Two additional models are also planned.

Nemotron 3.5 Bolt is designed for high-throughput, low-latency reasoning, while Nemotron Transcribe targets speech recognition.

Together, these models could help developers build more complete AI agent systems without having to assemble every individual capability from unrelated providers.

AI Moves Beyond Azure Into Local Infrastructure

aicerts.ai

Microsoft and NVIDIA are also expanding their partnership beyond traditional cloud infrastructure.

The expanded support for Nemotron 3.5 in Foundry Local brings reasoning, coding and tool-using agent capabilities to Azure Local environments.

Retrieval models will also help applications identify evidence across different types of business information, including text, document images, charts and tables.

Azure Local is gaining support for NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs from hardware partners, giving organizations additional options for running AI workloads on their own infrastructure.

This is particularly relevant for businesses that cannot move all their data into public cloud environments because of regulatory requirements, security policies or operational restrictions.

The move also reflects growing demand for hybrid AI architectures, where organizations distribute workloads between cloud data centers and privately controlled infrastructure.

NVIDIA Jetson Thor Brings AI Into Industrial Operations

windowscentral.com

The partnership is extending into industrial AI through NVIDIA Jetson platforms.

Azure Local is expanding to NVIDIA Jetson Thor and Jetson Orin edge AI platforms, targeting applications such as machine vision, robotics and autonomous industrial operations.

NVIDIA’s physical AI models, including Cosmos, are also being integrated into Foundry Local.

Physical AI represents an important development beyond conventional chatbots and digital assistants. These systems combine AI models with cameras, sensors, robotics and physical environments, allowing machines to interpret surroundings and interact with the real world.

Potential applications include automated inspection, warehouse robotics, industrial monitoring and autonomous machinery.

By connecting NVIDIA’s edge hardware with Microsoft’s enterprise infrastructure, the companies are attempting to make industrial AI easier to develop and manage.

Windows PCs Get More Local AI Capabilities

windowsforum.com

Microsoft and NVIDIA are also bringing their collaboration closer to individual developers.

At Ignite, the companies are showcasing local large language model experiences on Windows PCs powered by NVIDIA RTX Spark.

The demonstrations highlight how developers can run AI applications directly on local devices rather than depending exclusively on remote cloud services.

Local AI offers several potential advantages, including reduced network dependency, improved data control and the ability to experiment with models without continuously sending information to external servers.

For developers and businesses working with sensitive information, local inference could become an increasingly attractive option.

The broader direction is clear: AI computing is moving toward a distributed model in which cloud GPUs, workstation hardware and edge devices all contribute to the same ecosystem.

Windows 365 Gets GPU-Accelerated Direct Streaming

The companies are also improving Microsoft’s Cloud PC experience.

Direct Stream capabilities are coming to GPU-accelerated Windows 365 Cloud PCs, supporting demanding graphics applications through remote computing infrastructure.

The technology supports up to two 4K streams at 60 frames per second, with low latency even across challenging network conditions.

A limited preview is open for sign-ups, while a public preview is planned for early 2027.

This could benefit professionals working with graphics-intensive applications, engineering software and other workloads that require more powerful hardware than a conventional office PC.

It also strengthens Microsoft’s broader strategy of making powerful computing resources accessible through cloud-managed desktops.

Microsoft Discovery Adds NVIDIA Scientific AI Tools

nvidianews.nvidia.com

The partnership is also expanding into scientific research.

NVIDIA BioNeMo Agent Toolkit and NVIDIA ALCHEMI Toolkit are joining the Microsoft Discovery catalog, bringing additional capabilities for life sciences, drug discovery, materials science and chemical research.

BioNeMo Agent Toolkit allows scientists to use natural language to initiate computational biology workflows within Microsoft Discovery.

AI agents coordinate the necessary tools and model calls to carry out research tasks, potentially reducing the complexity of scientific computing workflows.

An initial integration uses the RFD4-Proteina model, developed in collaboration with the University of Washington’s Institute for Protein Design.

The model can generate candidate protein structures and amino acid sequences for subsequent evaluation and laboratory validation.

This is an important distinction: AI-generated protein candidates are not automatically validated discoveries. Experimental testing remains necessary before researchers can establish whether a candidate works as intended.

Nevertheless, integrating scientific models with agentic research platforms could help accelerate early-stage experimentation.

Microsoft Uses NVIDIA Dynamo to Improve Its Own AI Models

Beyond expanding access for customers, Microsoft is also adopting NVIDIA technologies within its own AI development operations.

Microsoft AI is using NVIDIA Dynamo inference software to support reinforcement learning rollout inference during model post-training.

This process generates experiences used to improve AI models, making inference performance an important part of the overall training pipeline.

NVIDIA Dynamo coordinates inference across large GPU fleets, optimizing performance while helping manage incoming requests, model updates and recovery from failures.

The deployment builds on joint engineering between Microsoft AI and NVIDIA.

Microsoft is also using NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs to accelerate Bing search models.

These deployments demonstrate that the partnership is not simply about selling NVIDIA infrastructure through Azure. Microsoft is also relying on NVIDIA’s accelerated computing ecosystem to improve its own AI products and development processes.

## What Undercode Say:

NVIDIA and Microsoft are building something much larger than another cloud computing partnership. Their strategy increasingly resembles a complete enterprise AI operating ecosystem, connecting data centers, model development, private infrastructure, robotics, scientific research and personal computing.

The most important development is the combination of NVIDIA hardware with Microsoft’s software and enterprise distribution.

NVIDIA has the accelerated computing infrastructure, GPU technologies, inference software and growing collection of open AI models. Microsoft has Azure, Windows, Foundry, enterprise customers and a massive collection of business applications.

Combining these strengths gives both companies an advantage that would be difficult for smaller competitors to reproduce.

The Vera Rubin deployment is particularly important because the next generation of AI systems will require increasingly powerful infrastructure. Reasoning models and autonomous agents can generate substantially more inference demand than traditional question-and-answer chatbots.

However, simply adding more GPUs will not solve every problem. Electricity availability, cooling, networking and inference efficiency will become increasingly important constraints.

The confidential computing announcement is another significant development. Enterprises are reluctant to give sensitive business information to AI systems without strong guarantees about how their data and models are protected.

Extending hardware-level protections across entire AI infrastructure could help address some of these concerns, although customers will still need to evaluate the complete security architecture.

The EY example deserves attention because enterprise AI adoption ultimately depends on economics. A 15x cost reduction is potentially more meaningful to many businesses than another impressive benchmark score.

It demonstrates why open models and customization are becoming important alternatives to relying exclusively on the largest proprietary models.

The expansion into Jetson Thor and physical AI could also become one of the most strategically important parts of this announcement. The next major wave of AI adoption may involve machines that understand physical environments rather than simply generating text and images.

Robotics, manufacturing, logistics and industrial automation could become major markets for these technologies.

Microsoft Discovery’s integration with NVIDIA scientific tools adds another dimension. Drug discovery and materials research are computationally demanding fields where AI could improve experimentation and accelerate scientific workflows.

But there is a competitive consideration: Microsoft and NVIDIA are also strengthening their mutual dependence. Microsoft needs NVIDIA’s hardware and software ecosystem, while NVIDIA benefits from Microsoft’s enormous cloud infrastructure and enterprise distribution.

That relationship creates opportunities, but it also introduces concentration risks if customers become too dependent on a small number of infrastructure providers.

The bigger picture is that enterprise AI is moving beyond the model itself. Hardware, inference optimization, security, data retrieval, customization and deployment are becoming equally important parts of the competitive landscape.

The companies that control these layers could capture a significant share of the next AI spending cycle.

Deep Anlysis: The Battle for the Complete AI Stack

## 1. Infrastructure Is Becoming the Foundation of AI Competition

The Vera Rubin announcement demonstrates that the AI infrastructure race is entering another stage.

The initial AI boom was heavily focused on training increasingly large models. The next stage involves operating sophisticated AI systems continuously, with reasoning, memory, retrieval and tool execution.

This changes infrastructure requirements.

Companies need systems capable of handling enormous inference volumes while maintaining predictable latency and operational costs.

NVIDIA’s combination of Vera Rubin, Blackwell, Dynamo and its networking ecosystem positions the company to compete across this entire infrastructure stack.

## 2. Open Models Could Change Enterprise Economics

The EY example illustrates a potential turning point.

A specialized model does not necessarily need to outperform every general-purpose model. It needs to perform sufficiently well for a particular business task at a competitive cost.

Enterprises can use post-training to adapt model behavior to internal workflows, reducing the need to pay premium prices for every inference request.

## 3. AI Security Will Become a Competitive Differentiator

Enterprise adoption requires more than accurate models.

Companies must protect proprietary information, control model access and verify the infrastructure executing sensitive workloads.

Confidential computing could become increasingly important as AI systems gain access to internal databases, financial records, intellectual property and regulated information.

The next generation of enterprise AI platforms will likely compete on security guarantees as much as model performance.

## 4. Edge AI Could Reduce Dependence on Centralized Infrastructure

Jetson Thor, Jetson Orin and Azure Local demonstrate Microsoft’s interest in distributing AI workloads.

Consider a manufacturing environment where cameras monitor production lines.

Instead of transmitting every video frame to a distant cloud data center, an edge AI system could analyze footage locally, identify defects and communicate only relevant results.

This reduces network traffic and potentially improves response times.

## 5. The Real Competition Is Between Ecosystems

NVIDIA and Microsoft are building an interconnected platform in which hardware, models, development tools and deployment services reinforce each other.

This creates a powerful commercial advantage.

A business can potentially train or customize a model, deploy it through Foundry, operate it on Azure or Azure Local, and eventually extend its capabilities to industrial edge devices.

The challenge for competing companies is no longer simply developing a better AI model.

They must provide a complete alternative covering infrastructure, developer tools, enterprise security, model availability and deployment.

The central prediction: The next major AI competition will not be decided exclusively by who develops the smartest model. It will be decided by who can deliver the most capable AI systems at the lowest operational cost, across cloud, enterprise and physical environments.
