## NVIDIA announces new Rubin HPC GPU for scientific computing, launching in 2028

NVIDIA has announced Rubin HPC, a new GPU designed for high-performance computing, scientific simulations and data analytics. Revealed at SC26, the GPU is part of the Vera Rubin platform but targets workloads that require large amounts of general-purpose GPU computation rather than primarily AI inference and training.

The company confirms that Rubin HPC features **more than 50,000 CUDA cores on a single GPU**, the highest count NVIDIA has announced for one GPU. The processor will also support seventh-generation NVLink technology, moving beyond the NVLink 6 interconnect used by the existing Rubin architecture.

### **Rubin HPC focuses on compute and data movement**

Rubin HPC introduces sparse data acceleration designed to improve access to scattered data during computation. NVIDIA says this capability will keep its CUDA cores supplied with data rather than leaving them waiting for memory operations. The GPU targets operations including sparse matrix calculations, stencil computations, sorting and reductions.

These operations are common in scientific and engineering software. NVIDIA lists climate modeling, genomic analysis, engineering simulations and financial risk analysis among the workloads that could benefit from the architecture. Rubin HPC will also work with NVIDIA’s CUDA-X libraries, including algorithms that AI agents can invoke when running simulations.

### **More specifications are still missing**

NVIDIA has not provided the exact CUDA core count, memory capacity, memory bandwidth, power consumption or FP32 and FP64 compute performance. For reference, the existing Rubin GPU features **288GB of HBM4 memory, u**p to 22 TB/s of memory bandwidth and NVLink 6 connectivity delivering 3.6 TB/s of bidirectional bandwidth. These specifications should not be assumed for Rubin HPC.

NVIDIA expects Rubin HPC to become available in **2028**, two years after the introduction of the standard Rubin generation. The company has not announced pricing, system configurations or manufacturing partners for the new GPU.
