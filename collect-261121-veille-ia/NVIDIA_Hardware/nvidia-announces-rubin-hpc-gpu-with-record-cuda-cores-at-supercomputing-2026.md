Nvidia unveiled the Rubin HPC GPU at Supercomputing 2026, a chip carrying more than 50,000 CUDA cores for agentic HPC workloads, set to arrive in 2028.

Nvidia unveiled the Rubin HPC GPU at the Supercomputing 2026 conference, a new member of the Rubin family built to accelerate agentic workflows and simulation for scientists, engineers, and analysts working with large data sets. The chip carries more than 50,000 CUDA cores, the highest count on any Nvidia GPU, and is scheduled to arrive in 2028.

The core count implies a multi-die package. Today's Rubin GPU ships with 224 streaming multiprocessors and 28,672 CUDA cores. Reaching past 50,000 cores would require more dies on a single package, a configuration resembling the four-die Rubin Ultra, which links dies through NVLink-C2C. Nvidia did not share the physical layout of Rubin HPC.

NVLink 7 will connect Rubin HPC GPUs. The interconnect retains the 3.6 TB/s of per-GPU bandwidth that NVLink 6 delivers and enlarges the scale-up domain. Nvidia shared no new details on bandwidth or physical layer specifics within the rack.

Nvidia disclosed no HBM capacity or bandwidth figures for Rubin HPC. The company did not say whether the part rebalances compute resources toward native FP64, a direction AMD and Intel promoted at the same conference with their own GPUs. Nvidia emphasized the chip's fit for agentic workflows, where faster simulation means faster discovery.

The company describes Rubin HPC as a balanced design: higher compute resources plus faster data movement between nodes, feeding the chip with the large data sets typical of climate research, genomic analysis, and financial simulation. That balance aims to reduce idle compute and raise utilization.

Researchers, engineers, and analysts running agent swarms can use Nvidia's CUDA-X domain-specific libraries on Rubin HPC. Those libraries tighten the loop from hypothesis to simulation to refinement, helping teams reach promising results faster.

Nvidia says Rubin HPC will arrive in 2028.
