In an official Nov. 16 announcement, Nvidia stated that its Vera Rubin platform made its MLPerf debut with top performance across training, post-training, and agentic inference workloads. The company reported sweeping every MLPerf Training v6.1 benchmark, claiming that Vera Rubin NVL72 delivered up to twice the performance per graphics processing unit of its predecessor GB300 NVL72 system.

In tests on the DeepSeek-V3 benchmark, Nvidia reported that Vera Rubin NVL72 scaled from 256 to 1,024 GPUs with roughly 97% efficiency, cutting training time from 16 minutes to about four. Nvidia stated that it also ran 10,240 GB300 GPUs, matching Microsoft Azure's 10,240 GB200 GPUs. The company credited NVLink scale-up connections alongside Quantum-X800 InfiniBand and Spectrum-X Ethernet datacenter networking.

On the MLPerf Endpoints benchmark evaluating agentic AI, Nvidia said Vera Rubin NVL72 achieved more than 10 times the throughput per watt of GB300 NVL72 at identical response speeds in a provisional test using the Kimi K3 model. The company added that submissions from 20 partner organizations, including Dell Technologies and Google Cloud, accompanied the training round.

Silicon Report reported on Nov. 5 that Nvidia claimed Vera Rubin NVL72 reached roughly up to 30 times higher agentic inference throughput per dollar than GB300 NVL72 at 125 tokens per second per user on SemiAnalysis AgentX tests.
