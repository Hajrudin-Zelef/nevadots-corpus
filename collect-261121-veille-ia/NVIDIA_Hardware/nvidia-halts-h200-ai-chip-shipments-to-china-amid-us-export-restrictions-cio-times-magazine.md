Nvidia has stopped shipping its H200 AI Chip (AI) accelerators to customers in China, marking a significant shift in the company’s access to one of the world’s largest technology markets. The decision follows renewed uncertainty surrounding US export controls and the future of advanced semiconductor trade between Washington and Beijing.

The development was confirmed by Nvidia’s interim CEO, **Colette Kress,** during an earnings call on Tuesday. She stated that the company was no longer shipping licensed Hopper data centre products to China. The H200 AI Chip accelerator, built on Nvidia’s Hopper architecture, had previously received conditional approval for sales to selected Chinese customers.

## US Policy Shift Changes Nvidia’s China Strategy

The suspension follows a decision announced by US President Donald Trump in December 2025 to permit Nvidia to sell H200 products to approved customers in China. The announcement indicated that the arrangement would require 25% of the revenue to be paid to the United States.

The approval had offered Nvidia an opportunity to regain access to Chinese demand for its AI chips, while operating under specific government conditions. However, the latest announcement signals that the arrangement is no longer translating into shipments.

The development also highlights the continuing uncertainty surrounding US technology export policies, particularly as Washington seeks to balance commercial interests with national security concerns and competition from China’s semiconductor industry.

## Nvidia’s Longstanding Push for Market Access

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has consistently argued against broad restrictions on advanced GPU exports to China, warning that excessive controls could undermine the competitiveness of American technology companies.

In 2023, following the introduction of tighter export restrictions under former President Joe Biden, Huang cautioned that China remained an important market for the global technology industry. He also warned that limiting access to US technology could encourage Chinese companies to accelerate the development of domestic alternatives.

For Nvidia, China represents a significant commercial opportunity, particularly as demand for AI computing infrastructure continues to expand.

The halt in H200 AI Chip shipments could therefore complicate the company’s efforts to capture demand in the Chinese market. It also underscores the broader challenges facing semiconductor manufacturers as geopolitical tensions increasingly influence global technology supply chains and international trade.
