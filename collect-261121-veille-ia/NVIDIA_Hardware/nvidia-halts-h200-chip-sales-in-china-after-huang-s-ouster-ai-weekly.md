## TL;DR

- Acting CEO Colette Kress ended H200 sales in China, closing a loophole that routed shipments through the US under a 25% tariff.
- Q3 China sales totaled $2.4 billion, or 2.2% of data center revenue, and have been stripped from Q4 guidance.
- Jefferies estimates the tighter export rules could cut Nvidia's data center revenue by $44 to $62 billion next year.

Nvidia will stop selling its H200 AI chip in China, acting CEO Colette Kress told investors on Tuesday's earnings call, ending participation in a loophole brokered by former CEO Jensen Huang and President Trump that routed shipments through the United States and let Washington collect a 25% tariff. TechCrunch reported the policy shift landed on Nvidia's first earnings call since Huang stepped down last week.

The near-term hole looks small on paper. Q3 sales to China totaled $2.4 billion, or 2.2% of data center revenue, and those products have been stripped from Q4 guidance. Kress said backlog for the forthcoming Vera Rubin chip means the new export regime would not dent top-line revenue.

Investment bank Jefferies disagrees, forecasting the rules could trim between $44 billion and $62 billion from Nvidia's data center revenue next year. New restrictions issued Monday bar advanced U.S. chips from foreign data centers serving Chinese customers remotely, closing a path by which Chinese AI labs had been reaching Grace Blackwell chips despite the export ban.

Huang's exit removes the loudest internal voice for staying engaged. He had long argued that walking away from China hurt the U.S. tech sector, saying 'to concede that market for the United States technology industry is a disservice to our country.' Kress's Tuesday announcement is the inverse bet.
