Nvidia is in advanced talks to acquire Inception, a two-year-old startup building a different kind of AI model, in a deal worth more than $500 million, The Information reported on Thursday, citing three people familiar with the matter.

The proposed structure includes earnout awards for Inception employees who join Nvidia, tied to meeting performance goals, one of the sources said. The talks remain under discussion, and neither company has confirmed them.

An acquisition would be one of Nvidia's clearest moves yet toward building AI models that compete directly with the dominant approach behind ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude — rather than simply selling the chips those models run on.

## A faster way to generate text

Inception builds diffusion large language models, known as dLLMs. Where today's leading chatbots generate text one token at a time, diffusion models refine an entire answer in parallel, drafting and correcting many pieces at once.

The approach promises much faster output. Inception's latest model, Mercury 3, was claimed to run at more than 1,000 tokens per second on Nvidia GPUs — several times faster than conventional models — while matching or beating rival systems on benchmarks.

Speed matters because inference, actually running AI models for users, is becoming the biggest slice of AI computing demand. A model that answers faster can serve more users on the same hardware, and for Nvidia, owning that technology could make its chips even more attractive.

## Why Nvidia wants the talent

People familiar with the discussions say Inception's technology and the team behind it would support Nvidia's development of its open-source Nemotron models, the company's own family of AI models offered to developers and enterprises.

Nemotron is already part of Nvidia's push to be more than a hardware supplier. The company offers the models alongside its chips, software and cloud partnerships, giving customers a ready-made starting point instead of a blank slate.

Bringing Inception in-house would deepen that bet, adding a team that has spent two years working on an architecture most of the industry has ignored — and that, if it works at scale, could redraw the economics of running AI.

## Dealmaking continues after Huang's exit

The talks come as Nvidia navigates a leadership transition. Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, and chief financial officer Colette Kress was appointed interim chief executive on November 11 while keeping her finance role.

Despite the upheaval, Nvidia's dealmaking has not slowed. The company invested $2 billion in cloud provider CoreWeave in January and another $2 billion in Nebius in March, and took part in a $300 million Series B round for Black Forest Labs in December 2025.

Earlier this month, Nvidia also licensed technology from AI chip firm Groq in a deal reported to be worth around $20 billion, absorbing its chief executive and other senior staff. The pattern is consistent: Nvidia is using its vast cash pile to lock in the companies and talent shaping the next phase of AI.

Whether the Inception talks close remains to be seen. Deals at this stage can still fall apart, and the earnout structure suggests Nvidia wants the team motivated to deliver after the sale. But the direction is clear — the world's most valuable chipmaker wants a bigger say in how AI models themselves are built.
