## TL;DR

- Nvidia is in advanced talks to buy diffusion-LLM startup Inception for more than $500 million plus performance earnouts tied to new-employee targets.
- All three co-founders are expected to join Nvidia: Stanford CEO Stefano Ermon, UCLA professor Aditya Grover, and Cornell professor Volodymyr Kuleshov.
- Inception's diffusion architecture, which the company claims runs 10x faster and at 1/10th the cost of traditional LLMs, would feed Nvidia's Nemotron models and robotics AI.

Nvidia is in advanced talks to buy Inception, a diffusion-based language-model startup, for more than $500 million plus performance-based earnouts, The Information reported this week. The outlet describes Inception as "a two-year-old startup that develops a type of AI that aims to perform faster than the one that powers products such as ChatGPT and Claude."

The earnouts are tied to whether new employees meet specific targets after close. All three co-founders are expected to move to Nvidia: Stanford professor and CEO Stefano Ermon, UCLA professor Aditya Grover, and Cornell professor Volodymyr Kuleshov. Ermon is a co-inventor of the diffusion methods widely used in modern image and video generation. The team's architecture refines a whole response in parallel instead of predicting one token at a time, which Inception claims runs 10x faster and at 1/10th the cost of traditional LLMs. The flagship model is called Mercury.

Inside Nvidia, the technology is expected to strengthen the chipmaker's Nemotron open-weight models and AI systems for robotics.

Neither Nvidia nor Inception has publicly confirmed the deal, and the $500 million is a floor rather than a final price. The earnout structure, pegged to post-close hiring, is unusual enough to flag. It lands in a busy stretch of Nvidia coverage on our tracker, 306 alerts in the last 90 days.
