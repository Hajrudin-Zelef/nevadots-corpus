**Shortly after the dismissal of CEO Jensen Huang, Nvidia is halting deliveries of its H200 chips to China. That ends the exports for which the company spent months trying to secure approval, both in Washington and in Beijing.**

Nvidia is no longer supplying its H200 chips to China. Interim CEO Colette Kress said so during the discussion of the quarterly results. In the third quarter, Nvidia still sold 2.4 billion dollars’ worth of Hopper data center products in China. Kress does not say why the company is halting deliveries.

Last year, former CEO Jensen Huang still obtained permission from the White House to export the chips to China after hard lobbying. Kress has taken over his duties since Huang stepped down after two affairs, one of which involved an alleged Chinese spy.

## No chips for China after all

“Our outlook does not include licensed Hopper data center products, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China,” Kress said. She does not say why Nvidia is halting deliveries or whether the decision is final.

The halt stands out, because Nvidia has lobbied long and hard for exports to China. In December 2025, Trump approved the delivery of the H200 under strict conditions. In January 2026, the US officially gave the green light, although by February no chips had yet been delivered because of a slow approval process. In August, Beijing also opened the door to the H200.

## Revenue rises to 115.8 billion dollars

In the third quarter of its 2027 fiscal year, which ended on 25 October 2026, Nvidia posted revenue of 115.8 billion dollars. That is twenty percent more than the previous quarter and 103 percent more than a year earlier. Net profit rose by 105 percent to 65.3 billion dollars. Gross margin remained almost stable at 74.8 percent.

For the fourth quarter, Nvidia expects revenue of 130 billion dollars, with a margin of three percent. The company no longer counts on revenue from data center computing power in China.
