Nvidia has stopped shipping H200 AI chips to China, despite President Donald Trump's decision in December 2025 to allow sales to approved Chinese customers. The US subsequently eased its export licensing rules in January.

Interim CEO Colette Kress confirmed the halt during the company's November 17 earnings call. Nvidia sold $2.4 billion worth of licensed Hopper-generation data center products to China in the quarter ended October 25 but has no further shipments planned under its current outlook.

The company did not explain why shipments stopped or whether the halt would be permanent.

Meanwhile, Beijing has restricted H200 imports as it seeks to reduce reliance on US technology. Earlier this month, Reuters reported that around 600,000 H200 chips were being held in Hong Kong awaiting approval for large-scale imports into mainland China.

Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance were among 10 Chinese companies approved by Washington to purchase the processors.
