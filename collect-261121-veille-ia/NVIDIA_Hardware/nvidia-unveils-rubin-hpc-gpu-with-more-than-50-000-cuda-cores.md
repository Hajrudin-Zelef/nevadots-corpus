# Nvidia unveils Rubin HPC GPU with more than 50,000 CUDA cores

*Photo source: Tom's Hardware*

*Nvidia says Rubin HPC is designed to accelerate agentic workflows and simulations involving large datasets. The company expects the GPU to arrive in 2028.*

At Supercomputing 2026, Nvidia unveiled Rubin HPC, a new member of its Rubin GPU family. The company says the chip has the highest number of CUDA cores in any of its GPUs: “more than 50,000.”

This is reported by Tom's Hardware.

That core count could indicate a layout similar to Rubin Ultra, which uses four dies connected by NVLink-C2C. Nvidia did not disclose Rubin HPC’s physical layout. Its current Rubin GPU has 224 SMs and 28,672 CUDA cores, and the company image accompanying the announcement did not appear to reflect the possible configuration.

Nvidia says Rubin HPC GPUs will use NVLink 7 to communicate. The company gave no new details about the interconnect’s bandwidth or physical layer within a rack. Past disclosures indicate that it retains NVLink 6’s 3.6 TB/s of bandwidth per GPU while expanding the scale-up domain.

Nvidia did not disclose Rubin HPC’s HBM capacity or bandwidth, or whether it shifts compute resources toward native FP64. AMD and Intel are touting their own GPUs at the conference this week.

Nvidia says the chip is suited to scientists and engineers using agentic workflows, and expects better agent performance in those fields to speed up simulations and create faster opportunities for discovery. In climate research, genomic analysis and financial simulation, the company says Rubin HPC’s “balanced design” - with more compute resources and, presumably, faster data movement between nodes - will help keep the chip supplied with large datasets, reducing idle compute resources and improving utilization.

Researchers, engineers and analysts using agent swarms to explore problems in their fields will also be able to use Nvidia’s CUDA-X domain-specific libraries to boost performance on Rubin HPC. Nvidia says this can shorten the process from hypothesis to simulation to refinement and speed the pursuit of promising results.
