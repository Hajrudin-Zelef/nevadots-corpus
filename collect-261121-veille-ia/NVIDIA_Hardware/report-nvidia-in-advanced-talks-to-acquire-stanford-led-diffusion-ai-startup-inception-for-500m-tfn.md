- Nvidia is reportedly in advanced talks to buy diffusion AI startup Inception for over $500M.

- The deal would bring Inception’s three co-founders into Nvidia full-time.

- Inception raised a $50M seed round in November 2025, with Nvidia among its backers.

Nvidia is reportedly in advanced discussions to acquire Inception, a two-year-old artificial intelligence startup developing diffusion-based large language models, in a transaction valued at more than $500 million, according to The Information.

The proposed deal would bring Inception’s three co-founders, Stefano Ermon, Aditya Grover and Volodymyr Kuleshov, into Nvidia full-time. It would also include additional earnout awards for employees, subject to performance targets, the report said, citing people familiar with the discussions.

Based in Palo Alto, California, Inception has more than 30 employees. It raised a $50 million seed round in November 2025 led by Menlo Ventures, with participation from Mayfield, Innovation Endeavors, Microsoft’s M12, Snowflake Ventures, Databricks Investment, and Nvidia’s venture arm NVentures. Andrew Ng and Andrej Karpathy invested as angels.

## What makes Inception’s diffusion models different?

Most large language models generate text one token at a time, predicting each successive piece of text based on what came before. Inception takes a different approach through diffusion-based language models, which generate and refine multiple tokens in parallel.

Its Mercury family of models uses an iterative refinement process designed to accelerate text generation. Inception claims Mercury is 5 to 10 times faster than speed-optimised models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google while matching their accuracy. Mercury has been integrated into developer tools including ProxyAI, Buildglare, and Kilo Code. Ermon, a Stanford professor, is Inception’s chief executive.

The approach is not Inception’s alone. Google demonstrated its own Gemini Diffusion in May 2025. Diffusion could offer another way to improve inference speed, particularly for applications where response time and computing efficiency matter.

## Nvidia’s push beyond chips

The potential acquisition fits into Nvidia’s broader strategy to expand beyond its role as a leading supplier of AI computing hardware. The company has been investing in AI models, software platforms, and research teams that could strengthen its wider ecosystem.

Inception is already connected to Nvidia through NVentures’ participation in its seed round. Bringing the startup inside the company would take that relationship further, giving Nvidia greater control over the development of its diffusion-based model technology.

The price is notably lower than the figure Inception was reportedly valued at earlier this year. Reuters reported in May 2026 that Microsoft was in discussions with the startup and that SpaceX had also courted it.

The Information reported that the Nvidia talks began before Jensen Huang’s resignation as Nvidia’s chief executive on November 12, 2026. Colette Kress is interim chief executive.

## From Hugging Face to Groq: Nvidia’s expanding AI strategy

The reported Inception discussions follow other major moves by Nvidia to strengthen its position across the AI ecosystem.

In September 2026, Nvidia agreed to acquire Hugging Face, the French-founded open-source AI platform, for about $12.9 billion. Nvidia expects the deal to close in the first half of 2027, and the US Department of Justice has asked the company how it could affect competitors. Nvidia had previously participated in Hugging Face’s $235 million Series D funding round in 2023.

In December 2025, Nvidia signed a reported $20 billion non-exclusive licensing agreement for Groq’s inference technology and hired its founder Jonathan Ross, president Sunny Madra, and other employees. It was structured as a technology licensing and talent arrangement rather than a conventional acquisition. Groq has since continued operating as an independent inference cloud company.

Nvidia has also announced planned minority investments in European startups including Doctolib, Genesis AI, Pigment, and Entalpic.

## What comes next for Inception?

For Nvidia, bringing Inception into the company could strengthen its Nemotron portfolio of open AI models, which businesses and government customers can customise for their own applications. Nemotron models are already used by organisations including Booz Allen Hamilton and Palantir Technologies.

However, the discussions remain at an advanced negotiation stage, and neither company has publicly confirmed an agreement. The price and terms could still change.
