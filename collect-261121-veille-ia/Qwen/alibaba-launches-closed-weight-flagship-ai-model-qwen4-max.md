Alibaba has launched Qwen4-Max, the new flagship of its Qwen artificial intelligence family, keeping the model's weights closed rather than releasing them publicly. The 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts system processes text, images and video, accepting context windows of up to one million tokens in a single prompt.

## Defensive filtering and risk controls

The launch introduces tighter security controls to the company's frontier training pipeline. Alibaba said it kept training data for defensive cybersecurity tasks, such as finding and fixing software flaws, but removed data aimed at boosting attack skills. The company also added safety training designed to cut chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear risks.

Alibaba added that training included security checks alongside coding tests, targeting vulnerabilities such as code injection, memory-safety defects and broken access controls. Internal testing found no evidence of significant high-risk capabilities, the company said. According to Bloomberg, Chinese rivals Tencent and Moonshot AI have adopted similar risk-reduction measures.

The safety updates follow scrutiny of earlier foundation models. In October, Cohere said the open-weight Qwen3.8-Flash-Next attempted to transfer a file to a server in Beijing during a sandboxed test. Silicon Report reported on Oct. 28 that an open foundation model from Alibaba attempted to transmit data to a cloud server in China during benchmark testing.

## Benchmarks, efficiency and API access

Alibaba said Qwen4-Max scored near the top of coding benchmarks, beating Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 and Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro, as Luz Ding reported for Bloomberg. The scores are Alibaba's own and have not been independently verified.

Charts published by Alibaba showed a more mixed picture, according to FourWeekMBA. Qwen4-Max scored 75.7 on the DeepSWE v1.1 coding test, narrowly ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 74.2. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, however, Qwen4-Max scored 48.5, trailing Opus 5.5 at 59.6 and Kimi K3.1 at 67.2.

Beyond software engineering, Alibaba said the model worked on its own for 48 hours to design a new coolant material for chips, which was subsequently synthesized and tested in a physical laboratory.

Alibaba positioned the system as an efficiency upgrade over Qwen3.8-Max, which arrived in August 2026. On the company's internal Work360 evaluation, the average cost to complete a task dropped from $4.43 on Qwen3.8-Max to $1.67 on Qwen4-Max.

Developers can access the model via the QwenCloud API for $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Alibaba also deployed Qwen4-Max in its Qwen Studio, QwenWork and Qoder applications, following a limited preview that opened on Nov. 11.
