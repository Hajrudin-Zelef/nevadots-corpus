# Alibaba unveils Qwen4-Max, claims it beats rival models on coding

Alibaba has a new flagship AI model, and it arrived with bold claims about coding performance.

The company unveiled Qwen4-Max and said it outperforms Claude Opus 5.5 and shows competitive results against Kimi K3.1 Pro on coding benchmarks.

Qwen4-Max launched on November 16, 2026, as the top model in the new Qwen4 series. It runs on a sparse mixture-of-experts architecture built around a 2.4-trillion-parameter backbone.

That architecture is worth a quick translation. Picture a hospital with hundreds of specialists, where each patient sees only the few doctors relevant to their case. A mixture-of-experts model works the same way, sending each request to a subset of its internal "experts" instead of firing up all 2.4 trillion parameters at once. The result is a very large model that does not have to pay the full computing bill on every task.

The model is multimodal, which means it handles text, images and video. It also supports a context window of up to 1 million tokens. A context window is roughly the model's short-term memory, so a bigger one lets it work with long documents or large codebases in a single session.

Developers can reach Qwen4-Max through an API on QwenCloud. Alibaba has also made it compatible with protocols established by OpenAI and Anthropic. For teams already built around those ecosystems, switching becomes less of a rewrite and more of a configuration change.

On the DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark, Qwen4-Max scored 75.7. That result put it ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and showed competitive results against Kimi K3.1 Pro, according to the research findings on the launch.

Independent rankings are less generous. They place Qwen4-Max behind competitors such as Kimi, even while highlighting its favorable cost per task.

Where Alibaba makes its strongest case is efficiency. Qwen4-Max uses approximately 50% fewer tokens than its predecessor, Qwen3.8-Max. Average task costs fell by over 60% compared with that earlier model.

Tokens are the chunks of text a model reads and writes, and they are also how most AI providers bill customers. Fewer tokens per job means a smaller invoice for the same work.

Alibaba set Qwen4-Max pricing at $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. That positions it as a cost-effective option within the flagship tier of AI models.

The research findings put its cost per task at roughly $3.27, lower than competitors.

Access did not begin on launch day. A Token Plan preview opened on November 11, 2026, and runs until November 25.

Alibaba also plans to release smaller open-weight models in the Qwen4 series. The flagship Qwen4-Max, however, will stay closed.

Alibaba's pitch leans away from the pure capability arms race. Instead, the company is emphasizing efficiency and practical use, targeting professional productivity and coding tasks. It is aiming the model at sectors such as healthcare, finance and law, where workflows are document-heavy and cost discipline is a real constraint.

For businesses buying AI, the most important number here may not be 75.7. It is the combination of lower token usage and lower per-token pricing. Those two savings stack, which is why Alibaba can point to task costs dropping by more than 60% versus its own previous model.

The compatibility with OpenAI and Anthropic protocols lowers the cost of trying Qwen4-Max for developers already invested in rival platforms.

Still, the gap between Alibaba's benchmark claims and independent rankings deserves attention. Companies evaluating the model should weigh third-party results alongside vendor numbers, especially for coding work where performance varies by task. The fact that independent rankings still credit its cost per task suggests the efficiency story holds up better than the outright performance crown.

The open-weight strategy is the other thread to watch. Releasing smaller Qwen4 models openly could widen Alibaba's developer base, while keeping the flagship closed protects its most valuable asset.
