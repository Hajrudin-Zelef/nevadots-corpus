Alibaba launched **Qwen4 Max** on November 16, and independent benchmark firm **Artificial Analysis scored it 43** on its Intelligence Index, up from 34 for the previous Qwen flagship. The new model also cost **$3.27 per index task**, compared with $10.02 for its predecessor, but ranked ninth among the 10 models listed on its Artificial Analysis page.

Alibaba describes Qwen4 Max as a model with a **2.4-trillion-parameter backbone**, 71 billion parameters active per token, and an additional 328 billion N-gram embedding parameters. The company says it accepts **text, images and video** and has a one-million-token context window. These specifications and the launch description come from Alibaba; the model weights are not available for outside inspection.

Artificial Analysis’s launch-day figures put Qwen4 Max at **43 on a 0-to-70 index**. Its predecessor, Qwen3.8 Max (0902), scored 34, while the median for models at a similar price was 41. On the 10-model page, Qwen4 Max was ninth; the leading model scored 65. The benchmark result indicates an improvement over the prior Qwen flagship, while the page ranking places it below most of the listed models.

Artificial Analysis estimated the cost of one index task at **$3.27 for Qwen4 Max**, against $10.02 for Qwen3.8 Max (0902). Alibaba separately lists API prices of $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. Those per-token prices and the benchmark’s per-task estimate describe different measures, so they should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Lower Costs, Ninth-Place Index Rank

The results give organizations weighing model costs and performance a more specific comparison than Alibaba’s launch claims alone. On Artificial Analysis’s measure, Qwen4 Max improved over its predecessor while reducing the reported cost of an index task. That combination may matter to teams running many model tasks, where costs can shape which systems are practical to use.

The ranking also sets a limit on the launch-day case for the model. Its **score of 43 was above the comparable-model median of 41**, but it remained ninth out of 10 models on the page, well below the leading score of 65. The index is one benchmark, not a complete measure of quality across coding, research, office work or other uses. Buyers still need task-specific results and reliable speed data to judge whether the price difference suits their workloads.

## Alibaba’s Claims and the Available Checks

Alibaba’s launch announcement presents Qwen4 Max as the first model in its Qwen4 family and as a work-focused, always-available AI assistant. It reports a score of **52.69 on its in-house Work360 test**, compared with 47.74 for Qwen3.8 Max, and says average cost per task on that test fell from $4.43 to $1.67. Work360 is Alibaba’s own evaluation, so those results are vendor-reported rather than an independent comparison.

The company also reports reduced token use on Work360 and Terminal-Bench 4.0, improved results on a secure-coding test, and examples involving manufacturing and other professional tasks. These figures and examples are claims in Alibaba’s launch material. Case studies and vendor benchmarks can show what a company says its model achieved, but they do not by themselves establish how it will perform for other customers or under independent testing.

Artificial Analysis published its Qwen4 Max and Qwen3.8 Max (0902) model pages on **November 16, 2026**. Its launch-day page provided index and cost figures but no speed or latency results. The available benchmark therefore offers an early comparison of measured score and estimated task cost, while leaving important operational questions open.

## Speed, Weights and Real-World Results

**Artificial Analysis had no speed or latency data** for Qwen4 Max on launch day, leaving response times and throughput unmeasured in the cited independent results. The model’s weights are closed, so outside researchers cannot inspect or run the full system locally. Alibaba says smaller Qwen4 models will be open-sourced, but the launch material gives no release date.

The available evidence also does not settle how Qwen4 Max performs across different customer tasks, workloads or operating conditions. Alibaba’s Work360 and case-study results are company-reported; Artificial Analysis supplies an independent index score, but one index cannot answer every use-case question. The launch-day figures do not establish how the model compares on speed, reliability or total cost for a particular deployment.

## More Independent Tests Needed

The next useful evidence would be **speed and latency measurements**, alongside further independent results on coding, reasoning and professional tasks. Those comparisons could show whether the lower reported index-task cost comes with tradeoffs that matter in day-to-day use.

Customers can access Qwen4 Max through Alibaba’s QwenCloud API, Token Plan subscription and QwenWork, Qoder and QwenStudio apps, according to the launch material. Alibaba has said smaller Qwen4 models will be open-sourced, but has not set a date. For now, the available record is a launch-day score and cost comparison, with broader performance and release details still pending.
