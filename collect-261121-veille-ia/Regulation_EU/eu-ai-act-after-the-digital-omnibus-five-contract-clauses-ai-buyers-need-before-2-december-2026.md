# EU AI Act After the Digital Omnibus: Five Contract Clauses AI Buyers Need Before 2 December 2026

The AI Act’s high-risk deadline moved to 2027, but the dates that matter to a buyer did not all move. Here is what the law says today and how to turn it into contract language.

**Quick answer:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, which amends the AI Act, is dated 8 July 2026 and was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026. It sets the high-risk obligations for Annex III systems (including employment and HR use cases) to 2 December 2027 and for Annex I systems to 2 August 2028. It does not pause Article 50 transparency duties, which apply since 2 August 2026. The one date closing in is 2 December 2026, when providers of generative systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 must have taken the necessary steps to comply with the machine-readable marking duty in Article 50(2). If you buy or deploy AI in Europe, those dates should now appear in your supplier contracts.

## What changed, and what did not

- **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** amends Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act). It is dated 8 July 2026 and was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026.
- **Annex III high-risk systems** (for example, AI used to recruit, select or manage workers): obligations in Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3 apply from 2 December 2027.
- **Annex I high-risk systems** (AI embedded in regulated products): from 2 August 2028.
- **Article 50(2) marking:** a new transitional rule in Article 111(4) requires providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text, and were placed on the market before 2 August 2026, to take the necessary steps to comply by 2 December 2026. Systems placed on the market after 2 August 2026 get no such grace.
- **Article 4 (AI literacy)** was amended. Providers and deployers must take measures to support the development of AI literacy of their staff and other persons operating and using AI systems on their behalf. The duty remains, but it does not require guaranteeing a specific literacy level for any individual (Article 4(1)).
- **Harmonised standards:** according to CEN-CENELEC (source below), EN 18286:2026, a quality management system standard, was cited in the Official Journal in October 2026 and is the first harmonised standard under the AI Act. Under Article 40(1), the presumption of conformity covers the Chapter III, Section 2 requirements to the extent a cited standard covers them. Article 17 sits in Section 3, so do not assume a quality management standard gives a presumption for Article 17.

## Why this matters for ROI, not only compliance

A later deadline lowers your urgency, but it also lowers your supplier’s. That is the window in which a buyer has leverage. Terms agreed now are cheap to write. Terms discovered missing in 2027, mid-contract, cost a renegotiation, a delayed rollout, or a switch of vendor. The return on doing this work early is avoided rework and a cleaner procurement file, not a headline saving, and no study I have seen puts a reliable figure on it.

## Five clauses to put in the contract

**1. Dated compliance commitments.** Ask the supplier to warrant, by named date, what they will have ready for Annex III uses by 2 December 2027: risk management (Article 9), technical documentation (Article 11), logging (Article 12) and a quality management system (Article 17). Ask separately about the conformity assessment procedure (Articles 16 and 43) and its timing. A general promise to “comply with the AI Act” is not testable.

**2. Marking and transparency for generative tools.** If the supplier’s system generates synthetic audio, image, video or text, ask how it meets Article 50(2), by which date for systems already on the market, and what the technical method is. Clarify who handles Article 50 duties on the deployer side, such as disclosing deepfakes, so roles are not left open.

**3. Standards used, by version.** Ask which harmonised standards the supplier applies, in which version, and whether their references are published in the Official Journal. Under Article 40(1), the presumption of conformity covers only the Section 2 requirements that a cited standard actually covers. A quality management standard speaks to quality management, not to every obligation in the Act.

**4. Change-of-law mechanics.** The deadlines moved once already. Add a clause that sets what happens to price, scope and timeline if dates or standards change again, and who bears the cost of adaptation.

**5. Evidence on demand.** Reserve the right to request documentation and evidence of compliance at defined points, not only at signature. This is the clause that most often gets dropped in negotiation, and the one buyers most regret.

## What is still not confirmed

- Guidance and standards still being drafted should be treated as pending until published in the Official Journal or by the competent authority.
- Statements that a party “has been approved by the EU” for a data practice should be checked against a published legal text. Proposals are not law.
- The references to EN 18286:2026 rely on the CEN-CENELEC announcement and are not checked against the Commission’s implementing decision. This article summarises the amending regulation and one standard. It does not cover national implementation in each Member State.

## FAQ

**Did the Digital Omnibus delay the whole AI Act?** No. It moved the high-risk dates for Annex III and Annex I systems. Article 50 transparency duties apply since 2 August 2026.

**When must generative AI providers mark AI-generated content?** The transitional rule in Article 111(4) covers only systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026, which must take the necessary steps to comply by 2 December 2026. It does not cover systems placed on the market after that date.

**Is HR AI high-risk now?** Recruitment, selection and workforce management are listed in Annex III, point 4, so the high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027. Check each tool’s intended use and the Article 6(3) derogation. A system that profiles natural persons is always considered high-risk.

**Do I still need AI literacy measures?** Yes. Article 4(1) requires providers and deployers to take measures to support AI literacy among their staff and others using AI systems on their behalf.
