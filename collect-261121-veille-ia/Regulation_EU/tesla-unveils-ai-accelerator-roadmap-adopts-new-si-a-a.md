## The story

Tesla has disclosed a revised roadmap for its AI accelerators, according to reports published between November 6 and November 8, 2026. The plan was presented in a recent company presentation, and a social media post dated November 4 pointed out that Tesla's chips, previously labeled AI4, AI5 and AI6, now appear as SI4, SI5 and SI6. Tesla has adopted "super intelligence" in place of artificial intelligence for its accelerators, and the same reports note that Elon Musk recently confirmed SpaceXAI would be renamed SpaceXSI for the same reason. The roadmap also brings back the Dojo training accelerator in a new form.

The sixth-generation inference chip, SI6, is expected to deliver 80 times the compute performance of the fourth-generation SI4, with about 9 times the memory. Using estimates in the roadmap table, the SI4 offers 216 to 576 TOPS, which puts the SI6 at roughly 17,280 to 46,080 TOPS, or 17.28 to 46.08 POPS. Tesla did not say which data formats the SI6 supports, so these figures are estimates. One report says the SI6 is slated to launch in 2029 to 2030, built by Samsung Foundry on a 2nm-class process through 2033. The table also lists the SI5 at 40 times the SI4's performance, with 96 GB of memory against 16 GB for the SI4.

For training, the third-generation Dojo, called D3, moves away from the wafer-scale, SRAM-centric design of the original D1. It will instead use a chiplet-based design with 2.5D and 3D packaging and stacked HBM memory. The D1 linked 25 processors with 11 GB of SRAM using wafer-level packaging. Tesla says D3 will be optimized for space, training and scaling, which suggests radiation hardening for at least some versions. The company has not disclosed D3's compute performance, memory capacity, manufacturing process or development schedule, and it remains unclear whether wafer-scale integration has been abandoned entirely.

The roadmap comes from Tesla, led by Musk, and the specifics of the SI6 and D3 plans come from the company's own presentation as relayed by the outlets. The social media post said the new terminology echoed a wish by President Trump to replace "Artificial Intelligence" with "Super Intelligence." On context, Musk cancelled the wafer-level Dojo project in August 2025, and in August 2026 he said SpaceX and xAI would use only Nvidia hardware in their terrestrial and space-based data centers. The SI6 is still in development, the SI5 is nearing production, and the SI4 is in volume production in vehicles.
