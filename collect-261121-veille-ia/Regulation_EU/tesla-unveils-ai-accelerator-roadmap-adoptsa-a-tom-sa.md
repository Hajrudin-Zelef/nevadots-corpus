# Tesla unveils AI accelerator roadmap, adopts new 'SI' branding in 'Super Intelligence' push

Tesla is ramping up its custom silicon ambitions with training-oriented Dojo D3 that can eventually go to orbital data centers and massive SI6 inference accelerator.

## The story

Tom's Hardware reports that Tesla has disclosed a revised roadmap for its AI chips. According to the publication, Tesla expects its sixth generation inference accelerator to deliver 80 times the compute of the current fourth generation chip. Tesla has also dropped "AI" in favor of "super intelligence" and renamed its accelerators with an SI prefix. Tom's Hardware adds that Elon Musk recently confirmed SpaceXAI would become SpaceXSI for the same reason.

The most notable item, in the outlet's view, is the return of the Dojo training accelerator. Tom's Hardware notes that Musk cancelled the wafer-level Dojo project in August 2025. It also says he later stated that SpaceX and xAI would use only Nvidia hardware in their terrestrial and extraterrestrial data centers. The third generation, called D3, will differ considerably from the first two, D1 and D2.

Tom's Hardware says Tesla describes D3 as a chiplet-based design that combines 2.5D and 3D packaging with stacked HBM memory. That is a sharp departure from the D1 system-on-wafer design, which the outlet says relied on on-chip SRAM across 25 interconnected processors. The publication reads the move to HBM as a sign that Tesla wants much more memory capacity. It says it is unclear whether Tesla has given up wafer-scale integration entirely, but it judges that approach less likely for D3 because of cost and complexity. Tesla has not disclosed D3's performance, memory capacity, manufacturing process or schedule, though it says the chip is optimized for "space, training, and scaling."

On the sixth generation inference chip, SI6, Tom's Hardware says Tesla's roadmap points to 80 times the compute and nine times the memory of the HW4 chip. The outlet's own estimate, which depends on how HW4 performance is measured, is 17,280 to 46,080 TOPS, and it tells readers to treat that with caution. It says Tesla has given no details about the data formats SI6 will support. For comparison, it cites Nvidia's Rubin GPU at 50 NVFP4 PFLOPS for inference and says Rubin is already shipping to customers. Tom's Hardware says SI6 is due in 2029 to 2030.

What this could mean for readers of The World of AI

For practitioners, the useful point is that Tesla is describing separate chips for training and inference, a split worth noting if you follow how custom silicon is organised. A roadmap states intent, so nothing here changes what hardware you can buy or rent today.

The article body was not read. From the description alone, be sceptical of "eventually" applied to orbital data centers, and of the new "SI" branding, which is a naming choice, not a capability. Treat "massive" as the vendor's word, not a measurement.
