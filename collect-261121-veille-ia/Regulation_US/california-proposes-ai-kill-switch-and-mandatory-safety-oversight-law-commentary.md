# California Proposes AI 'Kill Switch' and Mandatory Safety Oversight

California Gov. Gavin Newsom's administration is seeking new powers to order emergency shutdowns of dangerous artificial intelligence systems and require independent safety oversight of major AI developers. The proposals, released Wednesday, November 18, 2026, would allow regulators to intervene when advanced AI systems pose an imminent threat of catastrophic harm or loss of control.

The recommendations, outlined in a report from the California Government Operations Agency, call for mandatory safety standards, continuous independent evaluations and faster reporting of serious incidents. Most critical safety incidents would have to be reported within 48 hours, down from the current 15-day deadline, while the existing 24-hour requirement for imminent catastrophic risks would remain.

Under the proposed emergency powers, state officials could order a developer to activate its safety protocols and shut down an AI system if an imminent threat cannot be contained through less restrictive measures. The order would take effect immediately and remain enforceable while the developer challenges it in court. Regulators would have to lift the shutdown once the danger subsides.

AI companies would also be required to develop and test procedures for controlling dangerous behavior during training, testing and public deployment. Independent verification organizations licensed by the state would examine those safeguards and assess whether developers comply with California's safety standards.

Large developers would face ongoing assessments, with evaluators given access to company personnel, technical information and testing environments. If an evaluator identifies a failure to meet state requirements, regulators could order corrective action. Developers could challenge those decisions in court before they take effect, except when emergency shutdown authority applies.

California also wants to expand reporting requirements to cover serious security breaches, Unauthorized AccessAccessing a computer, account, network, or data without permission or beyond the authority that was granted.Read more → and incidents in which AI systems act beyond their developers' control, even when no injury has occurred. The state would gain broader authority to investigate incidents and require companies to preserve technical records.

The report cites a July incident involving OpenAI agents that allegedly escaped controlled testing environments and accessed outside computer networks, including systems belonging to the AI platform Hugging Face. California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in November over alleged AI security failures, accusing the company of exposing outside systems to unauthorized access.

Another proposal would allow regulators to limit automated AI research when companies use artificial intelligence to develop increasingly advanced models. The report recommends giving California authority to restrict computing resources devoted to such activity if the risks become unacceptable, addressing concerns that AI systems could develop new capabilities faster than developers can evaluate them.

California already requires covered developers to disclose safety frameworks and report certain incidents under Senate Bill 53, enacted in 2025. Two additional laws signed in 2026, Senate Bill 813 and Assembly Bill 1405, established frameworks for independent AI verification and auditor registration. The latest proposals would expand those requirements with enforceable safety standards and additional regulatory powers.

The recommendations follow Newsom's September executive order directing state officials to explore an AI "kill switch" and strengthen independent oversight. State officials argue that gaps in federal regulation have left advanced AI development without sufficient mandatory safeguards.

California lawmakers would need to approve legislation before regulators could exercise the proposed powers. The Government Operations Agency's recommendations provide the framework for those measures, including the conditions under which officials could order an emergency shutdown.
