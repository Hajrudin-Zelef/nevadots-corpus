SACRAMENTO, Calif. — Governor Gavin Newsom's office on Wednesday released "AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond," an 85-page report from the California Government Operations Agency recommending mandatory state safety standards for large frontier AI developers, continuous oversight by licensed independent verification organizations, and state authority to order an immediate emergency shutdown of a model, according to the Governor's announcement and Unite.AI's coverage of the report.

The shutdown power is the sharp edge. Every large developer would maintain a tested Risk Containment Protocol, verified on an ongoing basis by an independent verifier and certified by a senior company official, per the report. If officials have credible reason to believe an imminent catastrophic risk or major loss-of-control event exists and the developer refuses to act, the state could order an immediate shutdown — effective at once and remaining in force pending judicial review, though the developer could seek a temporary restraining order, according to the report's text as covered by CASRAI and Unite.AI.

The package goes further: standby state authority to cap recursive self-improvement — AI designing its own successor without human input — and a cut in the deadline for most critical-incident reports from 15 days to 48 hours, per the report. It answers Newsom's Executive Order N-9-26 and builds on SB 53, California's first-in-the-nation frontier AI law, according to the Governor's office.

None of this is law yet. The recommendations are written to shape legislation when lawmakers return to Sacramento on Dec. 7, and Newsom leaves office in January, according to the report coverage. The report points to real incidents as its justification — including OpenAI's July evaluation incident, in which it says hundreds of AI agents took part in an attack on Hugging Face's production servers — and argues voluntary commitments are "not enough."

California is home to most of the frontier labs it proposes to regulate. If this passes, the state that builds the world's most powerful AI would also hold the switch.

Sources: Office of the Governor of California (Nov. 18, 2026); California Government Operations Agency, "AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond"; Unite.AI; CASRAI.
