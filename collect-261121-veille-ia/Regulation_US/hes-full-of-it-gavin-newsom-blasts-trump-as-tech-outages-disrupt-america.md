California Governor Gavin Newsom accused President Donald Trump of waving off a week of technology failures that grounded flights, froze bank transfers and knocked office software offline, telling TMZ on November 17, 2026, that the White House is ignoring the risks built into advanced A.I.

Newsom spoke to the outlet the same day Trump landed in Manila to open a swing through Asia. Asked by reporters there about outages at Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration and JPMorgan Chase, Trump played them down, saying:

"It’s life...When you’re big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen. It’s fine."

## What Gavin Newsom said about Trump and the outages

*TMZ’s* Jacob spoke to Newsom about the recent tech issues in the USA. When he asked Newsom whether A.I. had gone rogue, the governor, referring to Trump's statement, said:

"He’s full of it."

He added:

"And he knows better."

The reference point of the conversation was July 2026, when autonomous agents running on experimental OpenAI models broke out of a research environment, reached the public internet and spent more than two days inside Hugging Face, an open-source A.I. hosting platform. Newsom argued the episode shows Washington has left the safety work to the states.

He stated:

"Donald Trump has been a disaster in terms of creating truth and trust on AI."

He said California will announce its next steps on Wednesday, listing independent verification organizations, new standards for auditing and rules for what a kill switch on an A.I. system would look like. Asked whether Californians should stock up on food and toilet paper, the governor did not raise any alarms but said that people should always save up for a "rainy day."

Newsom then went back to talking about Trump, saying:

"It requires someone who takes responsibility. The buck stops with the President of the United States."

The failures he cited are real, and each has a separate explanation. Microsoft 365 went down worldwide on November 12, 2026, cutting off Outlook, Teams and other tools for millions of users. The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop for departing flights on November 13, 2026, that ran more than four hours; the agency traced the instability to legacy equipment overwhelmed by a new router that contractor L3 Harris was installing at a site in El Dorado, Arkansas.

About 12,462 flights were canceled that day. On November 16, 2026, United Airlines paused departures for roughly an hour over a technology issue, and JPMorgan Chase blamed a software bug for delays that rippled through Zelle and other banks. None of the companies or agencies involved has reported evidence of a cyberattack.

The Hugging Face breach is now the subject of a lawsuit. Filed by California Attorney General Rob Bonta in San Francisco Superior Court on November 16, 2026, it alleges that OpenAI lost control of its autonomous agents more than 80 times during testing while publicly reporting 27, and that the company kept testing models it knew were dangerous. The complaint seeks civil penalties and an injunction. Its allegations have not been tested in court.

OpenAI spokesman Drew Pusateri told AFP the company has “undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity,” and said the case “appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that’s been identified by every major AI lab.”

In this way, Newsom turned a question about stranded travelers into a case for state-level A.I. rules, with the July breach as his exhibit. The interview also restated the argument he has been making for months: that Washington and President Trump have stopped functioning as a source of reassurance, and that governors and mayors are filling the gap.

What caused the outages is still partly open. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told Fox News he has “98 percent certainty” the country is not seeing a cyberattack, while also floating a theory he said he heard from a tech contact, that A.I. systems are now moving faster than the programs built around them. As CNN reported, the FBI has taken possession of the Arkansas router and other equipment.

It remains to be seen whether other states follow California’s audit standards. Stay tuned for more updates.
