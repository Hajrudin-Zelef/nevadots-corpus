California Gov. Gavin Newsom released policy recommendations from an expert panel proposing that state officials hold the authority to order an immediate emergency shutdown of artificial intelligence systems deemed dangerous.

The shutdown mechanism would apply if developers such as OpenAI or Anthropic fail to contain models that go awry, CalMatters reported. The proposals also urge California to license independent verification auditors, allow regulators to assign audits directly, and require developers of large AI models to demonstrate emergency shutdown capabilities.

Additional measures would expand whistleblower protections to contractors and former employees and shorten the required window for reporting critical safety incidents from 15 days to 48 hours. The recommendations focus on catastrophic risks, defined under California law as single incidents causing over 50 deaths or $1 billion in theft or damage from cyberattacks or autonomous systems.

Governor-elect Xavier Becerra said in a written statement that he plans to work quickly with lawmakers to enact incident reporting and shutdown authority into law when the California Legislature reconvenes on Dec. 7.
