What is Runway Gen-4.5?

Runway Gen-4.5 is Runway's current high-end video generation model, released as an update to Gen-4 with a stronger focus on realism and prompt accuracy. It covers text-to-video generation, so you can describe a scene in plain language and receive a finished clip, and image-to-video generation, where you upload a photo and describe the motion you want added to it.

The model also gives you motion and camera control, letting you direct how the frame moves instead of settling for a fixed, static shot. Runway built Gen-4.5 with an emphasis on prompt adherence, meaning the output matches what you actually typed rather than a loose interpretation of it. Visual fidelity and temporal consistency round out the model's core strengths, so faces, objects, and backgrounds stay stable from the first frame to the last instead of drifting or warping partway through the clip.

What makes Runway Gen-4.5 different?

Runway Gen-4.5 isn't a single new toggle bolted onto the old model. The differences show up in what you can actually produce, from full scenes built out of a text prompt to camera moves that used to require an actual film crew.

Create detailed scenes from text prompts

You can describe both the visual elements and the movement of a scene in one prompt, and Runway Gen-4.5 reads the two parts separately. The visual description sets the subject, the setting, and the lighting, while the motion description tells the model what happens once the clip starts. A prompt such as "a woman in a red coat waits at a rainy train platform, then turns toward the approaching train as steam rises around her" gives the model a subject, a mood, and an action to shape the shot around.

Animate images with controlled motion

When you work in image-to-video mode, the source image already sets the composition, subject, lighting, and style of the clip, so your prompt only needs to describe the movement. This matters because it changes how you write the prompt: instead of re-describing what's already visible in the photo, you tell Runway Gen-4.5 what should happen next, such as a character turning their head or a wave rolling toward the shore.

Control complex camera movements

Runway Gen-4.5 supports pan, dolly, tracking, handheld, zoom, camera arcs, and focus changes, and you can combine more than one of these in a single clip. A slow dolly-in paired with a subtle focus pull, for instance, gives a shot a cinematic quality that a static frame can't match. This level of camera direction is one of the clearest upgrades over earlier Runway models, where movement often looked shaky or disconnected from the scene.

Create sequences with multiple actions

Gen-4.5 can interpret prompts that describe more than one action happening in order, such as a character standing up, walking to a window, and opening it within a single clip. The model reads the sequence and times each action so the transitions look natural rather than rushed or overlapping.

Keep motion and details coherent

Subject movement, object interactions, environmental motion, material details, and temporal consistency all hold up better throughout a clip than they did in earlier Runway models. Water pours and splashes with real weight instead of a smeared texture, fabric drapes and moves naturally, and a character's face stays recognizable from the opening frame to the closing one. These small details are what separate a clip that looks generated from one that looks filmed.

Runway Gen-4.5 features tested

We ran Runway Gen-4.5 through its core modes to see how the model performs outside of Runway's own marketing claims. The results below show what stood out.

Text-to-video generation

Scenes with a clear subject and a simple action, such as a single character walking through a defined setting, produced the most consistent results. Crowded scenes with several moving elements took more attempts to get right, though the output still held together better than what we've seen from earlier Runway releases.

Image-to-video generation

Image-to-video generation with Runway Gen-4.5 covers uploading an image, prompting the motion you want, preserving the original composition, and controlling how the camera moves around the subject. The model held on to facial details and background elements from the source image while adding motion that matched our prompt, which is the balance this mode is meant to strike.

Camera motion control

We tested pans, dollies, tracking shots, and zooms separately, and each direction produced a smooth, deliberate move rather than a jittery one. We also combined two camera movements in a single prompt, such as a tracking shot with a slow zoom, and it worked in most attempts, though occasionally one movement dominated the other.

Complex motion and timing

Multiple actions within one short clip, such as a character reaching for an object and then turning to leave, generally landed in the right order. The results got less reliable as we added a third or fourth action to the same prompt, so shorter action sequences produced steadier outcomes.

Aspect ratio options

Runway currently supports formats including 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:3, and 3:4 for Gen-4.5, and availability depends on the generation mode you're using. This range covers the common needs for widescreen video, vertical social clips, and square feeds without extra editing after export.

Iterative generation

Instead of starting over from scratch, you can adjust a prompt and regenerate a clip, and the parts that already worked carry over into the new version. This iterative approach saves credits over time, since you're refining a single idea rather than generating several unrelated attempts.

Prompt accuracy

Prompt accuracy is where Runway Gen-4.5 shows the clearest gap over its predecessor. Specific instructions about lighting, camera angle, and pacing showed up in the output far more consistently than they did with Gen-4, and vague prompts still produced a usable clip rather than an unrelated one.

How to use Runway Gen-4.5?

Step 1: Open Runway and select Gen-4.5

Open Runway and, from the left-hand sidebar, click Tool to open the video-generation workspace. Make sure Video is selected at the top. Then click the model dropdown at the bottom of the generation panel, where the current model name is shown (such as Seedance 2.5). From the model list, find Gen-4.5 under the Runway models and select it. Gen-4.5 supports Image/Text to Video, so you can start with either a text prompt or a reference image.

Step 2: Write your prompt and adjust the settings

Enter a clear, specific prompt that explains what you want to see happen in the video. For Text to Video, describe the subject, environment, action, camera movement, lighting, and overall visual style. For Image to Video, focus mainly on the movement you want because the uploaded image already provides the visual details. Then choose the available duration, aspect ratio, and resolution settings that match your project before starting the generation.

Step 3: Generate, review, and refine your video

Click Generate and wait for Runway to create the clip. Watch the result carefully to see whether the subject movement, camera motion, timing, and overall look match your prompt. If something feels off, refine your prompt by being more specific about the movement or scene, then generate another version. Repeat this process until you get a result that fits your project.

How to prompt Runway Gen-4.5 for better results

A strong result from Runway Gen-4.5 comes down to how you structure the prompt, not just how detailed it is.

Describe what the viewer should see

For text-to-video, include the subject, the environment, the lighting, the composition, and the style you want. If you skip one of these, the model still generates a clip, but it leaves more room for guesswork, and guesswork is where the output drifts from your original idea.

Describe how the scene should move

Once the visual side of the prompt is set, describe the subject's action, the environmental motion, the camera movement, the direction, the speed, and the timing. Runway's current prompting guide recommends clear, direct language and identifies visual and motion descriptions as the core components of a text-to-video prompt.

Keep image-to-video prompts focused on motion

The image already provides most of the visual information the model needs, so the prompt should describe what changes and how it moves rather than repeating details the photo already shows. A prompt such as "the character turns her head toward the doorway" gives Runway Gen-4.5 a clear instruction, while re-describing her outfit or the room adds nothing new for the model to work from.

Start simple and add details gradually

A basic version of the prompt is worth generating first, so you can add detail only to the parts that need more direction. This iteration process gets you to a strong result faster than writing one long, dense prompt from the start and hoping every clause lands correctly.

Use natural language instead of keyword stuffing

Runway recommends natural, direct sentences over a list of disconnected keywords, since full sentences give the model more context about how the elements in a scene relate to each other. A sentence describing a scene reads more clearly to the model than a string of adjectives separated by commas.

Use timing when several actions happen

Timestamped instructions, such as noting what happens at the start of a clip versus the midpoint, give the model a clearer structure to follow when a shot contains more than one action. This matters most in longer clips where several things need to happen in a specific order.

Runway Gen-4.5 prompt examples

We tested six prompt types to see how Runway Gen-4.5 handles different creative goals.

Cinematic character prompt

Prompt: A detective in a trench coat stands under a streetlamp in heavy rain, looking up at a lit apartment window.

What we expected: A moody, still shot with rain as the main source of motion.

What Gen-4.5 produced: A steady shot with realistic rain movement and subtle coat movement from the wind, holding the character's face clearly through the frame.

What we'd change: This shot needs a specific camera direction, since the model defaulted to a static frame without one.

Product video prompt

Prompt: A ceramic coffee mug rotates slowly on a wooden table as steam rises from the cup.

What we expected: A clean product shot with visible steam.

What Gen-4.5 produced: Smooth rotation with natural-looking steam, though the steam thinned out earlier than expected.

What we'd change: The prompt could specify the steam's duration and intensity directly.

Action scene prompt

Prompt: A cyclist sprints down a mountain trail, kicking up dust as the camera tracks alongside.

What we expected: Fast motion with a tracking camera and visible dust.

What Gen-4.5 produced: A convincing tracking shot with dust that reacted correctly to the bike's speed.

What we'd change: Nothing major; this was one of the strongest results in our tests.

Camera movement prompt

Prompt: The camera dollies slowly toward a lit candle on a dark table, ending in a close-up as the flame flickers.

What we expected: A slow, controlled dolly shot ending on the flame.

What Gen-4.5 produced: The dolly movement matched the pacing we described, and the flame's flicker looked natural rather than looped.

What we'd change: A slightly longer clip duration to let the movement breathe.

Image-to-video motion prompt

Prompt (with an uploaded portrait): The person blinks, then turns their head slightly toward the light source on their right.

What we expected: Subtle facial motion without any change to the background.

What Gen-4.5 produced: Natural blinking and head movement, with the background and lighting from the original photo staying intact.

What we'd change: Nothing; this is one of the use cases where Runway Gen-4.5 handles image-to-video generation well.

Multi-action prompt

Prompt: A chef chops vegetables, then turns to place them in a hot pan, and steam rises as they hit the surface.

What we expected: Three actions in sequence within one clip.

What Gen-4.5 produced: The chopping and turning motions landed correctly, but the steam appeared a beat later than the vegetables hit the pan.

What we'd change: A timestamp on the steam's timing would fix this, since timed instructions work best in sequences such as this one.

Runway Gen-4.5 pros and cons

What we like about Gen-4.5

Motion looks weighted and physically accurate, especially with liquids and fabric.

Camera controls cover pan, dolly, tracking, handheld, zoom, arcs, and focus changes in one model.

Prompt adherence is noticeably stronger than Gen-4, particularly for lighting and pacing instructions.

Image-to-video generation preserves the source photo's composition and lighting accurately.

The model currently ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Video Arena leaderboard.

Where Gen-4.5 falls short

Complex sequences with three or more timed actions still need extra prompt refinement.

Credits run out quickly on the Standard plan if you generate longer clips regularly.

4K exports and priority rendering are locked behind the Pro and Max plans.

A single Runway subscription only gives you access to Runway's own models, not competing models such as Veo or Kling.
