Runway Gen-4 is a next-generation AI video generation model developed by RunwayML and released in March 2025, designed around a technical challenge that earlier generation models had not fully addressed: keeping the appearance of characters, objects and environments stable from one generated clip to the next. Where previous text-to-video systems produced compelling individual scenes but frequently altered a character's face, clothing or spatial surroundings when asked to continue the same narrative, Gen-4 introduced reference-based conditioning that anchors key visual elements to a supplied source image and carries those anchors across multiple separate generations. That mechanism — combined with physics-aware motion modelling that handles water dynamics, hair movement, cloth deformation and object weight realistically — repositioned the platform as the preferred tool for multi-scene video projects and structured narrative sequences. The Gen-4 model family now spans three variants: the standard Gen-4 for full-quality image-to-video generation; Gen-4 Turbo for credit-efficient production runs; and Gen-4.5, which extends the family to text-to-video generation without a source image, reaching the top position in the Artificial Analysis Text to Video benchmark with 1,247 Elo points. The same Runway subscription also includes Act-Two performance capture and Aleph post-generation in-video editing, both released in July 2025.

## About Runway Gen-4

### Reference-based world consistency across scenes

Gen-4's defining technical contribution is a reference-based conditioning mechanism that allows an uploaded image to act as a persistent visual anchor during generation. When a user supplies a reference photograph of a character, the model maintains recognisable face shape, hair colour, clothing and physical proportions across multiple separately generated clips — behaviour that earlier generative video models achieved inconsistently, requiring frame-by-frame editorial work to carry a character's appearance through a multi-shot sequence. The same consistency logic applies to location and object references: an uploaded photograph of a room or a product anchors the environment and visual styling so that lighting quality, spatial relationships and surface textures persist when a scene is revisited from a different camera angle or with different subject movement. Gen-4 also extends the consistency mechanism to stylistic elements derived from reference images, so a cinematographic register established in one clip — high-contrast backlit, warm practical-lit, flat outdoor natural-light — can continue through subsequent clips without manual colour grading. The result is a multi-clip workflow closer to structured production planning than to independent one-off generation, giving editors coherent material to assemble rather than a set of visually unrelated outputs.

### Three-variant model system: Gen-4, Gen-4 Turbo and Gen-4.5

The Gen-4 model family offers three distinct variants covering different quality and credit trade-offs within the same Runway subscription. Standard Gen-4 targets maximum generation fidelity for image-to-video work, with full reference conditioning and physics modelling, at 12 credits per second of output — appropriate for final production passes where visual quality takes priority over credit economy. Gen-4 Turbo runs at 5 credits per second, trading some quality ceiling for substantially more generation volume from the same credit budget; on a Standard plan with 625 monthly credits, that difference translates to roughly 52 seconds of Gen-4 video versus 125 seconds of Gen-4 Turbo video per month. Gen-4.5 addresses a capability gap in the original Gen-4: it accepts a pure text prompt without requiring a source image, generating video directly from text at 25 credits per second. Gen-4.5 holds the top benchmark ranking in the Artificial Analysis Text to Video benchmark with 1,247 Elo points at the time of introduction. All three variants are available to every paid Runway subscriber and share the same dashboard interface — plan choice determines the monthly credit allocation rather than which models are accessible.

### Act-Two — performance capture from an ordinary driving video

Act-Two is a motion capture feature RunwayML released in July 2025 that brings a capability previously associated with dedicated physical mocap pipelines into a browser-based workflow. The feature takes two inputs: a driving performance video — a clip of a human performer moving, gesturing or speaking — and a reference image of the character that should receive that motion. Act-Two transfers the movement from the driving video onto the character reference, preserving the performer's timing, body dynamics and expressive range while substituting their appearance with the character defined by the reference image. The intent is to let solo creators and small teams produce character-driven video content without access to a motion capture studio, controlled green-screen environment or specialised animation software. Unlike skeletal retargeting in 3D animation toolchains, Act-Two works from ordinary recorded footage with no markers, no calibrated camera rig and no professional capture equipment — a smartphone clip of a person walking, gesturing or lip-syncing is sufficient as the driving input. Act-Two is available to every paid Runway subscriber through the same dashboard used for Gen-4 generation.

### Aleph — text-driven in-video editing after generation

Aleph is RunwayML's post-generation in-video editing system, released in July 2025, designed to address a practical limitation of AI video generation workflows: once a clip is produced, modifying specific visual elements without regenerating the entire clip from scratch had required either external editing tools or accepting the cost of a full re-generation. Aleph accepts a natural-language edit instruction applied to an existing generated clip — instructions like "add rain to this scene", "shift the lighting to late evening" or "change the jacket to a blue coat" — and applies that modification while maintaining temporal consistency. The people, camera motion and foreground action in the clip continue to move as they did in the original generation; only the specified element changes through the edit. That temporal consistency distinguishes Aleph from frame-by-frame diffusion editing, which produces flickering and cut-point discontinuity when stitched together. Aleph is integrated into the Runway editing interface and is included with every paid plan, extending the platform value past the initial generation step into post-generation iteration without requiring a separate editing application.

## Good to know about Runway Gen-4

### What is Runway Gen-4?

Runway Gen-4 is an AI video generation model from RunwayML, released in March 2025 as the fourth generation of the company's flagship generative video system. Its defining capability is reference-based conditioning: an uploaded source image of a character, location or object acts as a visual anchor that the model uses to maintain consistent appearance across multiple generated clips. The model family spans three variants — Gen-4 (12 credits per second, full quality, image-to-video), Gen-4 Turbo (5 credits per second, image-to-video) and Gen-4.5 (25 credits per second, text-to-video, top Artificial Analysis benchmark ranking). All three are available on every paid Runway plan. The same subscription also includes Act-Two performance capture and Aleph post-generation in-video editing, both released in July 2025. Gen-4 outputs at 24 fps and supports controllable camera movement during generation.

### What is the difference between Gen-4, Gen-4 Turbo and Gen-4.5?

The three variants differ in input type, quality ceiling and credit cost. Standard Gen-4 requires a source image as a generation anchor (image-to-video) and produces the highest fidelity in the family at 12 credits per second. Gen-4 Turbo also takes an image-to-video input at 5 credits per second — less than half the cost — with a lower quality ceiling, suited for rapid iteration and production runs where credit volume matters more than maximum fidelity. Gen-4.5 differs from both in accepting a text prompt as its primary input without requiring a source image, enabling text-to-video generation directly. Gen-4.5 costs 25 credits per second and holds the top position in the Artificial Analysis Text to Video benchmark with 1,247 Elo points. All three are included in every paid Runway subscription: Standard ($12 per month, 625 monthly credits), Pro ($28 per month, 2,250 credits) and Max ($76 per month, 9,500 credits).

### How does Gen-4 keep characters and scenes consistent across clips?

Gen-4 achieves visual consistency through reference-based conditioning. You upload a source image — a photograph of a person, a room or a significant object — and the model uses that image as a fixed visual specification throughout generation. Rather than re-interpreting a text description of a character differently each time the model runs, the reference image provides stable visual parameters the model works from: face shape, hair, clothing and proportions for a character; lighting quality, surface materials and spatial arrangement for a location. Supplying the same reference image for a follow-up clip instructs the model to match those anchored parameters rather than deriving fresh appearance from the prompt. Gen-4 also applies physics-based motion modelling — hair, cloth, water and objects behave according to physical dynamics rather than arbitrary pattern matching — which contributes to coherent visual continuity across clips even when a reference image is not directly constraining those elements.

### Who is Runway Gen-4 built for?

Runway Gen-4 is primarily suited to professional creators and small production teams who work on structured multi-shot video narratives: independent filmmakers, advertising and content studios, game and entertainment concept teams, and motion designers building sequences where visual continuity across multiple clips is a core requirement. The reference-consistency mechanism is most valuable when the same character or environment needs to appear recognisably across a sequence of separately generated clips — a use case where earlier tools performed inconsistently. Gen-4 is less obviously necessary for creators who primarily work on single-clip generation without continuity requirements, for whom Gen-4 Turbo offers comparable creative results at lower credit cost. The Act-Two and Aleph features extend the platform's reach to users who need to retarget a performance onto a generated character or make targeted post-generation edits to an existing clip, rather than only those working from a blank text or image prompt.
