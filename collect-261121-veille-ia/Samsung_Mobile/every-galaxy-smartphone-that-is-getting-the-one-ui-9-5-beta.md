Everything starts with the Galaxy S26.

Abhijeet Mishra / SamMobile

One UI fans are rejoicing as the latest version, One UI 9.5, officially entered the open beta development stage earlier today. As usual, the list of Galaxy devices eligible for the stable One UI 9.5 is expansive, but far fewer devices actually get access to the beta. Here's all you need to know.

The Galaxy S26, Galaxy S26+, and Galaxy S26 Ultra are the first and, as of November 18, the only Samsung phones with access to the One UI 9.5 beta firmware.

Thankfully, Samsung will extend the beta program's reach to additional devices at a later date. **We will update this article whenever a new Galaxy phone joins One UI 9.5 beta.** Feel free to bookmark it for future reference.

Samsung fans interested in testing the latest firmware need compatible hardware and have to be located in one of the six markets where Samsung has opened the beta program.

So, if you have a Galaxy S26 in one of the markets listed above and want to test One UI 9.5, you can enroll in the beta program through the Samsung Members app on your phone.

Remember that Samsung won't keep the beta registration window open forever. Once enough people join, Samsung will stop accepting new testers.

Generally speaking, One UI 9.5 updates the camera experience, Samsung DeX, Samsung Health, privacy, accessibility, visuals, and animations.

A couple of standout features are the new App Lock and the latest additions to Samsung Health.

App Lock allows Galaxy phone users to lock individual apps with biometrics, a PIN, a pattern, or a password. Even notifications and widgets associated with locked apps are hidden.

Samsung Health enhances sleep coaching and introduces the Medications tracker feature globally. There's also a new Mindful Note feature that lets you record your thoughts through free writing or guided prompts. Samsung Health then uses this information to offer details about your mood trends.

One UI 9.5 offers many other new and updated features, so it's always a good idea to read the full changelog before committing to beta testing.

Keep in mind that the beta program's purpose is to test features and highlight potential bugs. Issues will be patched, and what you see in Samsung's One UI 9.5 beta firmware today could change by the time the first stable version rolls out.
