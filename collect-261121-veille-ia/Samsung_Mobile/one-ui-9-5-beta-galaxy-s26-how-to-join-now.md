Samsung has opened the One UI 9.5 beta programme for the Galaxy S26 series. The programme went live on November 18, 2026, and Galaxy S26, Galaxy S26+ and Galaxy S26 Ultra owners in six countries can sign up right now through the Samsung Members app.

This is the follow-up to a story we covered on November 5, when the first One UI 9.5 test build for the Galaxy S26 Ultra was spotted on Samsung’s test server. Back then, the beta was only a firmware string — there was no announcement, no confirmed feature list and no date. That has now changed: the programme is open, real users are installing the first beta build, and Samsung has confirmed the headline features.

The timing is quick. One UI 9, based on Android 17, only started rolling out to the Galaxy S26 series in late September, and it has since expanded to older flagships and mid-range phones. Less than two months later, Samsung is already publicly testing the next version. One UI 9.5 is a mid-cycle refinement rather than a full generational jump: it is still built on Android 17, not Android 18, and it focuses on polishing the interface, adding a handful of useful features and improving the AI tools that arrived with One UI 9.

One detail makes this launch unusual. Samsung normally opens its One UI beta programmes in the United States, the United Kingdom and South Korea first, then adds India and Germany later. This time the programme is live in all six eligible countries at the same time: the United States, the United Kingdom, South Korea, India, Germany and Poland. SamMobile notes this is the first time a One UI beta has launched in every eligible market simultaneously instead of staggering the rollout.

That does not mean everyone can join. Registration is open only while places last — Samsung stops accepting new testers once enough people have enrolled — so if you want in, the advice from every outlet covering the launch is the same: sign up as soon as possible.

Joining takes a few minutes, and you do everything on the phone itself. Here is the process, step by step:

1. Check you have the right phone and location. You need a Galaxy S26, Galaxy S26+ or Galaxy S26 Ultra, and you need to be in the United States, the United Kingdom, South Korea, India, Germany or Poland. No other Galaxy phone is eligible yet.
2. Make sure your phone is on the latest stable software. Samsung’s beta registration notice asks you to install the latest stable update first, and to use Wi-Fi for the beta download — the first file is several gigabytes.
3. Open the Samsung Members app. It comes pre-installed on Galaxy phones. If it is not on your phone, download it free from the Galaxy Store or Google Play and sign in with your Samsung account.
4. Tap the One UI 9.5 beta banner. The banner appears on the Samsung Members home screen in eligible countries. Tap it and complete the registration.
5. Check for the update. Once enrolled, go to Settings, then Software update, then Download and install. The beta firmware should appear there. Samsung notes that beta builds roll out gradually, so it may not show up immediately after you register — give it some time and check again.
6. Install, restart, then check again. This is the part that catches people out — see the install quirk below. After the first update installs and the phone restarts, a second update appears. Install that too before judging the beta.

Registration is free. Samsung notes that you pay for any mobile data used, which is another reason to download over Wi-Fi. And remember the slot limit: once Samsung has enough testers, the banner disappears and registration closes, so waiting a week could mean missing the window entirely.

The beta is deliberately narrow for now. Only the three Galaxy S26 models can join: the Galaxy S26, the Galaxy S26+ and the Galaxy S26 Ultra. Samsung has not named any other device, and it has not said when the programme will expand. SamMobile says the company will extend the beta to more devices later and that it will update its coverage whenever a new phone joins, but for now the S26 series is alone.

The six countries are the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, India, South Korea and Poland. Samsung has not said whether carrier-locked S26 units in the US — from Verizon, AT&T or T-Mobile — are included, or only unlocked phones. If you are on a carrier model and the banner does not appear in Samsung Members, that may be why, though Samsung has not confirmed the distinction either way.

Lists circulating online of every Galaxy phone that will eventually get One UI 9.5 are projections, not Samsung’s word. The stable update will reach a much wider range of phones, but the beta list and the stable list are different things, and only the beta list matters if you want to test the software today.

Samsung has not published a full changelog, and some of what is known comes from the beta build itself and early hands-on reports rather than an official feature list. What is confirmed so far is a mix of one long-requested feature, several AI upgrades and a round of polish across the system.

App Lock finally arrives. This is the headline addition. One UI 9.5 adds a native app lock, letting you lock individual apps behind biometric authentication, a PIN, a pattern or a password. Notifications and widgets from locked apps are hidden too, so a locked messaging app does not leak previews onto your lock screen. On the Galaxy S26 Ultra, app lock also works with Privacy Display, which can turn on automatically every time you open a locked app. Galaxy owners have asked for a built-in version of this for years instead of relying on Secure Folder workarounds or third-party lockers.

A more proactive Bixby. Android Police reports that One UI 9.5 brings a proactive Bixby assistant, and a smarter Now Brief that surfaces more useful information through the day. This builds on leaks we covered earlier about Bixby 5.0, which pointed to a redesigned interface and a memory feature that lets the assistant remember context across conversations. Samsung has not detailed exactly what the new Bixby can do in the beta, so treat the specifics as something to verify once more testers have the build in hand.

Tap to Share in Quick Share. One UI 9.5 adds Tap to Share, which lets you share files by tapping your Galaxy phone against another compatible phone. Both phones need NFC, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi switched on. It is a small feature, but it removes the usual dance of opening a share sheet and picking a device from a list.

Camera upgrades. The beta brings more precise camera controls, including enhanced Pro and Pro video modes with customisable controls and presets. These modes are only available on models that support them — Pro Lite and Pro Video Lite variants are excluded — so not every S26 will show every new option.

Samsung Health gets Medications and Mindful Note. Samsung Health adds a Medications tracker that reminds you to take prescriptions, vitamins and supplements, and a Mindful Note feature that lets you record thoughts through free writing or guided prompts, with Samsung Health then showing you trends in your mood. Sleep coaching is also improved. Note the limits: Mindful Note is not supported on Galaxy Watch, and Medications availability may vary by market.

Visual polish and animations. Early hands-on impressions show a new lock screen-to-home transition animation, and One UI 9.5 adds a tilt-responsive Live effect for lock screen wallpapers, which shift depth as you move the phone. SamMobile’s roundup also lists updates to Samsung DeX, privacy, accessibility, visuals and animations. None of this reinvents the phone — it is the usual “.5” treatment of smoothing out the big release — but the lock screen changes are the kind of thing you notice every time you unlock your phone.

One caution applies to all of this: Samsung says beta features can change before the stable release. What is in the first beta build is not a promise of what ships in the final version.

Early users report something odd about installing the beta, and it is worth knowing before you start. The update does not arrive as a single download. First comes the main One UI 9.5 firmware, reported at around 3.6GB. You install it, the phone restarts — and then a second update of about 540MB appears for download.

Do not stop after the first one. Install the second update as well, or you will be running an incomplete beta build. The exact sizes vary slightly by model and region — some reports put the first file closer to 3.7GB — but the pattern is consistent across early reports: two updates, not one.

The first beta build is reported to carry a build number ending in ZZKN, and to include the November 5, 2026 security patch, so joining the beta does not set you back on security fixes. Both of those details come from user reports rather than Samsung, so treat the build number as reported rather than official.

Beta software on your only phone is always a trade, and One UI 9.5 is no exception. Android Police’s advice is blunt: if the Galaxy S26 is your daily driver, consider holding off until Samsung irons out the biggest issues in later builds. The first beta may have bugs, faster battery drain, broken apps and stability problems. Samsung is expected to release at least two or three more beta builds before the end of the year, and each one should smooth things out.

Two practical warnings matter more than the rest:

Back up first. Installing the beta firmware will not erase data from your Galaxy S26. But things can still go wrong with pre-release software, and a backup is cheap insurance. Back up to your Samsung account, Google, or a computer before you tap download.

Leaving the beta wipes your phone. This is the warning most coverage skips. Once the beta is installed, rolling back to stable software before the final release means a full reset — Samsung says the rollback resets the device and removes data generated during beta testing. If you install the beta and change your mind a week later, you will lose everything on the phone unless you backed it up first. The safe play is to back up to a computer, not just to the cloud, before enrolling.

There is one more consideration carried over from the earlier test build. The spotted One UI 9.5 build for the S26 Ultra bumped the bootloader from version U5 to U6, and bootloader bumps are normally one-way — once a phone takes firmware with a higher bootloader version, rolling back to older builds is usually blocked. That detail comes from the November test build, not from the public beta, so it is not confirmed for the build you would install today. But it is another reason to think twice before putting beta software on a phone you depend on.

Do not expect the stable One UI 9.5 update any time soon. Samsung has not announced a stable release date. Android Police expects the stable build around January or February 2027, and expects it to debut on the Galaxy S27 series before reaching the Galaxy S26 and other Galaxy flagships in the weeks that follow.

That pattern fits Samsung’s usual rhythm: the new flagship launches with the new software, and existing phones get it afterwards. It also fits what we reported in November, when the S27 series was rumoured for a January launch with stable One UI 9.5 on board. The S26 series should not necessarily expect the stable update on the same day as the S27 launch — Samsung may take a few additional weeks to prepare the build for existing devices.

Between now and then, expect more beta builds. Android Police expects at least two or three firmware updates before the end of this year, and Samsung will likely widen the beta to more Galaxy phones before the stable release. If your phone is not an S26, the thing to watch is whether Samsung adds your model to the programme — not whether the stable update has a date yet, because it does not.

If you own a Galaxy S26 in one of the six countries and you like testing unfinished software, the window is open now — but it will not stay open forever. Check Samsung Members for the banner, back up your phone first, and remember the second update after the restart.

If your S26 is your only phone, the smarter move is to wait. The first beta is the roughest build Samsung will ship, and the stable release is only two or three months away. Watch the beta coverage, see what the second and third builds fix, and decide when the software has settled.

Either way, the story has moved on from where it stood two weeks ago. One UI 9.5 is no longer a firmware string on a test server. It is a live programme, with a real feature list and a real install path — and the countdown to the Galaxy S27 launch, and the stable release that comes with it, has now properly started.
