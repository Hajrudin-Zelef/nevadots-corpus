Samsung Electronics said that in the third quarter this year, sales of HBM4, its sixth-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM) product, rose more than threefold from the previous quarter, accounting for about 60% of total HBM sales. HBM4, the company's top-selling item this year, has effectively become the main product line. Demand for Nvidia's next-generation graphics processing unit (GPU) "Vera Rubin" has begun to be reflected in HBM4 sales.

Samsung Electronics said on the 29th that on a consolidation basis it posted sales of 195 trillion won and operating profit of 107.4 trillion won in the third quarter. Sales rose 126.6% from a year earlier, and operating profit jumped 782.5%. The operating margin was 55.1%. It was the fourth straight quarter of record results, and cumulative operating profit for the first through third quarters was 254.13 trillion won. Third-quarter operating profit beat the FnGuide consensus of 106.6401 trillion won.

◇ HBM4 share at 60%, share target achieved in Q3

At its third-quarter earnings conference call that day, Samsung Electronics said, "The goal of securing an HBM market share on par with our DRAM market share in the second half appears to have already been achieved in the third quarter." The company said, "Next year's HBM supply volume has been negotiated at target-level prices and is already sold out," adding, "Key customers continue to request additional HBM volume." HBM4E, the seventh-generation HBM, shipped samples in May as a world first and has received positive initial evaluations from customers, and the company said it "plans to begin mass production in the first half of next year and ramp up in the second half of next year."

According to major market research firms, Samsung Electronics' HBM bit shipments in the third quarter rose about 50% from the previous quarter. The industry expects the company's global HBM market share to approach 40% in the fourth quarter from 30% in the second quarter. Some analysts say it is securing a higher share than rivals such as SK hynix and Micron in high-end HBM4 products that adopt advanced processes. A semiconductor industry source said, "There is also a suggestion that Samsung Electronics could handle about 30% to as much as 50% of HBM4 volumes for Nvidia," adding, "As AMD adopts HBM4 in its next-generation accelerator 'MI455X,' the pace of customer diversification is key to expanding share."

Non-memory is estimated to have remained in the red in the third quarter, but losses appear to have narrowed as utilization rose. The industry has expected the foundry division to return to the black as early as the fourth quarter this year and enter a full-fledged profitability improvement phase starting next year. Shipments of 4-nanometer-based HBM4 base die have been contributing to foundry revenue since the second half, analysts say.

◇ "Ten LTAs signed, supplier-favorable conditions to continue in 2028"

Samsung Electronics said it has signed long-term memory supply agreements (LTAs) with 10 major customers and is negotiating with 10 more. Considering the current pace of negotiations, the company said the likelihood is rising that 60% to 70% of total capacity (CAPA) through 2030 will be secured under LTAs. The contracts operate on a rolling basis, with annual negotiations to extend after the base period ends, and some customers' requests for additional extensions significantly exceed the initially expected scale, it said.

Samsung Electronics said, "This year, the company's bit growth (growth rate in shipments) is expected to increase to the high-10% range for DRAM (from last year) and the mid-10% range for NAND flash," adding, "AI infrastructure investment is expected to accelerate as artificial intelligence (AI) customers build their own data centers." While mobile and PCs may see demand adjustments due to rising memory prices, the company expects growth in high-value AI server products to support the market. SK hynix said in its earnings release the previous day that the memory supply-demand structure is likely to remain a supplier-favorable market through 2030.

Samsung Electronics estimated that next year's capital expenditures (CAPEX) will increase sharply, while saying, "The tight supply-demand situation in memory is expected to continue in 2028." The company said, "Given the time from new construction to wafer production, it will be difficult for the increase in 2027 CAPEX to make a substantive contribution to production in 2028," adding, "Constraints on physical supply growth due to higher conversion ratios to HBM and commodity DRAM production will remain inevitable."
