# Samsung brings native app lock to Galaxy phones with One UI 9.5

- Samsung has introduced native app locking with the One UI 9.5 beta, allowing Galaxy users to protect individual apps without relying on Secure Folder.
- Locked apps require authentication to open, have their notifications hidden, and lose their home screen widgets.
- Galaxy S26 Ultra users can also have Privacy Display automatically activate when opening locked apps.

Samsung’s Secure Folder has long been a handy way to keep sensitive information away from prying eyes. But sometimes, all you want to do is hand someone your phone without giving them access to your WhatsApp conversations, photo gallery, or banking apps. That simple task is about to get a whole lot easier on Galaxy phones.

Among the many improvements arriving with the One UI 9.5 beta, Samsung has added a long-requested native app lock feature, as shared by Tarun Vats on X. Xiaomi has offered this convenience for years, and Apple introduced it with iOS 18 in 2024. Google is also catching up, having recently brought back native app lock in Android 17 QPR3 Beta 3 after pulling it from an earlier beta.

Until now, Galaxy owners wanting similar functionality had to rely on third-party app lockers, apps with their own authentication options, or Samsung’s Secure Folder. The latter is great for keeping sensitive data in a separate, protected environment, but it can be unnecessarily complicated for everyday use.

With One UI 9.5, you can protect your existing apps without changing how or where you use them. Opening a locked app requires biometric authentication, a PIN, pattern, or password, even when the phone itself is already unlocked.

Samsung’s implementation also extends beyond simply preventing someone from opening an app. Notifications from protected apps are hidden, and their widgets are removed from the home screen to prevent private information from being exposed.

Galaxy S26 Ultra owners get an additional perk. App lock integrates with the phone’s Privacy Display feature, which can automatically kick in whenever you open a protected app, making it harder for people sitting beside you to see what’s on your screen.

For now, the feature is available to Galaxy S26 series owners running the One UI 9.5 beta. Wider availability is expected with the stable update in 2027, although Samsung has yet to confirm exactly which Galaxy devices will support app lock.
