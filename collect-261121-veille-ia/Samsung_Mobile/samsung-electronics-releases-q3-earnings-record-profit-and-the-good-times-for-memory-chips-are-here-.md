By Li Hailun

Edited by Xu Qingyang

On October 29 local time in South Korea, Samsung Electronics officially released its third-quarter 2026 financial report.

Driven by large-scale investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure and the continued surge in demand for AI chips, the company's consolidated revenue for the quarter reached $145.52 billion (195.25 trillion Korean won), up 13.9% quarter over quarter and up 126.9% year over year.

Operating profit reached $80.07 billion (107.44 trillion Korean won), up 20.1% quarter over quarter and soaring 783.1% year over year, achieving an operating margin of 55.0%, making it the first Korean company to surpass 100 trillion Korean won in single-quarter operating profit.

Net profit reached $56.00 billion (75.14 trillion Korean won), up 514.6% year over year.

At the same time, the company's board of directors approved a cash dividend of $22.36 billion (30 trillion Korean won) as part of its annual shareholder return plan of $67.08 billion to $82.00 billion (90 trillion to 110 trillion Korean won).

### 01 Memory chip supply shortage may extend to 2027

Samsung Electronics' sharp profit growth in the third quarter was mainly driven by AI-driven demand for memory chips.

The DS division's revenue reached $109.63 billion (147.1 trillion Korean won), a significant increase from $24.67 billion (33.1 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and $95.03 billion (127.5 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter, while operating profit reached $79.37 billion (106.5 trillion Korean won), far higher than $5.22 billion (7.0 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and $66.50 billion (89.2 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter.

Among them, memory business revenue was $104.90 billion (140.7 trillion Korean won), a marked increase from $19.90 billion (26.7 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and $90.03 billion (120.8 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter.

Facing limited production capacity, the company prioritized the supply of AI-related products and improved profitability by adjusting its product mix. As server demand grew, memory chip prices generally rose. Sales of Samsung Electronics' high bandwidth memory (HBM) products expanded, and demand for server solid-state drives (SSDs) also continued to increase. Among them, HBM4 further expanded sales thanks to its product competitiveness, driving revenue growth in the memory business.

Samsung Electronics expects that AI infrastructure investment will continue to support memory demand in the fourth quarter, new-generation CPU and GPU platforms will drive sales of related memory products, and the development of personal AI agent services is also expected to boost demand for QLC SSDs. The company plans to continue expanding sales of HBM, server DRAM and SSDs, and to increase the supply of high-speed server DDR5 and QLC SSDs.

Regarding market trends in 2027, Samsung Electronics believes that as the AI ecosystem continues to expand, related infrastructure investment is expected to reach historical highs, and AI-driven chip demand may continue to exceed the industry's overall supply capacity, with the degree of supply-demand tightness potentially even higher than in 2026.

To respond to this trend, Samsung plans to leverage its technological advantages, further optimize its product portfolio, and stabilize sales through long-term contracts. At the same time, the company will accelerate new product development to ensure that products can meet the needs of AI applications in a timely manner.

### 02 Advanced process business improves

In addition to the memory business, Samsung Electronics' System LSI and foundry businesses also improved. System LSI business revenue grew in the third quarter, mainly due to mass production of SoCs and image sensors driven by new smartphone launches in the second half of the year. The company also secured custom SoC projects by expanding customer cooperation.

In the fourth quarter, Samsung Electronics plans to maintain stable production and sales of flagship SoCs and 200-megapixel image sensors. In 2027, the company will continue to expand flagship SoC sales and promote the expansion of high-resolution image sensors into non-mobile device fields. The foundry business benefited from increased sales of HBM base dies and the expansion of advanced process business, with both revenue and profitability improving.

Samsung Electronics said it has secured multiple 2-nanometer high-performance computing (HPC) related orders, including orders from large cloud service providers. The company expects foundry business revenue to achieve double-digit growth in the fourth quarter, and profitability to continue improving, mainly driven by new mobile products, mass production of 4-nanometer NPUs, and growth in memory-related product sales.

Looking ahead to 2027, 4-nanometer and 2-nanometer advanced processes will become important drivers of growth for the foundry business. With demand for AI and HPC continuing to grow, Samsung also plans to advance the start of production at its new factory in Taylor, Texas, USA, to expand advanced process capacity.

### 03 Mobile business still faces pressure

While the semiconductor business performed strongly, Samsung Electronics' device businesses still faced cost pressure.

In the third quarter, revenue from the MX and Network businesses reached $28.10 billion (37.7 trillion Korean won), compared with $25.41 billion (34.1 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and $24.74 billion (33.2 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter of this year; operating loss was $450 million (0.6 trillion Korean won), compared with operating profit of $2.68 billion (3.6 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and an operating loss of $520 million (0.7 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter of this year.

The launch of new flagship products such as foldable phones drove both quarter-over-quarter and year-over-year revenue growth. As the share of flagship model sales increased, business profitability improved compared with the previous quarter, but year-over-year performance was still affected by rising costs.

In the fourth quarter, Samsung plans to leverage the year-end sales season to focus on promoting high-end products such as the Galaxy S26 series, Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and Galaxy Z Fold8, and to control costs by improving operational efficiency.

For the network business, due to declines in North American market sales and high-margin software business, third-quarter profitability fell both quarter over quarter and year over year. In the fourth quarter, the company will seek to expand revenue in the North American, Korean and Japanese markets. In 2027, with investment growth in the telecommunications industry slowing, Samsung plans to drive business growth by winning new orders.

### 04 TV and home appliance businesses still weighed down by costs

Samsung Electronics' display business revenue in the third quarter was $5.96 billion (8.0 trillion Korean won), a slight change from $6.04 billion (8.1 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and $5.59 billion (7.5 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter, while operating profit was $820 million (1.1 trillion Korean won), higher than $520 million (0.7 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter and close to $890 million (1.2 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025.

The small- and medium-sized display business improved profitability on the back of demand for high-end mobile products; the large-sized display business saw both sales and revenue increase, driven by growth in the gaming monitor market.

Samsung Electronics expects that the year-end consumption season and new product launches in the fourth quarter will provide some support for the display business, but external factors may still limit improvements in profitability.

The performance of the TV and home appliance businesses was relatively weak. Combined revenue from the Visual Display (VD) and Digital Appliances (DA) businesses in the third quarter was $10.36 billion (13.9 trillion Korean won), flat compared with the third quarter of 2025 and lower than $10.81 billion (14.5 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter; operating loss was $150 million (0.2 trillion Korean won), wider than the loss of $70 million (0.1 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 and $7 million (0.01 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter.

The TV business was affected by falling demand after the end of major sporting events, with revenue declining, and rising costs further squeezed profits. Although the home appliance business benefited from increased marketing efforts in the Korean market and growth in B2B and subscription businesses, rising raw material and logistics costs continued to put pressure on profitability.

Harman business revenue in the third quarter was $3.21 billion (4.3 trillion Korean won), up from $2.98 billion (4.0 trillion Korean won) in the third quarter of 2025 but lower than $3.43 billion (4.6 trillion Korean won) in the second quarter; operating profit was $450 million (0.6 trillion Korean won), higher than about $300 million (0.4 trillion Korean won) in each of the previous two quarters.

The business benefited from growth in audio and automotive product sales, as well as a one-time gain from U.S. tariff refunds, and profitability improved. Samsung plans to continue expanding its automotive business and consolidate its market position by upgrading its audio product portfolio.

### 05 Capital expenditure continues to increase

Facing market demand brought by AI infrastructure construction, Samsung Electronics continued to increase capital expenditure to expand capacity and meet medium- and long-term business development needs. The company's semiconductor investment will mainly be used for new wafer fabs and related infrastructure to respond to growth in AI and HPC demand; display business investment will be used to upgrade existing production lines.

In the third quarter, the company's capital expenditure reached $21.61 billion (29 trillion Korean won), up $9.09 billion (12.2 trillion Korean won) quarter over quarter and up $14.76 billion (19.8 trillion Korean won) year over year. Among this, DS division capital expenditure was $20.27 billion (27.2 trillion Korean won), and display business was $820 million (1.1 trillion Korean won).

In the first three quarters, Samsung Electronics' cumulative capital expenditure reached $42.41 billion (56.9 trillion Korean won), up $18.41 billion (24.7 trillion Korean won) year over year. Among this, DS division spending was $39.35 billion (52.8 trillion Korean won), and display business spending was $1.79 billion (2.4 trillion Korean won).

Samsung Electronics expects full-year 2026 capital expenditure to reach $62.90 billion (84.4 trillion Korean won), of which the DS division will account for about $57.24 billion (76.8 trillion Korean won) and the display business about $2.91 billion (3.9 trillion Korean won).

In terms of shareholder returns, Samsung Electronics paid a cash dividend of $3.43 (4,601 Korean won) per share to common and preferred shareholders in the third quarter, with total cash dividends reaching $22.36 billion (30 trillion Korean won).

The company expects total shareholder returns for full-year 2026 to be between $67.08 billion and $82.00 billion (90 trillion to 110 trillion Korean won). Among this, about $26.10 billion (35 trillion Korean won) has been planned or completed, and the remaining approximately $41.00 billion to $55.90 billion (55 trillion to 75 trillion Korean won) will mainly be implemented through large-scale share buybacks and cancellation as well as cash dividends.

Samsung Electronics also said it will monitor the price gap between common and preferred shares and adjust the proportion of preferred shares in buybacks according to market conditions. The next phase of the shareholder return policy, the final return amount and the specific arrangement of remaining funds will be determined and announced at a board meeting in late January 2027.

For Samsung Electronics, the next step is to seize the memory chip demand brought by AI infrastructure investment while also dealing with the impact of high chip costs on its device businesses. Whether AI demand can continue to grow, and whether the mobile phone and home appliance businesses can gradually improve profits, will become important points to watch in the company's subsequent performance.
