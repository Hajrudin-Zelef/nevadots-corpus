# Samsung Electronics Says Memory Shortage to Last Through 2028; Next Year's HBM Sold Out, Long-Term Deals Signed With 10 Customers

Samsung Electronics expects the memory supply shortage to extend beyond 2027 and into 2028. At the same time, the South Korean company said all of its 2027 high-bandwidth memory (HBM) output has been sold out at target prices, and that it has signed long-term supply agreements (LTAs) with 10 major customers. Expanding AI infrastructure investment is driving demand, while the time required to build new fabs and install equipment is holding back supply growth, prolonging structural tightness in the memory market.

Samsung Electronics presented the outlook at its third-quarter earnings conference call on Oct. 29. Jaejune Kim, vice president in charge of global memory sales and marketing, said, "A tight supply situation will continue in 2028 as well," adding, "Even if overall output increases in 2027, it will be difficult to raise production further in 2028."

Four main factors were cited for the delayed supply expansion. First, a significant portion of the capital expenditure (CapEx) increase in 2027 will go toward buying new sites, which does not immediately translate into expanded production capacity. Rising construction costs also create an effect in which investment figures look inflated relative to actual capacity. On top of that, it takes a long time from breaking ground on a new fab to actual wafer production, making it difficult for 2027 investment to translate into 2028 bit output.

Constraints on the equipment side are also significant. The supply shortage is pushing up equipment prices, and demands for prepayments to secure production volumes are compounding the problem, making investment figures larger than they actually are. Delays in equipment delivery also slow the pace of expansion. In addition, as HBM4E and HBM5 become the main products, die sizes are growing larger than in previous generations and the number of metal process layers is rising sharply — a structural factor adding to the constraint. The conversion of general DRAM capacity to HBM also acts as a physical supply constraint, the company explained.

Samsung Electronics' response strategy is to "secure cleanrooms in advance while keeping the timing of equipment installation flexible and monitoring changes in demand." It reads as a two-track strategy aimed at reducing the risk of overinvestment if demand falters while enabling an immediate response if the supply shortage worsens.

**HBM4 Revenue Triples; HBM Share Reaches DRAM Level**

A clear rebound was confirmed in the HBM business. Third-quarter HBM4 revenue more than tripled from the previous quarter, and HBM4's share of total HBM revenue expanded to 60%. That matches the HBM4 sales target presented on the second-quarter call.

Samsung Electronics said it appears to have achieved its goal of raising its HBM market share to the same level as its overall DRAM share ahead of the original plan, in the third quarter. Given that Samsung's global DRAM share stood at 38% in the second quarter according to market research firm Counterpoint Research, its third-quarter HBM share is estimated at around 40%.

Preparations for next-generation products are also proceeding smoothly. HBM4E shipped samples to customers for the first time in the world in May and received positive initial feedback, and the company plans to begin mass production in the first half of next year and move to large-scale production expansion in the second half. Kim said, "All of the 2027 HBM supply has already been sold at target prices that reflect product competitiveness," adding, "However, major customers continue to request additional supply."

**AI Demand Spreads From HBM to Server SSDs**

Another point that drew market attention on the call is that AI demand is spreading beyond HBM to NAND-based server SSDs. Samsung Electronics explained that the spread of Agentic AI is driving server SSD demand in two ways.

First, as AI models grow larger and context windows expand, demand for key-value caches (KV caches) surges. Unlike simple conversational AI, Agentic AI generates far more KV cache during inference, leading to additional demand for high-performance, high-capacity TLC SSDs. Second, cloud-based personal AI agent services are creating a new model in which dedicated virtual machines are allocated per user, increasing preference for QLC SSDs to lower latency and raise throughput, according to the analysis.

Samsung Electronics already received large additional QLC SSD orders in the third quarter. V10 TLC products using key bonding technology reached a state ready for mass production in the third quarter, and a 256TB V9 2Tbit TLC product using 32-layer stacking technology is set for commercial launch in early next year.

**Multi-Year Deals With 10 Customers, Talks With 10 More; Target of 60-70%**

As the supply shortage drags on, the profit structure of the memory business is also changing. The weight is shifting from sales driven by short-term spot prices toward long-term contracts.

Samsung Electronics said it has so far completed multi-year supply agreements with 10 major customers, is negotiating with 10 more, and several of those are nearing the final stage. What is notable is that the pool of customers seeking long-term contracts is widening rapidly. Beyond hyperscalers, server OEMs and SoC customers — whose growth is directly tied to AI services — customers across a range of applications including mobile, automotive and networking are also requesting long-term supply.

The contract structure is a rolling one, extended through annual negotiations after the initial contract period ends. Samsung Electronics said extension demand from some existing contract customers is far exceeding initial expectations, and that additional supply requests are also coming in during contract periods. The company's mid- to long-term goal is to raise the share of output covered by multi-year contracts to 60-70%.

**Foundry 2nm Orders Double; Equipment Installation Begins at Taylor Fab**

The foundry business also continued to improve. In the third quarter, revenue and profitability both improved, helped by higher sales of 4nm HBM base dies and expanded revenue from advanced processes for global customers. With 2nm project orders continuing from major cloud service providers (CSPs) and AI and high-performance computing (HPC) customers, the number of 2nm project orders in 2026 is expected to more than double from a year earlier.

Sukchae Kang, a vice president, said cleanroom operations and equipment installation have begun at Fab 1 in Taylor, Texas, and that wafer input for line construction is under way. The company is also reviewing additional expansion plans to meet future demand from global customers, including building new fabs. With demand for all processes at 8nm and below far exceeding supply, utilization rates for those processes are expected to exceed 100%.

**55 Trillion to 75 Trillion Won Left for Shareholder Returns; Larger Share of Preferred Buybacks**

On the financial side, the company said that of this year's total shareholder return package of 90 trillion to 110 trillion won, 55 trillion to 75 trillion won remains after excluding 35 trillion won (approximately $26.2 billion) already executed or confirmed by board resolution (including 30 trillion won in cash dividends). The remaining funds will be returned through cash dividends and a substantial amount of share buybacks and cancellations.

Chief Financial Officer Soon Cheol Park, a vice president, said in response to market concerns over the price gap between common and preferred shares, "In our buyback and cancellation plan, we will actively adjust the proportion of preferred share purchases to reflect market conditions." A new shareholder return policy to take effect from 2027, along with this year's final return amount and execution method, will be finalized at a board meeting at the end of January 2027.

**Market Impact**

The announcement is being read as a signal that the memory industry's earnings cycle has entered a phase of structural supply constraints rather than a simple boom. The outlook for a supply shortage through 2028 supports downside rigidity in DRAM and NAND prices while foreshadowing prolonged cost burdens for device makers. Indeed, Samsung Electronics acknowledged internally that its mobile business is under profitability pressure from rising memory prices.

A higher share of long-term contracts is a factor that lowers earnings volatility for memory makers. However, if contract prices fail to fully reflect spot price increases, it could limit profit maximization during upcycles. Raising its HBM share to the DRAM level shows Samsung Electronics' position is recovering in the competitive landscape, but after 2027, when rivals' HBM4 mass production expansion gets fully under way, its pricing power is expected to be tested again.
