For the first time in One UI beta history, Samsung has launched the latest beta program in all participant countries on day one.

Abhijeet Mishra / SamMobile

Samsung launched its first One UI beta in November 2018, giving Galaxy S9 and S9+ owners an early look at Samsung’s new interface and Android Pie. How early depended on where they lived.

South Korea, Germany, and the US were in the first wave. China, France, India, Poland, Spain, and the UK were on the list for later phases. The UK joined almost two weeks after launch, and it took nearly a month for the Galaxy S9 beta to reach India.

The list and the order changed over the years. Samsung dropped France and Spain after One UI 2.0, and it took China off the list with One UI 7. The UK moved into the first wave in recent years, while India and Poland regularly had to wait. Essentialy, every major Galaxy S beta left some eligible countries for a later round.

Samsung usually started the later markets on the latest build, which could already be the second or third beta elsewhere. As recently as May, Galaxy S26 owners in India and Poland waited about two weeks to join the One UI 9 beta and got the same build other markets were getting as Beta 2.

After years of asking for day-one access, Galaxy fans had little reason to think Samsung was listening.

That finally changed: On November 18, Samsung opened the One UI 9.5 beta for the Galaxy S26 series in Germany, India, Poland, South Korea, the UK, and the US. This is the first time a major Galaxy S beta opened in every eligible country on launch day.

There was obviously an upside to joining later: the newer build often came with fixes for bugs found in the first round. But with One UI 9.5, Galaxy S26 owners in India and Poland can be involved in the testing and reporting process from the start instead of waiting for others to do that in the first couple of weeks.

All that said, Apple has been giving iPhone owners around the world day-one access to iOS public betas for years. With a beta program that still covers just six countries, Samsung has a long way to go.

It needs to make day-one access standard for future One UI betas and give Galaxy owners in more countries a chance to join.

Fans will also want Samsung to take the same approach with stable updates. The Galaxy S26 got stable One UI 9 in South Korea nearly two weeks before beta testers in India and Europe could download it.

The eventual goal should be to get stable One UI updates to all supported Galaxy devices and markets on the same day. Hopefully, opening the One UI 9.5 beta in every participating country on day one is a step in that direction.
