Samsung Electronics has announced plans to begin sampling its next-generation HBM4E in the second half of 2026, as part of a broader strategy to accelerate its high-bandwidth memory (HBM) development cycle. This move reflects the company's effort to align with the annual release schedules of AI accelerators from key clients such as Nvidia and AMD, ensuring timely support for evolving AI infrastructure demands.

The HBM4E is the successor to Samsung's recently mass-produced HBM4, which delivers a consistent transfer speed of 11.7 gigabits-per-second (Gbps) and can be enhanced to 13Gbps. The HBM4E is expected to offer even higher performance, with a projected per-pin speed of 16Gbps and a total bandwidth of 4.0 terabytes-per-second (TB/s). These advancements are made possible through Samsung's use of its 6th-generation 10nm-class DRAM process and hybrid copper bonding (HCB) technology, which improves thermal efficiency and enables higher stacking layers.

Samsung's shift to a one-year product cycle for HBM development marks a significant departure from the traditional two-year cadence. This strategic adjustment is driven by rapid innovation in AI hardware and the need to maintain competitiveness in a market dominated by SK Hynix and Micron. The company's vertically integrated manufacturing model allows for tighter coordination across design, production, and packaging, enabling faster time-to-market for new HBM generations.

The HBM4E sampling is expected to be a key milestone in Samsung's roadmap, supporting its broader AI Factory initiative and strengthening its position in the AI memory market. With HBM demand projected to surge, Samsung's ability to deliver cutting-edge solutions at scale will be critical to its long-term growth and market share in the AI-driven semiconductor landscape.
