**The News:** Samsung SDS announced on November 2nd that it has broken ground on a dedicated AI data center in Gumi, Gyeongsangbuk-do <sup>[1]</sup>. The facility spans approximately 46,000 m² in Gongdan-dong, with an initial power capacity of 60 MW expandable in phases to 120 MW <sup>[1]</sup>. Construction, approved by Gumi City in late August 2026, targets completion by end of 2028 <sup>[1]</sup>. Samsung SDS disclosed the KRW 427.3 billion investment in a January regulatory filing and signed an MOU with Gyeongsangbuk-do and Gumi <sup>[1]</sup>. The groundbreaking is the first concrete outcome of Samsung's KRW 60 trillion Yeongnam regional investment plan, announced in July to build a 'global physical AI innovation cluster' <sup>[1]</sup>. CEO June-hee Lee stated the center will deliver 'AI infrastructure that customers can trust' <sup>[1]</sup>.

## Samsung SDS Bets KRW 427B on Sovereign AI Infrastructure

**Analysis:** Samsung SDS is making a calculated infrastructure bet timed to intersect two converging forces: Korea's national push to lead manufacturing AI, and a global channel ecosystem where AI consulting is the top-ranked growth driver at 86.7% <sup>[2]</sup>. The Gumi facility is not a speculative build; it is the first physical proof point of a KRW 60 trillion regional commitment <sup>[1]</sup>, and its 2028 completion date lands precisely when the base-case channel market reaches $36.4 billion <sup>[3]</sup>.

### A Regional Anchor for Korea's Physical AI Ambitions

The Gumi data center is the cornerstone of Samsung's broader Yeongnam cluster strategy, which spans humanoid robotics in Gumi, all-solid-state batteries in Ulsan, AI server package substrates in Busan, and advanced shipbuilding in Geoje <sup>[1]</sup>. By concentrating compute infrastructure at the heart of Korea's manufacturing belt, Samsung SDS is positioning itself as the default AI backbone for industries undergoing rapid automation. The facility will directly support Samsung affiliates' Robotics Transformation (RX) programs, providing the large-scale AI training compute required for robots performing precision tasks at manufacturing sites <sup>[1]</sup>. This captive demand provides a reliable utilization floor before external colocation revenue matures.

### Technical Architecture Built for Density and Flexibility

The center's hybrid cooling design separates air cooling for general server areas from liquid cooling in high-density GPU zones <sup>[1]</sup>, a configuration that directly addresses what industry analysis identifies as the emerging 'grid-to-chip' imperative for AI data center competitiveness <sup>[4]</sup>. Rack power densities are flexible, and the GPU-agnostic design accommodates various AI systems without locking customers into specific GPU vendors or service stacks <sup>[1]</sup>. Starting at 60 MW with a clear path to 120 MW <sup>[1]</sup>, the phased capacity model lets Samsung SDS match capital deployment to demand rather than over-building on day one. This architecture reflects 30 years of Samsung SDS data center operational experience applied to next-generation workload requirements.

### Dual-Mode Business Model Opens the External Channel

Samsung SDS will operate two parallel revenue streams from the Gumi facility: its own GPU infrastructure delivering managed AI services, and a colocation model allowing external customers to install and operate their own GPUs <sup>[1]</sup>. This structure is well-matched to current channel partner priorities. Data center rollout is a top-ranked service growth driver, cited by 60.9% of channel partners <sup>[2]</sup>, while cloud infrastructure including IaaS and PaaS is cited by 58.8% <sup>[2]</sup>. With 52% of channel partners describing themselves as 'leading edge' in AI readiness <sup>[2]</sup>, the addressable partner ecosystem is both large and confident enough to engage with a new sovereign AI infrastructure provider. The channel market's 36% CAGR from 2022 to 2029 <sup>[3]</sup> provides a durable macro tailwind for Samsung SDS's external services ambitions through and beyond the facility's 2028 launch.
