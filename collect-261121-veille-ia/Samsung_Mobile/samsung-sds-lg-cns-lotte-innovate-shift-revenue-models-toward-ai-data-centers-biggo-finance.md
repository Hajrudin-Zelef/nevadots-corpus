# Samsung SDS, LG CNS, Lotte Innovate Shift Revenue Models Toward AI Data Centers

South Korea's major IT services companies are making AI data centers their next growth engine, shifting their business structures from one-off projects toward long-term operating revenue. They are broadening beyond design and construction into operations and maintenance, colocation and GPU leasing in a bid to secure a recurring revenue base.

Samsung SDS has begun building a 60-megawatt (MW) AI data center in Gumi, North Gyeongsang Province. The company will invest 427.3 billion won (approximately $318.3 million) on the site of Samsung Electronics' former Gumi Plant 1, completing 60MW by the end of 2028 and later expanding to as much as 120MW. That 60MW is equivalent to the electricity consumed in a year by 150,000 Seoul households. The center will serve as a computing base supporting manufacturing AI and robotics transformation (RX) demand from Samsung affiliates. It will use hybrid cooling—liquid cooling for power-hungry, heat-generating GPU servers and air cooling for general servers.

The business model has two tracks: a service that secures GPUs in-house and rents computing resources to companies, and colocation that provides space, power and cooling facilities for customer-owned equipment. Samsung SDS plans to combine these with cloud and AI platforms to capture corporate customers' AI transformation demand.

It is also widening its footprint in public infrastructure. A consortium led by Samsung SDS broke ground in August on the National AI Computing Center at Solaseado in Haenam, South Jeolla Province. The project, with total investment of 2.5 trillion won (approximately $1.9 billion), will build computing infrastructure with 15,000 AI chips by 2028, supplying computing resources to startups, universities and research institutions. An early service using part of the west building of the Dongtan data center is also slated to launch in April next year. Samsung SDS was recently selected as the preferred bidder for a design-build-operate (DBO) project for a data center of more than 200MW being pursued by Pacific Asset Management.

In an earnings conference call on Oct. 29, Samsung SDS said it will "expand AI infrastructure to more than 800MW by 2031 by combining direct capital investment, equity investment and DBO projects," adding that "securing a cumulative 500MW in DBO projects in particular is expected to generate annual revenue of more than 1 trillion won (approximately $744.9 million)." Lee Jun-hee, president of Samsung SDS, said, "We will bring together our accumulated data center construction and operating experience with our cloud and AI technology capabilities to build AI infrastructure that customers can trust and use."

LG CNS has delivered results overseas. On Nov. 1 it held a completion ceremony for a 30MW hyperscale AI data center in Jakarta, Indonesia. It is the first case of a South Korean IT services company directly building an AI data center for an overseas customer on site. The client is KMG, a joint venture between Indonesia's Sinarmas Group and Korea Investment Real Asset Management; the project was worth about 100 billion won (approximately $74.5 million) when the order was won in August last year. The 11-story facility has a gross floor area of 46,281 square meters and can accommodate more than 100,000 servers.

The center is designed to supply more than 300kW of power per rack—a high-density environment roughly 60 times that of a typical data center rack. The first phase uses air cooling, with liquid cooling to be added in follow-on phases. The power grid is redundant, allowing uninterrupted operation on backup power even if the main supply is cut. Notably, tenants were secured for the full capacity before completion.

The project applied the "One LG" integrated solution combining the capabilities of LG Group affiliates. It pairs LG CNS's DBO capabilities with LG Electronics' HVAC systems and LG Energy Solution's batteries to provide integrated power, cooling and energy infrastructure. Once follow-on phases are complete, total power capacity will rise to 400MW, above the original target of 220MW. The company plans to use this as a springboard to expand into the Asia-Pacific region, including Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines and Japan.

Hyun Shin-kyun, president of LG CNS, said, "Completing the Indonesia AI data center is a meaningful achievement that demonstrates our business competitiveness in the global market by providing overseas customers with the data center capabilities we built up in South Korea." He added, "Using this as a bridgehead, we will expand our AI data center and AI factory business in global markets, including the Asia-Pacific region, where AI computing demand is surging."

Lotte Innovate is increasing DBO and long-term outsourced operation orders from external customers. Based on roughly 20 years of experience building and operating four of its own data centers, the company is broadening its customer base toward financial firms and asset managers. It won the outsourced operation of the CamSquare data center in December last year, and in February this year secured a DBO project for a 9.9MW edge data center from IGIS Asset Management. In September it signed an outsourced operation contract for the roughly 40MW Ansan Global Cloud Center and will operate it for five years after completion next year.

According to a September report by IBK Investment & Securities, Lotte Innovate's cumulative data center orders total about 130 billion won (approximately $96.8 million), with 110MW under management. The company has set a target of raising DBO to about 30% of total revenue by 2028. It recently signed a memorandum of understanding with Vietnam's CMC Group for data center cooperation and is also considering entering the Japanese market. However, it has no confirmed new orders overseas yet.

What the three companies have in common is their revenue structure. Traditional systems integration (SI) business concentrates revenue during the project period, whereas data centers generate continuous revenue from operations and maintenance contracts, colocation and GPU services after completion. As asset managers and investment institutions enter data center development, there are also more ways to participate in construction and operation without directly owning facilities.

There is also the burden of managing profitability. Direct investment can generate service revenue using facilities and GPUs, but carries heavy initial capital costs and depreciation. DBO and outsourced operations, by contrast, reduce the asset burden, but profitability depends on order competition and contract terms. Kim Dong-woo, an analyst at Kyobo Securities, said, "In its AI data center business, LG CNS is choosing an expansion strategy that prioritizes certain demand and profitability based on its track record in modular data centers and AI factory construction," adding, "Revenue related to the AI factory reference site starting in the second half of 2027 is not reflected in current earnings forecasts, so revenue and profit estimates are expected to be revised upward once the business details take shape."

Industry observers believe the ability to generate stable operating revenue matched to customer demand, rather than simply how many data centers a company secures, will determine competitiveness going forward. As demand for AI infrastructure grows rapidly, South Korean IT services companies with strengths in design and construction are expected to keep widening their business scope into operations and GPU services for the time being.
