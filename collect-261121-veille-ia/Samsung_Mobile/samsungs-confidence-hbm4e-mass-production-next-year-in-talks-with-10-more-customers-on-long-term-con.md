During a conference call held after its earnings announcement on the 29th, Samsung Electronics highlighted its growing share of the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) market. Third-quarter HBM4 revenue increased at least threefold from the previous quarter. Samsung was also assessed to have achieved an HBM market share in the third quarter on a par with its share of the overall DRAM market. Counterpoint Research, a market research firm, put Samsung Electronics’ global DRAM market share in the second quarter of this year at 38%. According to Counterpoint Research, second-quarter HBM market share ranked SK hynix first at 50%, followed by Samsung Electronics at 33%. Kim Jae-jun, executive vice president and head of memory strategy marketing at Samsung Electronics, said, “HBM4E has also been receiving positive evaluations since we shipped the world’s first samples in May.” He added, “Our 2027 supply volume is already fully booked following price negotiations at target levels, but major customers continue to request additional increases in HBM supply.” Samsung Electronics said it plans to begin mass production of HBM4E in the first half of next year and expand output in the second half.

Regarding LTAs, the company said, “We have already completed long-term supply agreements with 10 large customers. The number of customers seeking to secure substantial volumes of memory is also growing, and we are negotiating multiyear plans with another 10 customers.” This suggested that the number of LTA customers, which the company said was five last quarter, would rise to at least 10. Some in the market also expect an LTA with Apple Inc. and others to be imminent.

Meanwhile, the foundry and System LSI businesses, which had been posting losses, are also seeing a turnaround in their results. Kang Suk-chae, executive vice president and head of foundry strategy marketing, said, “The number of projects secured for the 2-nanometer process this year has at least doubled year over year.” He added, “Next year, high-performance computing (HPC) will account for more than 50% of total foundry revenue.” Kang said, “Cleanroom operations and equipment installation have begun at the Taylor fab in Texas, U.S., and we are reviewing options to expand production capacity, including building an additional fab.”

Kang said, “Collaboration between the foundry and memory businesses helped us achieve HBM4 performance at the industry’s highest level.” He added, “Despite being in the early stages of mass production, we have achieved a base-die yield among the best in the industry.” The System LSI Business reported that revenue is growing as new SoC and sensor models enter the market in the second half of the year, and that its custom SoC business is taking shape through partnerships with new customers and project wins.

Business segments related to artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure thus reported positive results. However, even within Samsung Electronics, segments with a higher share of revenue from consumer products gave a negative outlook.

Samsung Display said revenue and profit in its small- and mid-sized display business increased from the previous quarter, as it delivered new products to major customers on schedule in the third quarter. However, it forecast that weak demand would persist in 2027 amid continued market uncertainty due to rising product prices.

The DX (Device eXperience) Division, which makes finished products, was hit across all its business units. The MX (Mobile eXperience) Business reported revenue growth from both the previous quarter and the same period a year earlier, helped by products including the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8, but recorded a quarterly loss of 600 billion won due to cost pressures, including for memory chips. The loss narrowed from the previous quarter as sales of premium products increased. Daniel Araujo, an executive at the MX Business, said, “Since the second half began, the business environment has become more difficult as the low-end smartphone market has contracted and competition has intensified.” He added, “In 2027, we will improve our profit structure by focusing on top-tier models.”

The TV and home appliance businesses also continued to post losses, recording an operating loss of 200 billion won, up from the previous quarter. In home appliances, results deteriorated under pressure from rising raw material prices and logistics costs, despite strong sales driven by stepped-up marketing in Korea and the expansion of subscriptions and business-to-business (B2B) sales.

Harman, which operates in the automotive electronics business, reported quarterly revenue of 4.3 trillion won, a significant increase from the same period a year earlier, and operating profit of 600 billion won. Minseong Hwang, research director at Counterpoint Research, said, “Samsung Electronics’ MX Business will prioritize improving its product mix, focusing on high-end models with higher margins.”
