Samsung rolled out the One UI 9 update for the Galaxy S26 series in late September. It then expanded the rollout to include its older flagship and mid-range devices. Now, less than two months later, it has begun public testing of One UI 9.5, the next major update to its Android skin. The beta program is now live for the Galaxy S26 series.

Samsung typically launches its One UI beta program in the US, UK, and South Korea in the initial wave, and then expands it to India and Germany. But this time around, the One UI 9.5 beta program is live in all six countries simultaneously: the US, UK, South Korea, India, Germany, and Poland.

As always, if you live in one of the eligible countries, you can join the One UI 9.5 beta program by tapping the banner in the Samsung Members app. After you enroll in the program, check for updates on your Galaxy S26 to download the OTA. Slots are limited, so join the program as soon as possible.

Based on early user reports, it appears that there are two updates this time around. As soon as you install the initial 3.6GB One UI 9.5 firmware update and restart your phone, a second, 540 MB update appears for download.

While One UI 9.5 is still based on Android 17, it brings several meaningful improvements, including UI refinements, a proactive Bixby assistant, a smarter Now Brief, and more.

Early hands-on impressions of the skin from Samsung fan @tarunvats33 shows a new lock screen-to-home transition animation.

In a newsroom post, Samsung has also highlighted some of the new One UI 9.5 features. This includes enhanced Pro and Pro video modes with customizable controls and presets, Mindful Note and Medications in Samsung Health, and App Lock for individual apps.

Ice Universe also shared the full One UI 9.5 release notes for the Korean build.

## One UI 9.5 won't release until early next year

While Samsung has started testing One UI 9.5, don't expect it to roll out until January or February next year. The stable build will likely debut on the Galaxy S27 series before reaching the Galaxy S26 and other Galaxy flagships in the weeks that follow.

The first beta may have some bugs and issues, but Samsung should address them in subsequent builds. Expect the company to roll out at least two or three firmware updates before the end of this year. If you use the Galaxy S26 as your daily driver, consider holding off on the beta until Samsung irons out the biggest issues.

While installing the beta firmware won’t erase data from your Galaxy S26, rolling back to the stable version will reset your phone and wipe its data.
