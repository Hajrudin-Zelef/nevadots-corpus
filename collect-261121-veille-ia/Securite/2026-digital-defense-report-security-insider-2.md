Cybersecurity has always been a contest of time, information, and access. AI is changing all three.


The 2026 Microsoft Digital Defense Report reveals a security environment where attack timelines are compressing, sophisticated capabilities are becoming more accessible, and agentic systems are beginning to automate more of the attack chain. The volume of reported vulnerabilities is also accelerating. Nearly 40,000 CVEs were published in the first half of 2026, putting the year on track to roughly double. And those are only publicly reported vulnerabilities. AI is accelerating vulnerability discovery and compressing portions of the attack chain from days to seconds.


This is one of the factors changing the protection equation. AI is helping researchers and defenders find weaknesses faster, while threat actors are using the same technology to increase the speed and scale of their operations. Taken together, these changes point to something larger than a new set of AI-enabled threats. AI is changing the physics of cybersecurity.


The implications run in both directions. Threat actors are using AI to increase speed, scale, and consistency. Security teams can use many of the same advances to discover risk earlier, correlate signals faster, investigate continuously, and respond at machine speed.


This poses an important question for security leaders: How do we make that shift without losing sight of the fundamentals that remain critical to preventing and containing attacks?
