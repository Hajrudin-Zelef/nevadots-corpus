A cyberattack has disrupted internal European Commission systems for more than 10 days, impacting critical administrative functions including payroll, leave requests and medical reimbursements, based on information published by Security Affairs.

The Computer Emergency Response Team for the European Union detected the activity and identified ARTEX, an AI-powered penetration-testing tool, as a potential instrument used in the intrusion. The attack affected several platforms, including the HR system Sysper and the medical reimbursement portal MyPMO. While the Commission has restored most services, the full extent of the damage and whether sensitive staff data was compromised remains under investigation. Although the original developer removed the ARTEX project from its repository in October, independent copies remain available for malicious use. This incident follows previous security challenges for the institution, including a significant breach in March that resulted in the theft of approximately 91.7 GB of data. Under European Union regulations, the institution is required to report confirmed personal data breaches to the European Data Protection Supervisor within 72 hours. However, officials have provided limited details to staff as they continue to assess the scope of the unauthorized access and determine if personal information was exfiltrated before systems were secured.
