Russian tech firm Yandex has managed to salvage around 97.16 percent of customer data on its cloud platform following a series of drone strikes on its data centers.

As reported by Reuters, the Russian cloud and search company said that "the remaining 2.84 percent cannot be recovered due to critical damage to equipment and the absence of backups on the platform by customers."

Three Yandex data centers were hit in a series of drone strikes in October, including the company's Vladimir data center; the Yandex DC Sasovo, which suffered a fire, bringing operations down at the facility; and the company's data center in Kaluga.

The Sasovo site houses two of Yandex's supercomputers, but the company said these were not damaged and had been relocated.

Yandex has said that, moving forward, it will enable backup by default to prevent data loss, and that "engineers continue to distribute Yandex Cloud computing resources across new independent locations in different regions of the Russian Federation. The number of locations will continue to increase."

The drone strikes were conducted in retaliation by Ukraine. The conflict, which began with Russia's illegal invasion of Ukraine, is now in its fifth year. Throughout the conflict, Russia has carried out strikes that have hit data centers in Ukraine. Multiple Ukrainian data centers have been hit in recent months.

Facilities in Ukraine operated by Parkovyi Data Center, MiroHost, Datagroup, Vodafone Ukraine, De Novo, Cosmonova, Omega Telecom, and Ukrtelecom have all suffered damage from drone strikes.
